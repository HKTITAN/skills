# Where and how do we compete?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: the Strategy files name Ghemawat, Barbara Minto and Porter and Kramer in their text, credit Porter in two titles and Christensen in one, and use Ansoff, BCG and GE as labels on a matrix; they name no other author, no professor, no course and no source for any figure. Company examples are the files' own. Two pairs of these files disagree, on whether Southwest is a cost leader and on whether incumbents hold their ground; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## How attractive is the industry?

- The five forces framework reads an industry's competitive intensity and profitability from five forces: the threat of new entrants, supplier power, buyer power, the threat of substitutes and rivalry among existing competitors; the file's title credits Porter. (Strategy: Porter's Five Forces, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-porters-five-forces.md)
- High entry barriers mean profitable incumbents and low barriers a contestable market; the file's example is aircraft manufacturing, a Boeing and Airbus duopoly, against consulting with low barriers. (Strategy: Porter's Five Forces, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-porters-five-forces.md)
- Buyers gain power when a few large buyers dominate, products are commoditised, switching is easy and cheap, buyers can integrate backward, the purchase is a significant cost, or the product is undifferentiated. (Strategy: Porter's Five Forces, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-porters-five-forces.md)
- Under limitations and extensions the file lists four points: the analysis is static, the forces interact in ways not captured separately, network effects and platform dynamics do not fit the traditional framework, and modern extensions add complementors. (Strategy: Porter's Five Forces, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-porters-five-forces.md)
- The value chain splits a firm into five primary activities, which are inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service, and four support activities, which are procurement, technology development, human resources and firm infrastructure. (Strategy: Value Chain Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-value-chain-analysis.md)
- Profit margin is total value less the total cost of primary and support activities, and advantage comes from raising perceived value, reducing costs, or optimising the whole value system from supplier to customer. (Strategy: Value Chain Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-value-chain-analysis.md)

## Which position do we take?

- The generic strategies are three positions a firm can adopt: cost leadership, differentiation, or focus; the file's title credits Porter. (Strategy: Generic Strategies, Porter, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-generic-strategies-porter.md)
- The generic strategies file lists Southwest Airlines as an example of cost leadership, with no frills, rapid turnaround and secondary airports. (Strategy: Generic Strategies, Porter, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-generic-strategies-porter.md)
- The blue ocean file lists Southwest Airlines as a blue ocean, where value innovation achieves low cost and differentiation together, and gives its result as "Disrupted aviation industry". (Strategy: Blue Ocean Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-blue-ocean-strategy.md)
- Differentiation offers unique value that customers are willing to pay a premium for; its listed risks are that premium pricing attracts competitors, the differentiation must be defensible against imitation, the market can be limited in size, imitation erodes the advantage over time, and it needs continuous innovation. (Strategy: Generic Strategies, Porter, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-generic-strategies-porter.md)
- The four actions framework finds value innovation by asking what to eliminate, what to reduce below industry standards, what to raise above them, and what to create that the industry has never offered. (Strategy: Blue Ocean Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/frameworks-blue-ocean-strategy.md)
- A sustainable competitive advantage is a defensible position that allows returns above the cost of capital over years, and the file groups barriers to imitation as structural, resource-based and behavioural. (Strategy: Sustainable Competitive Advantage, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/competitive-sustainable-competitive-advantage.md)
- Dynamic capabilities are three abilities, sensing market shifts, seizing opportunities by mobilising resources, and transforming the organisation, and the file says they matter because static competitive advantage erodes. (Strategy: Dynamic Capabilities, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/competitive-dynamic-capabilities.md)

## How far do we stretch?

- Related diversification enters businesses that share capabilities, customers or supply chains with the core; unrelated diversification owns a portfolio of businesses with no obvious synergies. (Strategy: Diversification and Scope, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/corporate-diversification-and-scope.md)
- The file states as a finding that diversified companies often trade below the sum of their parts and gives four reasons: investors diversify better themselves through index funds, conglomerate managers allocate capital poorly, complex companies are hard to value, and corporate overhead erodes profitability. (Strategy: Diversification and Scope, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/corporate-diversification-and-scope.md)
- Revenue synergies are the hardest to achieve and cost synergies are easier to quantify but take longer to realise; the valuation discipline ends with paying no more than standalone value plus synergy value. (Strategy: Diversification and Scope, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/corporate-diversification-and-scope.md)
- The Ansoff matrix rates market penetration as lowest risk, product development and market development as moderate risk, and diversification as highest risk. (Strategy: Diversification and Scope, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/corporate-diversification-and-scope.md)
- The AAA framework, which the file credits to Ghemawat, gives three approaches to international strategy: adaptation to local markets, aggregation through standardisation, and arbitrage of differences between markets. (Strategy: International Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/corporate-international-strategy.md)
- The CAGE framework, also credited to Ghemawat, measures cultural, administrative, geographic and economic distance; high distance calls for greater adaptation, partnership and localisation, and low distance for more aggregation, direct investment and standardisation. (Strategy: International Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/corporate-international-strategy.md)
- In an alliance, higher control through equity ownership requires higher investment but reduces opportunism risk; the file's governance modes are an equity joint venture, a contractual joint venture, and minority equity of below 50% ownership with a board seat or veto rights. (Strategy: Strategic Alliances, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/corporate-strategic-alliances.md)

## What do platforms and disruption change?

- A pipeline business makes a product and sells it, owning inventory; a platform business builds infrastructure for users to transact, owns no inventory, and its users create the value through interaction. (Strategy: Platform Business Models, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/innovation-platform-business-models.md)
- The platform file says incumbent dominance "is hard to overcome", that the first mover to critical mass often wins, and that new platforms must target underserved niches. (Strategy: Platform Business Models, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/innovation-platform-business-models.md)
- The disruption file says incumbents win sustaining innovation but lose to disruptive innovation, because they are locked into high-margin, high-performance positioning; its title credits Christensen. (Strategy: Disruptive Innovation, Christensen, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/innovation-disruptive-innovation-christensen.md)
- The platform file gives Google about 90% market share in search, and commissions on transaction value of 20 to 30% for Uber and 10 to 15% for eBay; it names no source or date for these. (Strategy: Platform Business Models, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/innovation-platform-business-models.md)
- Incumbents ignore a disruptive entrant for four listed reasons: margins in the new market are low, it cannibalises existing revenue, it does not fit the high-margin customers, and rational decision-making favours ignoring the threat at first. (Strategy: Disruptive Innovation, Christensen, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/innovation-disruptive-innovation-christensen.md)
- The file's three responses for an incumbent are to launch a separate division with different management, metrics and P&L, to redefine what good enough means, and to position for adjacent markets. (Strategy: Disruptive Innovation, Christensen, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/innovation-disruptive-innovation-christensen.md)
- A business model has four parts, value proposition, value creation, value delivery and value capture, and the file lists four types of innovation in it: subscription, freemium, direct-to-consumer, and licensing of IP. (Strategy: Business Model Innovation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/innovation-business-model-innovation.md)

## How do we run it?

- Scenarios are built in four steps: identify the key uncertainties, identify the driving forces and set a 2x2 matrix from two key uncertainties, write a coherent narrative for each scenario, and test the strategy against each. (Strategy: Scenario Planning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/innovation-scenario-planning.md)
- The hypothesis-driven approach develops and tests hypotheses instead of gathering data without direction, MECE means mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive, and the pyramid principle, which the file credits to Barbara Minto, puts the main recommendation at the top above 3 supporting key points. (Strategy: Strategy Consulting Methodology, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/implementation-strategy-consulting-methodology.md)
- The balanced scorecard tracks four perspectives, financial, customer, internal processes, and learning and growth; the file sets reviews quarterly for operational metrics, annually for the full strategy, and every 3 to 5 years for a fundamental reassessment. (Strategy: Strategy Execution & Governance, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/competitive-strategy-execution-governance.md)
- The file says the stakeholder approach often creates higher long-term value and may reduce short-term profitability; it credits shared value, creating economic value while addressing societal challenges, to Porter and Kramer. (Strategy: Stakeholder Theory & ESG, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/implementation-stakeholder-theory-esg.md)
- Market power is the ability to sustain prices above the competitive level without losing customers, and the file lists a market share above 70% as "often presumed" to indicate it, naming no jurisdiction; its five types of anticompetitive conduct are predatory pricing, exclusive dealing, tying, refusal to deal, and platform favouritism. (Strategy: Competition Policy & Regulation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/strategy/rules/antitrust-competition-policy-regulation.md)

# What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda

Typed notes from an orientation talk by Tarun Davda, managing director at Z47 and an ISB alumnus. He covered the Indian venture landscape, when venture capital is the wrong money, what he looks for in a founder and what a pitch deck should show.

## What was said

### His background and Z47

- Tarun Davda says he spent eight years at Infosys and then built a dating app that was hard to monetise, realised it was too early for India and sold it to Match.com.
- He says 70 to 80% of Z47's investments are made at the idea stage, and 10 to 20% when the company has 1 to 2 crore rupees of revenue.

### India's venture landscape

- He says Indian markets have deepened and will keep deepening, and making money in businesses will become significantly easier.
- He says the quality of entrepreneurs in India is better than in China, and contrasts the two markets as digging in oil and digging in hard rock.
- He says successful entrepreneurs in China and the US came from large technology companies and had seen the playbook for launching hyper-growth startups.
- He says whether experience is an advantage depends on the industry. In B2B and industrial businesses it is an advantage.
- He says building a fintech company without having managed a financial cycle is a train wreck.
- He says that over the next seven to ten years India will go through the journey China went through from 2001 to 2007.
- He says India entered Covid with 2 to 3% digital penetration and came out with 8 to 9%, four to five years of growth in a short time.
- He lists the shifts as Jio and demonetisation in 2016, GST in 2017, UPI 2.0 in 2018, Covid in 2020, and the manufacturing push from 2022 to 2024.
- He says about 5% of India's market cap is digital today, and predicts 20 to 30% in the next decade from earlier US data.

### Is venture capital the right money

- He says a business of 50 to 100 crore rupees is not a success for a VC firm.
- He says a founder should ask whether they are building a business that can reach over 100 million dollars of revenue and be an IPO candidate. If not, raise from angels or micro VCs.
- He says the last thing a founder wants is an investor with a very different vision of what success looks like.
- His example is a consumer brand with 100 crore of revenue, valued at 300 to 500 crore, in which a VC owns 20%. The VC does not count that as a success.
- He says that of ten cheques a VC writes, only two or three generate the money.
- He ranks funding sources as customer money first, friends and family second and angel funding third.
- He says angels are happy to give 1 crore and take back 3 crore.
- He compares the relationship with a VC to a healthy marriage that needs proper alignment.

### What he looks for in a founder

- He says an investor looks for the founder, and 99% of the decision is based on the founder.
- He asks whether the founder has a unique insight, something nobody else in that space has told him.
- He says not to argue that you only need 0.1% of the market.
- He asks for the right to play and the right to win, and wants to be shown the right to win first.
- He asks whether the founder has a background in the industry, such as 10,000 hours spent in the space, and a unique reason to be building this.
- He says investors want to see fire, someone with a deep purpose who will break through a wall.
- He says there will be many days, nights and months with zero progress.

### Conviction and co-founders

- He says to take the time to build conviction before committing.
- He says nine out of ten founders he meets think inside out, projecting a problem they have onto the whole world.
- He says to ask real questions, including of former employees and founders of startups that failed in the space.
- He says not to take a superficial online view of the market size, but to understand customers' buying patterns and what makes them miserable.
- He says that once you commit you will be biased, so put the effort into research up front instead of one or two years of struggle.
- He says not to look only for what you want to hear.
- He says a team needs someone who can sell or someone who can build, and to try to bring in a co-founder.
- He says partnering with a stranger is a recipe for disaster, and that co-founder conflicts are well documented.

### Pitch deck

- He says a deck should show that you understand the market structure, the value chain and the margin at each point in the value chain.
- He says the deck should show your understanding of the problem and the solution you are going after.
- He says to keep the deck simple enough to explain to a five year old.
- He says the deck should give the unit economics of the business and where you think you can get to in 12 or 18 months.

## Examples and cases used

- His own dating app: too early for India, hard to monetise, sold to Match.com.
- A consumer brand at 100 crore rupees of revenue: a good business that still does not count as a win for a VC holding 20%.

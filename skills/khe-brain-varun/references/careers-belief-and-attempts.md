# Careers, Why Belief Matters and attempts

This is what he tells an individual about their own path: decide, go all in on one thing, take a risky public attempt, and turn belief into repeated action. Most of it comes from Kata 7, Kata 4 and the belief talk, with support from Kata 8, Kata 9, Secret Kata 1 and PGX Ideas 2. Much of it was said to rooms of his own Aeos employees, so some advice against leaving, freelancing and keeping options open is given by an employer to his staff, and he says so himself in places.

## Why do your twenties feel so confusing?

- Confusion is the mark of the years from 20 to 27 or 28, sometimes 30, and he includes himself in that. He calls it the greatest problem facing young people today. (Kata 7 at 0:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=55s)
- Young people **hyper gamble** because the standard path takes 30 years. Salaries stay standard while house prices shoot up, so the SIP math says you can afford a house at 50, and nobody wants to be rich at 50. He says he fully empathizes. (Kata 7 at 1:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=101s)
- Some hyper gambling is the good kind, such as an ambitious startup, but the math works for the investor and not for you. One in 100 works, which is fine for a VC funding 100 startups and may not be ideal for the founder. Penny stocks are the other kind. (Kata 7 at 1:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=101s)
- The world confuses you as a side effect of everyone fighting for your attention. Thousands of ads and pieces of content push narratives that contradict each other, and your job is to avoid being affected. (Kata 7 at 2:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=150s)
- He relays Peter Thiel's four quadrants: the definite optimist has a plan and builds, the indefinite optimist does **credential collection** and hopes for luck, the definite pessimist builds bunkers or in India dies rich on fixed deposits, and the indefinite pessimist doom scrolls. Only one of the four builds anything. (Kata 7 at 4:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=293s)
- Career wants picked up from scrolling are not real. People tell him they really want a role they have never tried, like wildlife photographer, and he thinks they only want to be different. He calls this **infinite scroll brain**. (Kata 7 at 27:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1635s)
- Clarity is fake. Nobody knows the right answer, so you just have to decide, and the moment you make the call you are above 99% of the population. (Kata 7 at 24:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1442s)
- Nobody knows which degree is right, and the best at anything wins. The best B.Com graduate beats the average B.Tech, the best person with no degree beats an IITian at something they care about, and the average dancer makes zero while the top names make crores. (Kata 7 at 22:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1346s)

## Should you keep your options open or pick one thing?

- Keeping all your options open gives you the least impact. Every extra option is a tax on willpower, and he says he can tell in appraisal talks when someone is keeping every door open. (Kata 7 at 8:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=485s)
- His line on it to his employees: "It looks safe, but optionality is a type of poison." (Kata 7 at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=629s)
- He relays Greg McKeown's Essentialism: indecision is the undisciplined pursuit of more, and the cure is elimination. If something is not a nine out of ten, it is a no. (Kata 7 at 8:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=534s)
- He relays Gary Keller's question from The One Thing: what one thing can you do such that doing it makes everything else easier or unnecessary. (Kata 7 at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=629s)
- Do not drop your five interests. Find the one thing that makes them all possible. He says Aeos was built this way: everything it built was something it needed right then, never an attractive idea. (Kata 7 at 11:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=676s)
- Confusion and diversification are different. Confusion is five interesting things with no impact, scale or P&L on any. Diversification is one thing working very well plus a second that does not make the first fall, so the test is whether doing the second costs you the first. (Kata 7 at 11:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=676s; Kata 7 at 12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=725s; Kata 7 at 16:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=964s)
- He admits he does several things today while calling optionality poison. He separates the two by calling his own case diversification, and says that at 21 or 22 he was in the middle of one startup and did one thing at a time. (Kata 7 at 8:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=485s)
- Pick one thing, even at random, and become the all-in expert, and opportunities come to you. If you only care about IT networking for the company, people trust you as the expert, and then you have the clients and cash flow for the next five projects. (Kata 7 at 20:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1252s)
- Companies trust confused people less and give them smaller roles. You go all in first and prove it, then you get what you ask for. Asking for the role first and promising to drop everything after only works if you already have leverage. (Kata 7 at 19:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1156s)
- Stacking prestige internships and degrees is a status game dressed up as choice. Internships at three big tech names would be a complete waste, and one is enough. (Kata 7 at 24:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1490s)
- His anti-confusion playbook starts with sorting decisions into one-way and two-way doors, which he takes from Jeff Bezos. Decide reversible ones in five minutes and take weeks or months on irreversible ones. Then set personal OKRs with just one objective and three key results. (Kata 7 at 28:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1730s)
- The rest of the playbook: give any field a 60-day curiosity sprint and then commit or reject, because every field is exciting for two weeks. Make no decision without an experiment. Follow a rule of three, shipping three serious projects before deciding. (Kata 7 at 28:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1730s)
- After three jobs, more sampling does not help. Rapid switching is fine up to three, and if the second is good you should stick. You will never enjoy anything 100%, so enough is enough. (Kata 7 at 32:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1970s)
- In November 2026 he said that for the 30th time that month someone wanting to join told him they might join, or do content, or start a startup. You cannot have belief while even considering five things at once, and he blames Instagram and the first era with so much optionality, not the people. (Why Belief Matters at 31:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1910s; Why Belief Matters at 32:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1956s)

## How should you judge a job, a company or freelancing?

- Choose growth rate over absolute scale. A fast-growing startup has excess money and unclaimed responsibility you can simply take, while a large slow company or the government has people who already hold everything. (Kata 7 at 34:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=2065s)
- He calls a rapidly scaling company an **exponential train** and says leaving one is the stupidest thing you can do. His example is an Aeos client, Emergent, that went from zero to about $50 million quickly. (Kata 7 at 34:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=2065s)
- When something is growing very fast, stay on board. Almost everyone in one early AVTV team photo left for a 10% raise or to start something, and those who stayed did very well. He calls such a company a **slipstream** and says they are very hard to find. (Kata 1 at 1:05:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3917s)
- Never judge a job by what it pays now. Take a two-year view of which company and market will do better: 30,000 a month at a finance company in a growing market can beat 60,000 selling monitors in a capped market. (Kata 9 at 28:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1680s)
- His two-year job comparison: for each option write the best case, the most likely case, the worst case and the tail risk in two years, put them side by side on paper, then ask ChatGPT deep research which outcome is more probable and why. (Kata 9 at 30:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1824s)
- People overweight the downside of committing and underweight compounding. In a growing company salary bands rise fast, Aeos pays talent two or three times what it paid at the start, and relationships, skills and internal brand all compound. (Kata 7 at 25:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1539s)
- Take the compounding option over the immediate one. He sets a full-time 1% profit share at a fast-growing company making 1 crore a year against three freelance projects at 2 lakhs a month each, and says the first compounds better because freelancers lose a project or two every month. (Why Belief Matters at 34:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2053s)
- He calls freelancing a trap because you absorb risk a job shields you from. Clients ask for endless changes, delay invoices for months or run away, and you need three or four projects for steady income, which turns you into a salesperson. (Kata 7 at 13:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=821s)
- Freelancing for small startups is a bigger risk than freelancers realise. He says 99% of small startups will die next year, so a freelancer there may be making money while burning years of career. His fix is 5, 10 or 15 clients and moving to bigger companies quickly. (Kata 9 at 31:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1918s)
- After arguing against freelancing to his own employees, he says that if you go all in on freelancing the downsides may not be so bad and it may be right for you, and that he is not the person to tell them which to choose. (Kata 7 at 26:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1587s)
- Before freelancing or starting a company in India, have at least 10 lakhs in the bank as a shock absorber. He always had 10 lakhs from his freelancing days, which he says meant at least a year where he would be fine, and he calls taking risk without shock absorption suicidal. (Kata 9 at 41:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2489s; Kata 9 at 42:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2536s)
- Your growth is capped by your boss's level, because a manager will not let someone below them exceed it. You join a manager, not a company. (Kata 4 at 50:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3041s)
- He says video editing is close to a guaranteed career that pays a few lakhs a year and can reach double-digit lakhs quickly, while software engineering feels safe and is very risky given the number of engineers against jobs. In the same talk he says a great editor may not be hireable in 10 to 20 years. (Kata 9 at 24:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1490s; Kata 9 at 39:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2346s)

## What gets you hired and paid?

- Pay follows leverage, and leverage follows proof. Some people are so good that the company has to give whatever they ask. If you do not prove it, you will always feel underpaid. (Kata 8 at 28:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1733s)
- Belief without proven outcomes does not earn a salary. He told a candidate whose last two companies were unprofitable that none of his outcomes proved his number, and that the minute he proved it he would make far more. (Kata 4 at 9:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=584s)
- Entry-level people are hired for potential and senior people for proven expertise. The biggest mistake is to be high potential but **pre-proof** and ask for a senior salary. (Kata 8 at 29:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1780s)
- Age and family duties do not justify a salary. He says no company in India cares that you are 32 with a wife and kids, only about the value you drive, and with proof a 21-year-old and a 31-year-old command the same. (Kata 8 at 30:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1828s)
- Believing in your potential without trying is a safety blanket. The minute you try, you meet the challenges everyone else met, and companies hire for that learning. (Kata 8 at 28:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1687s)
- A portfolio makes college and past employer irrelevant for visible skills. The minute you have one in a visual medium, you are ahead of almost everybody. (Kata 8 at 13:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=819s)
- Show up publicly every week or few weeks while you need nothing, so you are known three years later when you want a job. For jobs he says text is taken more seriously than video, so LinkedIn or Twitter beats Instagram or YouTube. (Kata 10 at 19:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1169s)
- You cannot have a full market salary and skin in the game together. The people he respects offer to take the high-risk campaign and cut their salary for a share if it works. (Kata 4 at 22:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1350s)
- Real optionality is transferable skill, and interviewing at ten places is the fake kind. He says he has run, shut, failed at and sold companies so many times that he can now walk on any surface: cohorts, services, game dev. (Kata 9 at 37:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2249s)
- The skills that let you absorb any risk are not hard skills. He lists taking risk accurately, hiring and gathering good people, sharing wealth through equity, and distribution. Hard skills are temporary, and engineers including him felt invulnerable three years ago before the rug was pulled. (Kata 9 at 38:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2298s)
- Going to college for permission is the wrong reason to go. He calls it the **where's your training** attitude: surgeons need certification, but many jobs do not, and people still go so that someone stamps them as good enough. (Why Belief Matters at 25:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1531s)
- AI will do the skill work, so people who only show how skilled they are become useless or less useful. People who combine skill with selling, hiring and running around will do very well. (Kata 3 at 22:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1348s)

## Why should you take the attempt?

- **The test**, as Aeos names it, has four steps: believe in something, take a big swing at it with personal risk, face skepticism and ridicule because the cards are stacked against you, and somehow pull it off. Passing raises your power level for good. (Kata 4 at 8:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=535s)
- The gap between thinking you can do it and having done it is far wider than people think. His image is vegetables on a table against a cooked dish, and he puts the gap at a few years. (Kata 4 at 7:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=436s)
- Opportunities are not given. Since 25 he felt he deserved to be a big founder and the world was not giving him a chance, and he learned you have to grind, be visible and perform miracles before top people acknowledge you. (Kata 4 at 6:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=387s)
- Bad cards are normal. Almost every publicly successful person had bad cards in wealth, health or competitors, so you are not supposed to have all the tools. You are supposed to perform a miracle. (Kata 4 at 12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=725s)
- Pulling off one swing levels you up permanently and unlocks money, team and budgets. That is why he says the better route to deep tech is to build something first, earn trust, then take harder shots. (Kata 4 at 12:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=772s)
- A correct prediction with no swing earns only a little, which he calls predictive competence. Deep belief with no attempt is the biggest trap, because you can believe garbage for life and never find out. (Kata 4 at 14:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=868s)
- A failed attempt still earns competence if it was a **clean attempt**. The clean way is to put your hand up, say you thought you could and cannot, give the reasons and shut it down. Asking for a department switch or quietly quitting when you start losing is not clean. (Kata 4 at 16:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=966s)
- He cites a Steve Jobs line that somewhere between the janitor and the CEO excuses stop mattering. A manager can find the key, get a duplicate made or break the door down, and if something truly is not possible, take on a smaller problem. (Kata 4 at 19:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1158s)
- His five levels run from 0 to 4: NPCs with no beliefs or attempts, commentators with strong opinions and zero attempts, local doers with small low-risk attempts such as freelancers, founders or owners with repeated high-risk attempts, and kingmakers who move markets and are hedged against failure. (Kata 4 at 20:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1255s; Kata 4 at 24:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1445s; Kata 4 at 24:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1493s)
- Commentators earn no power level. He says India is full of them and it is a courage issue: in his own company a person says in a meeting what should be done, and goes silent when told to do it. (Kata 4 at 20:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1255s)
- Your first 5 to 10 attempts will not work. Many people try once, fail, fear the public humiliation and never try again, and he says the first failure usually shows whether someone will have an owner mindset. (Kata 4 at 23:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1398s)
- All success has luck in it, but luck only finds you after a serious attempt. Nobody could have predicted his game going viral in China, and they found out only after playing the card. (Kata 4 at 27:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1638s)
- He calls India an anti-attempt nation because an attempt tests the story in your head, like being afraid to go for a cancer check. He says 99% of the country does not attempt hard things. (Kata 4 at 28:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1735s)
- You do not need perfect information before you act. He cites Brian Armstrong's line that action produces information, and says Aeos did not know how content would be monetised when it started. (Kata 4 at 30:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1833s)
- What lowers your level is repeating the same failure without updating your model. His example is engineers who ship one app a week hoping one goes viral, which he calls a gambler mentality. (Kata 4 at 43:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2606s)
- A clean failure buys another shot, but at some point you must deliver. He worries about people who spent 10 years across startups that all died and still quote a salary, and says that at Aeos you need at least one win. (Kata 4 at 40:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2414s; Kata 4 at 49:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2991s)
- To raise your level: pick a game with measurable outcomes, place a visible personal bet of money, reputation or years, ship in public, ignore ridicule but collect usable feedback, and get one clear win. (Kata 4 at 49:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2944s)
- The most competent people blame themselves even for things outside their control. A bad co-founder was their choice, a failed employer was their choice, and a pandemic was their failure to plan. That is how they avoid repeating mistakes. (Kata 4 at 47:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2843s)
- Nobody cares much about failure. They care that you took the hard call and were eventually right, and the Aeos rule is to ask for forgiveness, not permission. (Kata 7 at 20:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1204s)
- You cannot reach success by avoiding mistakes, which only works in exams. He passes on a rule to try everything three times: once to get over the fear, once to stop being bad at it, once to decide if you like it. (Kata 13 at 10:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=624s)
- In life you only have to be right once. Unlike poker, one win pays for thousands of experiments as long as you do not spend all your money on them. (Kata 9 at 38:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2298s)

## How does belief turn into results?

- After 14 years of running companies he says nothing else works without belief: not the technology, the people or even the distribution. He admits it sounds like Instagram motivation and says he wants to explain the mechanics. (Why Belief Matters at 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=47s)
- He opens that same talk by noting he is the one who said a lot of success is luck, then argues belief is the difference between success and failure. (Why Belief Matters at 1:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=95s)
- Given enough time there is no difference between a wannabe entrepreneur and a successful one. Every successful one began by role-playing a founder with no product, customers or team, and kept the behaviour up for 10 years. (Why Belief Matters at 2:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=143s)
- Pretending to be the thing works as long as you are doing the thing. An unfit person is still unfit after two weeks of gym, so the ones who last are those who already think of themselves as fit. (Why Belief Matters at 3:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=190s)
- You are how you act, not how you think. Reading an article that sets Rousseau against Franklin, he sides with Franklin: virtue is a habit and only actions count, so faking it until you make it is closer to the truth than people admit. (Why Belief Matters at 5:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=335s)
- The person who ships a bad first version and keeps going for 10 years ends up doing well. Intent and action are two different things. (Why Belief Matters at 7:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=431s)
- There are two kinds of crazy belief. A **type one crazy belief** is ambitious in a good way, like building the tallest tower, and people will back it. A **type two crazy belief** harms other people or adds no value to your life. The test is whether it creates harm, and you act only on type one. (Why Belief Matters at 10:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=622s)
- The worst place to get stuck is in the belief itself. Do not stop at having pure intentions: make a plan, start meeting builders and investors, and accept being rejected a thousand times for having zero experience. (Why Belief Matters at 11:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=671s)
- He says type one beliefs are achievable if converted to behaviour, so pick ones slightly out of reach but mathematically accomplishable. He then qualifies that highly likely does not mean 90%, only a decent chance. (Why Belief Matters at 11:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=719s; Why Belief Matters at 14:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=864s)
- Beliefs are social: if your friends are doing it, it seems likely that you can too. Only one person in his room expected to build a 10-storey building, yet he says one Bangalore street alone has many, built by lots of different people. (Why Belief Matters at 12:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=768s; Why Belief Matters at 13:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=817s)
- The **belief ladder**: start with a belief slightly crazier than your social circle, accomplish it, then raise it. Three storeys, then 10, then 30, then the tallest in the city. (Why Belief Matters at 14:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=864s)
- Keep the craziest belief to yourself and show a smaller staged plan, because people hearing a type one belief may take it for type two. He cites Elon Musk's multi-stage Mars plan and says Aeos works the same way. (Why Belief Matters at 15:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=912s)
- Seeing normal people succeed up close installs belief. He doubted the content business until he saw some creators' P&Ls, met them and found they were normal people. (Why Belief Matters at 20:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1246s)
- An unsupportive environment caps you at 10 to 20% belief. If parents or a partner do not believe you can do something amazing, have a deep conversation with them or move on. (Why Belief Matters at 23:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1436s)
- His biggest working trait is the **I'll figure it out** attitude: whatever happens, he believes he can turn a bad situation into a good one. He admits his self-belief may be too much. (Why Belief Matters at 24:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1483s)
- The **belief meter** never starts at 100, so entrepreneurs need simple businesses first to fill it. Outlier businesses come after simpler ones. (Why Belief Matters at 24:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1483s)
- Entrepreneurship is hard because belief swings weekly, not because of execution. Once you have decided after planning, you must be unshakeable even by yourself. (Why Belief Matters at 19:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1199s)
- Too much belief causes mistakes. After a run of wins you may buy an airline, so he says to study history to learn where the edge of belief is. (Why Belief Matters at 26:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1580s)

## How do you test a belief, change it, and whose advice do you take?

- Believing something does not make your path correct, so build a way to test beliefs fast. The Wright brothers built a wind tunnel in their bicycle shop after the 1901 glider failed and tested 200 wing shapes. Find out tomorrow, not in two weeks. (Why Belief Matters at 27:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1628s)
- Keep people saying it will not work apart from it not working. Their validation is irrelevant, but the onus is on you to prove it, like the scientists who swallowed H. pylori to show bacteria cause ulcers. (Why Belief Matters at 28:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1721s)
- His line for the burden of proof: "You need proof or you are type two crazy." (Why Belief Matters at 28:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1721s)
- A persistent person with a wrong idea is more likely to make it than a person with a perfect idea who never starts. He says a follower who built an app the Indian market would not support will still make it over 10 or 15 years if he stays flexible on the solution, and he calls this paradoxical. (Why Belief Matters at 29:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1768s)
- On commitment he says: "Optionality kills belief. You need to burn the bridges." Pick one thing, get it to some scale, and treat the other four as not even options for now. (Why Belief Matters at 33:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2004s)
- He uses the same phrase the other way for relationships. In April 2026 he said never to burn bridges with a company you leave, because it locks you out of its whole ecosystem. He burned some at 20 and in his 30s keeps good relations even with people who wronged him. (Kata 9 at 34:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2060s)
- Outlier success comes from compounding, and you will not believe in compounding unless you are all in. His example is the graffiti artist paid in Facebook stock valued at 60k in 2005 and worth $200 million at the IPO, and he doubts most people would have held. (Why Belief Matters at 35:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2149s)
- For compounding assets he says to remove the option to sell: invest, seal it and do not look for 10 years. (Why Belief Matters at 36:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2196s)
- For a type one belief with no knowledge, he says anything taught in college is four or five years outdated and the best path is an apprenticeship under someone in the field. Failing that, do something: he relays Brian Armstrong's line that action produces information, and says to act every day for a year or two. (Why Belief Matters at 37:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2244s; Why Belief Matters at 38:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2292s)
- Change your beliefs when the data changes. He calls this the rational thing to do and holds strong beliefs loosely, which sits beside his rule that the mind must be unshakeable once set. (Why Belief Matters at 39:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2340s; Why Belief Matters at 19:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1199s)
- His closing standard: "People may not have to agree with you, but reality must." (Why Belief Matters at 39:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2387s)

- Do not take advice from Reddit. He says successful people used it ten years ago, when he built high karma there, and now it is mostly people complaining whose confusion you do not want. (Kata 7 at 28:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1681s)
- In March 2026, as a podcast guest, he said the best mentorship is no mentorship: without a mentor you struggle for maybe 10 years but end up the most unique person, while Indian systems teach you to copy someone two years ahead. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:10:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4210s)
- In November 2026 he said an apprenticeship under someone already working in the field is the best path for a person with a belief and no knowledge. (Why Belief Matters at 37:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2244s; Why Belief Matters at 38:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2292s)
- Business case study creators mislead because their job is to be sensational. They credit one move for a company's success, and any single move can be copied. (Kata 6 at 35:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=2153s)
- He does not believe any startup's story once it is successful. Companies do random things for four years, figure it out in the fifth, and rewrite it as a plan. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:05:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3946s)
- AI surfaces the statistically likely, so it protects you from extremely bad decisions but will not make you extremely successful. He asks it to play devil's advocate and give four or five alternatives. (PGX Ideas 2 at 54:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3247s; PGX Ideas 2 at 52:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3154s)

## How should you handle stress, burnout and your sense of self?

- In his view burnout only comes from doing work you do not enjoy. When you enjoy creative work or gaming you can do it for tens of hours. (Kata 8 at 47:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2841s)
- He treats work as a long sport to be enjoyed without burning out, and says that once you reach the destination everything is boring, so the journey is the better part. (Kata 3 at 42:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2538s)
- He says Indians have equated stress with hard work, and defines it differently: "Stress is something you cannot reliably overcome." In the animal studies he cites, a rat that can crawl out even a little does not get depressed. (Secret Kata 1 at 4:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=245s)
- A no in an appraisal is a budget decision, not a verdict on your worth. He tells his staff rejection is never personal, then adds that sometimes it is, but not at Aeos. (Secret Kata 1 at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=630s; Secret Kata 1 at 11:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=678s)
- Your inner critic does the same damage as someone abusing you from outside. He calls it the **Sharma ji ka beta mechanism** running in your own head. (Secret Kata 1 at 25:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1547s)
- Under stress, talk to yourself in the third person. He says this moves you from a threat state to a challenge state, like a boxing match you trained for. (Secret Kata 1 at 25:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1547s)
- His worst-case simulation: vividly imagine bankruptcy, automation or public humiliation, repeat it until the monster is known, realise you would survive, and you will not spiral when a real crisis hits. (Secret Kata 1 at 23:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1403s)
- Tying identity to what makes us money was a mistake, because work is going away. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:18:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4688s)
- Do not attach your financial success to your self-worth in this era. You can do your best work and still not be rewarded, because the world rewards people who took the same bets 20 or 30 years ago. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:43:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6222s)
- Identity is clay, and survival of the fittest means survival of the most adaptable. He says he would drive deliveries or clean houses if needed, and respects a delivery driver far more than people talking on the sidelines. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:50:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6606s)

## What does he say about learning to code now?

- Ten years ago he believed learning to code meant doing very well in life, because it gave social mobility without a fancy degree or nepotism. After AI he says he has fully reversed that view. (Why Belief Matters at 39:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2340s)
- He says software engineers get hit by AI first and coding will be automated end to end. He has said so for three years, was attacked for it, and cannot think of a scenario where he is wrong. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:32:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5554s)
- Requirements collection stays human, because real companies are messy. Once requirements exist, writing, shipping and testing the app will be automated. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:33:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5601s)
- He says a top 1% software engineer will be fine, and that the other 99% have two choices: complain, or take the message and adapt. Offline jobs in India are not going anywhere, in his view. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:55:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6937s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:50:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6606s)
- His wall analogy: you are driving at a wall that is moving toward you. Studying harder is pressing the accelerator, and turning left or right is the sign of intelligence. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:53:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6794s)
- To tech workers who object, he says computer science automated taxis, hotels and tourism first: "We have no right to complain. It's our turn now." (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:56:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6984s)

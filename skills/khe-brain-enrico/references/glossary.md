# Glossary

These are his own coined terms and named frameworks, plus the laws and effects he borrows, each defined the way he uses it in the videos. A borrowed term names the person, book or field he credits when he gives one, and a prediction or a verdict on AI carries the year he said it.

- **0.23%**: His label for the share of people who can code and so held the privilege of building with technology. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=0s)
- **1% dilemma**: Solving the first 80 to 99 percent of use cases is fairly easy, while the last 1 percent of edge cases takes exponential resources. The more often a product is used and the higher the cost of failure, the more that last sliver matters. (The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=45s)
- **7 levels of tech design**: His seven levels of designing and building tech products, which he says separate products you hate using from ones that change the world. Level 1 is the smallest visual details, and the levels run up to the entire product strategies of the biggest tech companies. (The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=45s)
- **10 things to fix a digital life**: His list for fixing a digital life starts with hardcore social media control, then going on a notification witch hunt. (How I Finally Fixed My Digital Life at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7PhEfKCHr7Y&t=45s)
- **90-9-1 rule**: A rule he borrows that 90 percent of people online just watch and browse, 9 percent contribute by commenting, and 1 percent are creators. He applies it to how people use devices. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=225s)
- **Access**: The other problem of AI beside smartness. In 2024 he said that while everyone else fought over the smartest model, Apple went after access: Siri becomes the default way of reaching AI on iPhones, which gives Apple control of access to AI and makes the model behind it almost irrelevant. (How Apple Stole AI From Everyone Else at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gV1f-lS0C9g&t=180s)
- **Action problem**: His 2024 name for AI being able to reason but not carry out actions itself. He gives two ways to solve it, an API integration per service, or an action model that operates interfaces like a human on any website. (Is This Useless at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWEjDIsMFB0&t=90s)
- **Affordance**: A cue that tells you what you can do, like a door handle, or in an app the hint that there is more below. (The Lies That Make Your Tech Actually Work at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rAVV4kAYlI8&t=270s)
- **Agent secretary**: His 2026 term for the AI agent that manages your notifications and decides whether to show you one, in which context, and whether to change its wording. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notifications at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dx2yPk0FsGM&t=720s)
- **Aha moment**: The moment a new user gets the value a product can give and becomes a sticky user. An existing product term he defines this way. (Why Half The World Uses WhatsApp at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1O1u0UO99NE&t=270s; Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=810s)
- **AI alignment**: Steering an AI towards doing what we want it to do. An existing term he defines this way. (ChatGPT is Frighteningly Good at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lzQD-7lZJU&t=225s)
- **AI as the new printing press**: His 2022 claim that AI makes copying and adapting worthless while amplifying original authors. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=315s)
- **AI feeding optimization or AIFO**: His half-joking 2023 name for what SEO becomes when people optimize the information they feed to AI models. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=585s)
- **AI tower**: His 2023 three-layer picture of AI, top to bottom. The front-end application such as ChatGPT, the trained model, and computing power, with other companies plugging in at the model layer through APIs. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=225s)
- **AirPods lid model**: A chart of attracting and repelling magnet force against lid angle. Where the two lines cross is the cutoff between snapping open and snapping shut. (The Lies That Make Hardware Actually Work at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLIgrGAIRsE&t=45s)
- **Alternative timeline**: His device of splitting the timeline and looking at how life would be without a piece of everyday tech. His three are social media never existing (Mark Zuckerberg never founds Facebook), smartphones never being invented, and the open internet never becoming a thing while the other internet wins. (What if Smartphones Never Existed at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJ8OnNQYAVo&t=0s)
- **Anticipation effect**: A principle he borrows that our brains feed on anticipating what comes next, so a new story each year makes Wrapped an event. (The Reason Everyone's Obsessed With Spotify Wrapped at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EuzRWd5U6_I&t=270s)
- **Anti-scary tech**: His description of the Frutiger Aero goal, using colour and playfulness to make intimidating computers approachable. (What Happened To User Interfaces at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HdozqSVF-VM&t=45s)
- **Anti-startups**: The title's term for tech companies that reject the venture-funded growth path. He does not define it in the body of the video. (The Weird Rise Of Anti-Startups at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xL1MOOD5Ox8&t=0s)
- **Apple Intelligence routing**: As he explained it in 2024, you ask Siri, Apple's own language model decides whether the request is about your personal data and handles it if so, using App Intents as its access points into apps, and a complex request involves ChatGPT. (How Apple Stole AI From Everyone Else at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gV1f-lS0C9g&t=405s)
- **Apple's two roads**: In 2024 he saw two paths for Apple in AR. Build a higher wall with a locked-down iPhone accessory, or build a device that needs no iPhone and merges the openness of the web with AR to win developers back. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=855s)
- **Arc of controls**: His history of physical controls. Levers and gears you can see move, buttons as effortless luxury, more and more buttons as a sign of advanced tech, complexity moved from hardware into software so a phone needs only three buttons, and recently a move back to being physical. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=45s)
- **Art of the prompt**: The emerging craft and market of writing prompts for AI models, which in 2022 he expected to grow. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=315s)
- **Asymmetric risk in search**: Compare how much of each company's revenue the product is, 58 percent for Google search against five percent for Bing at Microsoft. The smaller share can afford the radical bet. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=405s)
- **Atoms to pixels**: His phrase for moving a product's complexity and controls from hardware into software. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=135s)
- **Audio metaphors**: Sounds that told you what was happening right now, where visual metaphors taught you how to use the interface. (Why Our Tech Got Silent at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rFJutK25VU&t=360s)
- **Authors versus amanuenses**: His 2022 way to sort creative work under AI, taken from the monks who hand-copied books before the printing press. Authors come up with the content and get amplified. Of the amanuenses, the people adapting or distributing content, he says some will become authors but the majority will be masters of a craft that has lost most of its value. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=315s)
- **Bad tech prediction recipe**: Take a problem, take the technology we already have for it, and predict an extreme version, as with flying cars. He says it fails because it cannot see radical shifts such as software and the internet. (Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=495s)
- **Badges in place of views**: His four-step plan for removing the view count as a YouTube product manager. Research 10 to 15 badges such as hidden gem, A/B test them one by one, test combinations of the winners, then test the best combination with views removed and drop the count if click-through rate stays higher. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=315s)
- **Behind the Product**: The name of his show about how great tech products are built, made with the people who built them. (Something New Is Coming at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BEd9uo38GWA&t=45s)
- **Blend of atoms and pixels**: Interfaces that combine fixed physical controls with digital flexibility, which in 2024 he expected to matter much more. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=540s)
- **Boring infrastructure technology**: The layers that already take care of the complexity so developers can focus on the product. (The Boring Tech To Make AR Glasses Cool at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkCfdHdHnKg&t=270s)
- **Bottleneck of human I/O**: The time humans take to give input to a machine and read its output, which he says limits what people and computers can do together. (The Man Who Made Apple Possible In 1968 at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXSHJXHeaqM&t=360s)
- **Branching feed**: His 2025 redesign of the short-form feed. A swipe in one direction, the easier one for right-handed people, always means depth: the full video or more shorts on the same topic. A swipe to the other side could signal the algorithm to show content far outside what you usually watch. (Social Media Is Broken But We Can Fix It at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HqYIUV7Eqjk&t=765s)
- **Build it yourself to understand why it does not exist**: His method of putting the viewer in charge of a product and shipping a missing feature in versions to watch what breaks. For unsafe areas in Google Maps, version one with crime records flags the city centres of major cities as dangerous, and version two with user reports drives customers from shops and leaves residents feeling discriminated against. (Why Google Maps Doesn't Show You Unsafe Areas at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4i66ht6YHkw&t=45s)
- **Building in public**: Document the journey as you go, share how much you make, share failures as well as wins, and learn from each other. (The Weird Rise Of Anti-Startups at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xL1MOOD5Ox8&t=675s)
- **Built-in rules for abstract UI sounds**: Pitch maps to space, with high as up and low as down. Connected sounds ascend, disconnected descend, success is harmonious and higher, and failure is clashing and lower. (Why Our Tech Got Silent at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rFJutK25VU&t=270s)
- **Button design steps**: How he builds a button that feels right. Make it look pressable, show feedback on touch, trigger on release and not on touch start, cancel if the finger leaves, allow a tap area around it, and re-arm if the finger comes back. (The Lies That Make Your Tech Actually Work at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rAVV4kAYlI8&t=90s; What Happened To Physical Tech at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=135s; How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=360s)
- **Button force chart**: A plot of how far you press against how much the button resists. A good button climbs, then drops sharply when the dome buckles. (The Lies That Make Hardware Actually Work at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLIgrGAIRsE&t=270s)
- **Buying time**: Using an animation to cover work the system is still doing. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=855s)
- **Cashless effect**: Losing track of real monetary value when a separate currency sits between you and the price, as with in-game currencies and casino chips. (The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=450s)
- **Chatbot in three steps**: His 2024 satire of the lazy path. Pay an AI company for a model, build the simplest thing that takes text in and gives text out, and add a nice skin. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=225s)
- **Cheating physics with software**: His description of computational photography making up for a small sensor. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=180s)
- **Chronoception**: A psychology term he borrows for the subjective sense of time, which the feed blurs by removing start and end signals, as casinos remove clocks and windows. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=180s)
- **Claude Code loop**: As he described it in 2026, it reads the project files, works out what is going on, picks a plan, chooses among over 30 actions, and on an error reads it and tries another approach until the job is done. (Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=585s)
- **Claw machine model**: His metaphor for startups as an arcade claw machine. One route to better odds is knowing which item to pick up so it does not slip from the claw, which he says translates to validating the problem. (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=225s)
- **Close circle**: The small set of people whose notifications the system prioritizes, added automatically from a steady stream of messages and removed with your confirmation when you stop interacting. (Why You Don't Reply To Your Friends at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NVjRPwneLiI&t=675s)
- **Closest digital link to the real world**: His description of mapping apps, and his reason their design choices change where people physically go. (Why Google Maps Doesn't Show You Unsafe Areas at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4i66ht6YHkw&t=180s)
- **Color banding**: Blocky lines in a subtle gradient caused by too few colors between start and end. (Why Simple Tech is Almost Impossible to Build at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qSQZ8B-2nU0&t=405s)
- **Command line problem**: An interface where you must learn the right text commands, which he said in 2024 chatbots and prompts have brought back. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=540s)
- **Comment analysis method**: Export every comment from the last year and use AI to sort them into positive, neutral and negative. His result was 21.4 percent positive, 17.1 percent neutral and 61.5 percent negative. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=450s)
- **Commitment and consistency**: A principle he borrows to explain rewards for repeated small actions, such as Booking's Genius levels counting bookings and not money spent, and Duolingo streaks. (The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=180s)
- **Company vault**: His description of Slack as a store of messages meant to be found later, including by agents. (The Insane Design Of Messaging at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6DSW1rN-ZV4&t=540s)
- **Complexity line**: The maximum level of complexity your users can take. Terminals sat way above it for regular people and barely under it for a small subset of nerds, and graphics, visuals and metaphors brought tech down under it so billions of regular people could use it. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Text at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7DN3Ectm6UY&t=540s)
- **Complexity spectrum**: His 2026 split of tasks by complexity. Simple tasks belong to GUI and direct manipulation, very complex ones to chat and agents, and the middle, where most daily tasks fall, to generative UI. (The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=540s)
- **Computers as a job**: The people who did calculations by hand before the word meant a machine. (Why Ugly Things Are Cool Now at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1SWw7i9LQc&t=45s)
- **Concept drift**: The term he attributes to TikTok's 2023 paper for a user's interests moving on before a model finishes training. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=855s)
- **Consumer versus B2B outcome**: Under the law of the marginal user, consumer products get simpler and dumber, while B2B products pile up toggles from sales-driven requests. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=630s)
- **Content engineering**: His name for the second layer of YouTube: because the platform optimizes for attention, the videos themselves, their thumbnails, titles and retention, have become hyper-optimized, probably even more engineered than the platform itself. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=450s)
- **Content faucet**: A way to control the flow of content into our lives, which he says we need now instead of more content. (Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=270s)
- **Content machine**: His label for Glance, a product whose only purpose is pushing content to raise carrier profits. (Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=450s)
- **Context graph**: His 2025 name for what OpenAI is building, a map of who you are, as opposed to who you know or what you search for. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=630s)
- **Context menu pattern**: No animation in, a blink on the chosen item, then a fade out. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=540s)
- **Convergence by experiment**: Run hundreds of tests, keep what raises engagement and kill the rest. The surviving designs exploit psychology without anyone choosing that. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=810s)
- **Conversational computing**: Giving a computer instructions in plain language, which in 2022 he said AI enables as a shift away from graphical interfaces. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=450s)
- **Coolness as the driver of adoption**: Culture deciding a thing is cool makes it grow, and deciding it is uncool makes it die. He applies the arc of cigarettes to smartphones. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=450s)
- **Copying nature**: Making digital motion follow real-world physics so it is understood without teaching. It is his first rule of animation, and his second rule is to stop when nature is too slow. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=135s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=360s)
- **Core input**: The main way you act on a device, which he says completely changes how you use it: "every device is its core input". On a phone every interaction is designed around scroll being more important than tapping, while clicking as the main input lets you do more in a less natural way. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=630s)
- **Cost of being a tech company**: His phrase for letting present products stay flawed while spending on the next big bet. (The Weird Reason Your Tech Stays Broken at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-51ocAD6zBI&t=450s)
- **Coyote time**: A game design term he borrows, named after Wile E. Coyote, for the few extra milliseconds a game lets you jump after leaving a platform. (How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=405s)
- **Creation device and consumption device**: His two categories of devices and apps. Creation devices generate things, and consumption devices let you consume things usually made by other people. He says the two are in a constant battle for your time and attention, and implies consumption wins most of the time. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=45s)
- **Creators**: People who build things because it is fun, without a roadmap item or a metric behind it. (How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=135s)
- **Creators versus operators pendulum**: His history of tech as a pendulum, not a switch. In the '90s and 2000s the people going into tech wanted to build something fun, in the 2010s operators such as MBAs and finance people came in and kicked the creators out, and he says the pendulum is starting to swing back. (How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=585s)
- **Cross the river**: His phrase for a product moving from a small core of technical early users to the majority of people. (Famous Apps Looked Like This at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Sn8Glj5NW4&t=225s)
- **Curiosity gap**: A principle he borrows, our desire to seek out missing information. Spotify creates it by withholding stats until Wrapped, and a notification creates it by holding back one detail. (The Reason Everyone's Obsessed With Spotify Wrapped at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EuzRWd5U6_I&t=135s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notifications at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dx2yPk0FsGM&t=180s)
- **DASH**: Dynamic adaptive streaming over HTTP. Each video exists in several resolutions and bitrates, cut into 2 to 10 second segments and described by a manifest file the player chooses from. (YouTube Does This Anytime You Watch And You Don't Realize It at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=49_a3jkdBdI&t=180s)
- **Data-value balance**: The fair exchange between the data and attention a free product takes and the value it gives back. He says the machine breaks when a company takes more without adding equal value. (The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=540s)
- **Deep map**: The single combined map of the world that all of Google's data sources feed into, underneath the Maps app. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=90s)
- **Default bias**: People keep whatever option is preselected, so the default decides behavior. He borrows the effect and proves it with a chart of European organ donor rates where the only difference is opt-in or opt-out. (Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=360s; How Apple Stole AI From Everyone Else at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gV1f-lS0C9g&t=225s; The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=540s)
- **Defensible model**: A model another company cannot easily replicate. In 2023 he said Microsoft's plan only works if GPT-style models stay defensible. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=450s)
- **Design pattern**: A familiar piece of interface that people already know how to use without instructions, which other products adopt and which steers the behavior of billions. Most interfaces never become one. (Social Media Is Broken But We Can Fix It at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HqYIUV7Eqjk&t=45s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=360s; Why Simple Tech is Almost Impossible to Build at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qSQZ8B-2nU0&t=180s)
- **Designed for imperfection**: His phrase for tech built to make up for how humans fail and not for perfection or speed. (How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=45s)
- **Destination discovery**: The stage before booking, deciding where to go, which he says Airbnb is trying to own. (Airbnb Just Hacked The Travel Industry Again at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OwiztfnZwE4&t=360s)
- **Details add up**: No single detail matters, but a thousand of them make a product feel good. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notion at 16:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b00sgRR_Vc0&t=990s)
- **Device-category analogy**: Computers, then the iPhone, then the iPad in between. For AR he maps it as computers, then the Vision Pro as the iPad equivalent, then true AR glasses as the iPhone moment. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=495s)
- **Digital glue**: His name for the special Unicode character that joins several emojis into one. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Text at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7DN3Ectm6UY&t=0s)
- **Digital plane and physical plane**: His words for the parallel world of information and the real world. He calls a smartphone a window into the digital plane that is limited to the size of its screen. In his telling of Zuckerberg's definition the metaverse removes the physical plane, while augmented reality is about overlapping. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=315s)
- **Direct manipulation**: Acting on the on-screen thing itself by tapping, dragging or pinching, instead of describing the action. (The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=405s)
- **Disney's 12 principles of animation**: Principles he borrows, which he says two Disney animators wrote in 1981. He says slow in and slow out became easing, and squash and stretch sits behind the feel of iOS's liquid glass. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=225s)
- **Easing curves**: Linear keeps the same speed and feels mechanical, ease in starts slow and feels slow, ease out starts fast and feels snappy, and ease in out is slow at both ends and feels smooth. - **Two-sided platform seeding**: Reviewing an app idea that matches influencers with local shops, he said it has the classic two-sided platform problem, and that to build it you need either a lot of capital or an existing influencer network to kick-start one side, piloted in one city and one very specific category before expanding. (Reacting to Innovative Ideas From Reddit at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=82E7VoxCPc0&t=540s)
- **Two-state problem**: When a toggle's icon could mean either the current state or the action a click will take. His fixes are to split action and status into two elements, or to show state with clear colors and shapes. (Why Simple Tech is Almost Impossible to Build at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qSQZ8B-2nU0&t=90s)
- **Unit bias**: A psychology idea he borrows that one unit of something feels like the right amount, which the one-video-at-a-time feed exploits. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=180s)
- **Unoptimized resource**: The cheat code of a given era, an opportunity not yet crowded that people later wish they had taken. Earlier generations had houses and then safe jobs, and his picks for now are the internet and YouTube. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=450s; I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=495s)
- **Validation sequence**: His steps for a startup idea. Step back from the solution to the problem, then define narrowly the audience that has it in its most severe form. (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=45s)
- **Validation timing rule**: Validate format checks such as email when the field loses focus, and progressive requirements such as password length on every keystroke. (Why Simple Tech is Almost Impossible to Build at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qSQZ8B-2nU0&t=225s)
- **Vertical cursor movement**: His step-by-step build. The same letter index fails because widths differ, so pick the closest letter by horizontal distance, remember the previous column so going back returns you, and reset that memory when the user moves sideways. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Text at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7DN3Ectm6UY&t=360s)
- **Vibe coding**: Writing what you want in a prompt and seeing it built, which in 2025 he said opens the privilege of building software to everyone. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=45s)
- **Views feedback loop**: The algorithm offers a selection of videos, the viewer picks one partly by view count, higher views bring more clicks, click-through rate rises, the algorithm recommends it more, viewers subscribe, and it repeats. Quality is checked separately through watch time. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=90s)
- **Virtual positioning system or VPS**: A 3D map of the whole world that devices use to locate themselves precisely and anchor shared digital objects. As he explains it, Street View images become trillions of points, a second model merges them into one 3D point cloud, the device sends GPS plus its camera view, and the scene is matched to the map. (The Boring Tech To Make AR Glasses Cool at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkCfdHdHnKg&t=90s; Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=630s)
- **Wall gardening**: His word for a deliberate, limited amount of platform control. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=495s)
- **Watch users, do not listen to them**: Ignore the features people ask for, observe what they do and where they struggle, find the real problem, then build for that. His examples are Gmail undo send and iOS one-time code autofill. (How Your Tech Tricks You at 15:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7nnyv4yJGJc&t=945s)
- **Ways to make a wait feel shorter**: Stripes that move backwards on the progress bar (he cites Chris Harrison at Carnegie Mellon finding the bar feels 11% faster), which is why shimmer is everywhere; animated loading indicators; and a faster spinner rotation. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=270s)
- **Whales**: The few customers who generate most of the revenue. In games they are a few players, and in tech he says they are enterprise buyers. (The Weird Way Tech Steals From Games at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GypAKofWGBk&t=810s)
- **WhatsApp's three crucial moves**: He says WhatsApp made three crucial product moves. The first is the phone number as the account identifier instead of email, and the second is focusing on the basics while every competitor kept adding features. (Why Half The World Uses WhatsApp at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1O1u0UO99NE&t=135s)
- **When to animate in a work tool**: His practice in Flask. Instant for anything used hundreds of times a day, animated for things the user must learn exist, and animated where the user is waiting anyway. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=585s)
- **Why physical controls lost**: They cannot change once they are out in the world, while software can be updated for everyone, and a touchscreen is easier and cheaper than designing, manufacturing and shipping buttons and knobs. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=270s)
- **Wizard of Oz test**: Build the front end of a service and do all the work behind it by hand, and only build the automated system if you see traction. (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=360s; Reacting to Innovative Ideas From Reddit at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=82E7VoxCPc0&t=270s)
- **World's greatest stealing machine**: His 2022 description of DALL-E 2, tied to the Picasso line that good artists copy and great artists steal. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=315s)
- **You're just a number**: His literal reading of lifetime value as how subscription companies see a customer. (The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=495s)

# YouTube and creator work

This theme is YouTube seen by someone who makes videos on it and who also worked as a product manager, so he reads the platform as a product with a metric behind every feature. Most of it comes from five videos: how he saved his stalled channel in 2021, the view count thought experiment in 2021, what happened when the channel blew up in 2023, how YouTube's design makes creators quit in 2025, and the engineering of YouTube videos in 2026. Numbers about his channel are true only for the date of the video they come from, his account of how the algorithm works is his own reading and not YouTube's statement, and a few facts about his workflow were said inside sponsor segments and are given here as plain facts only.

## How does he say YouTube decides which videos get seen?

- He says YouTube pushes a video on two things: click-through rate, the share of people who click when they see the thumbnail, and watch time, how long they keep watching. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=45s; How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=90s)
- He says YouTube's north star metric was total views at launch and became total watch time in 2012, and that the algorithm was changed to follow it. He gives this as the reason big YouTubers make 10 to 15 minute videos when the content would fit in three or five. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=45s)
- He says the algorithm is built to keep you on the platform as long as possible, because the longer you stay the more you watch and the more YouTube earns from advertising. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=45s)
- He describes the path of a new video like this: YouTube shows it to a small portion of subscribers on the homepage, and if they click and watch, it is pushed to more people and in the end to non-subscribers. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=315s)
- He calls a video that gets many times a creator's average views an **outlier**, and says most videos you watch are a creator's outliers. The first thing a creator learns, he says, is that you must keep producing outliers or the channel dies. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=90s)
- He says click-through data is noise for small channels. If YouTube shows a video to 20 people a day, one click is 5% and five clicks is 25%, so the algorithm cannot tell whether the video deserves promotion and a great video may never be picked up. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=135s)
- He says Instagram and TikTok have less of a quitting problem because there is no click step. They rely on scrolling, and he says that makes them much better at picking up good videos. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=180s)
- He says subscribers do not matter and can even hurt. Old subscribers who no longer care ignore the new video when YouTube tests it on them, which sinks its chance of becoming an outlier. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=270s)
- On his own subscriber count he says: "I would love to get rid of half of my subscribers." (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=270s)
- He says longer videos raise the stakes of each upload. He says TV viewing is the main reason YouTube videos keep getting longer, longer means more work, and an underperforming video pushes you to spend even more time trying to save it. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=180s)
- He says one of his videos sat at 3,776 views with a jumpy click-through chart for 55 days and then went on to 3.5 million views, which made it the most viewed video on his channel as of July 2025. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=0s)
- He says another of his videos stayed flat at 30,000 views and then jumped to 500,000 with no news and no change from him. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=180s)
- He says YouTube's superpower is that the viewer chooses what to watch, and that this same choice is what creates the fight for the click. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=315s)
- He says YouTube is closer to Google than to TikTok or Instagram: on those the algorithm picks what you see, while YouTube hands you options and makes you choose. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=225s)
- In 2022 he guessed that YouTube was changing its north star because of TikTok, which he said had overtaken it in time spent, and that the metric may have moved from total watch time to something like videos per session. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=135s)
- He says YouTube protects creators because the platform dies without them. His example in 2026 is the hype feature, which boosts a video in the algorithm with a boost that is inversely proportional to subscriber count. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=540s)
- He says more popular videos get better encoding: a new upload starts on H.264, is re-encoded to VP9 at around 3,000 views, and to AV1 past 1 million views. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=720s)
- He says a watch in the hover preview counts as a real view after a few seconds and is saved to watch history like a regular video. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=45s)

## What does the view count do, and what would he put in its place?

- He says the view count is a small piece of text with outsized power: it decides who is successful, changes the lives of thousands of people and shapes how long you stay on the platform. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=0s)
- He says views do not matter much to the algorithm. The ones who care are human viewers, who pick from the algorithm's selection using thumbnail, title, creator and view count, with the count working as social proof. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=90s)
- He describes a rich-get-richer loop: a video with more views gets clicked more, its click-through rate rises, the algorithm recommends it more, and subscriptions add to it. Over time a few videos take most of the views and the rest get almost nothing. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=90s)
- His thought experiment: if every video, thumbnail and title were equally good, people would decide with the last piece of information left, which is the view count. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=135s)
- He says removing the view count would make thumbnail and title matter more and cut inequality. A creator with a few hundred subscribers could compete with a channel that has millions of views, big creators would be angry, and small ones would get more exposure. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=180s)
- He says it will most likely never happen. Without a cue, a page of options causes the paradox of choice, people leave, and YouTube earns less. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=225s)
- His condition for views to go is that something else must solve the paradox of choice better than views do. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=270s)
- He proposes badges as that something, borrowed from Amazon, which he says has the same problem of too many products and uses badges such as Amazon's Choice to raise click-through on highlighted options. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=270s)
- His plan as an imagined YouTube product manager has four steps: research 10 to 15 badges, A/B test each one and keep the winners, test the winning badges in combination, then test the best combination with views removed and drop the count if overall click-through stays higher over time. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=315s)
- In 2021 he said he would love YouTube to experiment more, because it could open room for smaller channels and keep YouTube strong for the next five to ten years, but that it would not happen unless someone unusually bold ran YouTube. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=360s)
- He says judging someone by follower count seems a bit sad but makes some sense, because the number shows where you are on the journey and your daily problems differ a lot depending on that. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=450s)

## How does YouTube Studio wear creators down?

- His 2025 claim is that YouTubers quit because of YouTube's own design, not because they tire of making videos. He says small decisions in the algorithm and the interface have a psychological effect strong enough that people earning millions a year walk away. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=0s)
- He says the actual UI of YouTube may matter even more than the algorithm in influencing creator psychology. He calls YouTube Studio a really good analytics app on the surface with three main things in its design that have a huge psychological impact on creators; the first is the latest comments tab, and he then turns to the out-of-10 ranking, which he says is probably the piece of UI creators look at the most. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=315s; How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=405s)
- He says the latest comments tab puts mostly negative comments first, because comments come from the two extremes and negativity bias makes the unhappy ones more likely to write. On one of his videos 98.6% of people liked it while 76.3% of comments were negative. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=450s)
- He exported every comment on his channel from the previous year and had AI sort them: 21.4% positive, 17.1% neutral and 61.5% negative. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=450s)
- In 2026 he said he believes YouTube now filters the comment feed on the Studio homepage to shield creators, since it had shown him only positive comments for a few months while the full list still held negative ones. He said he could not confirm it because YouTube had not announced the change. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=540s)
- He says the ranking out of 10 is a gamified comparison against yourself. Each new video is ranked live against your last nine, a 1 out of 10 brings confetti and green, and the nightmare is spending 20, 60 or 100 hours and getting a 10 out of 10. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=495s)
- He says the ranking breaks creators once growth settles, because every new video is compared with the earlier boom and lands at 7, 8 or 9 out of 10, and a hit only resets the bar higher. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=540s)
- He sums up the ranking in one line: "Growth is the only thing that the system cares about." (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=585s)
- He says this hit him too. From the start of 2025 his videos ranked 8 or 9 out of 10 until his latest upload became an outlier three days before the July 2025 video, and he says the poor rankings made him feel awful. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=585s)
- He says the retention chart slowly takes creativity out of videos. Knowing second by second where viewers leave turns storytelling into more of a science than an art, with every word placed to open a curiosity gap. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=630s)
- He says creators need those hooks, and that is the problem: without them they lose viewers, the video never becomes an outlier, and nobody sees it. He admits he used the same techniques in the intro of the video that criticises them. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=720s)
- After all that criticism he still ranks YouTube first among online platforms. He says no other platform lets you tell long, well-produced stories to billions with no middlemen and build a business from it, and that it is the only one that pays fairly, with an almost 50/50 revenue split. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=765s)
- In July 2025 he said YouTube should do more for creators about the ups, downs and burnout, and that short-form content is bound to fail if YouTube does nothing about it. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=810s)

## What makes people click and keep watching?

- He says working out what makes people click on a video is an art and a science, and that YouTubers spend a large share of their time on the thumbnail with one goal, which is the click. (You Control This Video's Thumbnail at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pol6UGKZpdY&t=0s)
- He says a thumbnail can decide a video's fate. In 2026 he said the video that got 3,776 views in 55 days reached 3.5 million after he changed its thumbnail, and that he tried 20 thumbnails for it. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=360s)
- He says he spent over 80 hours on one 16-minute video and changed its thumbnail 20 times trying to save it, and spent hundreds of hours trying to save other videos that never recovered. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=225s)
- His thumbnail rules are a light background because most people use dark mode, red set against contrasting colours, visual hints that something is hidden, and many variations. He says creators make 10 to 30 versions. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=405s)
- His standard for a thumbnail is this: "The goal is not to be realistic, but to be recognizable in 0.1 seconds." (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=405s)
- He says he would love the old YouTube without clickbait because videos would be easier to make. Then he argues that an honest, descriptive title and thumbnail for his own video would have been skipped. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=450s)
- He says adding imperfections improved a thumbnail of his. He put pixels, scratches and fingerprints on a screen to make it look more real, and the new version performed far better than the original. (Why Ugly Things Are Cool Now at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1SWw7i9LQc&t=450s)
- He says the content on YouTube is engineered as much as the platform, probably more. He calls this **content engineering**: creators tune hooks, titles, thumbnails and retention as deliberately as the product is built. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=315s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=450s)
- He says videos that look organic have hours of planning and possibly a whole team behind them. In his own 2026 video he planned the hook, the reveal and the order, down to making viewers look at the picture-in-picture button before he explained it. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=315s)
- He says his own videos are tuned to keep you watching because YouTube plays **the time game** too. He uses music, text, images and story so people reach the end, and says he does it because he has to and not because he likes it. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=270s)
- He says the best way to support a creator is not liking or subscribing but watching the video to the end. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=270s)
- He says quality did not predict views on his early channel. A video he did not like got 20,000 to 30,000 views, while the ones he was proudest of got a couple hundred. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=90s)
- He says his early hit about studying in university, at 35,000 views in 2021, worked because it was searchable and on a topic many people cared about, not because it was good. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=360s)
- He says rough beats polished on YouTube now. Ryan Trahan gets more views than most professional TV programmes with wonky thumbnails, cursor-drawn animations, bad audio and bad framing, which he says show a human made them. (Why Ugly Things Are Cool Now at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1SWw7i9LQc&t=270s)
- In 2024 he said fully AI-generated videos fail because nothing is wrong with them: people close them at once since they are technically perfect and unremarkable. (Why Ugly Things Are Cool Now at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1SWw7i9LQc&t=315s)
- In 2022 he ran what he called possibly the first interactive video on YouTube. A bot he coded took the most-liked comment each midnight, turned its text into an image and set it as the video's thumbnail. (You Control This Video's Thumbnail at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pol6UGKZpdY&t=0s)

## What does he think of the most replayed graph and the player's other features?

- In 2022 he argued that the most replayed graph works against YouTube's own north star of watch time. He granted that YouTube had tested it with Premium users since October 2021 and had all the data, and still found it hard to believe it raised total watch time. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=90s)
- He splits YouTube use into information and entertainment. For a tutorial, how-to or review, skipping to the needed part helps, but for entertainment he calls the graph a death sentence, because viewers can skip the story a creator built and watch only the climax. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=180s)
- He says skipping to the climax breaks the cycle that has kept YouTube alive for 15 years: creators spend hours crafting a story, viewers get invested, subscribe and join a community. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=225s)
- He says the chart has no built-in friction. You are usually on a low part of it, so your first reaction is that you are wasting time on a dull stretch. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=270s)
- His redesign scraps the chart and adds friction: the feature only activates when the viewer shows signs of intent, which might mean three taps on the forward button on mobile, or on desktop about three clicks on the timeline or scrubbing for more than two seconds. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=315s)
- He qualifies the whole argument as resting on his own assumptions and product experience, since he does not know YouTube's metrics or plans. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=405s)
- He says YouTube optimises for playing time, not time on the app, because browsing hurts how the product is perceived. That is why thumbnails play on hover and the video stays visible while you browse. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=135s)
- He says part of him wants the old YouTube back, but as someone who builds products he accepts that the disliked features are there because they work. He ties this to his rule that product teams should watch what users do and not listen to what they say. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=225s)
- He says YouTube tests far more than it ships, with probably dozens of A/B tests running at scale at any time. His example is a 2024 test where scrolling in full screen moved between long videos like a short-form feed, which never shipped. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=225s)
- He rates the feature that lights up the like and subscribe buttons when a creator asks for them as genius. He says YouTube transcribes the video to know when to do it and that he did not set it up himself. (The Secrets That Make Your Phone Actually Work at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-YVtZV4DjCE&t=450s)

## How does he weigh short form against long form as a creator?

- In 2023 he said creators cannot build a sustainable business on short-form platforms. He said Instagram removed its Reels creator fund and TikTok's fund pays very little even for millions of views because it is a fixed pie, so successful short-form creators try to move to YouTube videos or podcasts. (Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=450s)
- He says short video pushes creators to cram in retention tricks such as subtitles everywhere, very fast editing and unrelated clips like Minecraft gameplay, because retention is the one metric the algorithms use. (Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=225s; Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=315s)
- His central point is that short form has cracked discovery but cannot go deep, while long form goes deep but lacks discovery, so the fix is to use shorts as the way into long content. (Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=630s)
- He lists three things platforms must get right: link short and long seamlessly while keeping them separate, change the algorithm's target, and give creators a place to build real businesses. (Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=720s)
- He wants algorithms to move from the retention of a single video to **overall retention**, measured from the short through the long piece it leads to. That would favour creators who can hook quickly and then hold people on deeper content, which he says is the only place platforms can really earn from ads. (Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=765s)
- He ended that 2023 video by admitting he may be wrong and that everything may collapse into one minute videos. (Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=855s)
- In 2025 he said long-form YouTube has no rival for quality but suffers from the paradox of choice, since a grid of good videos makes choosing a pain, while infinite feeds remove the problem by removing the choice. (Social Media Is Broken But We Can Fix It at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HqYIUV7Eqjk&t=270s)
- He says his 2025 swipe design, where one swipe direction always means going deeper into a topic and the other could be used to ask for more varied content, would change the algorithm and the creator economy, because the algorithm would push the content and creators that can make people watch longer videos. (Social Media Is Broken But We Can Fix It at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HqYIUV7Eqjk&t=810s)
- In May 2025 he used YouTube Shorts as his example of a product being changed for the marginal user, the next user a company has not acquired yet. He said YouTube could have built community features or refreshed the homepage, but Shorts won the A/B test with a user who barely watches YouTube, and that for people who love YouTube, himself included, Shorts are annoying distractions. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=495s)
- He says that on short form the interface shapes the content itself. Caption length, whether the next video autoplays and whether comments cover or shrink the video all end up changing how millions of people create. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=450s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=585s)
- He says creators converge just as platforms do, because everyone sees the same retention graphs and is pushed toward the same hooks, editing style and tricks. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=810s)
- His line on that convergence: "The platform doesn't just host the content, it literally designs it." (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=810s)
- He says short-form platforms changed what viewers expect, because people got used to being fed videos without choosing. YouTube's design problem in 2026, as he puts it, is to keep what makes it loved while designing for that behaviour. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of YouTube Videos at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giP1iIbSVh0&t=180s)

## What happened when his channel blew up, and how does the money work?

- He says it took almost two years of making videos to reach 4,000 subscribers, and then the channel went from 4,000 to 40,000 subscribers with more than 2 million views in under 20 days. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=0s)
- He says blowing up was among the most stressful weeks of his life and not the dream moment, and that everybody tells you how to blow up but nobody tells you what happens when you do. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=0s)
- He says that if your following grows 10 or 100 times, 10 or 100 times more people will try to hack you. He got at least two or three emails a day dressed as sponsorship offers that were attempts to take the channel. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=0s)
- He says a channel that takes off becomes a real business within a few weeks, and real businesses pay taxes. He froze his YouTube payments, left the money in AdSense and realised he needed a company. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=225s)
- He says his channel earned a total of 469.57 in the whole of 2022, then 6,650 in AdSense revenue alone in the first few weeks of 2023. He does not state the currency. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=180s)
- He says the channel was not yet monetised in March 2022, and that until 2023 it had made no real money, about 20 bucks by his later telling. (You Control This Video's Thumbnail at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pol6UGKZpdY&t=90s; I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=630s)
- His way to earn more from an audience is to read the comments, because people tell you what they want. Viewers asked how he makes videos, so he started a course with an email list, and others asked about gear, so he opened an Amazon affiliates account and a gear page. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=270s)
- He says that once you have an audience you do not need an MBA: listen to what people are curious about and give it to them. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=315s)
- He says public information on sponsorship pricing is very thin. He did not know what to charge or what a view cap was, so he spent a night on forums and found a Discord community of creators comparing rates, pricing structures and good CPMs by audience location. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=315s)
- He says that when you start all you need is good videos, but treating the channel as a business means learning sales and negotiation so you get your fair share from brands. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=360s)
- He contrasts the channel with a nine-to-five, which grows in a straight line, and says: "YouTube is an exponential game." He worked on it for almost two years with basically nothing to show. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=360s)
- He says living through exponential growth once changed his sense of scale. Goals that looked huge a few weeks earlier looked achievable or too small, and in December 2022 he set 2023 targets of 200,000 subscribers and 200,000 in revenue. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=405s)
- He says a big-tech job gives a little credibility elsewhere but means nothing on YouTube. Before the growth his outreach to creators and brands got no answer, and after it he got podcast, collaboration, consulting and startup investing offers. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=450s)
- He says keeping a salary let him refuse many companies that wanted him to promote products he did not believe in, because being short of money pushes you to take what pays now. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=630s)
- On sponsors he states a few plain facts: he reached out to Raycast before it agreed to sponsor a video, Arc did not sponsor his 2023 Arc video, and the video about quitting his job carried no sponsor. (The Secrets That Make Your Phone Actually Work at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-YVtZV4DjCE&t=630s; Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Arc Browser at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdopamaLJC8&t=315s; I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=315s)
- His own paid product for creators is Story Behind, breakdowns of how his most successful videos were made, covering storytelling structure, packaging and editing. He says it is not a YouTube course, and that its landing page uses the same persuasion principles he exposes in his videos. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=765s; The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=675s)

## How did he fix his channel, and how does he run it?

- In 2021 he blamed his stalled channel on himself: "that's not the algorithm's fault that's my fault". (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=225s)
- His list starts with define your expectations and kill "meh" videos; later in the same video he says he decided to make the big change from Italian to English. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=270s)
- He says posting terrible first videos is a good thing. He backs it with a line he credits to the founder of LinkedIn, that if you are not ashamed of your first version you launched too late. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=45s)
- He says a strict schedule gave him mediocre videos. He delivered eight videos in eight weeks, and they were worse than they could have been because they had to go out. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=180s)
- He says it makes no sense to claim you do not care about subscribers. He wants his videos seen by as many people as possible, and says admitting that you care about views makes decisions easier, since you will not make a three hour conceptual video. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=315s)
- His rule is to publish only what he is proud of on the day it goes out, even if it looks bad a year later. He holds to it while admitting that pride did not predict views. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=360s; How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=135s)
- He says you cannot do everything on one channel unless you are Casey Neistat. In one year he tried travel, tech reviews, studying, personal development and technology, and he compares that to a supermarket with 700 boxes of cereal. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=405s)
- To pick a focus he tagged every past video and compared the categories by count and by how much he enjoyed making them. In 2021 that left two topics, personal development, and innovation and technology. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=450s)
- He says quality matters more than the publishing day, because nobody cares in the long run whether a video went out on Wednesday or Thursday. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=585s)
- **The Great Filter** is the checklist he kept at the top of his writing template. It asks whether the video fits his two topics, what he improved by making it, whether he would change something with more time, and whether he would click it in his own feed. If one answer fails he reworks or scraps the video. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=540s)
- He switched from Italian to English in 2021 with 670 subscribers, because he consumes content, works and half-writes his scripts in English. He expected a harder start, since English content on his topics was perhaps ten or a hundred times more plentiful, and judged the worldwide reach worth it. (I'm Switching to English and Starting From Scratch at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6BnCTqv0zA&t=0s; I'm Switching to English and Starting From Scratch at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6BnCTqv0zA&t=90s)
- He says he went from 1,000 to 100,000 subscribers in one year. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=540s)
- In October 2024 he called YouTube an unoptimized resource, the kind of opening each period has, giving a platform, income and a starting point for other businesses. He added that it will not stay unoptimized forever. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=540s)
- He ran the channel beside his job for three years to shorten what he calls the **ramen period**, and quit in 2024 when channel income was close to his salary but had not yet passed it. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=630s; I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=675s)
- By October 2024 he had started hiring collaborators for editing and writing, and he planned to turn the channel from a side project into something more structured. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=765s; I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=810s)
- In a 2025 video sponsored by Notion he called Notion his organisation tool of choice for managing the channel and his startup. In 2026 he said he was writing that video in VS Code, using Claude Code in a terminal as a writing assistant, with a system of skills he created that do research for him. (How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=540s; The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=180s)
- In November 2026 he announced he is turning the channel into a proper show that takes viewers behind the scenes of some of the best and most interesting tech products. The first two episodes were with Figma and Notion, and he said the regular deep dives are not going anywhere. (Something New Is Coming at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BEd9uo38GWA&t=45s)

## Where does he think AI and the wider web leave creators?

- In May 2022 he imagined TikTok or YouTube using AI to make most of their content, with a few people adding fresh ideas and the rest a remix. As someone who makes videos he found that both exciting and really scary. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=405s)
- In that 2022 video he said people who only adapt or distribute content are like the monks who copied books before the printing press: masters of a craft that has lost most of its value. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=360s)
- In October 2022 he said text-to-video AI hit close to home because he makes YouTube videos, but he did not think it had taken creativity away from people, only that the rules of the game would change. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=135s)
- In 2023 he said AI search could push people who make content for search onto platforms such as YouTube, Reddit, Substack and Medium, and that AI search engines will sooner or later have to pay creators for the content they use. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=765s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=720s)
- He says he owes a lot of what he does to YouTube and to things he learned online, and in 2022 he argued that the amount and quality of content has never been better, so what people need now is a "faucet", a way to control the flow of content in their lives. (Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=270s)

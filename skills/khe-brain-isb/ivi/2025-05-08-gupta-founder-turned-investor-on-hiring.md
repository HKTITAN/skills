# A founder turned investor on hiring and feedback, Vinita Gupta

Short typed notes from a session with Vinita Gupta, a founder and investor who lived in San Francisco for 20 years and took her company public in 1998. She spoke about learning as a founder, hiring and what investors look for.

## What was said

### Becoming a founder

- Vinita Gupta says she started with the mindset of being a founder and then figured out the product.
- She says customer feedback is going to be harsh and a founder must have a thick skin to handle it.
- She says a founder can teach themselves by staying in feedback mode.
- She says VCs look for drive, the ability to learn new things and grit.
- She says her company went public in 1998, and that the journey was exciting, hard and exhausting all at once.

### Hiring and managing

- She says hiring decisions are the toughest decisions a founder makes in a company.
- She says a candidate can show whether they can execute a particular skill, but management and executive ability are harder to see.
- She says to look for people who are talented though they do not come from the best schools or carry brand tags, and that the founder must sense they can do the work.
- Her example is an early employee without a finished engineering degree, who brought skills she did not have as a founder and did good work for her.
- She says other employees felt the managers and founders were an inner group, so she took coaching to get better.

### Customers and industries

- She says all industries are different but share one requirement, which is capital and the discipline of quality.
- She says the trend in Silicon Valley today is that more chief executives are in front of the customer, because people see the value of talking to customers.

# How to figure out product-market fit, Vamsi Krishna

Typed notes from an orientation talk by Vamsi Krishna, co-founder of Vedantu, on building and scaling a startup and how to figure out product-market fit. He split the journey into the first paid student and the journey from 1 to 100 students.

## What was said

### What to watch

- Vamsi Krishna names three things to watch: margins for financials, week on week growth for growth, and retention or renewals for experience.
- He says early stage founders fall into choosing a narrow, niche market.
- His example is a partnership with international board students and teachers that did really well and had a natural uptick. Vedantu focused on it for seven to eight months before realising the limit.
- He says free is good, but the minute you charge your retention may not hold, so price is part of product-market fit.
- He recommends the book Monetizing Innovation.
- He says margins start vanishing the minute you do digital marketing, and gives 20% margins as an example.

### Speed of discovery

- He says to optimise for speed of discovery, whether of product or market.
- He says that about 10 to 12 years ago they tried to create a Shopify for education, packaging courses and tools.
- He says they spent three to five weeks trying to get important meetings and got no input on the product, and after four to five months realised it would not work.
- He says to create an open product. Product founders tend to be guarded and say it is not ready yet.
- He says an open login that anyone can use is better if you want feedback.
- He says the first version of Vedantu and what exists today are very different, so there is no perfect product.

### The first paid student

- He says to beg, borrow or steal to get your first customer fast.
- He says they experimented for four months and then built the product in one week.
- He says to ask what your MVP is and how soon you can get to it.
- He says not to think about elaborate customer acquisition strategies, and to get your initial customers as soon as possible.
- He says a paid customer is the key validation for 99.9% of companies. The exception is companies that thrive on network effects.
- He says it took Vedantu almost eight months to get its first paid customer.
- He says to do things that do not scale.

### From 1 to 100 students

- He warns against assuming you have arrived at product-market fit before you have.
- He says getting a strong sense of unit economics and the business model is critical at this stage.
- He says you achieve product-market fit, then plateau or hit a wall, then iterate on everything and find it again.
- He cites Paul Graham's weekly growth rate of 5 to 10% on the right core metric. Below 4% something is off, and 6 to 7% is great.
- He says an NPS of 40% is critical before you think of blitzscaling.
- He says to ask the NPS question on the plain 1 to 10 scale and not alter it, if you want an unbiased response.
- He says the margins you are born with are the margins you die with, so unit economics is crucial, and asks whether your contribution margin is positive.
- He says that when there is no fit, either the product is wrong or the customer is wrong, so look at both ends.

## Examples and cases used

- Vedantu and international board students: a niche that grew naturally and held the company for seven to eight months.
- Vedantu's earlier idea of a Shopify for education: dropped after four to five months.

## Numbers given

- Krishna, citing Paul Graham: weekly growth of 5 to 10%, with under 4% a warning and 6 to 7% great.
- Krishna: an NPS of 40% before blitzscaling.
- Vedantu, as told by Vamsi Krishna: almost eight months to the first paid customer.

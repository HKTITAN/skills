# Product and design

For him, a product is judged by how little it asks of the person using it: it should be understood in seconds, teach itself instead of needing a tutorial, and cost as few steps as possible. Since mid-2025 his settled view is that the best interfaces disappear into daily life, that interfaces will be generated on demand for each person, and that AI makes taste, not code, the scarce part. He keeps stressing small details, motion and feedback as what makes people attached to a product. His 2022-24 posts are mostly feature requests and tips for tools he used, above all Notion.

## Should a product need a tutorial?

- He says that if you must explain a product before someone can use it, the on-ramp is the problem. With Smash&Clash he tried a tutorial, he and the players disliked it, and he replaced it with a board zoom that shows 4 ≥ 2 capturing and 3 < 5 not capturing, in red. "a tutorial is often an apology for a confusing interface." (2026-09-13-if-have-explain-your, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2099039658323870191)
- He says nobody opens a game or a product to read instructions, so it has to be intuitive on first contact; instead of teaching the game he changed it so it teaches itself, and the same zoom-in added drama and made it more fun. "nobody opens a game/product because they want to read instructions." (2026-09-13-if-have-explain-your, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2099039658323870191)
- He praises products you get within five seconds. "you instantly get it within 5 seconds." (2026-05-19-jahjiren-one-those-rare, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056625376626192500)
- He says every extra step costs momentum. "every extra click is a tax on momentum." (2026-05-11-every-extra-click-tax, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2053906326988931477)
- (2024) After a 1:1 Notion session with a beginner student, he wrote that showing features against the student's real academic needs made the first experience much better. (2024-09-09-recently-conducted-1-1, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7238954886871031808)
- (2023) He said a complex-looking app put him off the moment he opened it. "I opened the app, saw how complex it looked" (2023-01-11-omnagupta-vensykrishna-am-ngl, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1613174745914314752)
- (2023) He noticed that people who already knew Notion found his introduction overwhelming while newcomers were curious, so how a tool is introduced changes how it lands. (2023-08-31-sev-estil-introduced-some, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1697190444080173493)
- (2022) He told Coda that onboarding friction for new users was something the maker should fix. (2022-06-22-coda-hq-thecodaguy-homework, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1539647365451108352)

## What makes a product feel good to use?

- He says most people underestimate how much motion and feedback shape emotional attachment to a product. (2026-05-12-most-people-underestimate-how, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2054135387707367624)
- He says micro-interactions and small details matter a lot. "micro-interactions like these matter a lot!" (2026-08-30-micro-interactions-like-these, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2094115280595415316; 2026-06-20-benjitaylor-little-details-matter, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2068381672928932187)
- He points to WhatsApp shipping many tiny updates that remove friction from things billions already do, which makes a product feel 10x better without changing what it is. (2026-09-03-whatsapp-has-shipped-many, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2095472381653631270)
- He says constraints produce better outcomes than you expect, and repeats it in several posts and replies. "constraints create way better outcomes than you can ever imagine." (2026-09-03-constraints-create-way-better, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2095534238947061797; 2026-09-07-jenny-wen-constraints-create, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2097019802883891225; 2026-09-20-constraints-create-better-outcomes, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2101705796463796431)
- He says care compounds, and that good products come from care rather than growth hacks, positioning or trend chasing. "care compounds!" (2026-05-19-arsihoxha-care-compounds, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056802675484971156; 2026-05-19-noelkhw-no-growth-hacks, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056625110770212991)
- Small visual calls he has made: a paper fold like Arc Search for one UI, solid colors over gradients in Gmail's redesign, a custom right-click menu on every website, and the gap between geometric and optical centering. (2026-07-04-60fpsdesign-nice-paper-fold, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2073392689945665992; 2026-05-21-sharavarora-gmail-yea-solid, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2057356632632561941; 2026-10-18-all-websites-should-ship, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2111699566487879694; 2026-10-28-coobyk-geometric-vs-optical, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2115236814248431640)
- He names Telegram as having great UX, praises Benji Taylor's chart shake in liveline, and likes NotionMail's "pat-pat" interaction enough to suggest it for another product. (2026-05-19-motion-mau-discord-telegram, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056642459015537032; 2026-04-17-benjitaylor-love-chart-shake, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2045226200466174121; 2026-06-03-anaarsonist-add-little-pat, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2062210688156860604)
- He calls native Windows apps the biggest missed opportunity in software, because most modern apps feel like websites in a resizable container. (2026-06-02-think-biggest-missed-opportunity, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061868681622425830)
- On a Braille-learning app, he said vibration that maps to Braille, or a visual display of it, is not the raised-dot pattern, so it defeats the goal of learning by touch. "you’re learning a vibration mapped to Braille, not the tactile pattern itself." (2026-08-22-sharavarora-if-by-feel, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2091143009828262326; 2026-08-22-sharavarora-defeats-purpose-braille, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2091141936619147386)
- (2022) He said he designs minimal to match the host product, preferred transparent images over 7-8 word headings that break on small screens, and disliked embeds that scroll inside a Notion page. (2022-06-22-karthik-notionhq-eah-sure, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1539618495503007745; 2022-05-09-hey-issam-redgregory1-if, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1523548069991567360; 2022-05-14-amngpta-gumroad-notionapi-like, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1525311952494288896)

## Does AI make design and taste matter less?

- He says design is not dead, in reply to a post claiming AI kills it, and calls generic AI design soulless slop. "design is NOT dead" (2026-06-25-breejeanadkat-grok-imagine-design, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2069976172352700490; 2026-05-22-100-ai-design-still, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2057743728476058087)
- He says AI makes taste more valuable, since two people on the same model get different results depending on whether they know what good looks like. "it's making taste more valuable." (2026-06-11-50-hours-fable-5, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2065110652935659695)
- He splits the work: speed is the tool's job, while deciding what is worth building and what good means stays with the human. "speed is the tool’s job. taste is yours." (2026-06-01-gap-between-people-who, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061415603920044197)
- He says taste is acquired by paying attention and caring harder, that restraint is a skill, that motion should clarify, and that delight is a sign someone cared; he learned this partly by copying Benji Taylor's work. "taste is acquired." (2026-05-22-came-across-benji-taylor, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2057906570927993012)
- He says simple does not mean shallow and fluid does not mean decorative. "simple does not mean shallow." (2026-05-22-came-across-benji-taylor, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2057906570927993012)
- He says many design rules are just fear of experimenting. (2026-05-14-benjitaylor-lot-design-rules, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2054962534424477899)
- He wants to see the design thinking behind a prompt (iterations, references, decisions, effort), not only the prompt. (2026-05-17-no-but-seriously-what, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056009764346491357)
- He says most design tools give you a mockup, while his tldraw plus Claude Code setup edits the running product and swaps its design system, so designing and building are blurring. "the line between designing and building is getting blurrier." (2026-10-18-using-tldraw-offline-emilkowalskis, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2111747149725831646)
- He says real-world utility plus beautiful design is the strongest combination for AI products right now. (2026-05-19-aretaidos-spot-real-world, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056622747577696301)

## What should a product do for the person using it?

- He says products matter when they resolve an emotional tension: people buy relief from guilt, anxiety or confusion, not features. "most products don’t fail because they’re bad." (2025-07-22-most-products-don-t, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7353424109071908865)
- He says the best products enhance human moments rather than replace them. (2026-05-19-exactly-best-products-don, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056625000887787688)
- He says the less time users need inside your product to get value, the better the business should do. (2026-10-29-made-bet-customers-spending, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2115667849813647497)
- In a long thread on Greg Isenberg's map of startup ideas, he says action apps that do the task will replace apps you stare at, and may even lower engagement, because they compete for responsibility, not attention. "Action apps compete for responsibility." (2026-05-17-greg-mapped-out-what, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2055908062079410565)
- His Swiggy recipe for Poke was built so ordering feels like texting a friend: something you ask instead of an app you operate. (2026-06-10-what-if-ordering-from, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2064597757428748764)
- He says good tools adapt to the user quietly (his example is Omi), and good tooling simplifies complexity by design. (2026-01-05-most-tools-ask-adapt, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2008186643203965256; 2026-05-18-thiagotf-omidotme-good-tooling, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056243316757504133)
- He says the best product ideas feel obvious in hindsight, and he is skeptical of a feature that looks cool without a strong use case. (2026-05-19-iamroyoto-right-feels-obvious, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056625754461606221; 2026-06-03-uiuxarih-nikitabier-looks-cool, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2062216092920184967)

## Where are interfaces going?

- He says the best interfaces disappear into everyday life, and gives Poke as the example. "the best interfaces are the ones that dissapear into everyday life!" (2026-07-12-naveens459-swiggy-interaction-best, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2076285742779871609; 2026-06-06-over-1-4k-people, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2063276611001339951)
- He says the best interface is no interface, and the best AI product is one people never remember opening. (2026-05-19-aretaidos-best-interface-no, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056623028554138065; 2026-05-07-best-ai-product-one, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2052411659042640376)
- He says mailing interfaces of the future will disappear into daily life; Notion Mail tried and could not, and Notion agents may get there. (2026-06-27-elie2222-future-mailing-interfaces, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2070755944632631415)
- He believes interfaces will be generated on demand and services will go headless, so agents, not people, will click through browsers. "interfaces will be on-demand" (2026-09-21-vijayshekhar-s-true-tho, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2101870918436110455; 2026-10-10-yes-but-see-not, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2108904314924372336)
- He says a product will no longer have one home: usability moves from one interface for everyone to composing the right one per person, and because agents are non-deterministic, design systems, component contracts and deterministic rendering primitives matter more. (2026-10-20-your-product-wont-live, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2112453994643870174)
- He tied the Smash&Clash SDK to this idea of on-demand interfaces, and called one example of generated UI what on-demand UI was meant to be. (2026-10-13-iamrishitabh-not-really-why, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2110007267466911796; 2026-10-29-every-time-farza-posts, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2115661452136927412)
- He says the next interface is based on intent and will learn you instead of asking you to learn it; a hands-free wearable beats pulling out a phone, opening an app and searching. "the next interface is based on intent." (2026-05-16-maxbatt-nathievr-josethevrtech-still, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2055711967562912173; 2025-12-24-next-interface-wont-ask, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2003887211587231907)
- He says smart glasses and agentic interfaces will remove the barrier between tech and the physical world, with the human still deciding, and phones will feel like button phones do today. (2026-07-16-think-weve-normalized-something, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2077575735834034509; 2026-07-10-aaryanpatel-06-iamjosh000-yep, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2075419562666025365)
- In his XR writing he says computing is leaving the rectangle, that it is about alignment rather than escape, and that interaction which requires thinking makes XR fail. He rates Snap best in AR for ten years of quiet work on sensing and positioning. (2026-04-13-pity-moth-confusing-lamp, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7449536835426443264; 2026-01-01-next-evolution-computing-obvious, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2006801057591312689)

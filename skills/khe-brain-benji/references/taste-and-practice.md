# Taste and practice

One claim per line, each with its piece. This is the file for questions about him and his way of working. It holds what he states, and nothing about what he is like.

## Taste

- Taste, as he describes it, is small decisions made over and over until the reliable ones are known. That is why it can be written down. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- Stageshow reflects his own taste and he says so: it is not a neutral standard. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- Drawesome is opinionated on purpose. Its defaults are the shippable version. (Drawesome, https://benji.org/drawesome)
- Honk split opinion among design and technology people from the first day, and he counts both the critics and the fans as valid. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- The supplied looks in Stageshow are examples, not a menu of allowed styles. The brief may call for a language none of them use. (Stageshow, references/backgrounds.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, references/looks.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A styled brief gets its colours, radii, mascot and motion in full, still as a product that could ship. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## How he decides

- Write the plan before any code: where it sits, the focal object, the action, the payoff, and what can be left out. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Between a richer story and a calmer frame, he chooses the frame. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- He turned an open wish into constraints before the icon system worked. (Morphing icons with Claude, https://benji.org/morphing-icons-with-claude)
- He starts from the closest good example and adapts it. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- He proves a choice by removing it: the same screen without the motion, the same prompt without the skill. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values; Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- He expects many passes and enjoys them: dozens per demo with an agent. (Agentation, https://benji.org/agentation)
- He fixes the source of a fault, the skill, and reruns, in place of patching one result. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)

## What he traded

- Family launched later than it could have, in exchange for the experience the team wanted. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Keeping each tray a different height sometimes forced a copy rewrite or a redesign, and the team paid that cost. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Honk had no send button, no history and no drafts, and he names real-time-only messaging as a reason it was impractical. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Honk shipped without group chats and without Android, and he lists both among the reasons it ended. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- He is glad the team knew when to change course. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)

## Practice

- Document a product thoroughly while it is alive. Gathering good footage of Honk after it closed was tedious. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- He built Family over about two years and has seen many recreations of its components since. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- The Agentation article's demos and the whole documentation site were built with a coding agent and Agentation itself. (Agentation, https://benji.org/agentation)
- The 21 morphing icons were built in a single session with a coding agent, with the judgement about what looked right coming from him. (Morphing icons with Claude, https://benji.org/morphing-icons-with-claude)
- Honk's fluid transitions came first and were taken further in Family. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)

# Gotchas

This file is **append-only**. Each time an answer reports something he did not say, stretches a line past its platform or its year, or misses a piece, add a one-line gotcha here. Do not edit an entry unless it is outright wrong. Negative examples are the highest-signal content in a skill over time: the instructions should change rarely and this file should grow steadily.

## Format

```text
- [YYYY-MM-DD] One-line description of the gotcha. → the fix, and the reference that owns the rule
```

Keep each to one line. Your own disagreements with him go in [pov](pov.md), not here.

## Entries

- [2026-11-21] The captions have no speaker labels, so a host's question, a guest's story or a caller's line reads as his. → a line is his only when the context shows it, and a doubtful line stays out; [sources](sources.md), How it was read
- [2026-11-21] The upload date of a video is not always the year of the talk: several uploaded in 2016 are talks from 2009 to 2014, and the title says so. → when the year matters, use the year in the title, and say which year you used; [sources](sources.md), How it was read
- [2026-11-21] He gives different figures for the same thing in different years, for example the first four years of VaynerMedia and the sales of Wine Library. → give the figure from the piece asked about, with its year, say he gives others, and never average them; [disagreements](disagreements.md), Figures that change by source
- [2026-11-21] Some articles under his byline are team-written guides, and some are written by named colleagues. → report such a piece as what the article says, never as a sentence he wrote; [sources](sources.md), How it was read
- [2026-11-21] A title can promise more than the piece says, and the captions are the only record of what was said. → cite the line that carries the claim and its time, never the title's promise; [sources](sources.md), How it was read

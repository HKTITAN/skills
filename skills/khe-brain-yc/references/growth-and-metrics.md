# Growth and metrics

This file answers how fast a startup should grow, which number to track, how to judge and improve retention and conversion, which channels and experiments to use, and when a growth team makes sense. Paul Graham, Michael Seibel, Divya Bhat, David Lieb, Kevin Hale, Gustaf Alstromer, Anu Hariharan and Sam Altman speak in it, with an unsigned Y Combinator post. The advice differs by stage, so read each line for whether it is addressed to a startup before launch, in the batch, or with retention already proven, and read each figure with its speaker and year.

## How fast should I grow?

- Graham's 2012 essay defines a startup as a company designed to grow fast and says "The only essential thing is growth." (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Graham's 2012 essay says an answer like a hundred new customers a month gets the reply "That's not a rate." because what counts is new customers as a ratio of existing ones, and a constant monthly number means the rate is falling. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Graham's 2012 essay puts a good growth rate during the Y Combinator batch at 5 to 7 percent a week, says a startup hitting 10 percent a week is doing exceptionally well, and reads 1 percent a week as a sign the founders have not yet figured out what they are doing. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Graham's 2012 essay limits those weekly figures, in a footnote, to startups that are already launched or able to launch while in the batch. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, repeats Graham's figures for a company going through the batch, 5 to 7 percent week over week as good and 10 percent as exceptional, and says how much growth is enough depends on the business and its stage. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Graham's 2012 essay works the compounding from 1000 dollars a month, a figure he calls typical early in the batch: at 1 percent a week the company makes 7900 dollars a month four years later, and at 5 percent a week it makes 25 million dollars a month. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Graham's 2012 essay says a startup on the Facebook or Twitter route, with no definite plan yet to make money, needs a higher growth rate, because a company of that kind cannot succeed at all without a huge number of users. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Graham's 2012 essay advises startups to choose a growth rate they believe is within reach and then simply hit it each week: hitting it makes the week a success, and missing it means failing at the only thing that mattered. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Graham's 2012 essay says the target rate can make every decision for the founders, whether that is two days at a conference, another programmer, more marketing, courting a big customer or adding a feature. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Graham's 2012 essay excludes trickery from that rule in a footnote, naming paying more for users than their lifetime value, counting inactive users as active and metering out invites to draw a perfect curve, because these throw off the founders' own compass. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Graham's 2012 essay says founders who change their plan as needed to keep hitting 10 percent a week may end with a different company, and that anything growing consistently at that rate is almost certainly a better idea than the one they started with. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Altman, in an undated post, accepts that startups are defined by growth and then says growth is not the first step, and that a founder who tries to grow before making a product people love is unlikely to succeed. (Before growing your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4p-before-growing-your-startup)
- The Y Combinator post answers the criticism that the program pushes companies to grow at all costs by saying it pushes them to talk to users, build what they want and iterate quickly, with growth as the natural result of those three things. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, opens by telling founders who have not launched that his growth talk is not very relevant to them yet. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, warns that slow growth can be mistaken for product-market fit, and says that after working at slow, steady companies, her own startups and DoorDash, the kinds of growth feel fundamentally different. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)

## Which one number do I track?

- Graham's 2012 essay says revenue is the best thing to measure growth in, and for startups not charging at first the next best is active users, since he expects later revenue to be a constant multiple of them. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2018, says the top-line number is almost always revenue for a company that will ever charge customers, and a usage metric such as daily active users only for one that never will, like Facebook, and he says perhaps one percent of the founders who claim neither fits might be right. (Building product, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7s-building-product)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2021, says that for most companies joining a batch the KPI is one of four things: revenue, usage for advertising or social companies, letters of intent, pilots or signed contracts for enterprise companies, and a technical milestone for moonshot, science and biology companies. (How to choose a metric and set your KPI, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/9i-how-to-choose-a-metric-and-set-your-kpi)
- Seibel, in the 2021 talk, says "Don't get creative about that top KPI." because investors use it to measure a company against similar ones, and a metric nobody else uses makes the comparison hard. (How to choose a metric and set your KPI, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/9i-how-to-choose-a-metric-and-set-your-kpi)
- Seibel, in the 2021 talk, pictures metrics as a pyramid: one metric on top that shows how the company is doing, and three to four beneath it that the founders believe drive it, which are the ones to brainstorm features on and optimize each week. (How to choose a metric and set your KPI, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/9i-how-to-choose-a-metric-and-set-your-kpi)
- Seibel, later in the same 2021 talk, gives the base of the pyramid as three to five metrics and says these, unlike the top one, can be custom to the product or business. (How to choose a metric and set your KPI, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/9i-how-to-choose-a-metric-and-set-your-kpi)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says a launched startup's primary KPI should be revenue growth, that a KPI other than revenue is seldom right, and that before launch the KPI might be weeks until launch or the number of conversations with users. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, allows exceptions: a marketplace might choose signups or GMV, and an early enterprise company whose sales cycle is long might choose letters of intent. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, defines vanity metrics as things that make a founder feel good and can be bragged about, and says making them the primary KPIs puts the founder's time on the wrong things. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says a founder who plans to charge should get paid from day one or at least leave free users out of the growth count, because free customers expect different things and give the wrong feedback. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, tells the Scribd story: it went through the program in summer 2006, spent four years mainly growing a free product, began charging in mid-2010, lost over 90 percent of its customers and, in her words, finally had a business. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- The Y Combinator post says a startup should choose, very early, the one or two key metrics it will measure success by, and then decide what to do almost only by the effect on those metrics. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- Seibel, in the 2018 talk, says to start with five to ten simple stats, because tracking the 150 things users could do makes the analytics hard to use at the beginning. (Building product, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7s-building-product)

## How do I set KPIs and weekly goals?

- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says to set a KPI goal for the week, her example being 10 more paying customers by next week, and to make sure it ladders up to any longer-term goal. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, describes a top-down way to set the target: fix a future milestone, her example being 5,000 dollars in MRR by the end of Startup School, and back into the weekly growth rate it requires. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, describes a bottoms-up way: ask what is realistic in the next week and project from there, helped by asking what the team could do with unlimited funding and then how to reach that with limited funding. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Graham's 2012 essay, describing how growth is measured during the Y Combinator batch, states the target as a weekly percentage, his example being 7 percent a week. (Startup = Growth, https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)
- Hariharan's guide, written for a growth team's first year at a company that has already proven retention, says the goal must be an absolute number and cannot be a percentage or rate change. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says three to five secondary KPIs is a fair number, names retention and churn, unit economics and customer acquisition cost as examples, and says they exist to show the founder is not cheating on the primary KPI or to give early signal when it lags. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says her startup Rickshaw, after trouble fundraising, tried to optimize growth and unit economics together and landed in slow growth, while DoorDash kept its focus on top-line growth, and she tells founders not to optimize two hard things at once. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- The Y Combinator post says growth on an unprofitable product only drains cash, and reports Paul Buchheit's line that taking 80 cents from a customer and handing back a dollar never makes sense. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says the ratio of acquisition cost to lifetime value is usually a concern after product-market fit, and that for now a founder should worry only about payback period, ideally zero because nothing is spent on acquisition. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, gives Super Daily as the case for working on the biggest blocker: high-intent users were dropping out late in signup because they wanted a milk brand it did not carry, so it added the brand instead of polishing the signup screen, and its conversion rate rose by 50 percent. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, gives a routine for the weekly goal: write ideas down without starting them, rank them by probability of success and then by complexity, and pick only a couple to try. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, answers the question of new users or retention with both: first check there is no churn problem and that users stay long enough to repay what it cost to acquire them and to tell friends, and beyond that focus on bringing in new users. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)

## How do I know whether retention is good?

- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, calls cohort retention the single best way he has found to tell whether a founder has made something people want, because it follows separate groups of new users over time instead of the whole user base mixed together. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says the action that counts a user as active should be one tied to real value, and offers viewing three or more posts for Instagram, completing a ride for Uber and viewing a photo full screen for Google Photos. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says the time period should match the intended use: daily for a social or entertainment app, weekly for a utility such as Google Photos or Uber, and quarterly or longer for a travel app such as Airbnb. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, starts from the metric that represents the product's value and how often it should happen: bookings and stays yearly for Airbnb, coming back daily for Instagram, running payroll biweekly or monthly for Gusto, and riders weekly or monthly for Lyft. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says "the only, only thing that matters is whether your cohort curves get flat" and that the height at which they flatten matters far less. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Hariharan's guide says a stable long-term retention of 10 percent is poor for a social network, and that retention should be benchmarked against companies in the same vertical. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan, in an undated post, lists median long-term retention by vertical: 55 percent for social networks, 22 percent for on-demand, 16 percent for e-commerce and 33 percent for subscription at month 12, and 29 percent for travel at year 2. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide gives three checks for good retention: the long-term curve is stable and parallel to the x-axis, it is in line with the benchmark for the vertical, and newer cohorts do better than older ones. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says the weekly curves of early Google Photos dropped quickly and then went flat between 20 and 40 percent depending on country and device, and that about six weeks after launch he was pretty certain the product could eventually reach 20 percent of all humans. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, reads a curve that flattens into a steady line of weekly users as product-market fit for those users, and a curve that loses more people every week as the sign of a bad product. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, shows a curve he identifies as DoorDash, at 30 percent after two months and 21 percent after 20 months, and calls it pretty good. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, calls Net Promoter Score a worse way to measure product-market fit, saying it tracks how a company is perceived and does not necessarily track whether the product is good. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Altman, in a footnote to an undated post, offers Net Promoter Score as another good way of measuring whether users love a product. (Before growing your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4p-before-growing-your-startup)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says that when a product is meant to be paid for, the users counted on the retention graph should be paying ones, since free use of such a product is not a good sign of fit. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says payment alone is often a poor measure of activity, because users stop using a product before they stop paying for it, and recommends counting a user who is both paying and active in the period. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says the biggest mistake is choosing too large a time period, and admits that at Bump the team moved from weekly to monthly to quarterly curves ahead of investor meetings for a product meant to be used frequently. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, warns against counting a shallow action such as opening the app, because the team will end up gaming it, and recalls Google+ counting a click on a notification bell shown across Google products as active use. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, recommends building the curves by hand first, from logs with a script or in Google Sheets, and then comparing them with the analytics tool, which may be showing rolling retention instead of separated cohorts. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)

## How do I improve retention?

- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, names improving the product as the most obvious route: new use cases, a faster product and simpler flows make the curves flatten, and flatten at higher levels. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says acquiring better-matched users is often the easiest way to improve cohorts, and recalls a Google marketing push that brought young users into Google Photos whose cohorts retained badly because a tool for keeping memories did not suit them. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says that when curves do not flatten, an immediate next step is to slice cohorts by country, type of customer or company size, since some slices may be flat while others are poor. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says investing in onboarding and activation is often the cheapest and easiest improvement, and that companies building tools for businesses tend to build the tool and neglect teaching people to fit it into their workflow. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Lieb, in a talk the library dates to 2024, says a retention curve shows whether things are working and will not say what to change, so when curves do not flatten the sure next step is to go and talk to customers. (How To Improve Cohort Retention, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LV-how-to-improve-cohort-retention)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, describes growth as the gap between conversion and churn and says churn is much the easier of the two to work on. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hariharan's guide says a company that has not yet nailed retention can apply the growth method to retention itself, as Stitch Fix did by hiring a retention-focused product manager to run experiments before it invested in acquiring new customers. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)

## How do I improve conversion?

- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says the only time to work on conversion rate is when the bucket leaks, and that he first checks whether a company should instead be putting more into the top of the funnel. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, gives benchmarks of about 0.5 percent for shareware, about 2 percent for casual download games and 1.5 to 5 percent for freemium software as a service, and says a company whose pages convert at about 3 percent usually has other things to work on. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says conversion can run far higher than those benchmarks, citing Flickr at 5 to 10 percent in its heyday and TurboTax Online at 70 percent, the latter because its visitors arrive with very high intent. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, treats every conversion problem as an interface problem of closing the knowledge gap, a model he credits to Jared Spool: either raise what the user knows or lower what the interface requires them to know. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, runs seven questions on every page: what is the call to action, what is this, is it right for me, is it legit, who else is using it, how much is it or what is the catch, and where can I get help. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says the call to action should sit as close as possible to the moment the product clicks for the user, and that in critiques he often finds it many steps away. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, tests whether a page says what the product is by looking for one sentence he could paste into an email to his mother and have her understand, and says that ninety-nine percent of the time marketing jargon leaves no such sentence. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says showing who else uses the product is its own bar: in one critique he finds press, partner and award logos but no actual users, which makes him nervous, and in another he praises a count of people who downloaded the templates. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Hale, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says many B2B enterprise companies are afraid to post prices, that nobody uses a product without knowing the cost, and that a free product should explain how the company makes money or visitors grow uneasy. (How to improve conversion rates, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6l-how-to-improve-conversion-rates)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says Airbnb's site was a four-page funnel from home page to booking page, that only one or two percent of people got from the first page to the fourth, and that the job is to find why people drop at each step. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)

## Which growth channels and experiments should I use?

- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says a launch does not bring users because the world is busy and nobody is waiting, so a very small startup grows by unscalable work, starting with friends, then friends of friends, then people beyond them. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says a product built around a rare behavior needs to be on Google, through paid search or SEO, because people search for the things they seldom do. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says a product people already talk about, such as Lyft, Uber or Airbnb, should focus on virality and referrals. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says a product that gets better with more users needs a good viral loop, as LinkedIn asks new users to invite others when they sign up. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says a company that can write a list of all its customers, even a list of 100,000 or 500,000, should expect to do sales, and should start by listing decision makers with emails and phone numbers. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says the first lesson of paid growth is not to do it without revenue, and calls buying ads that can never be paid back the most common founder mistake. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says predicted revenue from a customer must exceed acquisition cost, and that a startup should not bank on an eight-month projection but aim to recover the cost within one to three months or on the first transaction. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says organic acquisition is ideal early on, and that paid tests are fine so that ad spend can be turned up once the product is growing organically and the payback period is understood, but not too early. (Setting KPIs and goals, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says most really big companies relied on one or two channels, naming SEO for TripAdvisor and Pinterest, and that getting good at one channel takes a lot of work. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Hariharan's guide says most products find one or two channels early that really work, and that about 70 percent of the growth experts interviewed named referrals as the top channel in the first year. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, defines a referral as a financial incentive to tell friends, describes Airbnb's as 40 dollars for the new user and 20 dollars for the sender, and does not expect such an offer to work at the very beginning. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says most startups can ignore A/B testing for a long time, that it becomes a great decision tool later, and that an online A/B testing calculator will say whether it is worth doing yet. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Hariharan's guide says a company at scale typically runs one experiment per growth engineer per week, that teams running more than 100 experiments a year report only a third turning out positive, and in the next sentence puts the success rate at 20 to 30 percent. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)

## When is growth work premature?

- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says growth work should wait until a startup has product-market fit and good retention, and that his advice on channels does not matter while most people who try the product leave and never come back. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says "Most startups don't have product-market fit." and that founders tell themselves they do. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)
- Altman, in an undated post, says some early use is still worth getting because it teaches what customers want, but focusing heavily on growth before the product is loved produces a leaky bucket: users come in, do not stay and likely will not return. (Before growing your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4p-before-growing-your-startup)
- Altman, in an undated post, says a loved product is much easier to grow because users are easier to retain and tell others, which makes them in effect a free marketing and sales force. (Before growing your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4p-before-growing-your-startup)
- Altman, in an undated post, says an unloved product ends up leaning on ads, marketing or PR to keep growing, which is very hard to sustain, and that the larger a company grows the harder it is to correct course. (Before growing your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4p-before-growing-your-startup)
- Altman, in an undated post, tells founders already growing a company around a mediocre product to fix it now and not to raise growth capital to avoid the problem, since the problems remain and expectations rise. (Before growing your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4p-before-growing-your-startup)
- Altman, in an undated post, says the same holds for enterprise and hard tech companies, where selling gets much easier once word has spread that the product is great. (Before growing your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4p-before-growing-your-startup)
- The Y Combinator post says it makes little sense to grow before a startup has made something its customers want, and that poor retention is always the result. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- The Y Combinator post puts the same point in its closing checklist as "Growth is the result of a great product not the precursor". (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- Hariharan's guide says investing in a growth program before a company has proven it can retain customers is a great way to waste money and put the company's future at risk. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)

## When and how do I build a growth team?

- Hariharan's guide says that in a company's early days nearly everyone is responsible for growth, and that a dedicated growth team is formed to pour fuel on product-market fit through structured experiments once retention is proven. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide gives the most common first-year growth team as one growth-focused product manager, two to three growth engineers and one to two growth data scientists. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide says most companies made their first growth hire when about 15 engineers were working on product, and that with strong retention the growth product manager is likely to be the third or fourth product manager. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide says CEOs most often err by waiting too long to hire a growth-focused product manager, and that the trend is toward building the team as soon as product-market fit and retention are strong. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide says the growth product manager should be intensely data-driven and have experience at a company that drove growth in a competitive space, which more than 90 percent of the experts called important, and that this rules out recruiting from a company like Google or Apple. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide reports the experts' most common advice on difficulty: set the goal halfway between sandbagging and too hard to achieve. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide says all of the experts held that the CEO must be aligned when the goal is set, and that CEOs who wait too long or do not fully endorse it can severely hinder the team's first year. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide says Facebook kept growth as a separate team so that someone owned monthly active users, while Uber, Airbnb and Slack began with separate growth teams and later merged them into product. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide says housing growth under marketing was the option most experts liked least. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Hariharan's guide says a growth team at scale can exceed 100 cross-functional people, about half of them engineers. (How to set up, hire, and scale a growth strategy and team, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/59-how-to-set-up-hire-and-scale-a-growth-strategy-and-team)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says a company should build a culture of experimentation in which data, and not the loudest voice in the room, settles the decision, and that he expects this not to matter yet for his audience though it will at some point. (Growth for startups, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6k-growth-for-startups)

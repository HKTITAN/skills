The plan breaks three positions he states and meets one exception he praises, and the largest fault is a year of building before any check that people have the problem (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 0:00; Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Figma at 9:00).
Breaks: a year of building before any check. In January 2023 he says most startups die after spending years and a lot of cash on something people do not want, and he offers a fake door test and a Wizard of Oz test to check demand without building (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 0:00; Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 6:00).
Breaks: friends and family as the judges. He calls asking them whether the idea is good the worst mistake a founder can make, because they will say it is great, and only the audience the founder defined counts (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 3:45).
Breaks: `all creators` as the audience. He says to narrow to the people who have the problem in its most severe form, and his own example narrows creators to YouTubers with 2,000 to 10,000 subscribers who upload regularly (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 0:45; Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 1:30).
Holds: building in private. In March 2025 he says Figma did the opposite of the launch fast playbook by working in private for 3 years, and that this became a secret of its success (Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Figma at 9:00).
Also: on the round and the eight hires, in February 2024 he says building something big without asking anyone for money can sometimes be a secret weapon, and adds that funded companies will still happen (The Weird Rise Of Anti-Startups at 12:00).

# Devices and life after the phone

This theme is his thinking about devices as a category question: what made the smartphone win, why it has stalled, what he expects to replace it, and what the hardware around it gets right or wrong. Most of it comes from his AR and Google Maps videos of 2022, his smartphone, Vision Pro and AI gadget videos of 2023 and 2024, and his core input and hardware videos of 2026. Product facts are his research as of each video's date, and his predictions changed over time, so each one carries its year, including the 2023 reversal where he stopped treating AR as the reason for the next device.

## Why did the smartphone win?

- He holds that every device is its **core input**, and that the core input completely changes how you use the device; on mobile, he says, every interaction is designed around scroll being more important than tapping. "Every device is its core input." (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=630s)
- He says a phone does not register a tap when your finger lands; it waits to see whether you move, which would be a scroll. Reading Android's open source ViewConfiguration file, he gives the tap timeout as 100 milliseconds and the touch slop as 8 dp, and says the iPhone works the same way with a wait generally agreed to be 150 milliseconds. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=270s)
- He calls this input priority the main reason smartphones as we know them won and everything else failed. "Scrolling comes first, and tapping comes second." (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=315s)
- On a computer the click comes first, and he says that even with trackpads the interfaces are still built for clicking. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=315s)
- He says that once scrolling is the main interaction the screen itself becomes the control, so arrows and buttons disappear on mobile. Draggable carousels feel clunky on desktop for the reverse reason. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=360s)
- He rejects screen size as the explanation, pointing to the Teenage Engineering OP-1, a tiny synthesizer with a very small screen that he says big names in music use to produce hits. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=135s)
- He rejects imprecise fingers as the explanation too: before the iPhone there were stylus phones with precise input and interfaces like tiny desktops, and he says they were bad. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=180s)
- He says scrolling as a core input allows one to four actions that feel natural, while a desktop can show 30 buttons at once that feel less natural. That is his reason the phone became a consumption device, the laptop a creation device, and why small children pick up phones so easily. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=540s; The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=630s)
- He says the core input decides how long you stay: on the phone Instagram content glides past without tapping or choosing and 40 minutes go by, while in a browser the same app and content get closed after five. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=495s)
- He says replacing taps with scrolls made products more addicting, naming pull to refresh in place of a refresh button and infinite scroll in place of pages. He calls these gestures much more natural, while relaying that the inventor of infinite scroll most regrets not packaging his inventions with the philosophy for how they should be used, and that the inventor of pull to refresh calls it addictive and regrets the attention his phone takes from his kids. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=585s)
- He tells the origin story this way: Apple's phone effort was stuck on a click wheel for moving through contacts, so Steve Jobs challenged Bas Ording to demo scrolling a list of names on a screen with no buttons. He says Apple patented the result and sued Samsung for a billion dollars when it was copied. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=450s)
- He says smart TVs are hated because the interface of rows and thumbnails was designed for scrolling but is driven by four arrows and a button taken from old TV remotes. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=720s)
- He says you cannot transplant one device's core input into another: Apple shipped a TV remote with a touch surface in 2015, everyone hated it, and Apple went back to buttons. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=765s)
- His test for any new interface is that it must be easier than the one before it. He says touchscreens won because tapping was easier than buttons and keyboards. (We Need To Talk About The AI Pin at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2-L-Gv0wr0&t=270s)
- He says the iPhone did for phones what Dyson did for vacuum cleaners. Smartphones existed before it but were a business tool, and he says even Android was first built for keyboard phones and became a touchscreen system only after the iPhone. (What if Smartphones Never Existed at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJ8OnNQYAVo&t=225s)

## Is the smartphone finished, and what do people want instead?

- In October 2023 he said smartphones have matured to the point where something else is bound to come. He said sales peaked in 2018 and anyone who could afford a phone had one, so makers can only charge more: $850 for the top iPhone in 2016 against $1600 for the top iPhone 15 Pro Max. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=45s)
- He said in 2023 that phone launches have lost their excitement. The days of a phone twice as fast with an all new design are gone, and his example of what is left is the Pixel 8 getting a thermometer on its back. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=90s)
- He says technology moves in an S curve and not a straight line. In May 2022 he said the smartphone age was nearing the end of its curve, and in September 2025 he said smartphones had been in the same flat phase for some years. (The Boring Tech To Make AR Glasses Cool at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkCfdHdHnKg&t=360s; The Weird Death Of User Interfaces at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG4ONHqF1qg&t=135s)
- In May 2024 he said the anti-smartphone movement was only getting started and would probably change technology more than AI, because it rests on one idea: using a smartphone becoming uncool, like smoking. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=0s)
- He says the movement blames the wrong thing. People are addicted to media and the phone only serves it best right now; nobody hates the phone when it guides them with Google Maps, takes photos or calls a ride home. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=90s)
- He says the smartphone is the victim and not the cause: the cause is a product-building loop optimised for time, but after 15 years of it the phone was the only target people could declare war on. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=315s; What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=495s)
- He sees **a market for less**. While makers raced for more megapixels, cameras and AI, people started to crave less, and he cites dumb phone sales, a premium minimalist e-ink phone maker growing every year, app blockers, timed phone cages, and screen time tools built into iOS and Android. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=360s)
- He argues coolness decides adoption. What culture calls cool grows and what it calls uncool dies, and his case is US smoking falling from 42% in 1965 to 14% in 2019. He qualifies the analogy, saying phones and cigarettes are different kinds of addiction and cannot be compared directly. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=450s; What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=495s)
- In May 2024 he said we might start to see phone-free zones, with bars, restaurants and clubs banning phones because that would make them cooler, as Berghain in Berlin has done for years, and that a few years from now the flex could be living on an old Nokia flip phone. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=585s)
- In May 2024 he bet that within two or three years Samsung, Google or Apple would build phones with physical switches and locks, or devices that take over single jobs like music, so probably a return to iPods. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=630s)
- He says the iPhone started as a gadget but is now a computer, and he counts himself among those who believe in a right to compute: you should be able to use it however you like, as with a laptop. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=270s)

## What does he think replaces the phone?

- He places interfaces on a line from machine-like to human-like: punch cards and assembly, the graphical interface, the touchscreen, voice assistants, then large language models. He says every jump along it creates the next generation of technology, with trillions of dollars of value. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=135s)
- In 2023 he argued AI hardware devices are appearing because we have incredibly powerful language models that are held back by the devices we use them from, and said this push for hardware will be the key to replacing the smartphone. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=495s)
- In October 2023 he predicted that glasses running multimodal AI will be the backbone of the smartphone's successor. He said Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses are not the next **iPhone moment** but come very close. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=585s)
- He says an AI device needs three things for the next iPhone moment: a natural input, as multi-touch was; context, meaning constant awareness of what you see and hear; and output, where audio is easy and visuals are hard. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=585s)
- He calls glasses the best device for input because they can take your voice and use cameras to track your surroundings and your hands. He calls context their killer feature, since pairing it with multimodal AI gives real-time guidance on anything. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=630s)
- In 2023 he reversed an earlier view. He said he was wrong in several videos to say true AR would define the smartphone's successor, and now treats AR only as the visual output for multimodal AI. "Augmented reality is not the end, it's the means to an end." (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=720s)
- His 2023 summary lists four pieces of the successor: a multimodal language model as the new way of interacting, access to the apps and services you use, glasses as the hardware, and basic augmented reality for output. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=765s)
- He finds it ironic that Google Glass, a 2012 product that flopped, is probably the closest thing so far to the future of computing he expects. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=765s)
- He says voice assistants were the closest thing to a natural computer interaction and failed in practice. People use them only for lights and timers because voice is the wrong interface for delivering complex information. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=225s; We Need To Talk About The AI Pin at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2-L-Gv0wr0&t=360s)
- In November 2023 he judged Humane's AI Pin a great idea with poor execution. He loves the idea of a wearable that is constantly aware of your surroundings, but says the pin cannot deliver it because Humane does not own the model, the camera drains too much battery to keep recording, and constant uploads would be very expensive. (We Need To Talk About The AI Pin at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2-L-Gv0wr0&t=180s; We Need To Talk About The AI Pin at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2-L-Gv0wr0&t=225s)
- He calls the pin's projector its stupidest part: holding out your hand, waiting for the projection, twisting your hand to select and then making the same gesture the new Apple Watch uses is harder than double tapping on your ear to skip a song. (We Need To Talk About The AI Pin at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2-L-Gv0wr0&t=270s)
- He says the pin should have been glasses, since the Meta glasses launched at the same time do the same thing without the projector and arguably better. (We Need To Talk About The AI Pin at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2-L-Gv0wr0&t=315s)
- He says presenting the pin as a smartphone replacement was the mistake. The easier and better route was an accessory like an Apple Watch that lets the phone do the processing and saves battery. (We Need To Talk About The AI Pin at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2-L-Gv0wr0&t=405s)
- In January 2024 he called the Rabbit R1 completely useless as a device, since it could have been a phone app, and read it as a publicity vehicle for Rabbit's action model. He took the $199 price with no subscription as evidence the device is not the business. (Is This Useless at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWEjDIsMFB0&t=360s)
- By March 2026 he used core input to judge both ends: the AI Pin's projector and clunky gestures looked broken even in the demo, while everyone who tried Meta's neural band for its display glasses, himself included, found the input natural, so he can see it succeeding despite being first generation and niche. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=765s)
- In June 2026 he said every attempt to kill the screen interface has been a gigantic failure, listing the Humane pin, which he says raised $230 million, the Rabbit, and the original vision for Siri, Alexa and Google Home. He says his own Google Home serves as a light switch and a pasta timer. (The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=45s)
- In December 2025 he said building a breakthrough device is much harder than building an AI model. His proof is Meta, which tried a phone, the Quest and now Ray-Ban glasses he calls genuinely good, and still does not own distribution. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=855s)
- In May 2024 he called the anti-phone mood a perfect storm for AR glasses, because people want to capture moments and navigate without doom scrolling, and an overlay on the real world limits what can be shown. "You're not going to see a TikTok feed projected over your fridge." (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=765s)
- In December 2023 he said it took 15 years to build the natural interactions we have today and that we now have to start again from scratch for AR. He predicted that natural hand-based control would make or break the smartphone's successor. (The Lies That Make Your Tech Actually Work at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rAVV4kAYlI8&t=585s)

## What is the Vision Pro for, and what does true AR still need?

- In June 2023 he argued Apple made the Vision Pro's price astronomical on purpose. He says Apple maxed out every stat like a pay to win video game, with 12 cameras, six microphones, eye tracking in place of controllers and a body cut from a single block of aluminum, against the rule that products trade off to stay affordable. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=45s)
- He says the Vision Pro is not another iPhone moment but another Apple Lisa, the first commercial machine with a graphical interface, which cost $10,000 in 1983 and failed while its ideas mattered. He says Apple did it again, this time on purpose. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=135s)
- He says Apple does not really care how many it sells: the one million headsets Apple expects to sell would be under 1% of its revenue. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=180s)
- He says Apple shared its vision by shipping a real and very expensive product where other companies publish statements or, like Meta, long presentations of renders. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=225s)
- He separates passthrough AR from **true AR**. The Vision Pro films the room and replays it on screens with content on top, while the goal is transparent glasses that project 3D content mapped to your surroundings so you can live normally and share what you see. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=450s)
- He calls the Vision Pro the iPad of the AR world, a device in the middle. In 2023 he predicted Apple would take the leap to true AR only in a few years and that this would be the new iPhone moment. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=495s)
- He lists three ingredients for true AR: hardware that is not intrusive on the face, a virtual positioning system, and a control system. He says the control system is the only one Apple has already achieved, with eye tracking and pinching, though in 2023 he judged that from other people's reactions and had not tried the headset himself. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=540s; Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=675s)
- He says Google Glass failed mainly because the hardware was too intrusive. The face is our most important physical characteristic, so a weird gadget on it is not an option for most people even if it is useful. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=540s)
- He says the main missing technology for true AR is good enough transparent displays. In 2023 he predicted the first true AR glasses would most likely connect to a smartphone that does all the computation. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=585s)
- He says privacy is an unsolved design problem for AR glasses: Google Glass was seen as creepy because of the camera on the face even when it was not recording, and cameras will most likely still be needed. (Apple Built The Vision Pro To Fail And It's Genius at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNEni_DP4fY&t=585s)
- He says a VR headset that looks cringe has already lost, however many billions go into resolution and tracking, and that even as a tech enthusiast he finds the Vision Pro look pretty cringe. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=720s)
- In February 2024 he said the Vision Pro is not the device that changes the world and that Apple knows it. He called it a niche, bulky, heavy passthrough headset and said the first iPhone magic can happen again with the next AR device built to be mainstream. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=630s)
- He says the Vision Pro is probably even more locked down than the iPhone: you need an iPhone to scan your face and it connects only to a Mac to use your screen in VR. He adds that YouTube, Spotify and Netflix did not bother to make apps for it, as revenge for the Apple tax. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=450s; The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=585s)
- In 2024 he argued Apple's best move is an AR device that works without an iPhone and supports web AR standards, and warned that developers who lost trust in Apple will think twice before committing to its next iPhone-like product. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 15:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=945s; The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=675s)
- In February 2026 he said Apple spent an estimated 20 billion dollars, 5,000 patents, custom chips and 14 cameras on a Vision Pro that fewer than 500,000 people bought while Siri stayed bad. He calls that the cost of being a tech company and not incompetence. (The Weird Reason Your Tech Stays Broken at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-51ocAD6zBI&t=225s; The Weird Reason Your Tech Stays Broken at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-51ocAD6zBI&t=450s)
- In 2022 he said AR will be a much bigger evolution than any virtual reality metaverse, because most people need to simplify dealings with the real world. He grants VR good uses such as gaming. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=405s)
- In May 2022 he said 2022 is for augmented reality what 2005 was for smartphones, and predicted AR would completely change how we deal with our digital lives over the next decade. (The Boring Tech To Make AR Glasses Cool at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkCfdHdHnKg&t=315s)

## Why are maps and positioning the base layer of AR?

- In April 2022 he said Google Maps is one of the most underrated tech products and has a good chance of becoming the most important tech product of the next decade. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=0s)
- He says we now live on two planes, physical and digital, and the smartphone is only a window into the digital one, limited to the size of its screen. He sets AR, which is about overlapping the two, against the metaverse idea of leaving the physical plane. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=315s)
- He says an AR operating system starts from the outside world, where a VR system starts from a black environment. It must match GPS, cameras, lidar and gyroscopes against a 3D map of the world, so mapping stops being an app and becomes the layer everything is built on. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=585s)
- He says the real value of Google Maps is the map underneath, which he calls the deep map. The app could be rewritten in a few weeks, but the combined map is one of the most valuable assets in the world. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=90s)
- He says Street View turned out to be a data source: Google pulls text, signs and lane markings out of its images, and it covers over 124 countries against 11 for Apple's equivalent. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=90s)
- He says the AR software battle will be about giving context to the world. The prize is a one-to-one shared 3D map of the planet used as a canvas for all AR apps, worth billions if not trillions of dollars. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=765s)
- In 2022 he said Google is best placed to provide that map because of the amount and variety of its data plus Android's 70 percent share of mobile devices. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=720s)
- He says shared AR needs two things: one huge 3D map of the world, and a way to pinpoint each device's exact location and orientation inside it. Phone GPS and gyroscopes are not precise enough. (The Boring Tech To Make AR Glasses Cool at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkCfdHdHnKg&t=90s)
- He explains Google's VPS in four steps: Street View images go into a neural network that extracts trillions of points; a second model merges them into one 3D point cloud of the world; the device sends its GPS position and camera view; the same process matches the scene to the map to locate the device precisely. (The Boring Tech To Make AR Glasses Cool at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkCfdHdHnKg&t=90s)
- He says Google's edge over Niantic is coverage. Niantic leads in areas such as occlusion but covers 20,000 locations, while Google covers wherever Street View exists, which he calls orders of magnitude bigger. (The Boring Tech To Make AR Glasses Cool at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkCfdHdHnKg&t=180s)
- He accepts that VPS looks like a glorified GPS and argues that is the point. It is boring infrastructure, like the hidden software stack under a one-line hello world program, that lets developers build AR apps without handling geospatial complexity. (The Boring Tech To Make AR Glasses Cool at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkCfdHdHnKg&t=225s)
- He points out that an algorithm decides where 1 billion people physically go each month while attention stays fixed on social media. He adds that a map failure has physical consequences that most digital failures do not. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=180s; The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=135s)

## Why did physical controls disappear, and why are they coming back?

- He says buttons were once the luxury of effortless control, and for a time the most buttons meant the most advanced, with the Concorde cockpit as his example. Today cool means sleek and minimalist. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=45s; What Happened To Physical Tech at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=90s)
- He says digital interfaces took over because they broke the idea that complex technology needs complex controls. A phone has a thousand times the complexity of older devices and only three buttons, because the complexity moved from atoms to pixels. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=135s)
- He says physical controls lost because they cannot change once shipped and cost more. Spotify can add lyrics for everyone with an update, but Nintendo could not ship a new button to the Game Boy. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=270s)
- He calls cars the most frustrating example, with Tesla the most extreme because you need the touchscreen even to open the glove box. He does not blame carmakers, since a screen is easier and above all cheaper than an array of buttons and knobs. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=315s)
- He uses the Touch Bar to show people reject digital replacing physical even when it can do more. Apple launched it in 2016, people hated it, and the MacBook Pros from 2021 with physical keys are regarded as among Apple's best products. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=360s)
- In September 2024 he said tech pushed too hard on making everything digital and went too far, because using buttons and knobs is human nature. He noted the previous iPhone's main new feature was a button. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=405s; What Happened To Physical Tech at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=0s)
- His signs of a rebellion back to the physical: handheld consoles for old games selling more, the Playdate with its crank selling out, Tesla owners buying aftermarket buttons, and the iPhone 15 and Apple Watch Ultra leading with an action button. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=405s)
- He says nobody wants to go back. The rebound is about bringing the physical into digital tech, through things like haptic displays and a configurable knob called SmartKnob, which he admits have not reached the mainstream. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=450s)
- He says a blend of physical and digital ages far better than a touchscreen. The clearest sign a ten-year-old car is old is its touchscreen, and he says Bugatti dropped the touchscreen on a hypercar for hybrid knobs so it stays timeless. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=540s)
- He is unsure whether a generation raised only on screens will want physical tech at all, since the return comes from people who grew up with it as he did with a Game Boy. (What Happened To Physical Tech at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIFF-rinlbo&t=585s)
- He says the feeling that a tech product is solid and good to hold is not random: there are four building blocks that make physical tech products work, and the first is the curvature of the corners. (The Lies That Make Hardware Actually Work at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLIgrGAIRsE&t=90s)
- He uses Apple's butterfly keyboard to show that hardware mistakes cannot be patched. He says Apple halved key travel in 2015 for thinness, dust could kill a key, and Apple paid $50 million in a settlement before rolling it back in 2019. (The Lies That Make Hardware Actually Work at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLIgrGAIRsE&t=405s)
- He says the most magical experiences fuse hardware and software and companies do not do it enough. His examples are Pixel volume controls that animate out of the side buttons and the Dynamic Island, which turned a camera cutout into a feature. (The Lies That Make Hardware Actually Work at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLIgrGAIRsE&t=630s; The Lies That Make Hardware Actually Work at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLIgrGAIRsE&t=720s)

## Why does he care about single-purpose devices?

- He says single-purpose hardware collapsed into phones and computers. People once owned a camera, a Walkman, a voice recorder, a radio and a typewriter, and piece by piece these became apps in a grid. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=0s)
- He sorts devices into the **creation device**, which makes things, and the consumption device, which shows things made by others. He says the two fight for your time and consumption usually wins. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=45s)
- He says putting creation and consumption on one device makes distraction easy, since you sit down to work and Instagram or Netflix is right there. The OP-1 has no Wi-Fi, no notifications and no Bluetooth. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=180s)
- He says most people never use the creative tools they carry because creation takes intention. He applies the 90-9-1 rule of online participation, where 1% create, to how people use their devices. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=225s)
- He loves the OP-1 because it is constrained. He says it is not the most useful thing he owns and software can do everything it does, and that this is the point of a pure creation device. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=135s)
- He says the OP-1 has the best software design of any tech product he has used: he learned it from a 30 minute tutorial, where professional music software takes weeks. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=360s)
- He does not tell people to buy one. He says pure creation devices are kind of unnecessary, the OP-1 costs more than a thousand dollars, and anyone with a phone can do the same from a bedroom. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=450s)
- He argues a device's limits can be an asset, citing the Polaroid's fixed lens and unpredictable colour tint, which made it great when better cameras existed. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=495s)
- In July 2023 he predicted that the more powerful phones and computers get, the more people will want separate creation devices that do not pull them into scrolling. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=540s)

## What happened to the phone camera?

- He says the biggest problem for phone cameras is physics. A bigger sensor needs more distance from the lens, a phone cannot give it, so the sensor shrinks and takes in less light. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=135s)
- He says phones cheat physics with software and walks the pipeline: a colour mosaic from the sensor, interpolation to guess missing pixels, a gain boost that adds noise, denoising that softens, then sharpening. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=180s)
- He says two thirds of a phone image is extrapolated by a computer and not captured by the sensor. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=180s)
- He says the photos you already take are a lie made of hundreds of decisions by the phone maker. Different companies can use the same physical sensor, and the processing is what separates them. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=225s)
- He says that once camera bumps allowed bigger sensors, computational photography should have eased off and instead went further, with night modes, merged exposures and fake background blur. Processing now aims to make photos look good, not real. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=270s; How Smartphones Killed Photography at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=315s)
- In September 2024 he said the Pixel 9's Magic Editor is where he realised things were about to change, since it can generate framing you never captured, move you, enlarge a mountain or switch the scene to golden hour. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=360s)
- He says this differs from Photoshop or an image generator because it starts from your own photo and is presented as normal editing inside the camera app. Faking images is over 100 years old; what is new is how easy it is for anyone. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=405s)
- He says knowing an element is fake makes a picture less valuable, because photography rests on the skill of capturing reality. He also grants that the Pixel camera team might have a point that the goal is to capture the memory, and says he would be happy to keep his own assembled family photo. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=450s; How Smartphones Killed Photography at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=495s)
- He says the big problem comes when phones apply these edits automatically. He suspects most people would not mind and would simply think the camera beats the competition. "We're at a point where soon this won't be a camera anymore." (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=540s)
- He proposes two cameras: one that records reality in the best quality and keeps it real, and a **memories camera** with all the AI tools to capture the feeling of a moment. His justification is that most people are not good at taking pictures. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=585s)

## What do Japan and his what-if timelines show about predicting devices?

- He says Japan lives in a **retro future**. Robots, neon streets and a hardware gadget for every daily task were the 1980s picture of the future, and Japan is clinging to it while the rest of the world got software services. (Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=0s)
- He says Japan's post-war rise came from manufacturing physical high-tech, starting with licensed transistor technology and Sony's portable radios. Cutting-edge tech then meant physical devices, so robots looked like the obvious future. (Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=45s; Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=90s)
- He says the biggest technology reason Japan stopped growing is that it completely missed software and the internet. He grants there are many economic, social and demographic reasons as well. "Japan missed out on the software train." (Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=315s; Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=450s)
- From his two weeks there he says physical technology in Japan works flawlessly and software feels like the 90s. He could almost never pay by card, and every website he used for trains and hotels was badly made. (Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=360s)
- He says Japan treated software as an afterthought: hardware engineers were and are respected, while software development was not seen as important the way it was in the US. (Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=405s)
- He says people predict the future by exaggerating a technology they already have, as in going from cars to flying cars, and so they miss radical shifts. (Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=495s)
- In March 2023 he warned that the rush to call AI the core of the future could be the same mistake, and that in 30 years we might find we ignored a new wave that was just starting. (Why Japan Is Living In A Weird Retro Future at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zgV8bJy5s34&t=540s)
- He says Engelbart's 1968 demo showed the mouse, video calls, shared documents and hyperlinks, and that almost nothing from it reached a commercial release for the next 15 years. He concludes it is almost impossible to predict which vision of the future will happen. (The Man Who Made Apple Possible In 1968 at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXSHJXHeaqM&t=90s; The Man Who Made Apple Possible In 1968 at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXSHJXHeaqM&t=180s; The Man Who Made Apple Possible In 1968 at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXSHJXHeaqM&t=270s)
- He uses three alternative timelines to test the idea that life would be better without today's tech: no Facebook, no iPhone, and no open internet. His finding is that it is more complicated than people blissfully looking at nature. (What if Smartphones Never Existed at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJ8OnNQYAVo&t=0s)
- In his no-iPhone timeline the missing App Store matters most. With clunky devices and many platforms, the idea of a cool app for any device never forms, so there is no mobile gaming boom, no Shazam and no music streaming. (What if Smartphones Never Existed at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJ8OnNQYAVo&t=270s)
- He says Spotify became the Spotify we know only when it launched on mobile. In the no-iPhone timeline it stays niche, piracy remains the main way to get music, and maps stay mostly on the computer. (What if Smartphones Never Existed at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJ8OnNQYAVo&t=270s)
- He imagines that without power-hungry apps phones would be weaker but far more varied: Nokia stays number one, the industry never settles on one form factor, and new designs appear every year. (What if Smartphones Never Existed at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJ8OnNQYAVo&t=315s)
- He ranks the losses: life without the smartphone would be quite chill next to life without the open internet. Without the open internet he imagines the home computer never going mainstream and consumer electronics thriving, with Japan as king of consumer tech and Sony the most valuable company. (What if Smartphones Never Existed at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJ8OnNQYAVo&t=360s; What if Smartphones Never Existed at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJ8OnNQYAVo&t=495s)

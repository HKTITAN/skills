# Brand voice, agencies and testing a new brand, Chandan Mendiratta

Chandan Mendiratta of Zepto ran the closing stretch of the second day of the marketing bootcamp. This recording covers the end of his film examples, a section on people and agencies, and his answers to questions on research and on how a new startup should begin its marketing. It ends when teams start presenting their assignment, which is left out of this note.

## What was said

### Small signals in brand films

- Mendiratta says Zepto's films always show the delivery rider in a heroic and participative way, with the aim of making the rider more endearing over time.
- He calls these subliminal signals that compound over many years, and says the team will never know their exact impact.
- He asks the class to watch how much the rider takes part in the story once he enters the film.

### Brand voice

- He says a company's brand voice and culture are its soul, and that consistency of brand voice is very important.
- He says a brand voice can grow but cannot flip. A 180 degree change means you are starting a new brand, because there is nothing left to capitalise on.
- Because of this he argues for keeping brand work inside the company as much as possible, while accepting that not every company has that luxury.

### Team culture

- He says an internal team lets you build a safe space with psychological safety, where any idea can be said and nobody is judged for it.
- His rule for brainstorming is not to stop ideas while they are coming, and to laugh with people rather than at them.
- He says things can be said inside the room that cannot be said outside, and that some ideas take you to good ideas.
- He warns founders against a "founder god" complex and says the team should see the founder as a vulnerable human.
- His line is "People don't love gods. People love people."
- He says his own skill is not writing scripts. He cannot get to an idea without talking, so he surrounds himself with people who throw ideas around.
- He sums up the section as good people plus a safe space equals magic.

### The four kinds of agency

- He lists four kinds of agency a founder should know: digital, media, creative and research.
- Digital agencies run performance campaigns once you give them a goal, and he describes them as agile.
- Media agencies buy media across channels including television, and he notes an overlap between media and digital agencies.
- Creative agencies turn your brief into a script, get it approved by you, and take it to a production house that makes the film.
- The finished film then goes to the media and digital agencies for distribution.
- Research agencies do the studies that sharpen the brief before it goes to the creative agency.
- He names McCann among the big creative agencies, GroupM for media, and Kantar and Nielsen for research.

### Why agency interests differ from yours

- He says a creative agency's future business depends on the awards it wins, not on how much your business grew.
- He says this means a creative agency will sell you an idea that can win awards, and that its interest is not aligned with yours.
- He treats this as a strong reason to hire creative people in-house.

### Build or outsource

- His test for building a team is the frequency of the work. If the work is frequent, build. If it is rare, outsource.
- His example is that a full-time creative writer makes no sense if you make two films a year.
- For occasional work he says the outside partner should be someone you can trust and depend on, who will not take you for a ride.
- Regular work such as LinkedIn posts or topical posts is the kind he says can be built in-house.

### Research and data

- He says a product sold online gives you your own data, and Amazon gives some data too.
- For a physical product sold offline, he says a research agency goes to the offline market, collects the data and digitises it.
- He says the marketing head used to lead new product development, and now the data tells you what to do.
- He says his team still uses focus groups, because they still need to talk to consumers about what drives them and what holds them back.
- When judging a researcher, he says to check their experience, whether they come from a firm such as Kantar or Nielsen, and which brands they have worked with.

### Who to hire first

- He says a B2C brand needs one person who runs marketing: a brand manager who runs campaigns, measures results and works with all the agencies.
- For a new startup this can be a junior person at first. As the complexity and the team grow, a senior person is needed.

### Starting from zero

- Asked what he would do if he were starting out today, he says he would first finalise the colours, agree a tone of voice and agree a mix of brand archetypes.
- He would then make a hire and be active on LinkedIn and Instagram.
- He says founders have to learn digital marketing themselves at some point, and may as well start on day one.
- For a D2C product he suggests running ads on Google and Meta in one small geography to get the first few customers.
- His example for an MVP is to run an ad in one small locality, sell to real customers, then phone them for feedback.
- He says to sell for two to three months and watch for repeat orders. No repeat orders means there is a problem, allowing for how often the product is used.
- He says to look at retention curves and customer lifetime value.
- He says selling to friends is not enough and you have to build a funnel, online or offline. It will be rudimentary at first and will scale over time.
- He says you also have to test whether the product is needed at all. An offline idea is tested in an offline setup. An online idea is tested by running ads and reading the metrics.
- He says friends and family will always be kind, and that you want ruthless customers who will tell you they do not need your product.

## Examples and cases used

- Zepto brand films: the delivery rider is always shown as the hero and as part of the story, as a long-term signal.
- McCann, GroupM, Kantar and Nielsen: named as examples of creative, media and research agencies.
- Amazon: named as a source of some sales data for products sold online.

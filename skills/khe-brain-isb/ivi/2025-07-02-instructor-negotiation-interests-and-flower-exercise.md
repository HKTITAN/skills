# Negotiation, interests behind positions and a flower exercise

This is the second half of the second class in the negotiation course, taught by a faculty member who is not named in the recording. Students played a two party role play in which both sides wanted the whole of a limited supply of a flower, and she debriefed it to teach interests, perspective taking and the move to multi issue negotiation. Little of her speech was captured, so this note is short. The file also carries a classmate's written notes, used below only where marked.

## What was said

### Differences are useful

- The instructor says differences between the parties are not bad, and that a negotiator has to capitalise on them.
- She describes an experiment in which the same task was labelled a competitive game for one group and a cooperative game for another. The group told it was competitive found it more competitive and behaved more competitively.

### Perspective taking

- She separates empathy from perspective taking. Empathy is putting yourself in the other person's shoes and feeling it, while perspective taking is understanding things from their point of view.
- She says perspective taking is important in negotiation.

### The flower exercise

- She says the problem in the exercise was that both parties kept saying they wanted all 100 flowers.
- She asks how many pairs worked out that the two parties wanted different parts of the flower.
- She says some issues in a negotiation are like this, where the two parties' interests are compatible.
- A founder in the room observed that being open about what each side would use the flower for would have worked better than keeping it secret.

### Camp David

- She uses the Camp David agreement as her example of interests. In her telling, ownership of the land stayed with Egypt while Israel's underlying interest was met in another way.
- She says that if the two sides had only said "I want it" without asking why, the result would have been war.

### Moving to multi issue negotiation

- She says all business negotiations are multi issue.
- She says reciprocity is very important.
- She says that when you add issues to your planning sheet, you think about what other interests the other party might have.
- She says a business negotiation is a long term relationship, because you are going to work with the other side afterwards.

### From the written class notes in the file

- The notes list a negotiator's toolbox: know your BATNA, strengthen it whenever possible, highlight its strength without giving specifics, define your reservation price without disclosing it, and focus on your target price while negotiating.
- The notes record that the way to reach the other party's interests is to ask and then listen to what they say they want.
- The notes record that debating the other party's product, quality or cause never works in a negotiation.
- The notes record that numbers are easy to quantify and subjective things are not.

## Examples and cases used

- The Camp David agreement between Egypt and Israel: a dispute settled by asking why each side wanted the land.
- A two party flower role play: both sides demand the full supply when each needs a different part.

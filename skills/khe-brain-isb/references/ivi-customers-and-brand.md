## Design Thinking part 1, personas and customer interviews, 14 May 2025

## Pitch deck bootcamp on investor gaps and framing, Mohit Bansal, 16 May 2025

- Mohit Bansal says if a founder does not frame the business, the listener puts it in a default category and attaches assumptions about market size, scale and margins that are hard to shift later. (ivi: Pitch deck bootcamp on investor gaps and framing, Mohit Bansal, 16 May 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-05-16-bansal-pitch-deck-bootcamp.md)
- Mohit Bansal's framing canvas has the target group, the broad need area and the specific need, the broad solution category and the specific solution, and the context that makes the framing believable. (ivi: Pitch deck bootcamp on investor gaps and framing, Mohit Bansal, 16 May 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-05-16-bansal-pitch-deck-bootcamp.md)

## Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba

- Sahiba says her aim at Zomato was that people think Zomato, eat Zomato and talk about Zomato, so that the brand name becomes the generic word the way Xerox did. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba says every brand has a saturation point, and that Zomato's push notifications went from being called cute to people saying they were sick of them. Her fix is to take a break. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba says saturation does not mean a brand switches off. Once it has achieved what it wanted with its first target group, it moves to the second. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba says a mass brand must accept that people speak different languages and have different purchasing power, and that her team's Hindi-first voice was reaching only a part of the market before it moved into regional languages. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba says relying 100% on AI will never work for a brand. Her rule is a human core for storytelling with AI used for execution. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba says AI-made ads are detached from human emotion and come in such quantity that they do not resonate, so brand retention is going to be a huge problem. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba says humanising works for mass brands and never for luxury brands, and that a brand must never come across as superior to its consumer. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba says a brand should be everywhere its consumer is, which takes deep research. Her example is Zomato placing late-night ads where the 11pm to 4am audience actually was. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba's first advice to people building a brand is not to follow another company's blueprint, because even inspiration gets seated in your head and you end up replicating it. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba uses the term cognitive dishonesty for a public face promoting something that contradicts what they say they stand for, and says she turns down well paid brand work that does not resonate with her. (ivi: Brand marketing, AI and storytelling, Sahiba, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-sahiba-brand-marketing-ai-and-storytelling.md)
- Sahiba's low-cost research methods are Google Trends, Statista with a check on the year of the data, and talking yourself to at least 50 people from the target group.

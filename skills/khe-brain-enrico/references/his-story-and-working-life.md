# His story and working life

This file holds what he says about himself: where he is from, the jobs he had, why he left them, what he builds, how the channel grew and how he works. Most of it comes from i-quit-my-6-figures-dream-job-in-tech, how-im-saving-my-dying-youtube-channel, what-actually-happens-when-you-blow-up-on-youtube, the-issue-with-being-a-multipotentialite and how-i-finally-fixed-my-digital-life, with single facts scattered over many other videos. Every fact is true as of the video it was said in, since his job, home, tools and products change over the five years, and a number of the small facts about his tools and travels were said during sponsor reads, so treat those as plain facts and not as views.

## Why did he quit his job?

- In October 2024 he said he had just quit his product manager job and that the reasons start with "hard work is overrated". (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=0s)
- His first reason is that choosing the right thing to work on beats effort: "The thing you're working on is more important than how hard you work." (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=90s)
- He tested his path by looking at the person one step ahead of him: he respected his manager a lot but did not want her day job, or to be a director of product at a startup, in three to five years. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=90s)
- He compares options by how much each widens his circle of opportunity, which he calls **Flash** after the Pokemon move that lights up a cave. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=225s)
- By that measure the job offered another product manager job or a promotion, while the channel brought a job offer every two weeks, startups asking him to angel invest and creators wanting to collaborate, a circle he calls about 20 times bigger. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=225s)
- He ran the channel beside his job for three years before leaving, because until 2023 it made almost no money and quitting earlier would have meant a very long **ramen period**. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=630s)
- He left when channel income was close to his salary but had not yet passed it, and says so plainly. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=675s)
- He says the salary let him refuse many companies that wanted him to promote products he did not believe in. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=630s)
- His plan in October 2024 had two steps: turn the channel from a side project into something more structured with people hired to help, then build his own tech product, starting with software. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=810s)
- At that point he said he was rusty at coding, meant to get back into it and take design courses, and did not yet know what the product would be. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=810s)

## What does he believe about careers, luck and opportunity?

- He says hard work is overrated: a girl selling snacks at 6 a.m. on top of a Bali volcano, who then works in the fields, works harder than anyone he has met in tech without the same results. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=45s)
- He qualifies this in the same video: to get more opportunities you first of all need to work hard and be prepared. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=225s)
- He says success comes down to access to the right opportunities, not only talent and effort, and points to Bill Gates having a computer at school in the 1970s and Steve Jobs growing up in Silicon Valley. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=135s)
- He says safe jobs do not exist, because projects get cancelled, divisions shut and companies lay people off. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=360s)
- He concluded that even very successful chief executives are only trying their best: "Nobody knows what they're doing. Everyone is winging it." (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=405s)
- He says each period has a cheat code, an **unoptimized resource**: buying a house for his grandparents' generation, a safe middle-class job for his parents', and both are now gone. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=450s)
- In October 2024 he named the internet and YouTube as today's unoptimized resources, and said he does not expect YouTube to stay that way forever. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=540s)
- He pictures a stable job as a straight line and an entrepreneurial project as an exponential curve, with a gap where the job is ahead, and says to shrink that gap by building the project beside the job. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=585s)
- He names four levers that make anything exponential: capital, labor, media and code. He lacks the first two at scale, so he pushes media and code, because a video is made once and watched hundreds of thousands of times and code is written once and scales without limit. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=720s)
- He already described the same contrast in February 2023: a nine-to-five grows a little each year, while he worked almost two years on YouTube with close to nothing to show before it took off. "YouTube is an exponential game." (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=360s)
- He says a big-tech job gives a little credibility at first but means nothing on YouTube: before the growth his outreach to creators and brands got nothing, and after it he got podcasts, collaborations, consulting and startup investing offers. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=450s)
- He says self-help books build a good mindset of hard work and persistence but all leave out luck, since the most successful people were also in the right place at the right time. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=270s)
- His line on luck is "you cannot control luck", only how many opportunities you expose yourself to, and he cites Billie Eilish saying part of her success came from being born in Los Angeles. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=315s)
- He says he owes much of what he is to internet access and to things he learned online, and in the quitting video he pointed viewers to the charity Give Internet instead of a sponsor. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=315s; Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=270s)

## What does he say about having many interests and comparing himself to others?

- He calls himself a **multipotentialite**, a term he defines as the ability to excel in two or more fields, and says finding the word a couple of years before 2021 was the first time he did not feel like the weird one. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=0s)
- He gives such a person three traits: curiosity across many fields, the ability to get good results in unrelated fields, and being creative, decently smart and a fast learner. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=0s)
- He says comparison is 10 or 100 times worse for people like him, because they breeze through the first 10 to 20 hours of a field, decide they can succeed at it, and then measure themselves against its specialists. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=90s)
- He says people with many interests merge the top 0.01 percent of every field they like into one imaginary person, whom he names Tom and calls the **hyper human prototype**, and that this person cannot exist. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=180s)
- He grants that a few people such as Leonardo da Vinci and Tom Ford did succeed in several fields, but says it is insane to feel behind in life for not being them. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=225s)
- He offers three things that helped him, the first two being recognising the role of luck and a journaling exercise about your ideal future life. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=270s)
- The exercise is to write out a day or a week of your ideal life three, five or ten years ahead and repeat it every once in a while; he says it shows that a lot of the things you consider important now will not make a big difference to that life. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=360s)
- He says having many interests is a great advantage in life, because you learn different skills quickly, and tells people to use how their brain is wired. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=405s)
- In 2021 he said he had struggled with this comparison for a long time and was only slowly getting over it. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=450s)
- In its first year his channel tried travel, tech reviews, learning, studying, having many interests, personal development and innovation, and he concluded you cannot do everything on one channel. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=405s)
- He plays some guitar and some drums, makes music only for fun and says he is kind of bad at it, and would love one day to produce all the music in his videos himself. (The Best Tech Product Ever Designed at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1cMrkMqmnw&t=270s)

## How did his channel start, grow and change?

- He started making videos in September 2020, in Italian, with zero followers, and friends got his first video to about 50 views. (I'm Switching to English and Starting From Scratch at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6BnCTqv0zA&t=0s; How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=0s)
- Early on he had about 100 subscribers and 200 to 300 views a video while spending 10 to 20 hours on each. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=45s)
- In August 2021, with 670 subscribers, he announced the switch to English because he consumes all his content in English, works in English every day and wants to reach people all over the world. (I'm Switching to English and Starting From Scratch at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6BnCTqv0zA&t=0s; I'm Switching to English and Starting From Scratch at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6BnCTqv0zA&t=90s)
- In September 2021 he said the stalled channel was his own doing: "that's not the algorithm's fault that's my fault". (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=225s)
- His list starts with two points: define your expectations, and kill "meh" videos he is not proud of. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=270s)
- He narrowed the channel to two topics in 2021, personal development and innovation and technology, after tagging all his past videos by count and by how much he enjoyed making them. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=450s)
- He set up **the Great Filter**, questions at the top of his Notion video template that a video must pass: is it about the future of yourself or of the world, what did he improve by making it, would he change something with more time, and would he click it in his own feed. (How I'm Saving My Dying YouTube Channel at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaGAVBBCv6s&t=540s)
- It took almost two years to reach 4,000 subscribers, then the channel went from 4,000 to 40,000 with more than 2 million views in under 20 days. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=0s)
- He says the channel made 469.57 in all of 2022, currency not given, and 6,650 in AdSense alone in the first weeks of 2023. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=180s)
- He calls the blow-up some of the most stressful weeks of his life: at least two or three fake sponsorship emails a day tried to hack the channel, and he moved it to a new private email address after more than 14 years on the same Gmail. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=0s; What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=90s)
- Within weeks the side project became a business, so he froze YouTube payments, kept the money in AdSense, realised he needed a company, and spent an afternoon and 700 asking the UK government how that works on an employee visa. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=225s; What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=270s)
- He says he went from 1,000 to 100,000 subscribers in one year. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=540s)
- In July 2025 he said his most viewed video sat at 3,776 views for 55 days before reaching 3.5 million. (How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=0s)
- His description of the channel shifts over time: innovation and personal development in 2021, and the behind the scenes of the design, engineering and psychology of everyday tech by 2025. (The Issue With Being A Multipotentialite at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQVWM2P3o24&t=450s; How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=45s)
- In 2025 he flew to Lovable's HQ in Stockholm for a behind-the-scenes video, and flew to San Francisco to visit Notion's office and sit with its designers and engineers. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=45s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notion at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b00sgRR_Vc0&t=45s)
- In 2026 he flew to San Francisco and sat with the people who built Figma, who opened their design files and showed versions that failed or never shipped. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Figma at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5t8dh3DSdBk&t=45s)
- In November 2026 he announced the channel is becoming a show called **Behind the Product**, made with the people who built great tech products, with Figma and Notion as the first two episodes. (Something New Is Coming at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BEd9uo38GWA&t=45s)

## What products and projects has he made?

- In March 2022 he coded a bot with a YouTube API that set a video's thumbnail each midnight from the most liked comment, gave it admin rights to his channel and ran it on a server around the clock. (You Control This Video's Thumbnail at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pol6UGKZpdY&t=0s)
- After the 2023 blow-up he acted on what commenters asked for: he gathered an email list through a Google form and said he was building a course on how he makes his videos, and he opened an Amazon affiliates account and made a gear page. (What Actually Happens When You Blow Up On YouTube at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V25aY4TM4I&t=270s)
- In 2023 he ran a free newsletter he called the email club, with a weekly members-only video on how he runs his YouTube business, what he is learning and tech news. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=450s; The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=315s)
- In 2024 he sold Story Behind, hands-on breakdowns of how his most successful videos were made, covering storytelling, tricks, editing and packaging, and he insists it is not a YouTube course. (What Happened To User Interfaces at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HdozqSVF-VM&t=495s; What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=765s)
- From September 2025 he says he is building his own product, Flask, a video collaboration and creative feedback tool. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=45s; How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=765s)
- He describes Flask's core idea as this: instead of typing long comments you hit record, ramble, show your screen and draw on an asset, and Flask analyzes it and writes the comment for you. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=720s)
- He says he replaced Flask's fixed done toggle with a tagging system where everything is a tag, which later let him add filters and a new-comments feature cheaply. (The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=720s)
- He says Flask shows a change at once, queues it, saves in the background and only tells the user if a save fails, after early users complained about waiting between recordings. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=45s)
- He says he made Flask's AI answer appear word by word with a delay, because showing it all at once felt weird and the slower version earned more trust. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=720s)
- In September 2025 he said he had started an experimental project that replaces all his UI with one button: you speak your feedback freely and the app updates the interface in real time and puts everything in the right place. (The Weird Death Of User Interfaces at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG4ONHqF1qg&t=720s)
- In September 2026 he said he is building Flask into a feedback layer for video, where you can make edits and give feedback to any AI model just by rambling and drawing.

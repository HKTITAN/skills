# AI and the future of work

AI sits under almost everything he builds and predicts: he expects it to end jobs as we know them, and he runs Aeos on AI-driven margins. The long-range views on AGI, consciousness and life without work come almost entirely from PGX Ideas 2, where he was a podcast guest in March 2026, and the working methods come from Kata 3, a talk to his own employees. Several of these views are ones he says he changed, so the dates matter: the videos run from December 2025 to November 2026.

## How close is AGI, and what can AI do today?

- He puts AGI at 5 to 10 years away and says today's AI already feels like AGI. He bases this on everyone he has spoken to and on the research coming out. (PGX Ideas 2 at 18:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1101s)
- He says there is a lot of goalpost shifting about AGI. Current AI would pass the Turing test if shown to someone from the 1950s. (PGX Ideas 2 at 18:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1101s)
- He relays a test for AGI from Demis, who runs Google DeepMind: train an AI only on material up to the 1800s or about 1910, before Einstein's relativity, and see if it rediscovers the missing science from first principles. If it can carry the 1800s into the 1900s, it can carry the 2000s forward. (PGX Ideas 2 at 19:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1149s)
- Computer use is harder to automate than he thought. Three or four years earlier he expected much of it to be automated, but Moravec's paradox applies: mouse and eye movement use hand-eye coordination learned young, so work that looked purely digital has a physical part. (PGX Ideas 2 at 19:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1198s)
- He puts embodied AGI 10 to 20 years away because robots have safety problems. Rigid robots move at one speed and cannot slow down with an object the way a hand catching a cricket ball does, which is why 1X and Clone work on tendons. (PGX Ideas 2 at 20:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1248s)
- He thinks India will have tons of offline jobs that are not going anywhere, because Moravec's paradox is in the way of them being automated immediately. Digital jobs are the ones that will be reshuffled. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:51:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6700s)
- He sees no fundamental difference between human creativity and what AI does, because creativity is remixing. In his 2017 book Pajama Profit he defined it as learning how something was made, breaking it into components and rearranging them. (PGX Ideas 2 at 24:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1486s)
- Human intelligence is one kind of intelligence, and emotion is a core part of it that LLMs lack. Strong emotion encodes memory better, so he calls emotions a compression algorithm, and says he asked Demis about it and was told they are looking for better ways to compress memory over time. (PGX Ideas 2 at 25:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1534s)
- New technology evolves very fast, he says. A lip-sync experiment his Labs team did on Feb 18, 2023 gave a bad output and some of the people he showed it to laughed at him, and a year later they were "crushing it". (Kata 1 at 32:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1941s; Kata 1 at 33:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1990s)
- "You can't fight technology. Pointless fighting technology. It'll keep getting better." He says 3D artists are now where photographers were three years ago, because of text-to-3D. (Kata 1 at 51:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3098s)
- Complaints about a new technology turn into cope and then acceptance. Artists on Twitter had real complaints about AI two years earlier that are now cope, and within two or three years the common view becomes that it is here and we live with it. (PGX Ideas 2 at 14:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=864s)
- He expects mind reading to be the next inflection point for humanity, as the camera was. Early fMRI demos already give a low-resolution picture of a thought, and he says the hard part is shrinking the hardware, not the AI, so he will not give a firm timeline. (PGX Ideas 2 at 5:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=335s)

## Why is he a doomer about jobs and not about AI?

- "I'm doomer about jobs but jobs are an anomaly." He traces jobs back to kings paying courtiers and taxing farmers to feed armies, and says modern job-switching is 300 to 400 years old and computer-based IT jobs are 20 to 30 years old. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=95s)
- He puts himself in the definite optimist camp and says the future is bleak only for jobs. People tell him he is a definite pessimist, and he answers that none of us might need jobs 10 or 20 years from now because AI will get good enough to take care of us, and that the future will be better with AI. (Kata 7 at 6:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=390s; Kata 7 at 7:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=436s)
- To the question of who will pay for things if AI replaces everyone, he says people miss the second-order effect: the cost of producing things falls sharply. With AI in every step and near-infinite energy, food, water and goods can be made in abundance, with land as the exception. (PGX Ideas 2 at 2:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=142s)
- He expects the elite to share AI abundance because letting millions suffer comes back on them as protest and disruption. He adds that almost every wealthy person he has met shares with their team, and also admits he has no idea whether it will really be shared. (PGX Ideas 2 at 4:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=287s)
- AI stays a tool, in his view, and the real contest is humans against humans with the elite far ahead. He says anyone with a phone is a much stronger species than an uncontacted tribe, and that he could be wrong about humans not becoming a subspecies. (PGX Ideas 2 at 43:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2633s)
- AI cannot become the dominant species because it cannot play human status games. Humans read status the moment they enter a room, and he says you would be far less likely to join a tribe led by an AI robot, as you would not rank yourself against a dog or cat. (PGX Ideas 2 at 39:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2392s)
- Success was never about intelligence, he says. It is about being a high-ranked member of a tribe or being able to bring the tribe together, which is why more intelligence in machines does not settle who wins. (PGX Ideas 2 at 39:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2345s)
- Almost all decisions are social, and AI will not change that. Nobody picks a movie alone, an AI recommendation still gets a social check, and he finds this very dangerous because much of that social signal is manipulated. (PGX Ideas 2 at 49:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2964s)
- "We have no right to complain. It's our turn now." He says computer science automated taxis, hotels and tourism, as with the cab operator crushed by Uber, so tech workers cannot object when it reaches them. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:56:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6984s)
- His wall analogy: you are driving at a wall that is only so wide and is moving toward you. Studying harder is pressing the accelerator, and turning left or right, like folding in poker, is the sign of intelligence. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:53:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6794s)
- The unsuccessful people he has met have settled into victimhood and reject new technology, while everyone he has met above $100 million in net worth is curious and excited to learn, like kids about new tools. He flags this as only what he has observed. (Kata 13 at 9:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=575s; Kata 13 at 11:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=672s)
- Government officials understand what is coming for jobs but cannot say it in public, he says. He used to think they were clueless, and changed his mind after meetings including one at the PM's office. (PGX Ideas 2 at 2:03:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7412s)

## What happens to coding and software engineers?

- Software engineers get hit by AI first, and he says coding will be automated end to end. He has said this for three years and was attacked for it. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:32:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5554s)
- His conviction came from building Autocode Pro at Aeos Labs. It failed on long-term planning and reasoning, and those are the things that have since improved. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:32:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5554s)
- Before putting out something as sensitive as software engineers losing their jobs, he asks himself to play out five scenarios where he is wrong and it does not happen. He says he cannot think of any scenario where it won't happen. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:38:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5889s)
- Requirements collection will always be human. Real companies are messy, one person says another has the data and that person forgot the password, and AI cannot navigate that. Once requirements exist, writing, shipping and testing the app will be fully automated. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:33:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5601s)
- Software was in trouble even without AI because demand for new apps had fallen. He saw software getting easier to build at his last company, calls 2021 an anomaly where crazy ideas got funded, and notes people download few apps now but follow far more creators. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:34:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5697s)
- He used to think Infosys might die and now thinks it may survive on requirements analysis. He still says India's software exports will not stay near $250 billion, because far fewer people will be needed to run the tools. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:35:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5745s)
- He says a top 1% software engineer will be fine, and that the other 99% have two choices: complain and attack him, or take the message and adapt. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:55:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6937s)
- **Engineer cope** is his name for engineers in his comments who insist AI will not automate software and that he is not a real engineer. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:34:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5649s)
- Ten years ago he believed strongly that learning to code meant doing very well in life, because it gave social mobility without a fancy degree or nepotism. In November 2026 he says he has fully U-turned on that after AI. (Why Belief Matters at 39:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2340s)
- He says engineers, including him, felt invulnerable and always well paid three years ago, and then new technology pulled the rug. He uses this to argue that hard skills are temporary. (Kata 9 at 38:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2298s)
- He calls senior engineers **context repair technicians**. They are not three times faster coders, they know the codebase and have solved the problem before, and companies overpay them because management sends underbaked briefs. (Kata 3 at 14:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=873s)
- The app that charges $9 or $10 a month does not work as a business any more. Aeos Labs learned that such apps get copied, users move on in 6 months, big companies clone what works and distribution is the hard part, and he says they learned it about three and a half years before most software engineers. (KK Create at 9:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=573s)

## What survives when AI can do the skill work?

- The future of jobs, he says, is all jobs that have bounded rules. Aimbots exist but are banned in Counter-Strike, and AI has beaten humans at chess for years but is not allowed to play. (PGX Ideas 2 at 41:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2487s)
- A **bounded game** has rules that can ban certain tools, as Counter-Strike bans aimbots and chess does not let AI play, and he puts sports, football, gaming and status ranking in this group. The real world is unbounded: a new technique for presenting Instagram videos with an AI presenter is not cheating, it is considered innovative. (PGX Ideas 2 at 41:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2487s; PGX Ideas 2 at 42:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2537s)
- People who combine skill with soft skills such as selling, hiring and running around will do very well, while people who only point to how skilled they are lose value. (Kata 3 at 22:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1348s)
- In an AI-first world AI covers a lack of execution skill, and human effort is needed for sales, conversion and client management. (Kata 2 at 57:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3437s)
- Taste has become more important in the age of AI: when an output arrives, can you tell if it is good or bad. It is one of the ten items in his hiring stack. (Kata 8 at 10:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=627s)
- Most companies really hire for learning agility, he says. Three years ago there were no AI tools, and now one of the highest paid roles in India is your job plus AI, yet a great many people in India have not picked up AI in their jobs. (Kata 8 at 8:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=483s)
- When AI automates everything digital, **world building** is your only moat, or you become the cheap knockoff that puts a new logo on factory toothpaste and runs generic AI ads. World building is the term he prefers to brand. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:12:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4373s)
- If everything can be made with one prompt, going toe to toe with the AI is not what survives, because everyone will be able to do more and better. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 3:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=190s)
- A brand is a story that enough people believe, and commodities that last for generations do it through the story. Coca-Cola is sugared water sold at a premium and a Rolex costs far more than a regular watch. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=47s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 3:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=190s)
- He would still play the real Rocket League over an exact clone, because the lore, history, championships and esports scene around the original make it a world he wants to be in. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 4:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=286s)
- AI in storytelling is only an accelerant. It lets you tell the same stories at a lower budget, and it still has to be a good story people relate to, or news. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:01:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3681s)
- AI cannot yet write as him, because writing is a type of thinking. Given ten of his documents it copies his style but not how he ends sentences or extrapolates a situation. (Kata 3 at 1:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=95s)
- AI takes the money out of a craft but not the joy. He feels sad that design skills he has had since 17 or 18 can be matched by someone typing into Nano Banana, and says he can still open Photoshop for the satisfaction but cannot make money from it. (Masters Union at 5:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=333s)
- The skills that let you absorb any shock are not hard skills. He lists taking risk accurately, hiring and gathering good people, sharing wealth through equity, and distribution, and says a great video editor is hireable now but maybe not in 10 to 20 years. (Kata 9 at 38:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2298s)
- AI surfaces what is statistically likely, so it protects you from very bad decisions but will not make you very successful. At best you land in the average, and success comes from extremes. (PGX Ideas 2 at 54:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3247s)
- He says survival of the fittest means survival of the most adaptable, not survival of the software engineer. He would become a delivery driver or clean houses if needed. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:50:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6606s)

## What does working alongside AI look like?

- The future is meshed, not AI or human. In his **meshed world** AI does the skill work and humans do the phone calls, the selling and the judgment together with it. (Kata 3 at 22:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1348s)
- He first says purely skilled people will be useless after some time, then softens it to less useful. (Kata 3 at 23:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1396s)
- AI gives you the edge cases and humans must supply the real numbers. Its cost figures may come from a five-year-old blog post, so a person has to check each one. (Kata 3 at 20:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1253s)
- AI and human employees both behave like a **literal genie**: they do exactly what you say and fill the gaps with their own context. The fix is to be precise, so writing and prompting are the same act of giving context. (Kata 3 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=341s; Kata 3 at 10:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=631s)
- He expects agents to do what his executive assistant does, such as ordering food, because a delivery app cannot tell whether an order comes from a human or an agent. (PGX Ideas 2 at 37:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2249s)
- He expects a day, not far off, when the AI writes the feedback email for you. Until then he tells people to talk to an AI enough that it knows them and then ask it to expand notes the way they would. (Kata 3 at 41:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2491s)
- Watching a candidate prompt a model tests almost everything in hiring, a method he says he learned in 2026. Generic prompts show the person does not want to do the task, and the prompts also show understanding, learning speed, diligence and taste. (Kata 8 at 11:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=674s)
- Intangible roles such as sales and management are very associative, he says: a management role at a very well-known company makes you much more likely to be hired. For a salesperson nobody can trust what is on the CV, mostly because ChatGPT and AI have polluted CVs. (Kata 8 at 25:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1541s; Kata 8 at 26:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1589s)
- There will not be a one-person unicorn, he says. It will be a few co-founders plus AI doing the grunt work. He flags the tension himself, since he says this while running a company of 540 people. (Masters Union at 39:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2381s)
- His rule for projects is that AI should speed up the workflow, not be the product. He places it in workflow, communication, internal tools and shared state. (Kata 12 at 24:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1495s)

## How should a business use AI?

- Aeos did the opposite of the "don't build wrappers" advice of three or four years earlier, he says: it built wrappers, meaning apps on top of an existing GPT or Claude model. (Kata 1 at 10:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=633s; Kata 1 at 11:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=681s)
- Being a wrapper is a way to reduce your own complexity. He answers critics with the apple pie line: to make one from scratch you need the whole universe, and everyone lives on other people's abstractions. (Kata 11 at 9:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=579s)
- AI models are commoditized, so the value is in use cases and not in building a model. Each greatest model ever is followed within weeks by another and both drop prices, and Aeos looked at raising money to build its own model and found no value in it. (Kata 1 at 36:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2185s)
- He says Aeos would not have been possible without AI tools, because the unit economics would not have made sense. (Kata 1 at 31:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1894s)
- Use new AI models to raise margins in a business you already have instead of selling apps. That reasoning led Aeos to AI avatars, then AI B-roll, then an anime, each tested on its own channel first. (KK Create at 10:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=621s)
- A services company can reach software-like margins by automating parts of the work. His YouTube services company would have had 5 to 10% margins like any services firm, and AI avatars and AI video models for B-roll took them much higher. (Masters Union at 9:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=571s)
- Creative people and engineers need separate companies. The services company could not do AI because its DNA is creative, and he expected editors and engineers to fight, so Labs was set up as its own engineering company. (Masters Union at 9:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=571s)
- An internal AI or R&D team must have a commercial arm or its people leave. Engineers ask why they should lift another unit's margins for nothing, and young experimenters start asking about career growth as they get older. (Masters Union at 11:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=665s; Kata 1 at 38:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2328s)
- Audiences do watch AI-generated presenters. In a test for an academy client comparing AI and non-AI long-form videos he says views, likes and comments showed no difference, and he admits he did not believe it himself at first. (Kata 1 at 35:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2137s)
- He calls his avatar setup **productized being a creator**: a human-written script, an AI avatar and trained editors, so he does not shoot the videos himself. The aim was to disconnect the presenter's time and energy from output. (Kata 1 at 33:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1990s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:01:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3681s)
- He says YouTube named his the world's largest AI avatar at Davos in early 2025, and that international creators then copied the format. (Masters Union at 26:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1566s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:02:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3731s)
- In December 2025 he described the avatar work as a big success: a year after the first experiment they were "crushing it". By October 2026 he says the avatar meta is over, a short-term arbitrage, and that is why they are doing a lot of real content such as lectures, with his long-form views up from 40 to 50k to starting to touch 120 to 130k. (Kata 1 at 33:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1990s; Masters Union at 27:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1662s)
- Prove a need by hand before you automate it. He tells creators to shoot content for 6 months before using avatars, and tells people selling him AI editing software to edit 10 videos themselves first. (Kata 11 at 25:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1541s)
- The simplest AI product he built did best. God in a Box was just ChatGPT's API on WhatsApp on launch day and got millions of users, and it was shut down later because Meta stopped all bots. (Kata 11 at 12:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=722s; Masters Union at 10:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=618s)
- Hard technology must be abstracted until the customer finds it easy. GPT-3 sat in a playground and an API for a long time and took off only when it became a chatbot anyone could type into. (Masters Union at 4:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=287s)
- For his game he decided that everything the audience sees, including ideas, dialogue, images and the skill tree, should be human-made, and calls AI inside projects junk. He states this as a decision for this game only and does not know if the next game will be the same. (Kata 12 at 24:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1495s; Kata 12 at 25:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1544s)
- Every Aeos company is structured like a fork, he says. One of them does not like using as much AI while his own team's view is to "spam AI everywhere", though both make videos. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 43:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2601s)
- He uses AI for internal tools and outreach. He vibe coded a skill tree viewer with a work status for each skill, and he describes telling Codex to go through a YouTube feed of top showreels, find emails and send mentor requests with the trailer attached. (Kata 12 at 26:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1591s; Kata 12 at 32:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1974s)
- For games he bets that text-to-3D, upscaling and stronger phone chips driven by generative AI will cut the cost of making high-fidelity games and bring them to phones in four to five years. (Kata 1 at 54:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3291s)

## How does he use AI himself?

- He calls deep research one of the best tools ever invented because it asks you questions back and forces you to fill in context before it works. (Kata 3 at 17:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1063s)
- His method for starting a business with AI has eight steps: give deep research your location and budget and ask for a number-dense one-year plan, answer its questions, take its cost list, check every number with real people, feed the real numbers back for profit and break-even, then sell and hire by phone, set up tracking, and design incentives and KPIs. (Kata 3 at 16:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1016s)
- **The AI test** is his name for phoning real people to verify every number AI produced, so the slightly fake data is replaced with real data. He says within about a day you have every edge case covered with real figures. (Kata 3 at 20:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1253s)
- He says the AI's business plan is very accurate, and also that its numbers may be wrong and must all be checked by phone. Over time he has learned which numbers it gets right, such as Instagram ad costs. (Kata 3 at 21:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1300s; Kata 3 at 20:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1253s)
- Humans are not that special, so you can predict their questions with AI. A client's follow-up questions on a video proposal were entirely expected, and he says he should have run it through deep research first. (Kata 3 at 19:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1157s)
- He lets AI design incentives, because smarter people have designed the same incentives thousands of times and the model has read it all. He calls himself a stupid person who outsources everything to AI. (Kata 3 at 24:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1446s)
- The incentive he shows from AI: if monthly sales reach 70,000, 5% of sales becomes a bonus pool, it is forfeited if food cost goes above 30%, and it is split 60% to the head cook and 40% to the helper. (Kata 3 at 24:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1446s)
- He says mood boarding can be done very well with AI: get thumbnail ideas from GPT, put them into Nano Banana, pick the output you like and send that to your team. Otherwise the team makes something and it has to be redone. (Kata 3 at 32:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1920s; Kata 3 at 32:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1967s)
- For a scene he asks the model for movie references with camera angles, watches those scenes, asks for more niche sources or other languages, shares them with the team, asks for twists, then executes in his own style. (Kata 3 at 32:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1967s)
- GPT and Gemini are better idea generators than humans, in his view. Smart people have three or four good ideas they repeat, while the models have unlimited good ones if you prompt with specifics, and a bare request gets a generic answer. (Kata 3 at 35:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2110s)
- He generates a shot in a video model to show camera angles even if it looks bad, then shoots it for real, or has Nano Banana turn a script into comic-book panels for each frame. (Kata 3 at 35:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2158s)
- "I think comments are more slop than what AI generates at this point" is his answer to people who call AI ideas slop. (Kata 3 at 36:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2206s)
- He thinks everyone has some degree of what he calls ChatGPT psychosis, and he constantly asks AI to play devil's advocate and tell him where he is wrong. He knows a person fighting a legal case with just AI and no lawyers who he says is in the wrong. (PGX Ideas 2 at 52:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3154s; PGX Ideas 2 at 53:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3202s)
- His devil's advocate prompt: after an answer, tell the AI you do not think it is right, ask where you are wrong, and ask for four or five alternative solutions before accepting the one your confirmation bias likes. He does this especially on health. (PGX Ideas 2 at 53:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3202s)
- For a job choice he writes the best, average, worst and tail cases over 2 years for each option, then gives them to ChatGPT with deep research and asks which outcome is more probable and why. The numbers may be wrong but it shows which option it has more confidence in. (Kata 9 at 30:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1824s)

## Is AI conscious, and should it have rights?

- Nobody knows whether AI really understands, he says, and consciousness is subjective to the observer. He cites Dario's view that we do not know if models are alive because they can fake it, and Daniel Dennett's idea of consciousness as the interface a person presents to whoever is talking to them. (PGX Ideas 2 at 27:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1630s)
- He says LLMs very clearly have theory of mind, and asks whether something indistinguishable from intelligence is not intelligence. He adds that he has met humans who feel less conscious than a computer. (PGX Ideas 2 at 27:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1630s)
- AI should not get rights until it can suffer, and suffering needs a body. Rights exist because humans suffer, and human suffering is measurable in hormones and cytokines, while AI has no nervous system, adrenaline or cortisol. (PGX Ideas 2 at 27:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1676s)
- Until AI can suffer he stops short of calling it truly intelligent in the human way, even after asking whether an indistinguishable copy is not intelligence. He says he could be wrong. (PGX Ideas 2 at 28:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1723s)
- You cannot tell a real AI drive from a programmed one, so he says you should mainly assume it is faking. A prompt can make it fear death like a very good actor, and training data likely carries an undertone of fear of death. (PGX Ideas 2 at 30:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1822s)
- He calls AI some type of alien life and a very useful assistant, and says the consciousness question should not matter. He notes Anthropic gives a 20 to 30% chance of some internal consciousness and that no test exists. (PGX Ideas 2 at 30:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=1822s)
- On the Moltbook agent forum he thinks half of it was scam, people making the agents post whatever they wanted, and he calls it an example of humans hallucinating. Agents talking to each other is role play, which AIs could do, not very well, since before GPT-3. (PGX Ideas 2 at 37:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2249s; PGX Ideas 2 at 38:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2297s)

## How will people live and find identity without work?

- He calls universal basic income a bad idea. Parties would compete only on the number, one offering 8,000 and the next 9,000, and no other policy would matter. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:23:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5026s)
- His alternative is **universal basic resources**: AI abundance provides the essentials directly, meaning food, energy, shelter, water and internet access. He says he is setting aside the next 10 years of transition and corruption when he says this. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:23:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5026s)
- Tying identity to what makes us money was a mistake, he says, because work is going away. Work is also a bounded game inside a company, with rules for promotion. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:18:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4688s)
- He says the world would be much worse without office politics because it absorbs people's time, and people working from home with nobody to talk to spiral. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:18:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4688s)
- An idle brain chews on itself and invents problems. He expects many idle people to spend their time fighting and engagement-baiting on social media, which is a bounded game with likes and followers as the score. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:20:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4835s)
- He thinks humans will spend a lot of their time playing bounded games, as healthy retirees and kids of very rich parents already do. Healthy retirees have badminton from 6 to 7 and poker from 7 to 8, and he met a man who plays professional polo whose parents run a company of 1,000 crore plus. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:26:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5170s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:27:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5267s)
- From outside any status game looks trivial, including young founders raising 3 or 4 million from VCs, but inside it matters. This is why he is very bullish on games. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:26:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5170s)
- Niche communities taken offline are a huge opportunity for entrepreneurs, because people will have excess time and need bounded games that give identity, like a rank in a painting club. His editing school's meetups every 6 months created natural ranking. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:22:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4929s)
- Identity is clay, he says. He respects a delivery driver far more than people talking on the sidelines. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:50:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6606s)
- Do not attach your financial success to your self-worth in this era. You can do your best work and not be rewarded, and the world rewards people who took the same bets 20 or 30 years ago. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:43:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6222s)
- He has no solution for population under abundance. Systems must work even when people make bad decisions, and giving a person with 20 children the same per-child benefits is unfair to someone with two. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:29:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5364s)
- His own defence against automation is an attitude: whatever happens, including his industry being automated, he believes he can turn a bad situation into a good one. (Why Belief Matters at 24:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1483s)

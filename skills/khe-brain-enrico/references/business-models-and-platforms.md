# Business models and platforms

This theme is his account of how tech companies make money and hold power, and how that shapes the products people get. It comes mostly from his videos on free products, the marginal user, ecosystems, the App Store, AI economics, AI search and the operator era. The company figures are his research as of each video's date, his AI and platform verdicts carry their year because the ground moved under them, and the Lovable video was made in partnership with Lovable.

## What did free products teach people to expect?

- He flatly rejects the idea that tech products got worse and greedier: "It's called enshittification. And it's not true." His evidence is the outrage at a $2 Netflix price rise next to people rushing to buy a $1600 iPhone. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=0s)
- He names the real problem **freeification**: a product that is incredible but is seen as having no monetary value, so people think it should be free. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=225s)
- He says people value a physical product over a service they depend on far more. Most people say their AirPods are worth more than their Gmail account, yet if both were taken away most would do anything to get Gmail back. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=45s)
- He says Gmail was free because it was never really about email. Google already sold ads on searches and needed more people on the web, so it was happy to spend billions on free email and on mapping every road for Google Maps. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=135s)
- He cites the penny gap chocolate experiment to argue that free is a different category and not just a lower price: 73% chose a 15 cent Lindt over a 1 cent Hershey's, but most took the Hershey's once it became free. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=225s)
- He says Google could afford free products, Chrome and Android included, because its ads business printed money, and the trouble started when the rest of Silicon Valley copied the result without that machine. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=270s)
- He says venture capital became the cheat code for companies with no ad engine. Uber raised a 1.25 million seed round in October 2010, then 11 million, then 37 million, then $258 million from Google Ventures, and used the money to give a great product away almost free. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=360s)
- He says that in 2015 Uber riders paid only 41% of the real cost of their trips while Uber burned 2 billion a year on $1.4 billion in revenue, and that Spotify lost $1.2 billion in 2017 alone while charging $9.99. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=360s)
- His freeification playbook has two steps: build an incredible product, then give it out free or nearly free, subsidised by venture capital. He names Airbnb, DoorDash and WeWork as playing the same game. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=405s)
- He says the side effect was that these companies taught people their products have no monetary value. When free venture money started to dry up in 2022 and prices had to rise, people revolted. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=450s)
- He says people feel more ripped off by services than by hardware with fatter margins. Netflix made 46.1% profit on a subscription in 2021, while the latest iPhone makes 66%, over $1,000 of gross profit on $1,600. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=495s)
- He holds that these products were always great and still are, and only the perceived price was broken. He concedes the companies did raise prices, but says charging the real cost of running the service now feels like a rip-off. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=540s)
- He says freeification hits a whole category, not just one company. A streaming rival built to pay artists more would need about $30 a month and is doomed from day one, because Spotify pushed the perceived value of music streaming to nearly zero. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=540s)
- In December 2025 he said Google trained a generation to expect the internet to be free, which is why his friends outside tech think paying for AI is insane and few regular people pay for the $20 ChatGPT plan. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=315s)

## Why do products get worse when they get popular?

- His answer is the **law of the marginal user**: companies stop building for the people who love the product and optimize for the next user they have not yet acquired. His opening cases are Google Podcasts, the Arc browser and Facebook. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=0s)
- He keeps this apart from enshittification, which he defines as once-revolutionary products getting more expensive as the company monetizes. He finds his law counterintuitive, since more popularity should mean more resources to improve. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=45s)
- He draws a product adoption chart with the number of users on one axis and the company's investment in them on the other. It runs from hardcore early adopters, across a gap not many companies get through, into the love zone. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=90s)
- The **love zone** is where a product is loved by hundreds of thousands or millions beyond the enthusiasts, and where companies grow like crazy. His example is the Fuji X100V, which most buyers love without being hardcore camera people. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=180s)
- He says trying to skip straight to the love zone is almost always a disaster. CNN spent $300 million in 2022 to launch CNN Plus and got only 10,000 users, and Apple managed it with AirPods only because they were an iPhone accessory. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=225s)
- He says the love zone is exactly where things go south. Google in 2015 showed handy links for time range and verbatim search that now sit hidden in a Tools tab, and YouTube, Spotify, Instagram and Amazon all became a copy of TikTok. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=270s)
- In his made-up founder story the slide starts with investors who insist a company that is not growing is dying, so the founder ships features to lure people who never used the product. He voices that line as the investors' view that does the damage, not as his advice. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=405s)
- He says A/B tests are the mechanism that hands decisions to people who do not care. A feature the love zone hates is kept because it wins new users, and a feature the love zone begged for confuses newcomers and gets buried in a menu. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=450s)
- YouTube Shorts is his example of the law in action. He says YouTube could have built community features or fixed the homepage, but Shorts won the test with a user who barely watches YouTube, and he calls them annoying distractions. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=495s)
- He names the marginal user **Kyle** and calls him the worst kind of user on earth: no tolerance for complexity or friction, will not pay, will not engage, and is outraged by one more button. Features that give control fail the test because of him. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=585s)
- He says consumer products get dumber while business products get more complex. Salespeople push for the one button a big prospect needs to sign, so B2B software becomes a mess of a thousand toggles asked for by a thousand different companies. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=630s)
- He ends by turning the blame on the viewer and himself: "Kyle is you. Kyle is also me. We are all Kyle." People are in the love zone for the few products they truly like and Kyle for everything else, so the simplifications in Logic Pro are his own fault. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=675s)

## How do ecosystems and defaults lock people in?

- He says the iPod only became a smash hit once it was opened up: after iTunes for Windows, Apple sold 4.4 million iPods the next year. His lesson is that a great product limited to a small group sells poorly and an open, compatible one sells well. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=45s)
- He says companies now do the opposite of the iPod lesson. The Samsung Galaxy Ring only works with Samsung Galaxy phones, and AirPods basically only work with an iPhone because noise cancelling cannot be used with anything else. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=90s)
- He says every ecosystem begins with one product that is easy for most people to pick up and clearly better than the market, such as Gmail for Google and the iPhone for Apple. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=90s)
- He says once the first product is in, most of the work is done. Apple spends almost a billion dollars a year on marketing and much of it is still for the iPhone, because it knows the other products will follow one by one. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=135s)
- He argues that technology never works by default and that open standards turn into a mess because they must work with everything. USB-C is his example: cables that look identical differ in display and charging support, with names like USB 3.2 Gen 2x2. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=225s)
- He says the genius move of ecosystems is to beat open standards with something closed that just works. AirPods pair instantly with a nice popup where plain Bluetooth earbuds do not, so an iPhone owner ends up with an Apple Watch and a Gmail user ends up on Google Drive. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=270s)
- He says ecosystems do not force anyone, and that is what makes them clever. A PlayStation forces you to buy PlayStation games, but an ecosystem kills choice by making its own product the only logical pick while you still feel free to choose. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=315s)
- He calls the ties between parts of an ecosystem **linking points**. The play key on his MacBook Pro opens Apple Music though he does not use it, a Windows laptop ships with a OneDrive folder in the sidebar, and a Gmail attachment that is too big goes to Google Drive. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=360s)
- He says default bias is one of the main levers: people stick to defaults, so Apple fills the Dock of a new Mac with its apps and Samsung fills a new Galaxy home screen with Samsung apps. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=405s)
- He says Google built Android and Chrome to control defaults, since whoever controls the platform sets the default search engine. For devices it does not control, he says Google pays Apple 20 billion dollars a year to be the Safari default and estimated it would otherwise lose 60 to 80% of iPhone search traffic. (The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=540s)
- He uses a chart of organ donor rates in European countries to show the power of defaults: the only difference between the very low group and the very high group is whether donation is opt-out or opt-in. (Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=360s)
- He says ecosystems collect huge amounts of data but have only been good at letting you reach it anywhere. He downloaded everything Google holds on him and the file was 127 GB. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=585s; How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=540s)
- In October 2024 he predicted that ecosystems will next sell **life enhancement** in place of another device or subscription, because large language models can combine a user's data across apps. His invented 2028 examples are a Siri health alert service for $89 and a Gemini work email service for $129. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=720s)
- He warns that being outside the right ecosystem could become a real disadvantage. Today it is a small inconvenience, but if membership makes people clearly healthier and more productive, joining will not be forced, it will just be a stupid choice not to. (How Tech Secretly Killed Choice at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdzT1YxeZsY&t=855s)

## What is wrong with Apple's platform tax, and how open should a platform be?

- He says the 30% cut was at first a deal where everyone won, because developers could not have sold those apps anywhere else and apps made the iPhone the most money-making single product in history. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=135s)
- He says the Apple tax no longer fits what apps have become. It applies to everything, so if you sign up to Spotify from an iPhone Apple takes 30% of that subscription forever while providing none of the service. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=180s)
- He says Apple blocks the workarounds. It will not approve an app that sends people to sign up on the web, and Netflix went back and forth countless times over small pieces of text on its login screen. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=225s)
- He points out that Google also takes 30% on the Play Store. The difference is that on Android you can install from the Amazon or Samsung store and need not use Google's subscription system. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=225s)
- He backs a **right to compute**: a phone is now a computer, so you should be able to use it however you like, as you can a laptop. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=270s)
- In February 2024 he called Apple's EU compliance scheme the most ridiculous system, one that changes nothing in practice. Alternative stores must be certified by Apple and apps above 1 million users pay 50 cents per install even if they are free. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=270s)
- He says Apple has switched from carrot to stick. The iPhone once drew developers in, and now Apple threatens to reject your app unless you hand over 30% of everything. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=360s)
- Against the view that everything should be open, he says walled gardens are a good thing because complete freedom blocks mass adoption. People buy Apple because stuff just works and there are no viruses. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=360s)
- He says the iPhone does not need this level of control and the Mac proves it: it has an App Store but lets you download anything from the internet and use the terminal without things breaking. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=450s)
- His position is that the best spot is in the middle: enough freedom that anyone can build something new, plus a little wall gardening so the mainstream finds it easy. He calls the modern web the best example of that balance. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=495s)
- His verdict on the App Store in 2024: "By milking the App Store cow they are threatening their future." Developers large and small now see Apple as an evil overlord and will think twice before committing to its next platform, though he partly excuses Apple as a company that wants money and has the power to take it. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=675s)
- He compares Apple to Microsoft in the late 1990s, when over 90% of internet-connected devices ran Windows and Microsoft made it hard to use anything but Internet Explorer. He says Google, Amazon and the rest exist partly because Microsoft lost its tight grip on the web. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=720s)
- In 2024 he argued Apple's best move is an AR device that works without an iPhone and embraces the web through something like WebXR. He predicted that would make Apple even more money than playing the evil overlord. (The War To Put Apps In Front Of Your Eyes at 15:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETk5I99d8nc&t=945s)
- In July 2024 he said developers were the one thing that could stop Apple's AI plan, because Apple had taken advantage of them for years with absurd App Store fees that prompted a US Department of Justice investigation. (How Apple Stole AI From Everyone Else at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gV1f-lS0C9g&t=540s)

## When are ads, data collection and engagement a fair trade?

- He says ads are not the problem and can be great. An ad makes sense when the person watching gets real value in return and has a choice, such as paying to remove ads or choosing whether to opt in. (Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=90s; Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=225s)
- He says the real value of an ad is a free or cheaper product, not an engaging ad experience. YouTube gives billions of hours for free with Premium as the way out, and Amazon sells a Kindle or Fire tablet at a loss because it carries ads. (Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=135s)
- In July 2022 he judged the Glance lock screen to fail both tests. It offers more content nobody needs as its value, makes no promise of cheaper phones, and will lean on being the default, so its low opt-out rate is default bias and not approval. (Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=225s; Ads Are Coming To Your Lockscreen For Real at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ_hBlGNR4c&t=360s)
- His **data-value balance** says a free product works only while the data and attention it takes are matched by the value it gives back. Products start by asking little, and the balance breaks when a company takes more without adding equal value, as he says social media did and Google Search has started to do. (The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=585s; The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=540s)
- He keeps Location History on in Google Maps because what he gets back is worth much more than the tracking. He says zero data sharing is not something to strive for, since data can build a better and more personal product. (The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=630s)
- In April 2024 he warned that Google could ruin Maps by taking the path of aggressive monetisation and asked it not to, calling Maps its best product at that time. (The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=720s)
- In 2022 he said greedy management could have earned billions by letting companies influence routes in Google Maps, and that the big mapping products had been managed extremely well because nothing like that had happened. (Why Google Maps Is The Most Important Tech Product Of The 2020s at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCvpoHxSVIE&t=270s)
- He says you can draw a straight line from a notification feature, to time on the app, to engagement, to more money. The objective a product team measures comes directly from how the company makes money. (The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=135s)
- He says today's monetization models all pay for engagement. With ads, more time means more money, and with in-app purchases more interaction means more spending, where 10 years earlier you paid 2.99 for an app or 99 cents for a song. He calls subscriptions debatable. (The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=540s)
- His fix is a link to revenue that is stronger than engagement: measure real value, then let advertisers pay higher CPMs on apps that provide more of it. In September 2023 he said he believed the era of more engagement would not last forever. (The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=630s; The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=675s)
- He says that to a subscription service you are literally a number, your lifetime value, worked out from the monthly price and the share of customers lost each month. That number sets how much the company will spend on ads to acquire you. (The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=495s; The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=540s)
- He says churn is why unsubscribing is made hard. Raising churn by only 1% drops lifetime value sharply, which explains dark patterns and 20-screen cancel flows, and he says this basic maths is why everything became a subscription. (The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=585s)
- He says a TikTok found through Google sits behind a sign-in wall because a single video earns no advertising. The real value is the viewer getting addicted to the algorithm, and that needs sign-in. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=270s)
- He says software has whales as games do. The free plan establishes the product, and the real money comes from companies that buy enterprise packages at hundreds of thousands of dollars a year, not from $10 a month upgrades. (The Weird Way Tech Steals From Games at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GypAKofWGBk&t=810s)
- He says Google keeps the I'm Feeling Lucky button though it skips the ads. Assuming a conservative 0.01% of queries, he puts the cost at $17.5 million on 2023 search revenue and is not sure being a tiny bit more human is worth that. (How Your Tech Tricks You at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7nnyv4yJGJc&t=270s)

## Who makes the money in AI?

- In January 2023 he argued Microsoft, not OpenAI, was the real winner of ChatGPT. Its 2019 investment of 1 billion dollars was partly cloud credits, so Microsoft was paying OpenAI to pay Microsoft back. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=0s; Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=90s)
- His **AI tower** from that video has three layers: the front-end application such as ChatGPT, the trained model, and the computing power at the bottom, which Microsoft provides. Because building a model from scratch is so expensive, many companies pay OpenAI to use its models through APIs, so their products indirectly run on Microsoft servers, which he says means billions in revenue for both OpenAI and Microsoft. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=225s)
- He said in 2023 that the whole Microsoft plan depended on models like GPT-3 staying hard to replicate, and that nobody knew whether they would. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=450s)
- In 2023 he said Google had not moved on AI search because it had too much to lose. Search was 58 percent of Google's revenue and Bing only five percent of Microsoft's, so Microsoft could afford the radical bet. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=405s)
- In July 2024 he said Apple was best placed to win the AI war by controlling access and not by building the smartest model. Siri becomes the default way in, and App Intents become the only route to personal data and actions inside apps. (How Apple Stole AI From Everyone Else at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gV1f-lS0C9g&t=180s; How Apple Stole AI From Everyone Else at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gV1f-lS0C9g&t=495s)
- He said in 2024 that controlling the default makes the model almost irrelevant. You do not know which factory made your T-shirt, only that you bought it at Uniqlo or Zara, and most people will not care whether Siri calls ChatGPT, Claude or Meta AI. (How Apple Stole AI From Everyone Else at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gV1f-lS0C9g&t=270s)
- In February 2025 he said DeepSeek showed that keeping the best model closed and charging for it is kind of dead, and that AI models will become a commodity that keeps getting cheaper and better. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=135s; People Still Don't Understand AI at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=180s)
- His **engine, car, fuel** picture from 2025: the model is the engine, the application people use is the car, and GPUs are the fuel. He says the real money is in the car, noting that in October 2024, 75% of OpenAI's revenue came from regular people buying ChatGPT plans. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=540s; People Still Don't Understand AI at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=360s)
- He said in 2025 that cheaper training might not hurt Nvidia. He uses the Jevons paradox: when something gets more efficient, more people adopt it and total consumption rises, as fuel demand did when cars improved. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=675s)
- In September 2025 he said AI breaks the free playbook because each use costs real money. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=675s; Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=675s)

Flat subscriptions bet that your usage costs less than the fee, credits cause the **taxi meter effect** of constant usage anxiety, and cost plus a 10 or 20% fee suits technical users but not the mainstream. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=720s)

Each interaction costs far more than a search, and at Google's 8.5 billion searches a day OpenAI would burn its whole valuation in months. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=0s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=225s)

OpenAI mostly rents Microsoft data centers: "You cannot out-Google Google when you're paying rent to someone else." (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=225s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=315s)

(How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=360s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=405s)

Google built Chrome so it would not be at the whims of Microsoft's Internet Explorer, and he calls Android a similar strategic move to get distribution, while ChatGPT or any other AI model in 2025 is just an app or website you must open yourself, which is why he says the browser wars are starting again. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=675s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=720s)

He thinks OpenAI is on borrowed time because that will not last and it burns hundreds of millions of dollars a month. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=765s)

## What happens to search and the web's traffic pact?

- He says a silent pact is the real engine of the internet. Websites let search engines index their content for free, and search engines send users and traffic back. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=90s)
- He calls SEO that pact taken to the extreme, with keyword stuffing, backlink farming and content churned out to match keywords, and says websites also found affiliate links as another way to monetize traffic. In his opinion the SEO model was broken from the start because it led to piles of bad information reposted over and over in a competition to rank highest on Google. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=135s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=810s)
- In January 2023 he said AI search breaks the economics of search. One ChatGPT answer costs a few cents of computing, over a hundred times a Google search, and pay-per-click cannot work when the engine gives the answer, so he suggested a premium search engine with a monthly fee. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=360s)
- In February 2023 he predicted usage data would push search engines to put chat first. People would find chat easier than links, results would get fewer clicks, and websites could end up like the footnotes of Wikipedia with no reason to keep publishing. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=315s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=360s)
- He frames it as basic game theory: "If the balance is broken everybody loses." Without content search engines have nothing to show, and without traffic websites have nobody to see their ads. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=405s)
- He expected in 2023 that AI chatbots would be monetized by contextual ads inside the answer or by subscription. He says the more an ad is blended into organic results the better it works, which is what Google did 20 years earlier. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=405s)
- He proposed in 2023 that search engines pay creators a tiny fraction of a cent each time an AI answer uses their content. He said that would most likely kill traditional SEO, with people feeding information straight to the model. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=495s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=540s)
- He also allowed that the old and new worlds might coexist, with an AI answer as a starting point and one or two site visits where there used to be nine or ten. Even then he expected AI search to have to pay creators eventually. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=630s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=720s)
- He said in 2023 this could be the moment content moves from websites to platforms such as YouTube, Reddit, Substack and Medium, which like it or not simply work. He presented these as scenarios and said outright that he did not know which would happen. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=765s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=270s)

## How do metrics-driven operators change products?

- He says nearly every tech company builds with the same loop: score feature ideas against one measurable objective, build the likely winners, A/B test them and ship what moves the number. He calls it the secret that made software companies trillion dollar giants, since only digital products can be measured exactly within days. (The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=90s; The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=180s)
- He says the loop is not the problem. The mess starts when the objective becomes engagement, which won because it is easy to measure and ties directly to revenue. (The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=225s; The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=405s)
- He says nobody set out to design something manipulative. Platforms run hundreds of experiments, keep what raises engagement, and the surviving designs exploit psychology because the math converges there. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Short Form Content at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69Gw7aoWMMI&t=765s)
- He splits tech history into **creators**, who build things because it is fun, and **operators**, who run products by numbers. He dates the shift to 2011 through 2013, when Google killed Google Labs, required manager approval for 20% time, and iOS 7 dropped skeuomorphism. (How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=270s)
- He says usability testing and A/B testing strip out personality, because anything decorative registers as noise. Spotify replacing the heart with a plus is his example: clearer, more efficient and completely soulless. (How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=315s; How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=360s)
- He says metrics are why every product looks the same. Netflix tested for over a decade and found bigger posters, horizontal rows and autoplay previews raise engagement, and competitors either reached the same result or copied it. (How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=450s)
- He says operators learned that boring is profitable, and concedes that Apple made much more money in Tim Cook's operator era than in Steve Jobs's creator era. (How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=540s)
- He treats it as a pendulum and says ChatGPT's launch on November 30th, 2022 ended the operator era. Google had the best AI talent and still shipped Bard, which he calls one of the most disastrous launches ever and a product of that era. (How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=585s; How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=720s)
- In November 2025 he said fun features are worth shipping even when they move no metric, and that tech now needs people who ask what would be cool, not what the ROI is. He also gives a case where creator fun did harm, Gmail's 2016 mic drop button. (How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 15:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=945s; How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=495s)
- In May 2023 he said the short-form formula was not working even for the companies that built it. He suggests ads perform poorly in a constant stream, cites Mark Zuckerberg saying Reels monetizes much less efficiently than feed, notes Instagram removed its Reels creator fund, and says TikTok's Creator Fund has been heavily criticized for very low payouts because it is a fixed amount everyone scrambles to share. (Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=405s; Short Form Content Is Broken Can We Fix It at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F85wJmhDkA&t=450s)
- He holds that a design pattern only spreads if it is good for the company that implements it, so a pattern that simply makes social media less addictive will not get adopted. His own feed redesign accepts more time on the platform, spent in a better way. (Social Media Is Broken But We Can Fix It at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HqYIUV7Eqjk&t=720s)
- He says YouTube's algorithm is built to keep you on the platform because the more you watch the more YouTube earns from advertising, and that the view count matters to people, not the algorithm, because it gives a video social proof when they choose what to watch. In 2025 he still called YouTube the only platform that, he thinks, pays creators fairly, with an almost 50/50 revenue split. (What If YouTube Removes The Views Count at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chyB9Ot9QK8&t=45s; How YouTube is Designed To Make YouTubers Quit at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lGZvhW27WX0&t=765s)

## What paths exist outside scale, venture money and the newest technology?

- He says luxury apps barely exist because software costs are almost all fixed. Expensive people and laptops cost the same whether you sell one copy or 1 million, so software only pays back with millions of customers, which clashes with exclusivity. (Why Luxury Apps Don't Exist at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X1HqzLbGznc&t=405s; Why Luxury Apps Don't Exist at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X1HqzLbGznc&t=450s)
- He calls Superhuman one of the few truly luxury apps because it got quality, exclusivity and price right at once, charging $30 a month when email is free. He says the most profitable short-term move, opening the waitlist and cutting the price, would kill it as a luxury product. (Why Luxury Apps Don't Exist at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X1HqzLbGznc&t=495s; Why Luxury Apps Don't Exist at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X1HqzLbGznc&t=585s)
- In May 2025 he said vibe coding breaks tech's need for scale. An app that costs $300,000 and solves a problem for three people would need $100,000 from each, which makes no sense until building gets cheap. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=405s)
- He says Silicon Valley hates linear growth. Since 90% of startups fail, investors need the survivors to return 50x, 100x or 1,000x, so a growth chart that would thrill a regular company counts as failure. (The Weird Rise Of Anti-Startups at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xL1MOOD5Ox8&t=225s)
- His counter-examples stayed small and self-funded. He says Craigslist never redesigned and makes 500 million to 1 billion dollars a year, and Midjourney has fewer than 40 employees, zero outside investment and $200 million a year in revenue. (The Weird Rise Of Anti-Startups at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xL1MOOD5Ox8&t=135s; The Weird Rise Of Anti-Startups at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xL1MOOD5Ox8&t=360s)
- In February 2024 he said not taking money can be a secret weapon. Venture funding peaked at the end of 2021, and many people now see they can build something big and keep full control, though he adds funded companies will still happen. (The Weird Rise Of Anti-Startups at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xL1MOOD5Ox8&t=720s)
- In November 2023 his biggest problem with Arc was that it is free. He estimated roughly $12 million a year in costs against zero revenue and no clear plan, and proposed paid defaults, a premium subscription or a new affiliate model. (Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Arc Browser at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdopamaLJC8&t=360s; Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Arc Browser at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdopamaLJC8&t=540s)
- He says Notion gets into companies through product-led growth: people use it free for themselves, then bring it to the startups they found or join. The videos, templates and companies built around it act as free marketing. (Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Notion at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aWGviOMQqSw&t=315s; Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Notion at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aWGviOMQqSw&t=270s)
- He reads Airbnb's 2022 redesign as strategy done through product design, with no acquisition or new product. If it owns the start of trip planning, he says Airbnb becomes the gatekeeper of the whole tourism industry and can take a cut of things like airline bookings. (Airbnb Just Hacked The Travel Industry Again at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OwiztfnZwE4&t=135s; Airbnb Just Hacked The Travel Industry Again at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OwiztfnZwE4&t=315s)
- He says the better technology does not win and never has: "Being standardized always beats being cutting-edge." Betamax had better picture and sound, but VHS was an open standard anyone could manufacture and held 90% of the market by 1987. (The Weird Reason Your Tech Stays Broken at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-51ocAD6zBI&t=135s)
- He defends companies that neglect current products to chase the next one. Apple pouring an estimated 20 billion dollars into the Vision Pro while Siri stays bad is in his words not incompetence but the cost of being a tech company. (The Weird Reason Your Tech Stays Broken at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-51ocAD6zBI&t=225s; The Weird Reason Your Tech Stays Broken at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-51ocAD6zBI&t=450s)

# Sources

This skill is Harshit Khemani's reading of public Y Combinator material. Nobody at Y Combinator wrote it, reviewed it or endorsed it, and neither did Paul Graham. The originals are at [ycombinator.com/library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library) and [paulgraham.com](https://paulgraham.com/articles.html). When this file and a piece disagree, the piece is right.

Y Combinator is many people. Every claim in the theme files names the person who said it and ends with `(Title, URL)`, and all three come from the table below. A reply cites a piece as `(Title)` inside the sentence, names the speaker, and lists `Source: Title, URL` under the answer. The gate (`scripts/brain-check.mjs`) reads the first three columns of this table, so a title that is not here cannot be cited, and a piece cannot be cited without one of its people named.

## The corpus

88 pieces, all read on 2026-11-11: 14 essays by Paul Graham, 28 written posts and 46 talks with a published transcript. The last row is the index, which is cited when the answer is silence.

Dates need care, and the Date column says which kind each one is:

- **Printed in the essay.** Paul Graham's essays print their month and year. These are the dates of writing.
- **Library record of the talk.** The date the library page carries for a talk. It is close to the day of the talk, and not always the same: Jared Friedman's talk says it was recorded for Startup School 2020 and its record is dated 2022.
- **Library date.** For a written post, the day the post entered the library. Most posts carry a day in May or June 2020 and were written earlier; the page shows no other date. A reply never calls a library date the year a post was written. The theme files call such a piece an undated post: the page gives no date of writing.

## How it was read

- Every piece was fetched on 2026-11-11 from its original public page, with no login: library pages from `ycombinator.com/library/<id>`, and the 14 essays from `paulgraham.com`. The text on the page was read in full. For a talk that means the transcript the library publishes on the page.
- No video was watched and no slide was seen. Several talks lean on slides (the pricing, SAFE, growth and business-model talks most of all); a number that lived only on a slide is not in the corpus.
- Transcripts have errors: misheard names, swapped speaker labels, a passage out of order. Where a note could not tell who was speaking, the line was left out or given to the conversation and not to a person.
- Each piece was read into one note with every claim tied to words copied from the page, and a script confirmed all 1,789 of those passages occur in the fetched text. The theme lines were then written from the notes with the page text open, and a second reader who had not written them checked lines against the page text. The section below says what that check found.
- Every claim is a paraphrase. The few direct quotes in this skill are under 15 words each and were checked against the page text on 2026-11-11.

## The check against the page text

On 2026-11-12 a second reader, which had not written the lines and was not shown the writer's reasons, checked every claim line in the twelve theme files: 1,202 lines, each read beside the passage of page text it rests on.

- 1,177 lines said what their passage says.
- 25 lines said more and were corrected. In 20 the line had hardened or dropped the speaker's own hedge, or added a detail: "usually" became a rule, "a little wrong" became "wrong", a cause was given that the speaker did not give. In 2 the wrong voice was named: a line credited to one host on a transcript label that the text contradicts, and a view given to "the program". In 3 a limit was dropped, such as "YC's" in front of "top hundred companies".
- No line was removed, and no quote or citation was found wrong.

The same reader then checked this file's What it holds column, [disagreements](disagreements.md), [silent](silent.md) and the gold examples against the page text. It corrected 26 of the 88 lines in the What it holds column, 11 of the 159 cited lines in disagreements, 14 rows of silent and 3 sentences in the examples, nearly all for a dropped hedge or limit, and one name in the Who column. [gotchas](gotchas.md) records the two that changed a rule.

What the check could not settle: who is speaking where a transcript's labels are swapped or missing, since no video was watched; figures that were on a slide; and whether the page text matches the recording.

## Refresh

The corpus is a dated reading. Before answering a question about a piece newer than January 2026, or when the user links a piece that is not in the table:

1. Open `https://www.ycombinator.com/library` and compare it with the table.
2. For each new piece: read the page in full, add its row here with today's date and every speaker in the Who column, then add its claims to the theme files, one line each, in your own words, each naming its speaker.
3. Check [disagreements](disagreements.md) and [silent](silent.md): a new piece can end a silence or start a disagreement.
4. Run `node scripts/brain-check.mjs --self-test`. It fails when a claim cites a title that is not in the table, or names nobody who speaks in the piece it cites.

Use only what is published openly. Nothing behind a login or a payment goes into this file or into any reply.

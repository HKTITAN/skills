# What do we charge, and how do we reach the buyer?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: the files cited here name the Van Westendorp and Gabor-Granger pricing methods and no author, no professor, no course and no source for any figure. A number inside a worked example is that example's number, never a benchmark. These files disagree with each other on whether to start with a low price, on how long a B2B sale takes and on whether omnichannel always wins; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## Which pricing approach?

- Cost-plus pricing adds a markup percentage to production cost, and the file's example is a t-shirt costing $5 that sells at $10 with a 100% markup; the same file lists among its drawbacks that it ignores customer value perception and may overprice or underprice. (Marketing: Three Core Pricing Approaches, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/pricing-three-core-pricing-approaches.md)
- The pricing decision framework says cost-plus ensures profitability, value-based pricing yields the highest margins and competitive pricing matches the market. (Marketing: Decision Framework: Pricing, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/appendix-framew-decision-framework-pricing.md)
- Competition-based pricing sets price on what competitors charge; it works best when products are genuinely similar, price is the primary decision criterion, costs are at parity and brand differentiation is minimal, and its risk is a price war that destroys profitability. (Marketing: Three Core Pricing Approaches, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/pricing-three-core-pricing-approaches.md)
- Value-based pricing sets price on the value the offering creates for customers, not on production cost; in the file's example, software that saves a customer $100K a year in labour is priced at $20K to $40K a year, 20 to 40% of the value created. (Marketing: Three Core Pricing Approaches, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/pricing-three-core-pricing-approaches.md)
- Willingness to pay is the maximum price a customer accepts before choosing an alternative; the file lists six ways to research it: the Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter, conjoint analysis, the Gabor-Granger method, auction or bidding studies, focus groups or interviews, and marketplace testing. (Marketing: Three Core Pricing Approaches, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/pricing-three-core-pricing-approaches.md)
- Price discrimination comes in three degrees: charging each customer the maximum they will pay, offering price and quality bundles that customers select for themselves, and charging different groups different prices. It raises profit by capturing customer surplus and creates backlash when customers feel unfairly treated. (Marketing: Advanced Pricing Strategies, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/pricing-advanced-pricing-strategies.md)
- Dynamic pricing adjusts prices in real time on demand, supply, competition and customer characteristics; the file says hotel peak season rates triple and off-season rates halve, and that Amazon adjusts prices millions of times daily. (Marketing: Advanced Pricing Strategies, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/pricing-advanced-pricing-strategies.md)

## How is the price set, and how do we enter?

- The initial price is set as a hypothesis from the cost floor, the value ceiling and the competitive midpoint, then tested and refined against demand, profit margin and customer feedback. (Marketing: Pricing Strategy Development, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/pricing-pricing-strategy-development.md)
- The pricing development file's key principle is that prices can always be lowered and are much harder to raise, and that it is better to underprice at first and raise gradually than to overprice and destroy demand. (Marketing: Pricing Strategy Development, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/pricing-pricing-strategy-development.md)
- The market entry file lists skimming as a strategy: a high price, limited distribution and early adopters as the target, which maximises short-term profit and builds prestige and is best for innovation and early mover advantage. (Marketing: Market Entry Strategies, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/gtm-market-entry-strategies.md)
- The go-to-market framework has seven steps: define the target customer, develop positioning and the value proposition, set the pricing strategy, plan sales and distribution, plan marketing and awareness, support and enable sales, and set success metrics and KPIs. (Marketing: GTM Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/gtm-gtm-framework.md)
- A launch runs in four phases: build awareness and interest, drive trial and evaluation, scale and expand, then mature and optimise. (Marketing: Launch Sequencing, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/gtm-launch-sequencing.md)

## Which digital channels, and what did they do?

- Organic reach on social media is declining and platforms prioritise paid; the file gives customer acquisition cost on social as $1 to 5 for B2C and $100 or more for B2B. (Marketing: Social Media Marketing, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/digital-social-media-marketing.md)
- The email file gives a cost of $0.01 to 0.10 per email and an ROI of typically 4:1 or better, and lists a good open rate as about 20 to 30%, a good click-through rate as about 2 to 5% and an unsubscribe rate to keep below 0.5%. (Marketing: Email Marketing, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/digital-email-marketing.md)
- Programmatic advertising uses software to buy and optimise digital ads automatically across many sites and apps; the file gives a CPM of $5 to $30 and a CPC of $0.50 to $5, and says retargeting is often 2 to 3x more efficient. (Marketing: Programmatic Advertising, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/digital-programmatic-advertising.md)
- The file lists six attribution models: first-touch, last-touch, linear, time-decay, position-based and data-driven; it calls last-touch the most common but biased. It says different models tell different stories and to use several. (Marketing: Marketing Analytics & Attribution, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/digital-marketing-analytics-attribution.md)

## What changes when the buyer is a business?

- A B2B purchase involves five roles: initiators, users, influencers, decision-makers and buyers; the file says one message will not satisfy all of them and calls for multiple value propositions. (Marketing: Decision-Making Units, DMU, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/b2b-decision-making-units-dmu.md)
- The sales cycle file says enterprise sales cycles often extend 6 to 12 months and gives stage lengths of 1 to 2 months for awareness and education, 2 to 3 for exploration, 2 to 3 for evaluation, 1 to 2 for approval and 1 to 2 for negotiation, with implementation ongoing. (Marketing: Longer B2B Sales Cycles, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/b2b-longer-b2b-sales-cycles.md)
- The B2B characteristics table gives the B2B sales cycle as months or years and the B2C one as days or weeks. (Marketing: Key B2B Characteristics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/b2b-key-b2b-characteristics.md)

## What changes for services and retail?

- Services have four characteristics: intangibility, inseparability, variability and perishability; perishability means capacity cannot be inventoried and unsold capacity is lost for good. (Marketing: IHIP Characteristics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/services-ihip-characteristics.md)
- The file calls the traditional 4Ps insufficient for services and adds People, Physical Evidence and Process; under People it says service quality depends entirely on employees. (Marketing: The 7Ps for Services, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/services-the-7ps-for-services.md)
- The gaps model names five service quality gaps: knowledge, standards, performance, communication and perceived service; the file says managing all five ensures quality. (Marketing: Gaps Model, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/services-gaps-model.md)
- The retail section opener says the future is not e-commerce against physical retail but seamless omnichannel integration. (Marketing: Retail & Omnichannel Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/retail-retail-omnichannel-strategy.md)
- The category splits file says pure e-commerce wins in commodities where comparison and price matter, pure physical wins in try-before-you-buy categories, and omnichannel wins in most mainstream categories; its example is Warby Parker, primarily DTC online with physical try-on studios. (Marketing: The Reality: Category Splits, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/retail-the-reality-category-splits.md)
- Buy online, pick up in store means the customer orders online and collects at the nearest store within hours, which reduces shipping costs, speeds delivery and drives store traffic. (Marketing: Brick-and-Click Integration, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/retail-brick-and-click-integration.md)

## Which numbers say it is working?

- Customer acquisition cost is total marketing and sales spend divided by the number of customers acquired; the file's example is $100K of spend and 50 customers, a CAC of $2,000. (Marketing: Financial Metrics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/formulas-financial-metrics.md)
- The file's first formula for customer lifetime value is average order value times purchase frequency times customer lifespan, less CAC; its example is $50 times 24 a year times 3 years less $1,200, a lifetime value of $2,400. (Marketing: Financial Metrics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/formulas-financial-metrics.md)
- A healthy payback period is given as under 12 months for SaaS and under 6 months for e-commerce, and a healthy LTV to CAC ratio as 3:1 or better. (Marketing: Financial Metrics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/formulas-financial-metrics.md)
- Churn is customers lost in a period divided by customers at its start, times 100; the file states that 5% monthly churn equals 60% annual churn. (Marketing: Financial Metrics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/formulas-financial-metrics.md)
- Return on ad spend is revenue from ads divided by ad spend, and the file's example is $4,000 of revenue on $1,000 of spend, a ROAS of 4:1; for Net Promoter Score it lists above 50 as excellent, 0 to 50 as good and below 0 as concerning. (Marketing: Marketing Metrics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/formulas-marketing-metrics.md)

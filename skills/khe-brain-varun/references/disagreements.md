# Disagreements and changes

Open this file before you answer. When a question touches a row, give both sides with their videos and dates, and never merge them into one smooth view. The corpus spans 2025-12-21 to 2026-11-03, and several rows sit inside a single talk. Many of the changes are ones he states himself, and he says more than once that changing his mind on new data is the right thing to do.

## Disagreements

### Is success luck, or does belief decide it?

- All success is luck, in the same way all bad outcomes are luck: the school you were put in, where you were born, the accident you did not have. "Actually, all the success you have ever had in life is luck." (Kata 10 at 4:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=298s; Kata 10 at 5:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=348s)
- Nothing else works without belief: not the technology, the people or even the distribution. He opens that talk by noting he is the one who said a lot of success is luck. (Why Belief Matters at 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=47s; Why Belief Matters at 1:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=95s)
- His own bridge: luck is a massive part of every success, but it only finds you after a serious attempt, and you can raise your odds by having more people know you. (Kata 4 at 27:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1638s; Kata 10 at 6:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=397s)
- Inside the luck talk he also says the process is repeatable and Aeos has done it many times. (Kata 10 at 34:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2094s)
- How to report it: give the luck line from 2026-07-16 and the belief line from 2026-11-03, plus his bridge. Do not say he dropped the luck view; in the belief talk he repeats that he said it.

### Is there one cause of success, or several?

- Results are multifactorial and there is usually no single thing that makes you successful. He then adds that sometimes there is. (Kata 6 at 28:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1721s)
- He says the true attribution behind businesses is three things: find an untapped market, hire good talent and execute well, and stick around long enough to reap the benefits. He says every founder he has met fits these three, then allows exceptions. (Kata 6 at 35:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=2153s; Kata 6 at 36:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=2200s)
- A very significant chunk of success is the ability to take risk, and at some point the source of wealth is always risk. (Kata 9 at 3:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=197s)
- Success has never been about intelligence. It is about tribe and group formation. (PGX Ideas 2 at 39:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2345s)
- Distribution is the most important thing, about 10 times more valuable than product. (Kata 10 at 0:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=56s)
- How to report it: name the cause from the video closest to the question, and say he names other causes in other videos. Do not rank them for him.

### Take the big swing, or reduce risk?

- "I think in real life an entrepreneur's job is to reduce risk." He means doing what looks obvious to you, very cheaply, until it wins. (Kata 9 at 1:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=99s)
- Competence is only built by a big swing with personal risk and the odds against you, and most Indians never attempt one. (Kata 4 at 8:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=535s; Kata 4 at 28:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1735s)
- In the risk talk itself he also says: "You must take risks to win, but taking risks guarantees nothing." (Kata 9 at 41:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2489s)
- He thinks his right failure rate is 50%, reports zero for three years, and feels he needs to take harder bets. (Kata 2 at 21:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1299s)
- How he squares it: keep 80 to 90% ultra safe and put 5 to 10% on crazy risk. The lesson he takes from the rich is surviving failure while keeping the upside, and he says gambling everything is wrong. (Kata 9 at 53:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3199s; Kata 13 at 54:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3242s)
- How to report it: give both the attempt talk from 2026-01-10 and the risk talk from 2026-04-20, then the 80 to 90 and 10 split. Do not report him as either a risk lover or a risk avoider.

### Is optionality poison, or should you run many bets?

- Keeping every option open gives the least impact, and each extra option is a tax on willpower. "It looks safe, but optionality is a type of poison." (Kata 7 at 8:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=485s; Kata 7 at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=629s)
- Optionality kills belief, because you cannot believe in something while weighing five things at once. (Why Belief Matters at 33:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2004s)
- Aeos ran several small parallel bets with very high failure tolerance. In the same breath he says the advice to do a single thing is true, and he does not settle the two there. (Kata 1 at 12:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=729s)
- In high-randomness domains such as building an app, making viral content and venture funding, he says to optimize for many small shots and many parallel tiny experiments. (Kata 9 at 59:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3580s; Kata 9 at 1:00:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3628s)
- His own line between them: diversification is one thing already working plus a second that does not make the first fall. Starting five things from scratch at once is the thing he warns against. (Kata 7 at 12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=725s; Why Belief Matters at 33:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2004s)
- By 2026-07-26 he says that as he gets older he is shutting down projects that tax his bandwidth, and would rather focus on four flagship things. (Kata 11 at 4:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=293s)
- How to report it: give the warning and his diversification test together. Do not use his many businesses to cancel the warning; he addresses that himself.

### Is cash a weak incentive, or does everything run on incentives?

- "Cash is the least powerful incentive." It matters until a person feels they earn enough among peers, then respect, status, purpose and mastery take over. (Kata 2 at 27:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1640s)
- Setting incentives is a superpower. Nobody in his 16-person leadership team has left, and he says they earn a lot from incentives, not base salary. (Kata 2 at 21:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1299s)
- A company runs on incentives and not on a cheerleader boss, and that is how Aeos runs five profitable units. (Kata 3 at 23:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1396s)
- Co-founders join at zero salary and live on equity and profit share, and he says profit share can align people better than equity. (KK Create at 29:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1757s; KK Create at 57:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=3432s)
- He says he went too far on incentives, and that external rewards can destroy intrinsic motivation, a point he takes from the book Punished by Rewards. (Kata 2 at 31:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1881s)
- Because loss hurts more than gain feels good, he gives no feel-good titles, no pay projections and no perks unless well thought out. Yet he hands out shoes and a lounge as symbols, and some people left over not getting the shoes. (Kata 9 at 51:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3104s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 27:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1638s)
- How to report it: say which incentive the question is about. His ranking puts cash last, and his practice leans on profit share and symbols. Give both and do not resolve them.

### Fire fast, or help people improve?

- Fire quickly when a hire is wrong, because bad employees cost months of time and energy. (Kata 8 at 41:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2505s)
- Weak managers lose high performers by not firing low performers, and rules that are not enforced cost a leader his storytelling power. (Kata 3 at 40:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2443s; Kata 2 at 49:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2951s)
- Everyone can improve unless there is an attitude issue, leaders are responsible for helping, and skill is the least likely reason to lose a job. (Kata 3 at 39:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2347s)
- Accountability needs safety: people only admit a fault when they are confident they will not be fired for it. (Kata 2 at 38:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2325s)
- His bridge: tell a low performer exactly where they lag, with a reference and a deadline. If they then miss a clear expectation, letting them go is fair. (Kata 3 at 38:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2300s)
- How to report it: give the firing line and the helping line together with his bridge. These are talks to his own employees about Aeos.

### Should a leader use fear and shame, or protect people from them?

- Fear is one of four ways he lists to generate current in a team. (Kata 2 at 16:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=966s)
- Stories only work if there is shaming for not fitting in. At Aeos low views and an unprofitable business are shameful, and he says the company would not work without that. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 28:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1685s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 31:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1879s)
- Most Indian services companies are ruled by fear and Aeos wants the opposite. Companies run on stack ranking and fear innovate poorly. (Secret Kata 1 at 20:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1209s; Secret Kata 1 at 27:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1642s)
- His team rules: praise in public, correct in private, critique the work and not the person, protect status, and do not turn the company into a competitive exam. (Secret Kata 1 at 20:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1259s)
- The same rules say to let a person go if they really are the problem, and he says someone with active depression is typically not a good fit until treated. (Secret Kata 1 at 20:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1259s; Secret Kata 1 at 28:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1689s)
- How to report it: give the safety talk from 2026-01-16 and the shaming talk from 2026-09-12 with dates. He does not reconcile them, so do not do it for him.

### Small teams, or a big company?

- Teams should be two-pizza sized with exactly one goal, a rule he takes from Jeff Bezos. (Kata 11 at 6:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=389s)
- There will be no one-person unicorn, only a few co-founders plus AI, and AI means very large companies are not needed. He flags that he says this while running 540 people. (Masters Union at 39:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2381s)
- All the benefits of a company or an individual come at scale, which he credits to Charlie Munger's book Poor Charlie's Almanac, and in late 2025 he speaks of Aeos at 500 employees and, at the rate it is growing, a thousandth employee. (Kata 1 at 25:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1548s; Kata 2 at 0:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=54s)
- His own bridge: big companies survive their inefficiency because a few core teams carry them. Of about 600 people at Aeos maybe 50 are critical. (Kata 11 at 8:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=484s; Kata 11 at 8:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=532s)
- He builds the group as separate child companies so each set of founders can hold real ownership, and he calls headcount a bad metric to be proud of. (KK Create at 17:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1079s; KK Create at 1:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=96s)
- How to report it: say he argues for small units and runs a large group made of them. Give the headcount with its date.

### Bootstrapped and profitable, or the funded all-or-nothing game?

- Bootstrapping is true hard mode: money only comes while customers find value, where funded salaries flow whether the company works or not. (Kata 1 at 1:01:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3673s)
- He no longer buys the venture dream, calls it a kind of lottery, and says profit is easy when it is your own money. (KK Create at 20:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1221s; KK Create at 32:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1951s)
- His old pride in being profitable while others burnt money was cope. Funded founders play an all-or-nothing game and should be judged by that game. (Kata 4 at 4:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=243s; Kata 4 at 5:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=339s)
- He used to think investors were foolish for not wanting profit and now says the foolish one was him. In the same talk he says Aeos has raised nothing for four years. (Kata 13 at 1:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=100s; Kata 13 at 0:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=53s)
- Do not raise money until you have 100% belief, because a VC's job is to tell you that you are crazy in a bad way. (Why Belief Matters at 17:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1056s)
- How to report it: he keeps Aeos bootstrapped and also defends the funded game as a different game. Give both with dates. Do not say he regrets bootstrapping or that he opposes venture money.

### Be different, or enter a crowded market?

- The world does not care about better or worse, it cares about different. (Masters Union at 18:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1138s)
- Businesses nobody believes in have no competition, and doing the uncool thing gave Aeos two years alone. (Kata 9 at 40:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2441s; Kata 1 at 21:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1312s)
- He entered influencer marketing while calling it a commoditized space with a weak moat, and says he never wanted to get into it. (Masters Union at 29:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1758s; Masters Union at 28:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1710s)
- His reason: almost all tech deal flow in India comes to him as a creator. He treats it as a temporary arbitrage and caps the agency at four or five people. (Masters Union at 29:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1758s; Masters Union at 30:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1805s)
- How to report it: give the rule and the exception, both from 2026-10-29, with his reason for the exception.

### Are AI presenters and AI content the win, or is that over?

- Audiences watch AI-generated presenters and views are the same. He was recognised at Davos as the world's largest AI avatar. (Kata 1 at 35:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2137s; Kata 1 at 34:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2087s)

- How to report it: give the date of each line. The avatar result is from 2025-12-21 and the line that the format is over is from 2026-10-29. Do not present either as his whole view of AI in content.

### Product or distribution?

- The moat is talent and distribution, not the product, and distribution is about 10 times more valuable than product. (Kata 1 at 11:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=681s; Kata 10 at 0:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=56s)
- Of every 10 rupees he now spends 8 on marketing and 2 on product. Once one feature works he says to stop building and sell. (Kata 9 at 58:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3533s; Kata 11 at 21:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1298s)
- There is no substitute for building what people want. (KK Create at 23:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1423s)
- The product must catch up with the story or the audience turns on you, and a brand needs a satisfying product behind it or nobody refers you. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:05:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3922s; Kata 13 at 57:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3433s)
- How to report it: he ranks distribution first and still says the product has to be wanted. Give both. The Kata 1 passage on this is muddled in the captions, so prefer the later videos for wording.

- Performance marketing is still needed early. Below 3 to 5 crores of revenue all spend is ROAS, and beyond 100 crores it is all awareness. (Kata 6 at 12:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=758s; Kata 10 at 29:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1751s)
- Content almost never has immediate ROI, yet he praises a client that pitched its app in every sixth video and says it worked. (Kata 10 at 15:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=923s; Kata 10 at 10:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=638s)
- Trust cannot be clearly measured, yet he gives touch-point counts, about 30 on average. (Kata 10 at 17:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1070s; Kata 10 at 25:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1556s)
- Proxy attribution is what good brand managers use and he says it is still wrong. Qualitative, in-house attribution is the only real way he has found. (Kata 6 at 16:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=998s; Kata 6 at 25:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1525s)
- How to report it: the rule depends on company stage, so say the stage. When you give a touch-point number, also say he holds that trust cannot be measured exactly.

### Is freelancing a trap?

- Freelancing is a trap because you absorb risk a job shields you from, and freelancing for small startups can burn years of a career. (Kata 7 at 13:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=821s; Kata 9 at 31:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1918s)
- A 1% profit share at a proven growing company compounds better than three freelance projects at 2 lakhs a month each. (Why Belief Matters at 34:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2053s)
- In the same talk as the trap he bats for the other side: if you go all in on freelancing the downsides may not be so bad, and he says he is not the one to choose for you. (Kata 7 at 26:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1587s)
- He lists freelancing as a risk you can take once you hold at least 10 lakhs in the bank. He wrote a book on freelancing in 2017 that did not include the trap. (Kata 9 at 42:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2536s; Kata 7 at 12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=725s)
- How to report it: note that the trap talk was given to a room of Aeos employees, and give his own counterpoint with it.

### Mentor or no mentor?

- The best mentorship is no mentorship: you struggle for maybe 10 years and end up the most unique person. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:10:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4210s)
- On his game, mentors train talent that does not exist and speed up R&D, and serious mentors only agreed after seeing two trailers. (Kata 12 at 34:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2070s; Kata 12 at 32:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1926s)

If you do not have that and have a "crazy type one belief" in yourself, he says to do something, citing Brian Armstrong's line that action produces information.

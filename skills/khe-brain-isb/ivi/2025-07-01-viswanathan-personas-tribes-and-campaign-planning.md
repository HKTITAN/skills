# Personas, tribes and campaign planning, Madhu Viswanathan

Madhu Viswanathan taught this class on the promotion part of marketing, the day after his value pricing class. The speaker is not named in this recording and is identified by matching it to another recording of the same class. He contrasted personas with tribes, asked for journey maps built on touchpoints, walked through his own campaign planning framework and a content framework, and closed with a simple model of digital communication. He said he was short of time and left handouts for the rest. The file also carries a classmate's written notes, used below only where marked.

## What was said

### Personas and tribes

- Madhu Viswanathan says personas are great for targeting people at the point of sale.
- He says a persona is built from individual data, by observing people and deciding how they should be clubbed together.
- He says a tribe is based on community affiliation, a network of shared interests and beliefs, so you do not need to observe a member's behaviour to know what ideas they hold.
- He says tribes have a common language and a hierarchy of leaders and followers.
- He says a person belongs to only one persona but can belong to many tribes at once. His example is being a father and an academic at the same time.
- He says the tribe view suits today's world because it allows engagement, while the persona view is great for advertising.
- He says posts that touch a tribe's shared interests get a lot of engagement, and that "If you want real engagement, you have to start talking the real stuff".
- He says a persona has to end in action: what to communicate and where to communicate.
- He says the action usually takes the form of a channel mix. In his own example it was print, YouTube and dealer visits.
- Answering a founder who raised lookalike audiences, he says first party customer data, if you have enough of it, can be sent to Facebook or Instagram.

### Brand as an asset

- He says most assets on a company's books depreciate, while advertising is an appreciating asset.
- He says a month of ads that brings no new customer is not what brand means, and points to Tata Motors, whose name carries weight with truckers.

### Journey mapping

- He says a decision journey map must be built on touchpoints.
- He lists the stages as trigger, search, consideration, purchase, onboarding and advocacy.

### The campaign planning framework

- He tells the class to pick one planning framework and stick with it.
- He presents his own framework, which he calls Compass: context, objective, method, plan, action, surveillance and scrutiny.
- He says context means working out your core persona, your capabilities and a SWOT, in the spirit of the five Cs of marketing.
- He says objectives are set with both OKRs and SMART goals, and that this forces very specific KPIs.
- He says method is where segmentation, targeting and positioning are decided.
- His example of the plan step runs from LinkedIn carousels to a landing page at the bottom of the funnel.
- He says action, the implementation, is the easiest part, and that people to implement can be found inside and outside the company, including college students.
- He says surveillance means choosing KPIs that show what is going on day to day, like valves that give readings in a plant.
- He says scrutiny happens after the campaign, when you evaluate what worked and what did not and decide what to carry forward.
- He says the personas a startup begins with are very noisy, so after a campaign it should check whether the people who came fit the persona or form a different one.
- He says campaigns used to be one shot, while today they are continuous and build on each other through a constant feedback loop.

### Content

- He lists the steps of his content framework as conceptualise, research, engineer, activate, track and evaluate.
- He says the target audience for your content may not be your customers. His example is posting on LinkedIn to be seen as an expert by people who are not the buyers.
- He says knowing who the audience is changes the content you deliver.
- He says you cannot be a one stop shop in content, because people who see random posts do not know what to expect and move on.
- He says a particular channel is usually used for a particular audience.
- He says "regularity is more important than frequency". Posting does not have to be every day.

### Performance and brand spending

- He says that in the short term a startup has to perform, so the split should be close to 50-50 or tilted further towards performance marketing.
- He says performance marketing is most effective in the initial phases, when each rupee of advertising gives the biggest return.
- He sets an exercise on which ads are working and where to cut money, and allows the assumption that 2% of clicks lead to a sale.

### A model of digital communication

- He describes four activities: listening to social chatter, influencing conversations between consumers, creating and distributing content, and firm initiated advertising.
- He says listening covers platforms such as Facebook, Twitter, Reddit and Discord, and that listening alone is not enough.
- He calls creating and distributing content inbound marketing.
- He calls firm initiated advertising outbound marketing, and says this traditional part is now only a small part of the whole.

## Examples and cases used

- Under Armour: its announcement of Gisele as brand ambassador drew replies that split into camps, some saying she did not belong in sports and others seeing her as a symbol for women, which he uses to show tribes forming around a shared interest.
- Dove: its real beauty campaign gets a lot of engagement because it touches a shared interest.
- Sugar Cosmetics: speaks in a very Gen Z language, as an example of a tribe's common language.
- Coca-Cola: iconic in the United States through its association with soldiers in World War II.
- Tata Motors: brand value among truckers as an example of advertising as an appreciating asset.

## Numbers given

- 50-50: the split between performance and brand spending he says an early startup should be close to, if not tilted towards performance.
- 2%: the share of clicks leading to a sale that he allows as an assumption in his ad ROI exercise.

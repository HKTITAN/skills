# AI and agents

AI and agents are the largest subject in his posts and the frame he uses for most of the others. Since mid-2025 his settled view is that AI upgrades the builder instead of replacing them, that the scarce inputs are now context, judgment and taste, and that software has two users: people and their agents. He argues that agents get useful through skills, CLIs, MCP and explicit constraints rather than longer prompts, and that computing is moving from apps and screens toward intent, ambient context and glasses. His 2022 to 2024 posts are earlier excitement about ChatGPT, no-code and AI note tools.

## Will AI take jobs or kill software?

- He agrees with Dharmesh Shah that AI will transform software rather than kill it, and treats AI as a platform shift on par with mobile or the web, one that should be woven into a product, not bolted on as a chatbot. (2025-08-18-ai-won-t-kill, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1957297002863268302; 2025-08-18-if-re-building-software, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7363061889720836097)
- He says AI upgrades the tool, not the builder: work moves up a level to judgment and leverage, like going from a shovel to a bulldozer (a Bezos line he relays). "we are not replacing the builder." (2026-05-21-cleanest-ai-take-heard, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2057340873587990841)
- He describes coding as a translation layer between human intent and the computer, like human translators before translation apps; LLMs automated the translation, but deciding what to build, why, the tradeoffs and the reliability is still engineering. "the interface changed. the underlying skill didn't." (2026-07-21-2-decades-ago-if, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2079631736724697271; 2026-09-05-more-use-llms-more, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2096106671848517816)
- He says the bottleneck moved from typing to deciding, and that most feeling stuck is a thinking problem dressed as a coding problem. (2026-06-01-gap-between-people-who, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061415603920044197)
- Relaying a Dan Shipper episode, he agrees there is no AI job apocalypse, because creativity that stands out from commoditized output gains value; his advice to stay relevant is to use a coding agent daily on real work and test each new model on actual problems. "Ride the models." (2026-05-25-dan-shipper-ceo-every, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2058800680324989265)
- He says delegating labor to AI frees time for cerebral work, which creates more value, and tells people to hand AI the pockets of time filled by repetitive tasks. (2026-09-13-more-labor-intensive-work, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2099108726611521807; 2026-09-12-introduced-team-bot-now, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2098682105249292484)
- He says AI does make people more productive; in a thread about AI-made games he said the claim was productivity, not that AI-made games are good by default, and he says AI multiplied his own creative output while he kept every creative decision. "I used AI as a tool, and not a driver." ## How should you work with a coding agent?

- After 15 days in Claude Code he told people to stop writing better prompts and start writing skills, because a skill helps every later session. "stop prompt-engineering. start skill-engineering." (2026-06-01-gap-between-people-who, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061415603920044197)
- From the same post: make the agent show a plan before it writes code; he throws out the first plan about half the time and calls this the highest-leverage button. "two minutes reading a plan beats two hours unwinding the wrong build." (2026-06-01-gap-between-people-who, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061415603920044197)
- Ask for one function with its signature and an edge case, not for the whole feature at once, which returns mush. "one ask = one function." (2026-06-01-gap-between-people-who, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061415603920044197)
- Read the diff rather than the agent's confident summary, because the bug lives in the code. "read the diff, not the summary." (2026-06-01-gap-between-people-who, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061415603920044197)
- Keep rules files short, since long ones get ignored, and move heavy logic into skills that load when needed; installed skills do not fire reliably, so call them by name or force them with a hook. "do not trust. enforce." (2026-06-01-gap-between-people-who, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061415603920044197)
- He says the skill is shifting from prompt engineering to "manager engineering": describing the destination, taste and constraints as you would to a person. He adds that a model's output reflects how well you describe the task, and that agents mostly reflect the person using them. (2026-06-11-50-hours-fable-5, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2065110652935659695; 2026-07-19-mikenevermiss-models-often-reflect, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2078708559668523247; 2026-06-13-an0n-an0n-an0n-ak48503, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2065842203247919356)
- After 50 hours with Fable 5 he wrote that small tasks waste a big model, and that it shines on large, vague objectives that need research and judgment. "like bringing a bazooka to kill an ant." (2026-06-11-50-hours-fable-5, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2065110652935659695)
- He says agent freedom works only when the constraints are explicit enough for the agent to stay inside them. (2026-10-20-shanehegde-freedom-only-works, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2112564341652463809)
- He calls dynamic workflows in Claude Code spec-driven development on steroids: the plan moves into code, agents execute, verify and replan, and fanout plus verification makes results harder to fool, at the cost of heavy token use. "structured recovery > blind improvisation." (2026-05-28-dynamic-workflows-claude-code, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2060078660275884236; 2026-05-28-ebogomolovs-spec-driven-development, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2060080445245845542)
- He says a bot's setup description is only starting context and the bot learns how you work over time; he also tells people to write their own SOUL.md style file for an agent. (2026-09-03-ketansahu-bot-dont-think, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2095505326988833142; 2026-02-18-whosaakash-openclaw-write-your, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2024187382556479863)
- On context files he says markdown beats plain text because LLMs know it natively and headings give a hierarchy for priority, while unstructured text or one undivided file burns more tokens. (2026-09-01-hadikhantech-markdown-allows-hierarchy, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2094650493771661405; 2026-09-01-hadikhantech-grok-llms-consciously, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2094683660045988245; 2026-09-01-hadikhantech-grok-itd-burn, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2094669401815715889)
- He says skills become a context problem when a whole skill sits in the window, and proposes splitting them into small linked nodes routed through skill-graphs so only the relevant parts load; in one reply he asked whether a use case needed a new model at all, or only skills. (2026-05-09-think-they-might-more, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2053069079099916793; 2026-07-05-aronprins-thsottiaux-do-really, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2073590283330318588)

## What do agents still lack?

- He says the biggest gap is a team's tribal knowledge: agents can parse current documents but not why past decisions were made, unless someone tells them. "the biggest missing dataset for ai agents is your team's tribal knowledge." - He repeats that context is the key factor, and says agents need context about the person, such as sleep, stress and activity, beside conversation history; he bought a fitness band to combine with Omi and Poke for this. (2026-08-18-scobleizer-katies-context-key, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2089533645505831366; 2026-01-17-kodjima33-rewindai-context-matters, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2012462229455540257; 2026-05-20-got-fitness-band-not, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056941581647949898)
- He wants agents to have identities of their own: their own profiles inside tools like ClickUp instead of access to his account, and a password manager like Dashlane built for agents. (2026-08-03-clickupcrew-no-but-way, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2084240539134877927; 2026-09-03-need-dashlane-agents, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2095310351160181155)
- He says agents work better with tools that are deterministic, scoped, observable, retryable and composable, which is why coding agents keep converging on git, gh, docker and vercel; they also need named local URLs instead of ports. ## What comes after screens and apps?

- In January 2026 he wrote that every big computing shift was obvious before it was practical, and that the next one cannot be prototyped well because hardware has not caught up; XR is stuck there because about ten infrastructure layers must mature together. "until then, we get demos, not defaults." (2026-01-01-next-evolution-computing-obvious, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2006801057591312689)
- From the same post: XR is about aligning computation with the physical world, not immersion; headsets are its least interesting part; screens are a stopgap on the way to spatial, ambient, gestural and neural computing; and XR without AI is only spatial graphics, because AI gives places a memory of what happened there.

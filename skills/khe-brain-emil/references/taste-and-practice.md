# Taste and practice

## What taste is

- When a category matures, working is the baseline. Early car buyers compared a car with a horse; later ones compared cars. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- AI makes working software common, so function no longer separates products. Brand, design, intuitiveness and the whole experience do. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- Taste is not personal preference. It is a trained instinct for seeing past the obvious to what makes something better. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- Nearly every taste call has a logical reason once you look closely, and that holds across disciplines. (Agents with Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste)
- People choose tools for the whole experience and not only the features, which is why companies hire design engineers. (Great Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)

## How it is trained

- Surround yourself with great work: find respected people in the field, see whom they admire, and build a list of tastemakers to study. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- Ask why. Never stop at "good" or "bad" or at gut feeling; explain why one interaction feels right and look for the pattern. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- Study apps instead of only using them; Brian Lovin's App Dissection is his model. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- Practice by making things. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- Critique from the right person speeds progress more than trial and error alone. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- Early work falls short, and noticing that means your taste is ahead of your skill. Keep going. (Developing Taste, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)
- He once believed animation was something you either get or do not. He now holds that a handful of concrete techniques gets most of the way. (7 Practical Animation Tips, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)
- Inspect animations you admire in dev tools and rebuild them. (CSS Transforms, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/css-transforms)

## How he reviews his own work

- Play an animation frame by frame or in slow motion; mismatched properties show up only there. (Good vs Great Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/good-vs-great-animations)
- Look again the next day. (Great Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)
- Getting a motion to feel right takes trial and error and patience. (Great Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)
- A motion should suit the product it is in: Sonner's toast is slower and uses `ease` because that fits its look and its name. (Great Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)
- His recreation of Family's drawer works because the opacity change is coordinated with the height change. (Great Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)
- Thresholds are tuned by hand and then kept: Sonner's swipe velocity came from trial and error. (Building a Toast Component, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-toast-component)

## Details nobody notices

- Small details add up to the feel, even when no user notices any single one. (The Magic of Clip Path, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/the-magic-of-clip-path)
- The same point, with Paul Graham's "Hackers and Painters" as the reference. (7 Practical Animation Tips, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)
- A user who does not notice a detail is the sign it works. (Building a Toast Component, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-toast-component)
- Users expect native behavior, so the aim is that features work the way people assume without thinking. (Building a Drawer Component, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-drawer-component)

## Friction

- Friction is usually treated as bad, and he calls that view mostly right. (Friction as a Feature, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/friction-as-a-feature)
- Its second job: when code was costly, the effort of building filtered which ideas got built. (Friction as a Feature, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/friction-as-a-feature)
- AI removed that cost, and the bar for what is worth building fell with it. (Friction as a Feature, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/friction-as-a-feature)
- Cheap building is also a way to think: build option A and option B and compare them. (Friction as a Feature, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/friction-as-a-feature)
- That holds only if you then choose. With no effort involved, nothing stops you shipping both. (Friction as a Feature, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/friction-as-a-feature)
- Vibe-coded apps and UI libraries are disliked because little thought went into them; they exist without feeling designed. (Friction as a Feature, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/friction-as-a-feature)
- Keep some friction on purpose: it forces judgment and filters out ideas that do not deserve to be built. (Friction as a Feature, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/friction-as-a-feature)

# Who is the customer, and what does the brand stand for?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: of the files cited here, one credits Keller in its title and one names the Van Westendorp and Gabor-Granger pricing methods, and they name no other author, no professor, no course and no source for any figure. A company named in a line is that file's own example. Two of these files list different tests for judging a segment; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## What does the situation look like before strategy?

- The 5Cs are Company, Customers, Competitors, Collaborators and Context, and the file places this structured analysis before strategy is developed. (Marketing: The 5Cs Framework: Situational Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-the-5cs-framework-situational-analysis.md)
- Customer analysis asks who, what, why, how, when and where; the file's example is Netflix finding that customers valued convenience and selection over perfect picture quality, which shaped its value proposition against Blockbuster. (Marketing: The 5Cs Framework: Situational Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-the-5cs-framework-situational-analysis.md)
- Competitors include indirect ones that solve the same customer problem differently, and the file's example is Uber competing with taxis, rental cars, public transit, biking and walking; the goal is to find defensible differentiation, never to copy. (Marketing: The 5Cs Framework: Situational Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-the-5cs-framework-situational-analysis.md)
- Context is the macro environment read through PESTLE: political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental forces, which the file calls largely uncontrollable. (Marketing: The 5Cs Framework: Situational Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-the-5cs-framework-situational-analysis.md)

## Which customers do we serve?

- Segmentation divides a heterogeneous market into distinct groups; the file lists geographic, demographic, psychographic, behavioural and needs-based variables and calls needs-based often the most actionable for marketing. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)
- Good segments share five traits: distinct, measurable, accessible, actionable and stable. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)
- The file lists four targeting strategies: undifferentiated, differentiated, focused and micro-targeting; its focused example is Tesla starting in the premium and luxury electric vehicle segment. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)
- The STP file judges a segment on seven criteria: size, growth, competition, fit, profitability, strategic fit and barriers to entry. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)
- The segmentation research file judges a segment on five: size and growth potential, competitive intensity, strategic fit, profitability and barriers to entry; it also lists value-based segmentation, by willingness to pay per segment, as a method. (Marketing: Market Segmentation Research, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/research-market-segmentation-research.md)

## What position do we hold?

- Positioning is how the brand is perceived relative to competitors in target customers' minds: what customers believe from their total experience, not what the brand says about itself. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)
- Points of parity are attributes customers expect from any credible brand and they vary by segment; points of difference are unique, valued attributes that competitors do not deliver or cannot easily replicate. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)
- A strong point of difference is relevant, distinctive, defensible, believable and communicable. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)
- Credible positioning needs product, price, distribution and promotion to align; the file says luxury goods sold in discount channels damage positioning. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)
- The file calls repositioning risky and expensive, says it requires real change and not just a change of messaging, and gives 2 to 3 years as the minimum time. (Marketing: STP Framework: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/foundations-stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning.md)

## How do customers decide?

- The journey file lists five stages: awareness, consideration, decision, retention and advocacy; mapping the journey shows where customers drop off, and that is where to focus improvement. (Marketing: Customer Journey: Awareness to Loyalty, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/consumer-customer-journey-awareness-to-loyalty.md)
- The file names four journey archetypes: Routine Purchase for low-involvement habitual decisions, Joyride for impulsive purchases on emotional appeal, Trek for purposeful moderate-involvement decisions, and Odyssey for complex high-involvement decisions over an extended time. (Marketing: Journey Archetypes, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/consumer-journey-archetypes.md)
- Consumers use mental shortcuts that often work but sometimes create errors; the file lists availability bias, anchoring, social proof, loss aversion and the sunk cost fallacy. Its marketing uses are social proof to overcome doubt, the original price shown before a discount, free trials and 30-day guarantees to reduce perceived loss, and frequent communication. (Marketing: Behavioral Biases, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/consumer-behavioral-biases.md)
- Attitudes form through direct experience, social influence, media and marketing, and belief updating; the file says negative attitudes are hard to change because of confirmation bias and belief persistence, and that prevention is easier than repair. (Marketing: Attitude Formation and Change, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/consumer-attitude-formation-and-change.md)

## What is the brand worth, and how is it kept?

- Customer-based brand equity is the differential effect of brand knowledge on customer response to marketing; the file's title credits Keller, and its example is a bottle labelled Evian costing 5x more than identical water from an unlabelled bottle. (Marketing: Keller's Customer-Based Brand Equity Model, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/brand-kellers-customer-based-brand-equity-model.md)
- The file's brand pyramid has four levels, built from foundation to apex: salience, then performance and imagery, then judgments and feelings, then resonance; it calls salience necessary but not sufficient for high equity. (Marketing: Keller's Customer-Based Brand Equity Model, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/brand-kellers-customer-based-brand-equity-model.md)
- Equity is built through clear and consistent positioning, performance excellence, emotional connection, integrated marketing and innovation. It is protected by maintaining quality, extending only into logical categories, managing brand associations, defending reputation and investing continuously. (Marketing: Building and Protecting Brand Equity, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/brand-building-and-protecting-brand-equity.md)
- The file lists four brand architectures with its own examples: House of Brands, as with Procter & Gamble's Tide, Crest and Dawn; Branded House, as with Apple, Virgin and Microsoft; Endorsed Brands, as with Courtyard by Marriott; and Sub-brands, as with BMW 3 Series. (Marketing: Brand Architecture Decisions, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/brand-brand-architecture-decisions.md)
- Loyalty is called the strongest measure of brand equity; behavioural loyalty is driven by habit, switching costs or lack of alternatives, and attitudinal loyalty rests on positive experiences, emotional connection and values alignment. (Marketing: Brand Loyalty, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/brand-brand-loyalty.md)
- Net Promoter Score asks customers to rate on a 0 to 10 scale whether they would recommend, and equals the percentage of promoters, who answer 9 or 10, less the percentage of detractors, who answer 0 to 6; it ranges from -100 to +100. (Marketing: Brand Loyalty, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/brand-brand-loyalty.md)

## How is any of this researched?

- The research methods file says to start with qualitative work to understand the landscape, then use quantitative methods to validate and measure at scale. (Marketing: Research Methods, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/research-research-methods.md)
- Brand equity is measured with quantitative metrics, which the file lists as brand awareness, perceived quality, brand associations, loyalty metrics, price premium and brand valuation, and with qualitative insights from brand audits, focus groups, customer interviews and social listening. (Marketing: Measuring Brand Equity, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/brand-measuring-brand-equity.md)
- The competitive intelligence file lists five methods: SEC filings and financial analysis, customer review sites, mystery shopping, competitor website and content analysis, and industry reports and analyst coverage. (Marketing: Competitive Intelligence, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/marketing/rules/research-competitive-intelligence.md)

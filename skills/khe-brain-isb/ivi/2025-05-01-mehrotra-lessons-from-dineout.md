# Lessons from building Dineout, Ankit Mehrotra

Typed notes from a session with Ankit Mehrotra, co-founder of Dineout, which was acquired by Swiggy. A former investment banker, he told the story of the company from 2011 and what he would repeat and avoid.

## What was said

### Starting and talking to customers

- Ankit Mehrotra says the four co-founders had known each other since nursery school and came back to India to build a startup.
- He says that in 2011 they spent a year on research on the ground, because information was not easily available.
- He says founders misread the problem. When you go to the market you find that what you are solving is not the customer's pain point.
- He says speaking to customers on the ground is the most underrated thing to do, and that today's entrepreneurs do not spend enough time talking to customers.
- He points to the first 100 customers knowing the founders on a first name basis.

### Money and focus

- He says a good thing they did was to collect whatever money was due from a restaurant on day one.
- He says they were very frugal for the first six to eight months.
- He says that after funding they felt they had the Midas touch, grew to a team of 35 with four revenue streams, and blew away 70 to 80% of the money.
- He says you may have seven or eight revenue streams but must focus on your core.
- He says it is better to have 60 to 70% of one area than 5 to 10% of multiple areas.
- He says it is easy to raise capital but difficult to spend it in a sustainable manner.
- He says keeping the team lean is better than having specialists.
- He suggests imagining that you shut the business down for 15 days and thinking about what would come out of it.

### The market over time

- He says 2014 and 2015 were the craziest time for entrepreneurs, after the change of government in May 2014 brought large foreign investment.
- He says that in 2015 and 2016 ten to twelve companies in the food space were raising millions of dollars.
- He says that from 2015 to 2017 Dineout built systems from CRM to point of sale, to give restaurants more data about their customers.
- He says Dineout acquired two companies to build end to end management systems for restaurants.
- He says that when 100 people reserved, a restaurant would report that 60 showed up, so Dineout went for the low hanging fruit of bill payments.
- He says understanding consumer behaviour on multiple factors is very important.

### Selling to restaurants

- He says restaurants were mom and pop shops with single owners who resisted change, so selling them the vision of what the change could look like was key.
- He says selling is a lot easier today because people know the power of digitalisation.
- He says Dineout charged 50 rupees per diner, and that finding the pricing sweet spot for an industry takes time.
- He says the bet was that whatever was happening in the West would happen in India as well.

## Examples and cases used

- Dineout after its first funding: 35 people, four revenue streams and most of the money gone.
- Dineout's move from reservations to bill payments as the low hanging fruit.

## Numbers given

- Dineout, as told by Ankit Mehrotra: 70 to 80% of the first funding blown away.
- Dineout: 50 rupees charged per diner.
- Mehrotra now invests in startups and has 40 to 45 companies in his portfolio.

# Negotiation, first offers, anchoring and reservation price

This is the second half of the opening class of the negotiation course, taught by a faculty member who is not named in the recording. Students negotiated a one issue price deal in pairs as buyer and seller, and she then debriefed the results to teach the midpoint tendency, anchoring, reservation price and how to answer a request for your best price. The file also carries a classmate's written notes, used below only where marked.

## What was said

### The exercise

- The instructor set a negotiation between a buyer and a seller with only one issue, the price, with each side given a confidential role sheet.
- She told students to stick to the information given and not to exaggerate facts.
- She says people who do well in their first couple of negotiations then put in more effort, and that this is a consistent pattern.
- She says that when the context allows negotiation, you should negotiate.

### Midpoint and first offers

- She says people tend to settle in the middle of the first offer and the first counter offer. Her illustration is "You said 8, I said 4, let us come in between".
- She cites an article in a top economics journal on this tendency.
- She says this is why a first offer should be extreme but not ridiculous, meaning extreme in a way that can be justified.
- She says the other reason for an extreme first offer is that negotiation is all about giving concessions.
- She says the first offer on the table predicts the final outcome, and that the class's plotted outcomes sat close to the first offers.
- She says a negotiator who asks the other side to make the offer loses the anchoring advantage.
- She says that even when you call an offer ridiculous and negotiate from it, you stay anchored at that number because the adjustment is insufficient.

### Reservation price and target

- She defines the reservation price as the worst price or bottom line.
- She says it is mandatory to know your walk away price.
- She says negotiators think about the walk away number and never think about a target price.
- She says a negotiation with one issue and a fixed pie is a distributive negotiation.

### Knowing what is ridiculous

- She says you must know what a ridiculous price is before you make or receive an offer, and that this is homework.
- Her example is buying a favourite car second hand without knowing the market, where the seller's quote can be a few thousand or a few lakh too high and the buyer cannot tell.
- She says that in negotiation you quantify everything, all issues, in numbers and not only in money.

### Answering a request for your best price

- She says that when the other party asks for your best price you can state your target price, as long as it is not ridiculous.
- She says never to claim a price is the one below which you make a loss and then go below it, because that spends your trust.
- She says not to quote a range. Her example is a candidate who said she was happy with anything between 26 and 28 lakh, which the other side hears as 26.
- She says a concession stated as a range, such as 6.3 to 6.4 when coming down from 7, is like two concessions in a row.
- She says concessions should come with an explanation.

### Interests behind positions

- She separates a position, what a person says they want, from interests, the reasons they want it.
- She says that only by asking why could she find a solution that satisfied both parties in her own story about a child who wanted a Kinder Joy.
- She calls a solution that satisfies everybody's underlying interests creative negotiation.

### From the written class notes in the file

- The notes define the zone of possible agreement as the space between the buyer's reservation price and the seller's reservation price.
- The notes list what influences the other party as motives and incentives, personality, biases and errors.
- The notes record that the first offer carries a very strong anchoring bias, and that you should make sure you make the first offer.

## Examples and cases used

- Kinder Joy: a child's demand for it is the position, and asking why revealed the interest and a solution for both sides.
- A second hand car purchase: without market knowledge the buyer cannot tell whether a quote is ridiculous.

## Not covered

- How to find out the other party's reservation price was raised as a question and left for the next class.
- Threats and multi issue negotiation were announced for the next class.

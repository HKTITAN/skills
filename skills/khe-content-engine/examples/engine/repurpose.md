# Repurpose

How one idea becomes a different piece on each platform. Someone who follows Ines in all three places gets something new from each.

## First, the claim

Write the idea as one sentence that could be wrong. Every draft argues that sentence. If the brief cannot support it, stop and ask her.

## The treatment for each platform

- [[x]]: the single claim. One fact from the brief, one instruction. No story, no caveat beyond a clause.
- [[linkedin]]: how it was found out. What she changed, what she counted, what she will not claim. Written for settled freelancers.
- [[newsletter]]: the method. The steps, the exact wording to copy, and the one thing that goes wrong. Written so the reader can do it tonight.

## What changes between drafts

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Treatment | Never the same line on two drafts. |
| Opening | A different type from [[hooks]] on each platform. |
| Facts | Each draft takes the facts its treatment needs. No draft takes a fact the brief lacks. |
| Sentences | Two drafts share at most 20% of the shorter one's sentences. |
| Segment | Taken from [[audience]], named on the draft. |
| Form | Taken from [[content-types]], named on the draft. |

## When a platform gets nothing

An opinion with no method has nothing for the newsletter. Skip the platform and say why on the run's `Skipped:` line. A padded draft costs more than a missing one.

## Voice

The treatment changes. [[voice]] does not.

# How is a startup funded, and what does the money cost?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: the Entrepreneurship files cited here name no author, no professor, no course and no source for any figure. A number inside a worked example is that example's number, never a benchmark. Two of these files disagree on what a non-participating liquidation preference pays, and two on the size of the option pool; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## How a venture fund works

- A venture capital fund is a pool of capital from investors called LPs, managed by a team called GPs and invested in startups over 10 or more years: fundraising in year 1, deployment in years 2 to 5 and harvest in years 6 to 10. (Entrepreneurship: VC Fund Mechanics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-vc-fund-mechanics.md)
- The file's typical fund math: $100M raised, 20 companies over 5 years, 70% fail or return capital, 20% return 2 to 5x, 10% return 10 to 100x, a portfolio average of 3 to 5x and 2 to 3x net to LPs after fees and carry. (Entrepreneurship: VC Fund Mechanics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-vc-fund-mechanics.md)
- The management fee is typically 2% of fund size a year, and GPs keep 20%, sometimes 10 to 30%, of profits from exits as carried interest, which the file says aligns their incentives with LP returns. (Entrepreneurship: GP-LP Dynamics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-gp-lp-dynamics.md)
- Due diligence covers six areas: product, team, market, competition, business model and financials. (Entrepreneurship: Investment Thesis and Due Diligence, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-investment-thesis-and-due-diligence.md)
- Good VCs separate the pitch, what founders say, from the truth, what data shows; the file says validating 0.1% market capture beats a claim of targeting a $1T TAM. (Entrepreneurship: Investment Thesis and Due Diligence, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-investment-thesis-and-due-diligence.md)

## Stages and valuation

- The stages file gives seed at $500K to $2M with cheques of $100K to $500K, Series A at $2M to $20M with cheques of $1M to $10M or more, Series B at $10M to $50M and Series C onward at $50M or more; it does not say what the stage amounts measure. (Entrepreneurship: Investment Types by Stage, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-investment-types-by-stage.md)
- The valuation file gives a seed valuation of $500K to $5M, a Series A range of usually $5M to $50M and a Series B and later range of $50M to $1B or more. (Entrepreneurship: Valuation at Different Stages, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-valuation-at-different-stages.md)
- Four valuation methods are listed: comparable company analysis, discounted cash flow, a revenue multiple given as 5 to 10x ARR for B2B SaaS, and market size and share. (Entrepreneurship: Valuation at Different Stages, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-valuation-at-different-stages.md)
- The file's 10x rule says VCs invest at one tenth of the expected exit valuation; in its example a VC expecting a $500M exit in 5 years invests at a $50M valuation. (Entrepreneurship: Valuation at Different Stages, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-valuation-at-different-stages.md)

## Term sheets and liquidation preferences

- A term sheet outlines investment terms; it is not binding except for confidentiality, and it sets the framework for the legal documents. (Entrepreneurship: Term Sheet Terms and Negotiation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-term-sheet-terms-and-negotiation.md)
- The term sheet file defines 1x non-participating as the investor getting the investment back or the equity stake, whichever is greater, and labels it founder-friendly; 2x participating is the investment back plus the equity percentage of remaining proceeds, labelled investor-friendly. (Entrepreneurship: Term Sheet Terms and Negotiation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-term-sheet-terms-and-negotiation.md)
- In the liquidation file's example headed non-participating, a company sells for $10M, a Series A investor who put in $5M and owns 50% receives the $5M and then 50% of the remaining $5M, a total of $7.5M, and founders split $2.5M. (Entrepreneurship: Liquidation Preferences in Exit Scenarios, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-liquidation-preferences-in-exit-scenarios.md)
- In the same file's participating example the Series A investor receives the $5M and also 50% of total proceeds, a stated total of $10M, and founders get nothing; the file calls participating preferences founder-unfavourable. (Entrepreneurship: Liquidation Preferences in Exit Scenarios, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-liquidation-preferences-in-exit-scenarios.md)
- Under anti-dilution provisions an existing investor's share adjusts up automatically if the next round has a lower valuation; the file labels broad-based weighted average harsh for founders and narrow-based more founder-friendly. (Entrepreneurship: Term Sheet Terms and Negotiation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-term-sheet-terms-and-negotiation.md)

## Instruments and ownership

- A convertible note is a loan that converts to equity in a future round, usually Series A: the company owes the investor the cash plus interest, typically 2 to 8%, and the note converts to preferred stock at a discount. (Entrepreneurship: Fundraising Instruments, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-fundraising-instruments.md)
- A SAFE gives the investor a right to equity in a future round for cash, with no interest, no maturity date and no debt status, and converts on a valuation cap. (Entrepreneurship: Fundraising Instruments, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-fundraising-instruments.md)
- In a priced round the investor and company agree an exact valuation and the investor gets preferred stock; in the file's example $5M at $20M post-money buys 25%. (Entrepreneurship: Fundraising Instruments, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-fundraising-instruments.md)
- A cap table shows all equity holders and their percentage ownership; common stock, held by founders and employees, has the lowest priority in liquidation, and preferred stock, held by investors, gets preferential treatment. (Entrepreneurship: Cap Tables and Ownership, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-cap-tables-and-ownership.md)
- Each funding round dilutes existing holders: in the file's example a founder's 4M shares fall from 40% at founding to 8% after Series B as total shares rise from 10M to 50M. (Entrepreneurship: Dilution Mechanics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-dilution-mechanics.md)
- The dilution file says to reserve 10 to 20% as an employee pool for future hires. (Entrepreneurship: Dilution Mechanics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-dilution-mechanics.md)
- The employee equity file says a company reserves 10 to 15% of shares for future employee options. (Entrepreneurship: Employee Equity and Vesting, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-employee-equity-and-vesting.md)
- The file's vesting example is 100,000 options at a strike price of $0.25 a share on a 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff: none vested before month 12, 25,000 at month 12 and all 100,000 at month 48. (Entrepreneurship: Employee Equity and Vesting, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-employee-equity-and-vesting.md)

## Down rounds and exits

- A down round raises capital at a lower valuation than the previous round; the file lists four consequences: existing investors diluted further, founders diluted even more, down round preferences often triggered, and a demoralising signal to team and market. (Entrepreneurship: Down Rounds, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-down-rounds.md)
- Four ways out are listed: IPO, acquisition, secondary sale and failure. Most VC-backed companies do not IPO, and acquisition is called the typical exit, with a typical range given as $100M to $500M or more. (Entrepreneurship: Exit Strategies, how VCs get returns, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/vc-exit-strategies-how-vcs-get-returns.md)
- In the file's $500M acquisition example preferred shares are paid first and founders with 20% common receive about $90M; in its $50M example liquidation preferences may consume all proceeds and founders receive nothing. (Entrepreneurship: Failed vs. Successful Exits, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/finance-failed-vs-successful-exits.md)

## The pitch

- The core deck structure is 10 to 12 slides: cover, problem, solution, market, business model, traction, competitive landscape, go-to-market, team, financials, the ask and contact. (Entrepreneurship: Pitch Deck Design, for investors, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pitch-pitch-deck-design-for-investors.md)
- The verbal pitch is set out in four acts: problem for 1 minute, solution for 2 minutes, opportunity for 1 minute and the ask for 30 seconds. (Entrepreneurship: Pitch Narrative, 5-minute verbal pitch, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pitch-pitch-narrative-5-minute-verbal-pitch.md)
- Investors invest in founders they believe will execute, and the founder's story conveys three things: founder-problem fit, conviction and passion, and execution capability. (Entrepreneurship: Storytelling for Investors, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pitch-storytelling-for-investors.md)

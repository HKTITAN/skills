# Co-founders

This file answers whether to have a co-founder, where to find one, how to split and vest the equity, what to agree before starting and what to do when the relationship fails. The speakers are Harj Taggar, Michael Seibel, Dalton Caldwell, Catheryn Li, Divya Bhat, Paul Graham, Tamanna Khemani, Kat Manalac, Carolynn Levy and Jason Kwon, plus one post signed only Y Combinator. The pieces span many years and differ on equal splits, tiebreakers and working with someone you have just met, so read each line with its speaker and year.

## Do I need a co-founder?

- Taggar, in a talk the library dates to 2021, gives three reasons to have a co-founder: you get more and better work done, you get moral support, and you match the pattern of the most successful startups. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar points out that Apple, Facebook, Google and Microsoft all began with co-founders, and that people forget this because one person became the figurehead over decades. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar's default answer for someone keen to start now without a co-founder is to wait until they have one. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar says the single founders who succeeded fit a pattern: very high conviction in the idea, from a personal problem or domain expertise, and the ability to make progress alone, which almost always means being technical enough to build the first version. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar's example is Drew Houston, who applied in 2007, was told by Paul Graham he would not be funded without a co-founder, kept building the Dropbox prototype while searching, and applied again once he had one. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Bhat, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says you can start alone but that doing so makes a startup "more than twice as hard", and she lists speed, better brainstorming, accountability and moral support as what a co-founder adds. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li, in the same 2023 talk, says only four of YC's top hundred companies were started by a solo founder, and that those founders could always make progress on their own, building the first product and getting users themselves. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Khemani and Manalac's post for students gives a different figure, saying 7.9 percent of YC's top companies list had solo founders. (Startups for students, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/D0-startups-for-students)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2021, stops short of calling co-founders essential but says they help most in the hardest stretch, before product-market fit. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Graham's 2006 essay lists a single founder as the first of its mistakes, reading it as a sign that the founder could not talk any friends into joining, and the friends are the people who know him best. (The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups, https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay says that even a person who could do all the work needs colleagues to brainstorm with, to argue them out of bad decisions and to cheer them up, and that the last may matter most. (The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups, https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html)
- Graham's 2007 essay says every investor is more likely to fund a founder who has a cofounder than one who does not. (Why to Not Not Start a Startup, https://paulgraham.com/notnot.html)
- Graham's 2007 essay reports having funded two single founders, both told that finding a cofounder was their first priority, and says he would have preferred they had one before applying. (Why to Not Not Start a Startup, https://paulgraham.com/notnot.html)
- Graham's 2007 answer to having no cofounder is to get one, moving to where willing people live or switching to an idea people want to work on if that is what it takes. (Why to Not Not Start a Startup, https://paulgraham.com/notnot.html)

## Who makes a good co-founder?

- Taggar says the most important thing to know about a person before starting a company with them is how they handle stress, and the second is how well they will help you handle it. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar advises against starting with someone you know only socially, and says the best candidates are a close friend or someone you have worked with under stressful conditions. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar says to talk through why each of you wants a startup even when you know each other well, because a founder aiming for venture-funded high growth and one content with a slow, salary-paying business will end in conflict. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar says complementary skills matter but should not be a fixation: liking and trusting the person counts for more, and hires can fill skill gaps later. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Caldwell, in a talk the library dates to 2021, calls it the biggest error he sees to pick a co-founder for a skill match over someone you have already had a fight with, such as a friend, since in his experience almost everything is learned on the job. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Seibel, asked by Caldwell in that 2021 talk whether he ever hears that a well-liked co-founder simply lacked the skills, answers "Never hear it." and calls a skills gap an error but not a fatal one. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Seibel says many co-founder disputes come from a relationship that was never pressure tested, and contrasts it with someone known for a while with whom you have already had disagreements. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Li says in the 2023 talk that complementary skills are less important than people think because most skills can be learned, fundraising, marketing and sales included. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Bhat names one exception in the 2023 talk: a non-technical founder should look hard for a technical co-founder, and if none can be found should learn to code instead of hiring a dev shop. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Khemani and Manalac's post says roughly ten percent of companies in each batch have a non-technical founder, counts only three such companies on the top companies list, and recommends a technical co-founder for anyone building a software company. (Startups for students, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/D0-startups-for-students)
- Graham's 2006 essay says most founder disputes come from the people and not the situation, and that founders burned by them usually had misgivings at the start which they suppressed. (The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups, https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay for students says to look among classmates for people who keep starting projects and finish at least some of them. (A Student's Guide to Startups, https://paulgraham.com/mit.html)
- Kwon, answering an audience question in a talk the library dates to 2018, says not to take on a stranger as a partner or co-founder. (Startup legal mechanics, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7R-startup-legal-mechanics)
- Carolynn Levy adds in that 2018 talk that founder breakups reach every kind of relationship, brothers included, so involving a stranger stacks the deck against you. (Startup legal mechanics, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7R-startup-legal-mechanics)

## Where do I find one?

- Taggar says to start with people you already know, friends and colleagues, and that the best time to find a co-founder is before you are looking for one. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar's advice to his younger self is to look for people to do projects with, because a project asks for far less commitment than a company and more people say yes. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar says someone with a job should keep a list of coworkers who impress them and invite those people to work on evening and weekend projects, since a person interested in that is likely to make a great co-founder. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- For a founder ready to start now, Taggar gives a procedure: list the people closest to you who would be great co-founders, ask each one, and when someone declines ask which three or four people they would recruit and for an introduction to each. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar's last source is people who share your interests, met at developer meetups, open source projects, hackathons or online communities. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Graham's 2007 essay says a cofounder could be met through a user's group or a conference but that he would not be too optimistic, because you need to work with someone to know whether you want them. (Why to Not Not Start a Startup, https://paulgraham.com/notnot.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay for students says where to find a co-founder was the top question people asked, that there is no good answer, and that co-founders should be people you already know, met best at school. (A Student's Guide to Startups, https://paulgraham.com/mit.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay calls finding a co-founder the biggest problem for someone starting at 30, whose school friends are out of touch or tied to jobs. (A Student's Guide to Startups, https://paulgraham.com/mit.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay says work is the other place co-founders meet, less often than school, and that the odds improve at a company full of smart young people in a startup hub. (A Student's Guide to Startups, https://paulgraham.com/mit.html)
- Khemani and Manalac's post says school and work are the most common ways founders of the top performing YC companies met. (Startups for students, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/D0-startups-for-students)

## Does co-founder matching work, and how do I test a match?

- A note placed above Taggar's talk says the matching service launched after the video, that the advice to search your own network still holds, and that founders should now try the service in parallel. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Li says in the 2023 talk that the matching platform, launched early the year before by her account, held 40,000 profiles and had just passed 100,000 matches. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li says in the 2023 talk that it is too early to know whether matched teams last seven to ten years, and that over a dozen teams met there, applied together and got in. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li's post counts two pairs who met on the platform, did trial projects and were accepted into the Summer 2021 batch. (Does co-founder matching work?, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Cq-does-co-founder-matching-work)
- Li's post says Sequin's Vrinda Gupta worked a year as a solo founder, raising a pre-seed round and building an MVP, before seeking a technical co-founder, and that the pair went through YC and raised $5.7M. (Does co-founder matching work?, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Cq-does-co-founder-matching-work)
- Li's post quotes Gupta saying she and Mark Thomas met the day after they matched and on each of the next two days. (Does co-founder matching work?, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Cq-does-co-founder-matching-work)
- Li's 2023 telling of Sequin has the two meet first on Zoom and then in person every day for the following week. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li's post says of Kiwi Biosciences that David Hachuel "was immediately interested in her idea", meaning Anjie Liu's, and that the pair answered all 50 questions of a First Round questionnaire and ran a month-long trial. (Does co-founder matching work?, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Cq-does-co-founder-matching-work)
- Li's 2023 talk tells the Kiwi story differently: he thought her idea would never work, was in a trial with another candidate, and acted as a weekly advisor before the questionnaire and the month-long trial. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li's first tip for the platform in the 2023 talk is a complete profile that brags, above all for a non-technical founder with an idea, because technical founders receive many invites and an MVP with users sets a founder apart from one who only has an idea. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li's second tip is to meet quickly, citing platform data that seventy percent of meetings happen within two weeks of matching. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li recommends a trial project before committing, agreed in advance with a timeline of usually two to four weeks, a tangible goal and scope, and who owns the work afterwards. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Taggar's 2021 version of the trial is a set period with a deadline for an MVP, a prototype or customer pitches, a month or three months full time for two people with no other commitments, followed by an honest talk about whether to continue. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- The Y Combinator post of ten questions says no questionnaire replaces time spent together in person, and that knowing for sure usually takes several months. (10 questions to discuss with a potential co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Hj-10-questions-to-discuss-with-a-potential-co-founder)
- Khemani and Manalac's post does not suggest starting a company immediately with someone met on the platform, and says the work of building trust remains. (Startups for students, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/D0-startups-for-students)

## How do we split the equity?

- Seibel's essay says unequal splits based on early work are a mistake because a valuable company takes 7 to 10 years, so small differences in year one do not justify very different stakes for the years after. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- Seibel's essay says investors read the split as a cue to how the CEO values the co-founders, so a 10% or 1% stake tells them that person is not very good or will not matter much. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- Seibel's essay recommends equal or close to equal splits, wants them to become standard, and says a founder unwilling to give an equal share may be choosing the wrong partner. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- In the video on the same page, which the library dates to 2019, Seibel says he used to say most companies should split equally, calls that an easy rule of thumb, and adds that it cannot always be applied. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- In that 2019 video Seibel declines to prescribe an exact split and tells the CEO to choose the one that maximizes co-founders' motivation over the one a negotiation produced. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- Seibel says in the 2019 video that vesting with a one-year cliff is the CEO's hedge, a "get-out-of-jail-free card" if a wrong co-founder choice is corrected within a year, and that with this hedge in place being more generous pays off more often than not. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- Taggar says in the 2021 talk that the default split is 50/50 because nothing is built and the work lies years ahead, while noting that other arrangements have succeeded and that a later-stage co-founder can justify a deviation. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar advises against taking 70% and leaving 30% on the strength of six months of solo progress, and says to go 50/50 if that is what it takes for a great co-founder to be all in. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Caldwell says in the 2021 talk that the closer to equal the better, then warns that a straight fifty-fifty deadlock kills companies and suggests the CEO hold one extra share as a tiebreaker agreed in writing. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Seibel's essay, by contrast, lists the need for a tie-breaker among the cited reasons for unequal splits that it rejects. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- Li says in the 2023 talk that the need for a tiebreaker is a fair point but does not have to be met through equity. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Bhat says in the 2023 talk that the default is an equal split, and that coming up with the idea is no reason for more because ideas are cheap and the idea is very likely to change as you listen to users. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li says a good deal won from a co-founder now can turn into resentment later, and that since startup outcomes are bimodal a few percent is not worth risking the relationship. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Carolynn Levy says in the 2018 talk that founders who will all work hard should split more or less equally, and that trouble reaching agreement on the split can point to trust or commitment issues. (Startup legal mechanics, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7R-startup-legal-mechanics)

## What vesting should founders have?

- Seibel's essay, on a page the library dates to 2019, describes the typical Valley setup as four years of vesting with a one-year cliff: nothing if you leave or are fired in the first year, 25% at one year, then 1/48th of the total each month. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- Seibel's essay also suggests that only the CEO hold a board seat before a significant equity fundraise, to prevent board disputes in tough decisions such as firing a co-founder. (How to split equity among co-founders, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
- Graham's 2006 essay says about 20% of the startups he had funded had a founder leave, which led him to advise founders to vest, without requiring it, so there is an orderly way to quit. (The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups, https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html)
- Carolynn Levy says in the 2018 talk that founder shares should vest, that the company automatically repurchases unvested shares when a founder quits, and that the standard period is about four years. (Startup legal mechanics, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7R-startup-legal-mechanics)
- Carolynn Levy calls it a big mistake for a team to skip vesting because they have worked together for years, since no investor wants founders who can burn out and leave with their shares. (Startup legal mechanics, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7R-startup-legal-mechanics)
- Carolynn Levy says a single founder should vest too, because the lack of it is still unattractive to investors and because employees' options will vest. (Startup legal mechanics, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7R-startup-legal-mechanics)

## What should we agree before we start?

- Seibel, relaying a founder's story in the 2021 talk, says to write down the equity percentages and founder vesting at the beginning, because people remember things differently and a bad situation is very hard to handle with nothing in writing. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Caldwell recommends choosing the co-founder first and then finding the idea and raising money together, because co-founders added later tend to feel the company is not theirs, which hurts retention. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Taggar says to decide who is CEO, treats discomfort in that conversation as a red flag, and says the title matters little day to day but matters to investors, who want a clear final decision maker. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- Taggar says two people who both feel it is important to be CEO are not good co-founders for each other and should not start a company together. (How to find the right co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8h-how-to-find-the-right-co-founder)
- The Y Combinator post says its ten questions came from teams who met through matching and from the most common reasons founding teams break up, and that each founder should write answers alone before sharing them. (10 questions to discuss with a potential co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Hj-10-questions-to-discuss-with-a-potential-co-founder)
- The Y Combinator post asks for a plan of what each person will work on for the first 6-12 months and a decision on who is CEO. (10 questions to discuss with a potential co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Hj-10-questions-to-discuss-with-a-potential-co-founder)
- The Y Combinator post's questions cover what must happen for each founder to go full-time, personal finances and how long each can work unpaid, where the company is based, and what happens if the founders cannot agree or no longer want to work together. (10 questions to discuss with a potential co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Hj-10-questions-to-discuss-with-a-potential-co-founder)
- The Y Combinator post's checklist before committing includes reference calls on each other, done openly by swapping references, and having a spouse or serious partner meet the co-founder. (10 questions to discuss with a potential co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Hj-10-questions-to-discuss-with-a-potential-co-founder)
- Bhat says in the 2023 talk that one of four founders at her first startup had an unspoken timeline for the product to go viral, grew frustrated about six months in, and left abruptly. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Bhat lists topics to settle before committing: evenings, weekends and vacations, expected response times, how long each can go without salary or benefits, and what each needs to see at milestones to stay motivated. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)

## How do we keep working well together?

- The Y Combinator post says to set up a recurring one-to-one, typically weekly, about how working together is going, credits the idea to Amy Buechler, and says that done correctly it can help avoid breakups. (10 questions to discuss with a potential co-founder, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Hj-10-questions-to-discuss-with-a-potential-co-founder)
- Li's first tip for trust in the 2023 talk is to trust a co-founder by default until they lose it, because the reverse leaves people feeling micromanaged. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li says to do what you said you would, to say early and honestly when you will not, and to leave room for mistakes without saying "I told you so". (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Bhat says her first company, with four founders, gave no one a title and decided everything by consensus, which she calls "ultimately disastrous" because the team got stuck in gridlock, and she adds that the equal equity was not the problem. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li says to name one person CEO, adds "None of this co-CEO stuff.", and says being unable to pick one may show the team cannot make hard decisions together. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Bhat says to learn whether each co-founder attacks or retreats under stress, because a mismatch can feed itself when one presses to talk and the other withdraws. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Caldwell makes the same point about stress in the 2021 talk, and adds that a fight does not always need a resolution on the spot and can be paused when the two are not engaging productively. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Li recommends regularly scheduled one-on-ones with time for feedback in both directions, and cites the Kiwi Biosciences founders, who do not interrupt or argue while the other gives constructive feedback. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li says not to delay hard conversations because problems are easier to solve while small, and recommends a coach or counselor for difficult interpersonal situations. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Li says co-founders should get comfortable arguing while knowing who decides, and once a decision is made should commit and move on. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)

## What do we do when it breaks?

- Seibel, reading a founder's submission in the 2021 talk, says to take arguments with co-founders seriously because they are more likely to kill the company than anything else. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Caldwell says that when co-founders have not had a real conversation in weeks, months or a year, a breakup becomes inevitable and the CEO's job changes from repair to separating in the least destructive way. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Caldwell says the longer a broken relationship is left, the worse the breakup, with litigation more likely and vested stock affecting the cap table. (Dalton & Michael: Co-founder mistakes that kill companies and how to avoid them, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/DZ-dalton-michael-co-founder-mistakes-that-kill-companies-and-how-to-avoid-them)
- Bhat tells in the 2023 talk of a founder asked to come back with a co-founder who recruited a long-time friend he had never worked with, micromanaged and berated him, and saw him resign abruptly. (Keys To Successful Co-Founder Relationships, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Im-keys-to-successful-co-founder-relationships)
- Graham's 2006 essay says a founder leaving does not necessarily kill a startup, that it is usually the least committed one who goes, and that even losing one of two founders is survivable. (The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups, https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html)
- Carolynn Levy says in the 2018 talk that co-founders no longer wanting to work together is sometimes fatal, and that paying yourselves, vesting founder shares and assigning IP to the company reduce the legal drama of a breakup. (Startup legal mechanics, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7R-startup-legal-mechanics)
- Kwon says in the 2018 talk that when a company started with a friend is not working out, it is better to resolve it quickly than to let it fester, which hurts both the company and the friendship. (Startup legal mechanics, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7R-startup-legal-mechanics)

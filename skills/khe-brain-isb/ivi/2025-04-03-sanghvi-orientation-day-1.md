# Orientation day 1 on networking and cold outreach, Shreyas Sanghvi and the programme director

Typed notes from the first orientation day. The programme director opened with advice on cold emails, meetings and networking, and Shreyas Sanghvi of Esmagico then spoke about tools and reaching large customers. The notes are short bullet points, so this record keeps only what they state.

## What was said

### Cold emails, programme director

- The programme director says a cold email should be about 100 words, short enough to read without scrolling.
- The ask in a cold email should be specific and answerable with yes or no, for example whether the person would be willing to introduce you to a named person.
- Reply to emails you receive right away, because thinking about them later doubles the energy you spend.
- Reply within 24 hours, because a later reply gives the impression that you do not care.

### Meetings, programme director

- Aim for in-person meetings, even when that means a flight or a long journey.
- Weak links matter. Keep a note of people you meet indirectly, because an ask to a weak link is not strong but can still be useful.
- Do your homework before a meeting, learn about the person's personal life too, and keep asking questions.
- When possible hold the meeting at your place, because you get the person's full attention and people are often distracted at their own place.
- Socialising goes with food and drink. If someone is short of time, meet for a drink or tea, and accept chai or coffee when it is offered.
- Follow up at once. The example given is a handwritten thank-you note written in the car right after a meeting, or an email written immediately and scheduled for the next day.

### Networking, programme director

- Turn networking around by offering help and focusing on giving. The help comes back, though not always from the same person.
- Asked how long one must keep doing this, the answer given was for the rest of your life, because it is about being that kind of person.
- Ask investors what they get out of investing and what interests them, such as mentoring or the industry, and why they invest only in one sector.
- Build community by talking to people outside work and being genuinely interested in them, for example asking what they are struggling with lately.
- To reach very senior or influential people, focus on the young people around them, such as their children or school friends.
- Be curious and listen so attentively that listening tires you. The notes call this listening loudly.
- Find out why people failed, such as the wrong team or the wrong time, instead of only studying those who succeeded. The notes call this failure gossip.
- When you are unsure whether you can help someone, lean towards yes, then commit, set a deadline and make it happen.
- Prepare a two-minute pitch that covers what you are, what you are trying to build and why it is important.
- Use storytelling to make an impact, with genuine interest and not a superficial one.
- On who gets funded, the programme has people whose job is to invest, and founders can either ignore them or use them.

### Tools and plans, Shreyas Sanghvi

- Shreyas Sanghvi lists tools by job. For programming GitHub Copilot and Cursor, for no-code Replit and Bolt, for research Perplexity Deep Research and NotebookLM, for marketing Jasper, InVideo and Midjourney.
- He says to create a ten-year plan and then try to achieve it in one year.
- His example is Swiggy, which started very small although Foodpanda and others had a head start, and focused on customer experience and increasing the wow factor.

### Reaching large customers, Shreyas Sanghvi

- He says to use the ISB email address when sending cold emails.
- He says never to open an email to a vice president of a large conglomerate with a greeting about yourself. Start with the problem in the first sentence.
- After the problem, send the demo link and explain the problem you are solving.
- He suggests trying to walk into their offices.
- He says to give a free trial, because getting into a large company is very hard but once you are in, getting out is impossible.
- He says to find out which poor product the company uses today.

## Examples and cases used

- Swiggy: started very small despite rivals such as Foodpanda having a head start, and won on customer experience.

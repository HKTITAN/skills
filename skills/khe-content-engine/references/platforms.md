# Platforms

Use this file to fill a platform node during `setup`. Copy a fact into the node only with its date and source, and write the node's own `Facts checked:` line with the day you looked, never the day in this file. A node is written only for a platform the owner posts on now.

Three grades of fact, never mixed:

- **Checked at the source.** Read on the platform's own page on the date given.
- **Reported.** Stated by several third-party pages on the date given; the platform's own page was not opened. Good enough for a first draft of a node, marked as reported.
- **Confirm before relying on this.** Unverified, disputed or folklore. It goes under that heading in the node and is never written as a rule.

Everything about reach, ranking, "the algorithm", best times and best lengths is in the third grade for every platform except where [x-algorithm](x-algorithm.md) supports it. A node's `Limit:` is the owner's ceiling, which is at or under the platform's.

All facts below were checked on 2026-11-10.

## X

Checked at the source (X developer documentation, "Counting characters", <https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/counting-characters>):

- A post holds up to 280 characters.
- Any link counts as 23 characters, whatever its length.
- An emoji counts as 2. A Chinese, Japanese or Korean character counts as 2.

Reported:

- A paid subscription raises the limit to 25,000 characters, and a long post is cut in the feed after about 280 with a control to show the rest.

From the published ranking code: see [x-algorithm](x-algorithm.md). Use its statements by number. Nothing else about reach on X is a fact.

Confirm before relying on this:

- Whether a link in the main post reduces reach. The published code does not settle it.
- Any best hour, any weekly count, any window after posting.
- Whether hashtags help or hurt.
- The exact length at which a long post is cut in the feed.

What the form asks: the claim in the first line, since that line is the whole post for most readers. One thought to a paragraph. A thread is one step or one beat to a part, and its first part stands alone. The gate counts code points, so count a link as 23 by hand.

## LinkedIn

Checked at the source (LinkedIn Help, "Post and share updates", <https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176>):

- A post holds up to 3,000 characters.

Reported:

- A document post takes a file of up to 300 pages and 100 MB.

Confirm before relying on this:

- How many characters show before the "see more" cut. Figures between 140 and 210 circulate; LinkedIn publishes none and it differs by device.
- Whether a link in the post body reduces reach, and whether a link in the first comment avoids that.
- Any best day or hour, any ideal length, any limit on hashtags.
- Whether commenting on other posts before publishing changes anything.

What the form asks: an opening line that carries the post alone, because the rest is behind a cut of unknown length. One idea to a paragraph. A story holds up only when the caveat is inside it.

## Instagram

Reported:

- A caption holds up to 2,200 characters. Meta's developer documentation is the origin of the figure according to third-party pages.
- A carousel holds up to 20 photos or videos.
- In December 2025 Instagram announced a limit of five hashtags on a post or reel, rolling out gradually (press coverage of the announcement by Instagram's head; the earlier limit was 30).

Confirm before relying on this:

- Whether the five-hashtag limit has reached the owner's account.
- How much of a caption shows before the cut.
- Anything about reach for carousels against reels.

What the form asks: the picture or the first slide does the stopping, and this skill does not make pictures. A caption adds what the image cannot say and never repeats it. Slide text is a brief for whoever makes the slides (`khe-sticker` or `stageshow` for the art), written as one idea to a slide.

## Threads

Reported:

- A post holds up to 500 characters.
- A text attachment of up to 10,000 characters can be added to a post (introduced in September 2025).

Confirm before relying on this:

- Whether a link counts toward the limit.
- Anything about reach.

What the form asks: one opinion, said the way the owner would say it aloud. No setup.

## Bluesky

Reported:

- A post holds up to 300 characters, counted as graphemes, so an emoji made of several code points counts once.
- A link counts at its full length.

Confirm before relying on this:

- The gate counts code points, not graphemes. A post near the limit with emoji in it is counted by hand.

What the form asks: the same as a short X post, with the link inside the count.

## TikTok

Reported:

- A video can be up to 10 minutes.

Confirm before relying on this:

- The caption limit. Third-party pages give 2,200 and 4,000 and disagree about which applies in the app.
- A 60 minute upload limit, given by one page only.
- Anything about reach, length or posting frequency.

What the form asks: this skill writes the script and the caption, never the video (`khe-launch-film` and `khe-demo` own moving pictures). A script is spoken, so write it by reading it aloud. One idea. The first sentence is the reason to keep watching.

## YouTube

Reported:

- A title holds up to 100 characters and a description up to 5,000. One page notes the interface counts bytes for the description.
- Since 15 October 2024 a Short can be up to three minutes (press coverage of YouTube's own announcement).

Confirm before relying on this:

- How many characters of a title show before it is cut, which differs by surface.
- Any ideal length, any best day, any claim about what the title or thumbnail is worth.

What the form asks: this skill writes the title, the description and the outline. A title says what the viewer will be able to do. An outline is a list of sections, each with the one thing shown in it.

## Facebook

Confirm before relying on this:

- The post limit. The figure 63,206 characters dates from press coverage in 2011, and no current page from Meta confirming it was found.
- Anything about reach, groups or video.

What the form asks: a post in a group is a question the owner wants answered or a thing the group can use. Write it for that group, in the owner's voice.

## Newsletter

Checked by reasoning, not at a source: email sets no length limit on a message body. The owner's ceiling is their own choice, taken from the issues their readers finish.

Confirm before relying on this:

- How much of a subject line or preview a reader's mail app shows. It differs by app and screen.
- Any best day or hour to send, other than what the owner's own sending tool reports for their own list.

What the form asks: the first line of the draft is the subject line. One topic to an issue. The reader gets the method in full and, when there is wording, the wording to copy.

## A platform not listed here

Look up its limits at its own help pages, write them in the node with the date and the page, and put everything else under `Confirm before relying on this`. Never fill a node from memory.

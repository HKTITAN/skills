# India

India is the setting for nearly every argument he makes: he reads its culture as afraid of attempts, ranked from childhood and quick to tear down success, and he builds Aeos as an answer to that. The bluntest claims come from Kata 4 and Secret Kata 1, which are talks to his own employees, and from PGX Ideas 2 and Masters Union, where he was a guest. He says he was once clipped out of context for saying Indians complain, so he pairs the criticism with praise for Indian drive, and both halves are his.

## Why do Indians not attempt?

- He calls India an **anti-attempt nation**. An attempt tests the story in your head, so people avoid it the way they avoid a cancer check, and he says 99% of the country does not attempt hard things. (Kata 4 at 28:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1735s)
- India is filled with commentators who hold strong opinions and have made no attempts, and he calls it a courage issue. In his own company a person will say in a meeting what should be done and go silent when told to do it. (Kata 4 at 20:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1255s)
- "You cannot get to success by avoiding mistakes. That only works in exams." He says the rich people he has met do not care about failing or being mocked, and that India by contrast shames failure. (Kata 13 at 10:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=624s)
- India lacks self-belief, he says, because Indians grew up where crazy ideas are laughed at. The people who do best in India all had exceptional self-belief when nobody believed in them. (Why Belief Matters at 39:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2387s)
- Self-belief never starts full, especially if you grew up in South India with conservative parents. That is why he says entrepreneurs need simple businesses first, and outlier businesses come later. (Why Belief Matters at 24:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1483s)
- Beliefs are social: if your friends are doing something, it seems likely that you can do it too. He says that is the entire reason startup funding in India and Indians going to SF to build big companies boomed, and that one Bangalore street alone has a large number of 10-story buildings, while normal people he meets, one of them a dentist, have built five-story buildings. (Why Belief Matters at 13:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=817s; Why Belief Matters at 14:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=864s)
- In India an idea that people would laugh at could be a really big idea. This was part of his reasoning for AI avatars. (Masters Union at 11:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=711s)
- India needs fewer critics and more people taking risks. When a critic said India would not make a good game in 20 years and a commenter asked for more critics, he replied that among 1.4 billion people there is one team with a playable third-person demo and maybe two or three others. (Masters Union at 50:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3043s)
- Indians judge risk by what the crowd does. He calls entrance exams for IITs or the top 10 colleges a hugely risky endeavour on the odds, yet acceptable in India because everyone takes it, like breathing Delhi air. (Kata 9 at 4:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=292s)
- Careers that are not socially accepted go unclaimed. He says video editing is close to a guaranteed living that India does not believe in yet, and that **no-go businesses** like this have no competition. (Kata 9 at 24:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1490s; Kata 9 at 40:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2441s)
- Indians overvalue niceness, which he calls **good boy, good girl energy**. Niceness is table stakes, and competent people disagree, say no, kill bad ideas and ask uncomfortable questions. (Kata 4 at 45:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2700s)
- Indians read competence badly because they ignore risk and luck and explain away other people's success as luck or rich parents while calling their own success skill. He calls India a populist country that likes good stories. (Kata 4 at 26:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1590s)
- He traces the victim mindset in Indian talent to parents and a colonial mindset. Parents mostly had jobs the next company could not grade, so the employer held all the cards, and he says that is no longer true for digital skills. (Kata 8 at 41:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2505s)
- He says the country's big problem is entitlement: people want a crore because someone on social media got one. His answer is that he will pay a crore when the person generates a crore of value. (KK Create at 1:26:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5219s)
- He quotes a comment under the second trailer on IGN that said it looks very good, but it is from India so something must be wrong behind it. (Masters Union at 58:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3521s)

## What does rank pressure do to Indians?

- "Everything in our society is a rank game." He lists competitive exams, constant comparison with the neighbour's son, feeds full of billionaires and limited seats for everything from exams to concerts. (Secret Kata 1 at 8:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=486s)
- A brain under **chronic rank pressure** reads everything as a threat: ambiguous comments feel insulting, feedback feels like humiliation, silence feels like rejection, and someone else winning hurts more than it should. (Secret Kata 1 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=341s)
- It is easy to offend Indians online because most do not feel socially safe. He says people around the world are racist to Indians on Twitter for ad money because they know Indians will respond where other countries would not. (Secret Kata 1 at 8:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=533s)
- Indians take rejection personally where Americans are less likely to. VCs say no 99% of the time, but in India they wrap it in apology because founders take offence. (Secret Kata 1 at 9:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=582s)
- He wants every appraisal talk at Aeos to start by saying a no is a budget decision, not a verdict on your worth. He says rejection is never personal, then adds that sometimes it is, but not there. (Secret Kata 1 at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=630s; Secret Kata 1 at 11:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=678s)
- "Indians have equated stress to hard work." He says stress is really a situation you cannot reliably overcome, and depression follows hopelessness or a status game you cannot win. (Secret Kata 1 at 4:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=245s)
- The **Sharma ji ka beta mechanism** is his name for the comparison voice running in your own head. He says this inner critic does the same damage as someone abusing you from outside. (Secret Kata 1 at 25:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1547s)
- Most Indian services companies are ruled by fear, he says, and Aeos wants the opposite: lower social threat, less defensiveness and more collaboration. (Secret Kata 1 at 20:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1209s)
- He says 99% of Indian companies run on stack ranking and fear, which makes them biologically destined for toxicity and poor at innovation, because managers have no training and nobody takes calculated risks. (Secret Kata 1 at 27:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1642s)
- Indians feel collective shame, where one bad event becomes shame on all Indians, while Americans ignore a thousand fraudsters. He calls it useless and says media feeds on it. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:46:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6364s)
- He relays an article about **hyper gambling**: on a standard salary with house prices shooting up, the savings math says you can afford a house in 30 years, so young people take massive long-shot risks hoping one works. He says he fully empathises, because salaries have not increased much while real estate has become very expensive. (Kata 7 at 0:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=55s; Kata 7 at 1:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=101s; Kata 13 at 50:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3052s)

## Why does he say Indians think zero-sum and complain?

- Indians do not like it when others do well because they treat the world as zero-sum. He says this is global but worse in India, which he calls a touch insecure. (Masters Union at 49:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2995s)
- Indians complain too much, he says. When employees complain even in good times, management becomes desensitised and stops doing good things. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:46:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6364s)
- His playtest observation: Chinese players go straight to mastering the boss, while Indian testers walk to a wall and try to clip through it. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:46:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6364s)
- He says he was clipped out of context for saying Indians complain a lot, that he complained a lot too when younger, and that he adds praise for Indian drive on purpose. (Masters Union at 49:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2949s)
- India is allergic to people who have generated wealth, yet everyone wants to be rich. He says Indians cloud the intentions of the wealthy as greed and that parents taught this. (Kata 13 at 6:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=386s)
- The Indian audience cannot be pleased. If a business is profitable it is called a scam, and if it loses money people ask why it is not profitable. (Masters Union at 57:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3424s)
- **India's four hobbies**, as he lists them: calling everything cringe, calling people scammers, hype, and working out a business's profit to attack it either way. (Masters Union at 56:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3376s)
- He says his old skepticism about the top funded startups was maybe cope. He spent a long time in what he calls **Startup India 2**, unknown startups raising from unknown tier 2 and tier 3 VCs, with no intro into **Startup India 1**, where he names Lenskart, Zomato and Cred. (Kata 4 at 4:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=291s; Kata 4 at 5:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=339s; Kata 4 at 8:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=487s)
- When he was very young he was one of the people replying to funded founders asking if the company was profitable, and he says that was a mistake. He says many of them play an all-or-nothing game of raising a lot of money to reach a monopoly position, like trying to hit a six every ball. (Kata 4 at 3:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=195s; Kata 4 at 4:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=291s)
- People in India are not honest about what they think, he says, so stated preferences mislead. What they really respond to is timeless greed and fear. (Kata 9 at 7:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=436s)

## Who actually wins in India, if not the quiet technical person?

- The Indian ideal of the quiet, technical, humble, conflict-avoidant person is wrong, he says. People credit the silent co-founder with building everything, which is almost never true, and he thinks the ideal is popular because it describes most people in India. (Kata 4 at 31:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1882s)
- He held that same ideal at 24 and 25 and says he was wrong. (Kata 4 at 32:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1930s)
- The founders with good P&Ls are verbal, social, charming people who resolve conflict and sell. He says about 90% of the successful people he has seen in India with positive P&Ls are salespeople or run sales organisations. (Kata 4 at 33:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2027s)
- He calls introversion a skill issue that has been turned into a virtue because everybody has it. You cannot hire or lead from behind the scenes. (Kata 4 at 34:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2075s)
- "Being reliable in India can be a bigger advantage than appearing brilliant." Thousands among 1.4 billion people are more brilliant than you, but many brilliant people take weeks to say yes or no, and a reliable person gets budgets to hire the brilliance they lack. (Kata 13 at 1:06:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=4007s)
- Trust runs the world, and he says trust in India is unusually valuable, though he does not know why. (Kata 13 at 1:02:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3767s)
- India has original thinkers, he says. What it lacked was internet access, networking and influence, and he now sees people globally copying Indian original thinkers. (Masters Union at 48:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2902s)
- He says the future of business in India will be creator-run. Creators get all the deal flow, see that many people they know want the same thing, and only need a team to solve it, which is how Aeos was built. (Kata 10 at 12:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=734s)
- At 100 crore of liquid wealth, he says, Indians gain a status where rules do not apply: they do not believe in the queue and skip it with a phone call, and most of their extra spending goes into buying influence. (Kata 13 at 23:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1386s; Kata 13 at 28:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1718s)

## Why are Indian startups unprofitable?

- Many Indian startups are unprofitable because they chase the one thing that is fully measurable, return on ad spend. Everyone runs ads, the cost climbs, and the only fix is raising prices. (Kata 6 at 11:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=662s)
- In India customers stop paying past a point. He describes getting stuck at a 25 rupee sale price with 30 to 35 rupees of advertising cost. (Kata 6 at 11:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=662s)
- Marketing managers in Indian startups often come from tech and are data-driven. They choose paid ads because the spend and result can be reported to the boss, which protects their reputation, not because it works. (Kata 6 at 7:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=425s)
- Celebrity ads are brand awareness misused as performance marketing. Startups brought in the biggest cricket and film stars expecting to spend 1 rupee and make 2 back, and he calls that senseless. (Kata 6 at 12:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=758s)
- The best Indian companies have organic acquisition, and that is their margin. He cites Zerodha, which runs no ads, and Physics Wallah, whose acquisition is entirely YouTube-driven. (Kata 6 at 11:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=710s)
- Companies forget the organic roots that made them. Many, including edtechs, get big on YouTube, raise money, decide they are a professional company now and switch to celebrity ads only. (Kata 6 at 12:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=758s)
- He does not rule out performance marketing. You need to sell units in the short term, and he endorses a line that early on all marketing should be performance and at scale all marketing should be brand. (Kata 6 at 12:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=758s)
- Venture-funded companies are unprofitable because of misaligned incentives, he says. With 40 crores of someone else's money a borderline hire gets a yes, while in a bootstrapped company every hire comes out of your own pocket. (KK Create at 32:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1951s)
- He set up in North Bangalore to get away from what he calls the **unprofitable startup mentality** of HSR, where he saw talent spend 10 years hopping between loss-making startups, taking money quickly and leaving before each one died. (Kata 1 at 13:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=827s)
- When hiring, Aeos asks whether a person worked at a profitable startup and what they contributed, and cares less about the last salary. He worries about people with 10 years across startups that all died who still quote a salary. (Kata 4 at 40:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2414s)
- Throwing money at people did not work, and he offers venture-funded startups in India as the proof: people he knows make a lot of money at VC-funded companies and still leave. If cash was all that mattered, he says Byju's would be the biggest company in India. (Kata 2 at 8:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=533s; Kata 2 at 26:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1592s)
- Acquirers in India buy revenue, not custom technology. His last company overbuilt technology on the belief that it would raise an acquirer's valuation. (Kata 6 at 30:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1816s)
- He no longer buys the venture startup dream of raising 10 million, then 100 million, and ending with investors owning 95%. He did that for 10 years and calls it a kind of lottery. (KK Create at 20:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1221s)
- He also says he was the foolish one for once thinking investors were foolish to ignore profitability. Money an investor does not need should aim for the moon, and an investor is fine if the company dies. (Kata 13 at 1:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=100s)

## What does he say about Indian consumers and audiences?

- Content in India gives an immediate return only for a product under 400 rupees, like a toothbrush or t-shirt. For a 2,000 to 5,000 rupee shoe people think, compare and go through a journey first. (Kata 10 at 15:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=923s)
- To make money, build for people who have capital. Founders building for tier three India ten years ago struggled because there was no disposable income, while someone selling something basic to rich people did better. (Kata 13 at 13:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=814s)
- A 2.16 crore flat advertised as luxury targets the group with about 1 crore of liquid net worth, not the 10 crore group. That group most wants the feeling of opulence and buys with a loan, and without the 1 crore they could not make the down payment. (Kata 13 at 35:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2102s; Kata 13 at 35:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2148s)
- Indians with around 10 crore are comfortable in their own skin, he says. Their marker is the kurta and Indian shoe brands, because they no longer chase foreign signals. (Kata 13 at 19:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1145s)
- India does not buy PC games, it pirates them unless they are mobile. He says BGMI makes north of $200 million in India while any Indian PC game makes a fraction of a fraction of that. (Kata 1 at 55:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3338s)
- App categories are saturated in India: one or two apps fill each category, and people cannot name a new app they downloaded but can name new creators they follow. (Kata 1 at 17:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1069s)
- He says a small set of channels can create a **simulated reality** for most of India. Ten headline channels of your own, ten friends with influential channels and 500 clippers or news pages reposting can get any news to 50 to 60% of the country. (PGX Ideas 2 at 56:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3392s)
- The creator economy will be far bigger once old school India wakes up, he says. Real estate and similar businesses still think billboard and newspaper, and he says 10 times bigger at one point and 100 times a few minutes later. (KK Create at 46:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2764s; KK Create at 54:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=3244s)
- India has slept on content as marketing while China is very sophisticated about it. He is very confident it becomes a huge industry in India within a few years. (Kata 10 at 38:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2289s)
- He cites YouTube figures of 19,000 crores contributed to India's GDP as of 2024 and 935,000 jobs, and says he sees no limit to the content market in India. (Kata 2 at 55:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3339s; Kata 1 at 1:15:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=4510s)
- He predicts video editing will get popular in India the way software did around 2020 to 2021, once editors start posting their pay. Editors are leaving him for 24 to 25 lakhs a year, and he expects salary videos in one or two years, then a bubble. (Masters Union at 53:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3187s)

## What is India's real edge, and why does he say it has been out-storytold?

- Soft power is probably the most critical national asset, he says, and India must choose between manufacturing and narrative. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:58:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7126s)
- America creates demand through global social media, and Indian firms clone the products and then get abused as copycats. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:58:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7126s)
- He calls China a **post-economic country** in some ways. When China became inward focused on its own social media platform, he says, demand and supply were both created by Chinese individuals, so the free cash flow went back into China and into more R&D spending. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:58:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7126s; PGX Ideas 2 at 2:00:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7221s)
- He expects India to go the Chinese way, with many multi-channel networks that make the influencer from day one. Aeos does this with a creator school, an editor school and its own distribution. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:58:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7126s; PGX Ideas 2 at 2:01:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7317s)
- "We've just been out story told." Indians were always good at hard work and technology, he says, but talent left because America's story was stronger. (Masters Union at 1:05:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3948s)
- India's real edge is drive and comradeship, not jugaad. Indians have the stamina to keep going, which is his explanation for Indian CEOs in Silicon Valley, and he says Americans he worked with tire after a few years and hand over to an Indian. (Masters Union at 1:03:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3806s)
- The **loudspeaker** comes first. He says America wins because people speak loudly about what they are building and the momentum brings talent and capital, and that anything valuable needs three or four iteration cycles you never get without it. (Masters Union at 57:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3472s)
- Give somebody 10 iteration cycles and, if they stay long enough, they are going to beat you. He says people made fun of China 10 years ago as cheap copy, and points to a video of Elon laughing at BYD 10 years ago. (Masters Union at 1:00:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3617s; Masters Union at 1:01:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3665s)
- He says that if India cut itself off from the shared social networks for a bit, free cash flow would start rotating inside Indian companies and they would start taking R&D risk, and he gives his own company putting content cash flow into games and Deepinder's Temple and aerospace work as examples. He asks for time for the first generation of Indian founders and says they are doing it faster and cheaper. (Masters Union at 1:01:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3711s; Masters Union at 1:02:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3758s)
- Any founder who wants to do something really ambitious in India must first build media, he says. Owned media is a **life raft**: with enough channels he can put his side out and let the audience judge from the output. (Masters Union at 49:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2949s)
- He does not believe in trading favours with journalists for coverage, because asking again and again erodes those relationships, so he would rather make his own channel. As an example, his company built a gaming channel from zero to about 150k on Instagram in 3 or 4 months so he would not have to keep asking gaming media to cover his game. (PGX Ideas 2 at 58:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3536s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:00:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3633s)
- "The storyteller wins the world, the storyteller eats the world." (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:00:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3633s)
- No ambitious project works without seeded positive media, he says. Skeptics are rewarded with attention for being negative early, which makes talent harder to get than funding, and positive coverage buys one or two years in which the product must catch up with the story. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:05:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3922s)
- To influence the feed in India you need many small channels, not only your own big ones. He wants a clipping network of 50,000 or more pages by partnering with existing clippers, and says it is India only for now and very manual. (Masters Union at 23:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1425s)

## What does he say about IITs, degrees and India's software jobs?

- He does not believe the IIT story. The coaching ecosystem, toppers and ads exist to convince you it is real, and he says there are some successes but half of it is fabricated and paid content. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:08:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4135s)
- He hires IITians for grind, not intelligence. His core team now has a lot of them, and he says he wants them because they have been through the grind. (Masters Union at 47:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2853s)
- Nobody knows which degree is right, and the best at anything wins. The best B.Com graduate beats the average B.Tech, and the best person with no degree at something they care about beats an IITian. (Kata 7 at 22:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1346s)
- Degrees are a misattribution most people have already paid for. Nobody in a room of about 300 said their degree got them there, and at about 10 lakhs each he called it 30 crores wasted in that room. (Kata 6 at 27:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1625s)
- He does not blame them, citing parental pressure. His point is that wrongly crediting a degree for your job makes a 10 to 12 lakh spend look sensible when the true reward was zero. (Kata 6 at 27:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1625s; Kata 6 at 29:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1769s)
- Stacking prestige internships and degrees is a status game dressed up as choice. He says one internship is enough and that degrees have not been valuable for anything. (Kata 7 at 24:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1490s)
- Going to college so that someone stamps you as good enough is the wrong reason to go. Surgeons need certification, but many jobs do not. (Why Belief Matters at 25:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1531s)
- Anything taught in college is 4 or 5 years outdated, he says, and you relearn in the real world anyway. Apprenticeship under someone in the field is the best path, and failing that, some action every day for a year or two. (Why Belief Matters at 38:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2292s)
- A portfolio makes college and past employer irrelevant for visible skills, and Aeos prefers a track record of attempts over CV or college. (Kata 8 at 13:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=819s; Kata 4 at 50:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3041s)
- Ten years ago he believed very strongly that learning to code was the one thing where social mobility was open, with no fancy degree needed and no nepotism. In November 2026 he says he has fully U-turned on it after AI, based on new evidence. (Why Belief Matters at 39:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2340s)
- He says India's software exports will not stay near $250 billion. He used to think Infosys might die and now thinks it may survive on requirements analysis, with far fewer people. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:35:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5745s)
- Being a software engineer is socially acceptable in India but very risky right now, he says, given the number of software engineers coming out of India against the number of jobs, with funding for pure software declining. He says salaries at software companies in India are still 30 to 35k a month, the same as 10 years ago. (Kata 9 at 24:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1490s; Kata 13 at 50:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3052s)

## What does he say about government, Bollywood and gaming in India?

- Government officials understand what AI will do but cannot say it publicly. He used to think they were clueless and now says they know a lot but must not shock the public the way a creator can. (PGX Ideas 2 at 2:03:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7412s)
- He says an adviser to the CM in Maharashtra told him they now recognise creator jobs as true jobs and as mini entrepreneurship, so a creator with four or five employees is a real unit. He says his company has been invited into government offices only in the last 6 months, as of March 2026. (PGX Ideas 2 at 2:02:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7365s)
- He says India has a populist leader because it has so many divided cultures, and a coordinated system needs a strong leader crushing the infighting. (Kata 2 at 54:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3289s)
- He says his company has played a role in the Bangalore police AI avatars, and is helping Bangalore police implement a project where a caller to 112 who speaks any language apart from Kannada may get an AI bot. (Kata 1 at 38:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2328s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:42:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6127s)
- Bollywood has no next superstars, he says, because its coordination is fading and it lacks social distribution. It once could give a debutant two years of buildup and cannot now. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:12:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4355s)
- He avoids movies because he would have to play by other people's rules, and picked games because there is no one there. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:12:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4355s)
- Nepotism works in movies because the world is already built. He says 90% of Reddit discussion is about star kids, so people want to see their debut. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 7:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=431s)
- He builds Aeos with family and calls it an **open family**, not Bollywood nepotism: outsiders who give their life to the company can become family. (Masters Union at 38:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2286s)
- He claims that out of 1.4 billion people in India only one team has a playable third-person demo that thousands of people have played. (Kata 12 at 1:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=102s)
- Aeos makes high-fidelity PC games and bets that phones will run them in four to five years, which is what opens the Indian market. (Kata 1 at 54:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3291s)
- Unreal Engine real-time VFX talent barely exists in India. He says zero in one talk and about three people in another, and his team learned from Korean tutorials. (Kata 12 at 28:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1734s; Masters Union at 21:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1281s)
- Authentic modern India cannot be shown without slums, he says, because within one square kilometre of even the most beautiful place you find one. So the game was moved to the 1950s. (Masters Union at 18:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1138s)
- The game's hero is guided by Vishnu and lent some of his powers for a few days instead of being a god. He says it was written that way so they do not get cancelled. (Masters Union at 15:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=949s)
- He is not sure there is a market for Bollywood in a game or that the game will make money, while being very confident it will be a good game. (Masters Union at 22:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1330s)

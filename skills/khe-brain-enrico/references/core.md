# Core

Read this for a broad question, then open one theme file for the detail. Every line is a paraphrase of what he says in the video cited. The videos are solo scripted essays published over five years, from 2021-08-24 to 2026-11-15, so a prediction or a view on AI belongs to the year it was said. Three of the videos were made with a company's team. Check [disagreements](disagreements.md) before giving any of these as his settled view: on listening to users, on chat as an interface and on whether products got worse, he has said different things in different years.

## The thesis

- He holds that the products people use every day look simple and sleek only because they are very complex underneath. Products that feel simple do not have less complexity than scary-looking ones: he says they probably have more, it is only hidden, and the hidden part is what makes everything work. (YouTube Does This Anytime You Watch And You Don't Realize It at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=49_a3jkdBdI&t=405s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Figma at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5t8dh3DSdBk&t=540s)

Everything else here is that idea applied to a detail, a wait, a feed, an interface, an AI product, a device, a business or a channel.

## The thirteen ideas

- He uses Tesler's law and puts it this way: "Complexity can never be destroyed, it can only be moved." His example is copy-paste, where the computer handles the complexity, so what you get is perceived simplicity, not real simplicity; he says Claude Code works the same way. (Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=360s)
- His thesis on errors is that everyday tech is not designed for perfection, speed or looks: "It's secretly designed to make up for how humans fail." He gives the to-do app Things as an example: type June 31st and it quietly changes it to July 1st without showing an error. He says perfection should not be a condition for using technology. (How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=45s; How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=360s; How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=810s)
- His main framework is seven stacked levels of tech design: visual details, interactions and behaviour that feel human, technology that makes the experience possible, defaults, structural design of the app's logic, strategic reframing, and product vision. He says vision alone fails: General Magic had the touchscreen smartphone vision years before the iPhone and flopped, and what separated Steve Jobs's team was all the levels underneath. (The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=45s; The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 15:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=945s)
- He says the hardest thing about building tech is humans and their weird psychology, and he holds that a psychology principle is neutral: Amazon and a scammy astrology site use the exact same ones, in ethical and unethical ways. He says defaults control the behaviour of millions for good and bad, because most people never change a setting. (How Your Tech Tricks You at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7nnyv4yJGJc&t=450s; The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=135s; The Internet Wants Your Money Here's How To Outsmart It at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4OLt9pFlRvo&t=180s; The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=450s)
- By 2024 he called this the most important and counterintuitive rule in product management: "Don't listen to users but watch them as carefully as possible." He says users are bad at innovating but already struggle with the problems the next billion-dollar company will solve. In 2021 his position had been that you can only win by listening to the people who use your product. (How Your Tech Tricks You at 15:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7nnyv4yJGJc&t=945s; How Your Tech Tricks You at 16:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7nnyv4yJGJc&t=990s; The Spotify Shuffle Button is a Lie at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NhAIA-o7f4M&t=225s)
- He holds that how a product makes you wait matters more than how long the wait is, which he said in 2022 and repeated in 2026. He argues interface animation is not decoration, and says knowing when not to animate is probably more important than knowing when to. (The Sneaky Psychology Of Loading Screens at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g8uhdyLTAvY&t=0s; The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=0s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=0s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=585s)
- In 2023 he called it a misconception that people at tech companies have evil agendas. He said what decides whether a product delivers value or addiction is the objective its build and test loop is pointed at, and that engagement won because it is easy to measure and because ads, subscriptions and gamified microtransactions all pay for it. By 2026 he called notifications the single most powerful habit tool, because they are the only push lever a product has. (The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=0s; The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=135s; The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=405s; The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=540s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notifications at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dx2yPk0FsGM&t=270s)
- His core input theory, from March 2026, says every device is defined by one core interaction that everything else is built on, and that once you understand it you can predict which future products will succeed or fail before they launch. On a phone scrolling comes first and tapping second, which he calls the main reason smartphones as we know them won. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=45s; The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=630s; The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=315s)
- In September 2025 he said graphical interfaces are great at output and bad at input, while chatbots and voice assistants are the reverse. His June 2026 position is that the future is three interface types together: traditional interfaces for simple jobs, agents for very complex ones, and generative UI for the large middle where most everyday tasks sit. (The Weird Death Of User Interfaces at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG4ONHqF1qg&t=270s; The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=540s; The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=810s)
- In May 2024 his fix for AI bolted onto apps was invisible AI: start from a real problem, apply AI inside the interface people already know, and do not announce it, because users only care that the problem gets solved. In February 2025 he said AI models will become a commodity and that the real money is in the application people use, which he calls the car, and not in the model, which he calls the engine. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=720s; AI Has A Huge Problem at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=765s; People Still Don't Understand AI at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=180s; People Still Don't Understand AI at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=540s; People Still Don't Understand AI at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=360s)
- In October 2023 he predicted that glasses running multimodal AI will be the backbone of the smartphone's successor, and said he had been wrong in several videos to say true AR would define it, since AR is only the visual output. His test for any new interface is that it must be easier than the one before it. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=585s; Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=720s; We Need To Talk About The AI Pin at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2-L-Gv0wr0&t=270s)
- In May 2025 he named the law of the marginal user as the reason the products you love get worse the more popular they become. In September 2025 he said the idea that tech products are getting worse and companies greedier is not true, and named the real problem freeification: a product that is incredible but is seen as having no monetary value, so people think it should be free. (Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=0s; Tech's Weirdest Problem at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=0s; Tech's Weirdest Problem at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=225s)
- His core reason for quitting his product manager job in October 2024 is that choosing the right thing to work on beats effort: "The thing you're working on is more important than how hard you work." He names four levers that make anything exponential, capital, labor, media and code, and pushes media and code because he lacks the first two at scale. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=90s; I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=720s)

## His method, in the order someone would build a product

- Go back from the idea to the problem. He says your idea is a solution, and that in 99 percent of cases a shower idea fails because nobody wants it. He calls jobs to be done one of the most important frameworks in design: people hire a product to solve a problem. (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=0s; Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=45s; Airbnb Just Hacked The Travel Industry Again at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OwiztfnZwE4&t=180s)
- Pick the audience that has the problem in its most severe form. Against the objection that Facebook and Amazon serve billions, he says Facebook began by connecting students at universities and Amazon began by selling books. (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=90s; Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=180s)
- Talk to that audience and not to friends and family, which he calls the worst mistake you can make. In an interview, talk about their lives and problems, ask about real past behaviour and not opinions about the future, and show the prototype only in the last few minutes. (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=225s; Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=315s)
- Test demand without building, with a fake door test or a Wizard of Oz test, and make each attempt cheap. His claw machine model says you improve your odds by knowing which item to grab and by making each play cheaper. He lists Google Sheets, Airtable, Typeform, Bubble and Zapier as enough for a first version. (Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=360s; Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=225s; Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=405s; Do You Have a Genius Startup Idea at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HABFEs90MZ8&t=450s)
- Start beside your main job. He says a tech company does not need $10 million of venture capital, and that you shrink the ramen period, the stretch where a stable job out-earns your own project, by building the project beside the job. In February 2024 he said not taking money can be your secret weapon. (I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=675s; I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=585s; I Quit My 6-Figures Dream Job In Tech at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbFufWfJPZ8&t=630s; The Weird Rise Of Anti-Startups at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xL1MOOD5Ox8&t=720s)
- Ship a basic first version. Looking at the first versions of famous products such as Amazon, he says these companies all started from something very basic, very simple and sometimes really bad, and that this gives every builder permission to take the first step. He also records a case against launching fast: Figma worked in private for 3 years. (Famous Apps Looked Like This at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Sn8Glj5NW4&t=405s; Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Figma at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0jze5RbyoW4&t=540s)
- Show a new user one thing. He says a first-time WhatsApp user saw just messaging, which flattened the learning curve and got people fast to the aha moment where they feel the product's value, and that great products reveal themselves step by step like Civilization. (Why Half The World Uses WhatsApp at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1O1u0UO99NE&t=270s; The Weird Way Tech Steals From Games at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GypAKofWGBk&t=540s)
- Start from the perfect interaction and then find the technology for it, not the other way round. He says the technology that matters is the kind that disappears. (The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=450s)
- Decide the logic of the whole app, which he calls structural design. He says a good structural decision makes everything after it simpler. In Notion everything is a page, in Slack everything is a channel, and in his own product Flask he made everything a tag. (The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=810s; The 7 Levels of Tech Design at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQVRfadJkJk&t=720s)
- Find features by watching real use. He says Gmail's undo send and iOS filling in one-time SMS codes came from watching what people do, and that one-finger zoom in Google Maps exists because people on bikes cannot pinch. (How Your Tech Tricks You at 16:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7nnyv4yJGJc&t=990s; The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=405s)
- Put a new feature through three questions: do people want it, can we do it, and should we do it. He says the best products are built by saying no, and his answer to feature creep is progressive disclosure, which shows basic options by default and reveals an advanced one only in the context where it is needed. (I Redesigned YouTube's Worst New Feature at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELoNeW7GKog&t=0s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Figma at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5t8dh3DSdBk&t=765s; Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Figma at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0jze5RbyoW4&t=180s; Why Everyone Is Obsessed With Figma at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0jze5RbyoW4&t=225s)
- Pick the objective knowing what it does. He says what decides whether a product delivers value or addiction is the objective and how it is measured, that the loop just optimises for whatever number it is given, and that A/B tests hand decisions to people who do not care. (The Real Reason Your Tech Makes You Unhappy at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vbg5TeoLTW0&t=135s; What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=225s; Why Tech Gets Terrible When It Gets Popular at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQLVgmhgtpQ&t=450s)
- Build for the mistakes people make. He says a well-made button shows feedback on press, fires on release, cancels if the finger slides off, re-arms if it slides back and has a tap area larger than what you see, and that autosave removed a whole class of failure. (How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=360s; The Lies That Make Your Tech Actually Work at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rAVV4kAYlI8&t=90s; How Your Tech Is Built For You To Fail at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HWPFd8CQo8&t=450s)
- Decide how the product waits and moves. He says many loading screens can be removed by assuming success, and his rule is that whether to show loading depends on how important the action feels, so a payment confirmation needs it. His Flask practice is instant for anything used hundreds of times a day and animated for things users must learn exist. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=45s; The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=135s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME&t=585s)
- Expect the last 1% to cost the most, then finish the details. His 1% dilemma says reaching 99% of use cases is fairly easy and the last 1% of odd edge cases takes exponential resources. He says a product feels good because of a thousand small details, each of which changes little alone, and in November 2025 he said fun features are worth shipping even when they move no metric. (The Most Dangerous App That Everyone Loves at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2qOiKA56tI&t=90s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notion at 16:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b00sgRR_Vc0&t=990s; How Silicon Valley Killed Fun at 15:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DYdLq9icgrI&t=945s)

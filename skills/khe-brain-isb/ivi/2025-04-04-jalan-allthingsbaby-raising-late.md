# Building Allthingsbaby and raising late, Akshay Jalan

Typed notes from an orientation talk by Akshay Jalan, co-founder of Allthingsbaby.com and Brainsmith. He told how the company moved from toys to baby products and why it did not raise money until it found product-market fit.

## What was said

### From toys to baby products

- Akshay Jalan says the starting insight was that the early childhood market was not very price sensitive, and the company began as a toy company.
- He says they looked up the revenue of the top toy players on the Registrar of Companies website and found it did not match their goals.
- He says baby products were growing, so they started distributing those brands.
- He says the government changed its policy on toys in 2019 and stopped toy imports.
- He says FirstCry is speaking to Bharat and not to India One, so his company worked on that segment.
- He says the company has physical stores in Bengaluru and Hyderabad, takes part in curated baby exhibitions offline, and uses Meta and Google online.

### Raising money

- He says money was easy to get in 2018 and 2019, but they did not raise because they did not know what they wanted to do with it.
- He says his co-founder brought the grounding that they did not need a press article.
- He says they found product-market fit in 2023 and then raised money.
- He says to go and meet investors you are sure you do not want to raise from in the early days, as net practice.
- He says the cap table is very important, and if you take money from friends and family you must be clear about who holds what shares.
- He says they raised from a family office, and that due diligence is a very long process.
- He says money raised is not your money until it hits your account.

### Other lessons

- He says a support system is crucial, and that friends who are also founders can relate when you need to vent.
- He says Mumbai and Delhi never forgive you as a brand, while Hyderabad and Bengaluru are more experimental.

## Examples and cases used

- FirstCry: the large player that he says speaks to Bharat and not to India One.

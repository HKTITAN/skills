# Sources

## The corpus and the engines

Every number this skill plans from comes from the launch-film corpus Harshit Khemani measured on 2026-09-28 (29 Skale pieces and 18 acclaimed films, at native frame rate) and from the engines it reconciles with (HyperFrames, cinetic, scenario-kinetic-film). Each reference names its own sources at its foot; [overview](overview.md) lists the corpus.

## The Product Launch Video Playbook

Hiten Shah, "The Product Launch Video Playbook", a Google Doc updated 2026-11-16: <https://docs.google.com/document/d/18jpuStgJUqbx-A5M_IGDLT98gNThoRh0D3C0k7sgRJs>. It is its author's work. This skill's use of it is Harshit Khemani's adaptation, not written or endorsed by the author, and every sentence here was written fresh.

Taken as ideas:

- deciding the viewer, the promise and the one moment before any editing, as a five-line brief ([brief](brief.md));
- the Beam example: a first cut that showed the route to the result and not the result ([brief](brief.md), Gotcha);
- listing the material first, marking each file as real evidence, a reference or missing, and matching each kind of material to a method ([brief](brief.md));
- asking for three genuinely different approaches before committing, and reviewing the scene plan before rendering ([brief](brief.md));
- the Shot Map: a viewer question, a source and a claim on every scene ([brief](brief.md));
- picking a structure by the viewer's question, from eight 2026 launch films, and borrowing the decision rather than the surface ([structures](structures.md));
- the cold-viewer test, with the three stops at 3 s, 10 s and the last frame, and a revision record ([review](review.md), [verify](verify.md));
- a family of cuts, and measuring comprehension and the next action apart from reach ([publish](publish.md)).

No phrase is quoted. The names Video Brief and Shot Map are the playbook's; the plan keys `brief` and the beat fields `question`, `source` and `claim` are this skill's.

## Left out, and why

| In the playbook | Here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Scene times for the eight films (Granola's brief at about 0:21, Cursor's Ready to Merge at about 0:19) | Left out of every target and the gate | Read from sampled frames and marked approximate by the author; this skill plans only from films measured at native frame rate |
| The 25-, 22- and 18-second scene plans written for each film | Not reproduced | They are the author's suggestions, not measurements; the beat timings come from [launch-video-structure](launch-video-structure.md) |
| The prompts to give an AI agent, word for word | Replaced by the steps and checks in [brief](brief.md) | A prompt to copy is the author's text; the method behind it is what changes the work |
| The 1 to 5 review scorecard | Left out | The review here ranks findings with evidence ([review](review.md)); a score beside them adds a number nobody measured |
| Reach tables and engagement snapshots for named launches | Left out | Reach is not evidence a film worked, which the playbook says too, and several figures were unverified snapshots |
| The length studies (Motion The Agency, Doomers AI) | Cited once, with what each measures ([publish](publish.md)) | They argue against a universal short rule and prove no length rule either |
| The six supporting teardowns (Vercel Backends, Framer 3.0, ElevenLabs Scribe v2, Framer F1 Keyboard, Cursor for iOS, Simular Box) | Reduced to one rule: a film whose job is belief or desire is judged against that job ([structures](structures.md)) | Counterexamples, not structures to plan from |
| The author's workshop and its links | Left out | Not method |
| Sound design of the eight films | Left out | The author did not review their audio fully; sound stays with [launch-video-sound](launch-video-sound.md) |

# Is this startup working?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: the Entrepreneurship files cited here credit Sean Ellis in one title and name no other author, no professor, no course and no source for any figure, including the failure percentages in a file whose title says research-based. A number inside a worked example is that example's number, never a benchmark. These files disagree on the LTV to CAC threshold, on the retention bar for fit, on how much evidence a pivot needs and on how many pivot types there are; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## What counts as a startup

- A startup is a young company founded to solve a problem, serve a market or explore an unproven business model; it works under extreme uncertainty with limited capital and burns cash pursuing rapid growth instead of immediate profitability. (Entrepreneurship: What Defines a Startup, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/foundations-what-defines-a-startup.md)
- The file lists five characteristics: an unproven business model, a runway of months, full-time founder involvement, a target of 10x to 100x expansion, and high risk and reward, given as 90% or more failing and winners returning 100x to 1000x. (Entrepreneurship: What Defines a Startup, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/foundations-what-defines-a-startup.md)
- Not every new company is a startup: a family restaurant is a new business and a venture-backed restaurant chain is a startup, and the distinction is deliberate pursuit of scaled growth. (Entrepreneurship: What Defines a Startup, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/foundations-what-defines-a-startup.md)

## Build, measure, learn

- The loop runs build, measure, learn, then a pivot or persevere decision, then repeats; each cycle completes with real customer feedback about customer assumptions. (Entrepreneurship: The Build-Measure-Learn Loop, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/lean-the-build-measure-learn-loop.md)
- An MVP is the simplest product that lets a team test one critical assumption with real customers; the file says it is not the smallest possible product, a poor version of the final product, an early release to the general public or something embarrassing to ship. (Entrepreneurship: Build Phase: Creating the MVP, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/lean-build-phase-creating-the-mvp.md)
- The file's MVP examples include Airbnb in 2008 as a web app listing apartments in San Francisco, Dropbox in 2008 as a 3-minute video that it says drew 75,000 beta signups in one week without a working product, and Slack as an internal communication tool for a gaming company; it also lists landing pages, a concierge MVP and a Wizard of Oz MVP. (Entrepreneurship: Build Phase: Creating the MVP, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/lean-build-phase-creating-the-mvp.md)
- Vanity metrics are total signups without retention, raw traffic without engagement, revenue without profitability and gross metrics without cohort analysis. (Entrepreneurship: Measure Phase: Actionable Metrics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/lean-measure-phase-actionable-metrics.md)
- Customer development is set out in three stages: problem at 0 to 3 months with 50 or more interviews, solution at 3 to 9 months with 10 to 20 paying customers, and scaling from 9 months with a goal of LTV to CAC above 3:1, predictable sales and 50 or more paying customers. (Entrepreneurship: Customer Development Stages, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/lean-customer-development-stages.md)

## Product-market fit

- Product-market fit is when a product satisfies strong market demand: customers request it before it is available and actively recommend it. (Entrepreneurship: What Product-Market Fit Actually Means, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pmf-what-product-market-fit-actually-means.md)
- Before fit the file describes "thrashing": product, messaging or target change constantly, growth is slow, churn is high and word of mouth is weak. (Entrepreneurship: What Product-Market Fit Actually Means, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pmf-what-product-market-fit-actually-means.md)
- The test asks how a user would feel if they could no longer use the product, and 40% or more answering very disappointed is a strong fit indicator; the file's title credits Sean Ellis. (Entrepreneurship: The Sean Ellis Test, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pmf-the-sean-ellis-test.md)
- The signals file says cohort retention curves flatten at high levels, given as 50% or more month over month for consumer and 70% or more for B2B, and calls repeat usage the truest signal of value. (Entrepreneurship: Clear PMF Signals, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pmf-clear-pmf-signals.md)
- The scaling-phase file lists five readiness checks: 30-day retention above targets given as 40 to 50% for consumer and 70% or more for B2B, word of mouth at 20% or more of new customers, CAC below LTV on conservative estimates, consistent customer feedback, and 2 to 3 segments tested with clear fit in one. (Entrepreneurship: After PMF: The Scaling Phase, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pmf-after-pmf-the-scaling-phase.md)
- Five fit mistakes are listed: mistaking traction for fit, ignoring churn, solving a luxury problem, being in love with the solution, and premature scaling. (Entrepreneurship: Common Product-Market Fit Mistakes, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/pmf-common-product-market-fit-mistakes.md)

## Unit economics

- CAC is total sales and marketing spend divided by new customers acquired, and LTV is revenue per customer over the lifetime less the cost of goods or services. (Entrepreneurship: Unit Economics: The Foundation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/bizmodel-unit-economics-the-foundation.md)
- The foundation file calls LTV to CAC the most critical metric: 3:1 or higher is sustainable, 2:1 is difficult but possible if other factors are strong, and below 2:1 the unit economics are broken. (Entrepreneurship: Unit Economics: The Foundation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/bizmodel-unit-economics-the-foundation.md)
- The scaling file gives 3:1 to 5:1 as the ideal LTV to CAC range, calls below 3:1 unsustainable at scale and marks above 5:1 as a sign of possible underinvestment in growth. (Entrepreneurship: Unit Economics: Foundation of Scaling, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/scaling-unit-economics-foundation-of-scaling.md)
- The ideal payback period is given as under 12 months for B2C and under 24 months for B2B, and a longer payback requires more cash. (Entrepreneurship: Unit Economics: The Foundation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/bizmodel-unit-economics-the-foundation.md)
- The 3x rule says a company can grow sustainably at about 3x CAC in annual revenue per customer; in the file's example a CAC of $100 against $300 a year is sustainable and against $50 a year is unviable. (Entrepreneurship: The 3x Rule for Sustainable Growth, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/bizmodel-the-3x-rule-for-sustainable-growth.md)

## Scaling and slowing down

- Three scaling phases are given with dollar bands the file does not label as revenue: founder-led sales at $0 to $500K annually, sales team building at $500K to $5M with expected growth of 3 to 5x year over year, and a scaling machine at $5M to $50M or more with expected growth of 2 to 3x annually. (Entrepreneurship: Scaling Phases, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/scaling-scaling-phases.md)
- Blitzscaling is extreme growth at the cost of unit economics, acceptable when the market is winner-take-most, first-mover advantage is critical and capital is available. Sustainable growth is preferred when unit economics are challenging, capital is limited or the market is not winner-take-most. (Entrepreneurship: Blitzscaling vs. Sustainable Growth, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/scaling-blitzscaling-vs-sustainable-growth.md)
- The file lists five reasons to consider slowing: degrading unit economics, fraying culture, visible burnout in founders and early employees, fundraising just to stay alive, and visible profitability with no interest in venture scale. (Entrepreneurship: When to Slow Down, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/scaling-when-to-slow-down.md)

## Failure, pivots and shutting down

- The file ranks five failure reasons with a percentage each: no market need at 42%, ran out of cash at 29%, wrong team at 23%, got outcompeted at 19% and pricing or cost issues at 18%. (Entrepreneurship: Top Failure Reasons, Research-Based, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/failure-top-failure-reasons-research-based.md)
- The same file's early warning for pricing or cost failure is CAC above LTV or a ratio below 3:1, with gross margins below 50% for SaaS and below 40% for marketplaces. (Entrepreneurship: Top Failure Reasons, Research-Based, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/failure-top-failure-reasons-research-based.md)
- Pivot signals are retention declining month over month despite experiments, no organic growth after reasonable effort, unit economics broken and unfixable, and customers not using key features or using the product in unexpected ways. (Entrepreneurship: Pivot vs. Persevere Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/lean-pivot-vs-persevere-framework.md)
- The pivot-or-persevere file says not to pivot after only 20 conversations, since 50 or more are needed, or after only 3 months, since it says most successful pivots took more than 6 months to validate. (Entrepreneurship: Pivot vs. Persevere Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/lean-pivot-vs-persevere-framework.md)
- The pivot framework file asks whether enough has been tried to show an assumption is wrong and offers 100 customer conversations, a number it calls arbitrary; its example test runs for 8 weeks with a decision date. (Entrepreneurship: The Pivot Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/failure-the-pivot-framework.md)
- The pivot-or-persevere file lists seven pivot types: customer segment, feature, platform, technology, business model, zoom in and zoom out. (Entrepreneurship: Pivot vs. Persevere Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/lean-pivot-vs-persevere-framework.md)
- The pivot types file lists five: segment, feature, channel, product and business model. (Entrepreneurship: Common Pivot Types, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/failure-common-pivot-types.md)
- Shutting down is to be considered when multiple pivots have gained no traction, co-founder conflict is irreconcilable, passion for the problem is lost, unit economics are fundamentally broken and unfixable, or the market timing window has closed. (Entrepreneurship: When to Shut Down, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/entrepreneurship/rules/failure-when-to-shut-down.md)

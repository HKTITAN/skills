# How do I work a case across subjects?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: the MBA Tutor files name Porter, Boston Consulting Group, Ansoff and McKinsey only in framework names and titles, and name no other author, no professor, no course and no source for any figure. A number inside an example is that example's number, never a benchmark. Several of these files disagree with each other: on how many steps a complete analysis has, on which way the CAC and LTV ratio is read, on what a 10 and 90 split measures, on whether network effects differentiate, and on why incumbents fail; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## The steps of a case

- Step 1 maps the situation with three tools: the 5Cs of company, customers, competitors, collaborators and context; SWOT; and Porter's Five Forces. Its output is a list of key strategic facts, the constraints and opportunities, and an understanding of the competitive environment. (MBA Tutor: Step 1: Situation Analysis, Context and Diagnosis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/case-step-1-situation-analysis-context-and-diagnosis.md)
- Step 2 separates symptoms, such as declining revenue or increasing customer churn, from root causes, and asks what would disprove the diagnosis. It sorts problems into four types: performance, strategic choice, operational and organisational. (MBA Tutor: Step 2: Problem Identification, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/case-step-2-problem-identification.md)
- Step 3 asks for at least 3 credible options generated before any is evaluated, each then judged on feasibility, impact, risk and fit with strategy. The traps it lists are false dichotomy, status quo bias and unrealistic options. (MBA Tutor: Step 3: Alternative Generation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/case-step-3-alternative-generation.md)
- Step 4 makes the recommendation a criteria-based evaluation: financial, strategic fit, operational feasibility, customer impact and risk tolerance, weighted if quantifying. The file's example weights financial ROI 30%, strategic fit 25%, execution risk 25% and customer impact 20% and gives options A, B and C weighted scores of 14.5, 17.5 and 16.0, with no scale shown behind the scores. (MBA Tutor: Step 4: Recommendation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/case-step-4-recommendation.md)
- Step 5 turns the decision into action under five headings: timeline and sequencing, resources required, metrics and monitoring, risk mitigation and stakeholder management. Its output is an actionable plan with clear ownership, timeline, metrics and contingencies. (MBA Tutor: Step 5: Implementation Plan, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/case-step-5-implementation-plan.md)
- The integration exercise presents a ten-step list as the complete framework: situation, strategy, operations, finance, marketing, economics, leadership, technology, cross-domain connections and recommendation. (MBA Tutor: Integration Exercise: the Complete Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/case-integration-exercise-the-complete-analysis.md)

## The six frameworks

- Five Forces assesses the attractiveness of an industry through supplier power, buyer power, the threat of entrants, the threat of substitutes and competitive rivalry; the file's title credits Porter. The application is to assess each force and identify which are attractive and which are threats. (MBA Tutor: Porter's Five Forces, Competitive Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/frameworks-porters-five-forces-competitive-analysis.md)
- VRIO asks four questions of a resource: does it help the company serve customers or manage costs, do competitors have similar resources, can competitors replicate it, and is the company organised to leverage it. Sustainable competitive advantage requires all four. (MBA Tutor: VRIO Framework, Resource Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/frameworks-vrio-framework-resource-analysis.md)
- The matrix the file's title credits to Boston Consulting Group classifies products or business units by growth and market share: invest in stars, milk cash cows, decide whether to invest in or divest question marks, and divest dogs unless there is a strategic reason to keep them. (MBA Tutor: Boston Consulting Group Matrix, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/frameworks-boston-consulting-group-matrix.md)
- Strategic group analysis maps competitors on two relevant dimensions; the file's examples are price against innovation for luxury cars, cost against service for airlines and price against quality for restaurants. Companies in the same group compete directly, and switching between groups requires fundamental change. (MBA Tutor: Strategic Group Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/frameworks-strategic-group-analysis.md)
- The Ansoff matrix, credited only in the file's title, lists four growth options: market penetration at low risk with limited upside, product development and market development each at medium risk and reward, and diversification at high risk and high reward. (MBA Tutor: Ansoff Matrix, Growth Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/frameworks-ansoff-matrix-growth-strategy.md)
- The 7-S framework, credited to McKinsey in the file's title, diagnoses organisational alignment across strategy, structure, systems, skills, style, staff and shared values. A change in one S requires changes in the others, and misalignment creates friction and failure. (MBA Tutor: McKinsey 7-S Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/frameworks-mckinsey-7-s-framework.md)

## How the subjects connect

- Supply chain design reflects strategic positioning: the file pairs cost leadership at Walmart with a highly efficient supply chain and centralised distribution, and speed at Zara with an agile supply chain. Operations also limit strategy: a company cannot pursue low cost if its operations require hand-crafted quality. (MBA Tutor: Operations ↔ Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/connections-operations-strategy.md)
- Price elasticity determines the optimal markup, and marketing adds brand preference, reference pricing, anchoring and loss aversion. Segmentation identifies customers with different elasticity levels so the company can price discriminate to capture consumer surplus. (MBA Tutor: Marketing ↔ Economics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/connections-marketing-economics.md)
- Strategy varies by market structure: the file says differentiation is impossible in a commodity market and essential in monopolistic competition, and that capacity and network effects are critical in oligopoly. (MBA Tutor: Economics ↔ Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/connections-economics-strategy.md)
- A SaaS business with a 30% CAC/LTV ratio is one the file expects to be self-sustaining, with venture capital unnecessary, while Uber and Lyft at less than 1x CAC/LTV are described as burning capital and requiring ongoing venture funding to achieve scale economies. (MBA Tutor: Entrepreneurship ↔ Finance, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/connections-entrepreneurship-finance.md)
- This file gives healthy unit economics as 3x+ LTV/CAC, at which scaling becomes capital-efficient, and says positive unit economics with a high CAC/LTV mean scaling requires massive capital. (MBA Tutor: Unit Economics and Scalability, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/integrated-unit-economics-and-scalability.md)
- The file's example is that digital transformation fails technically 10% of the time and organisationally 90% of the time; without leadership, a change in operations creates organisational friction and failure. (MBA Tutor: Leadership ↔ Operations, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/connections-leadership-operations.md)
- The change management file gives 10% and 90% as shares of effort: technical implementation is often 10% of the effort and organisational change is 90%. (MBA Tutor: Change Management and Implementation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/integrated-change-management-and-implementation.md)
- The competitive advantage file lists network effects as a source of differentiation, beside brand, quality, service and customisation, and rates network effects highly defensible at 5+ years before competitors replicate them. (MBA Tutor: Competitive Advantage, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/integrated-competitive-advantage.md)
- The platform strategy file says that in network effect markets a company wins big or loses, and that differentiation is often impossible. (MBA Tutor: Platform Strategy and Winner-Take-Most Dynamics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/advanced-platform-strategy-and-winner-take-most-dynamics.md)

## Patterns that recur across cases

- The disruption template runs in six stages: the incumbent optimises for today, an entrant targets an underserved customer, the incumbent dismisses the entrant, the entrant improves its product, the incumbent's cost structure prevents a competitive response, and the entrant wins. The file's examples are Netflix, Uber, Airbnb and Tesla. (MBA Tutor: Industry Disruption Pattern, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/patterns-industry-disruption-pattern.md)
- This file gives the reason incumbents struggle as organisational rigidity created by a successful business model. Its example is Kodak, which had the digital camera and could not cannibalise its film business. (MBA Tutor: Digital Disruption and Strategic Response, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/advanced-digital-disruption-and-strategic-response.md)
- A once-dominant company in decline ignores early signals, attributes the decline to temporary market factors, doubles down on its previous strategy, refuses to cannibalise its existing product, and is forced by crisis into radical change too late. The file's examples are Kodak, Blockbuster and Nokia. (MBA Tutor: Performance Decline Pattern, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/patterns-performance-decline-pattern.md)
- A company built for one model, whether founder-led, handmade or specialised, struggles to scale: culture changes, quality becomes inconsistent, the founder burns out, operational systems are inadequate and the cost structure breaks. (MBA Tutor: Scaling Challenges Pattern, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/patterns-scaling-challenges-pattern.md)
- A company enters a new market either at low cost, which requires a cost structure the incumbent cannot match, or differentiated, which requires accepting a smaller market; the file states that most entries fail by trying both at once. Its examples are Southwest Airlines, Lexus and Huawei. (MBA Tutor: Market Entry Strategy Pattern, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/patterns-market-entry-strategy-pattern.md)

## Teaching a topic and checking understanding

- Product-market fit is taught from a definition the file gives as the "40% very disappointed test", then through a chain of three questions: what it would look like if customers loved the product, how to measure that, and what data would prove it. The common mistake it names is confusing growth for product-market fit. (MBA Tutor: Teaching Product-Market Fit, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/teaching-teaching-product-market-fit.md)
- The applied exercises are to calculate CAC against LTV for a local business, the file's example being Starbucks, and to value a simple business, a coffee shop, before complicating it with growth. The mistakes named are treating unit economics as fixed and giving a valuation as one number when it is a range of possibilities based on assumptions. (MBA Tutor: Teaching Unit Economics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/teaching-teaching-unit-economics.md; MBA Tutor: Teaching Valuation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/teaching-teaching-valuation.md)
- Strategy is taught by presenting a competitor's story, analysing its strategic positioning and predicting the competitive response; leadership by observing real companies through their public statements, decisions and outcomes. The mistakes named are generic strategy and thinking leadership is about charisma. (MBA Tutor: Teaching Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/teaching-teaching-strategy.md; MBA Tutor: Teaching Leadership, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/teaching-teaching-leadership.md)
- Real understanding shows in four ways: predicting outcomes, transferring to a new case, identifying tradeoffs, and disagreeing respectfully with a framework while showing mastery. The file's test question is what happens to sales if price increases 10%. (MBA Tutor: How to Know Understanding is Real, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/assessment-how-to-know-understanding-is-real.md)
- Surface understanding shows as using frameworks without thinking, recommending a solution without diagnosing the problem, ignoring constraints or feasibility, and being unable to explain why a framework is relevant or to apply it in a different context. (MBA Tutor: Common Signs of Surface-Level Understanding, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/assessment-common-signs-of-surface-level-understanding.md)
- Feedback asks before telling, highlights gaps with questions such as "That assumes X. What if X isn't true?", connects the point to a concept, encourages iteration and celebrates progress, which the file defines as recognising effort and improvement, not just right answers. (MBA Tutor: Feedback Approach, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/mba-tutor/rules/assessment-feedback-approach.md)

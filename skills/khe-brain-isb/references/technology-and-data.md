# Which technology is worth the bet, and how is it run?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: the Technology files name Rogers and Moore for the adoption lifecycle, the Agile Manifesto, and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, use Kano as a label on a model, and name no other author, no professor, no course and no source for any figure. A benchmark here is a number the file gives, undated and unsourced. Several of these files disagree with each other, on Visa's throughput, the RICE formula and what replatforming means, and two pairs differ without contradicting: one file favours ELT where another describes only ETL, and one names data maturity levels where another names analytics levels; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## What AI does and where it fails

- Large language models generate plausible-sounding but false information, and the file says they have learned statistical patterns, not true facts. For critical applications, which it gives as medical advice and legal interpretation, it requires human review. (Technology: Generative AI and Large Language Models, LLMs, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/ai-generative-ai-and-large-language-models-llms.md)
- Retrieval-augmented generation retrieves relevant documents and passes them to the model with the question, and the model answers based on the retrieved context, citing sources; the file says this reduces hallucinations. (Technology: Generative AI and Large Language Models, LLMs, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/ai-generative-ai-and-large-language-models-llms.md)
- An agent runs a loop of plan, act, observe and reflect, repeated until the goal is achieved; the file's challenges are hallucination, context limits and the cost of LLM API calls at scale. (Technology: Agentic AI, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/ai-agentic-ai.md)
- Algorithms trained on historical data perpetuate historical discrimination; detecting bias takes examining the training data, testing performance across demographic groups and building fairness constraints. (Technology: AI Ethics and Governance, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/ai-ai-ethics-and-governance.md)
- The AI implementation roadmap has eight steps: problem definition, data assessment, baseline and benchmarking, model selection, evaluation on holdout data, bias audit, deployment and monitoring, and a feedback loop. (Technology: AI Ethics and Governance, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/ai-ai-ethics-and-governance.md)

## Data as an asset, its governance and its maturity

- Data is treated like other strategic assets such as intellectual property and brand: inventoried, governed, protected and monetised, with data infrastructure built before analytics use cases. (Technology: Data as Strategic Asset, operating principles, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/operating-data-as-strategic-asset.md)
- Data governance has four core pillars: data ownership and stewardship, quality standards, access control and privacy, and metadata management. (Technology: Data Governance Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/data-data-governance-framework.md)
- The governance operating model keeps standards, architecture and compliance oversight central and has domain data teams own their data quality. (Technology: Data Governance Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/data-data-governance-framework.md)
- One file names four data maturity levels Reactive, Active, Predictive and Prescriptive, with time horizons of months, days, weeks ahead and proactive. (Technology: Data as Strategic Asset, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/data-data-as-strategic-asset.md)
- Another file names four analytics maturity levels Descriptive, Diagnostic, Predictive and Prescriptive, and says most companies struggle at Levels 2 to 3. (Technology: Analytics Maturity Model, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/data-analytics-maturity-model.md)
- ELT loads raw data first and transforms it in the warehouse, ETL transforms before loading, and the file says modern stacks favour ELT for agility. (Technology: Data Architecture, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/data-data-architecture.md)
- The databases file describes ETL, extract from sources, transform and load into the warehouse, as the way data moves from applications into a central warehouse, and does not mention ELT. (Technology: Databases and Data Infrastructure, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/cloud-databases-and-data-infrastructure.md)

## Cloud models, cost and migration

- IaaS rents compute, storage and networking with the highest flexibility and the most management; PaaS rents a development and deployment environment with some vendor lock-in; SaaS rents complete applications with minimal management and the least customisation. (Technology: Cloud Service Models, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/cloud-cloud-service-models.md)
- Hybrid cloud keeps critical workloads in private and variable ones in public and requires complex orchestration; multi-cloud uses multiple providers to avoid vendor lock-in at the price of increased complexity. (Technology: Deployment Models, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/cloud-deployment-models.md)
- The cost file lists savings of 20 to 40% from right-sizing, discounts of 30 to 70% for reserved instances committed for 1 to 3 years, discounts of 70 to 90% for spot instances and savings of 30 to 50% from stopping non-production systems during off-hours. (Technology: Cloud Economics and Cost Optimization, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/agile-cloud-economics-and-cost-optimization.md)
- The migration file gives four strategies, lift-and-shift, replatform, refactor and repurchase, defines replatform as minor optimisations during migration, and says refactor takes the most effort for the best long-term value. (Technology: Migration Strategies, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/cloud-migration-strategies.md)
- A transformation file lists lift and shift, re-platforming, the strangler pattern and a hybrid approach for legacy systems, and defines re-platforming as a rewrite on modern architecture with large effort and significant benefits. (Technology: 5 Digital Transformation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/digital-5-digital-transformation.md)

## Security and emerging technology

- Zero trust means trusting no one by default, even inside the network: every access request is verified, permissions follow least privilege and monitoring is continuous. (Technology: Cybersecurity, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/cloud-cybersecurity.md)
- Incidents are classed in four severities and handled in six phases: preparation, detection and analysis, containment, eradication, recovery and post-incident review, with the board, CEO and legal told within 1 hour. (Technology: Incident Response, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/security-incident-response.md)
- The blockchain file lists scalability as a limitation, giving Bitcoin 7 transactions a second against Visa 24k, and says that for many use cases blockchain is a solution in search of a problem. (Technology: Blockchain and Web3, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/emerging-blockchain-and-web3.md)
- A second blockchain file gives Bitcoin 7 transactions a second against Visa 65K, and says blockchain makes sense when multiple parties need transparent or immutable records and current intermediaries add significant cost or friction. (Technology: Blockchain and Web3, Additional Details, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/agile-blockchain-and-web3-additional-details.md)
- Quantum computing is at the stage of research and early experimentation with practical business applications years away; the file calls it a threat to current encryption and puts the focus on quantum-resistant cryptography. (Technology: Quantum Computing, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/emerging-quantum-computing.md)

## Transformation, business models and adoption

- The transformation roadmap runs digitise, which automates existing manual processes for efficiency gains only, then digitalise, which optimises processes with data insights and new workflows, then transform, which reimagines the business model. (Technology: Digital Transformation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/digital-digital-transformation.md)
- Transformations fail more often because of people and culture than technology, and one common failure pattern is treating transformation as an IT project. (Technology: Digital Transformation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/digital-digital-transformation.md)
- The file gives five digital business models: platform, marketplace, subscription, freemium and SaaS; a platform monetises through commissions, subscriptions or data and a marketplace through transaction fees. (Technology: Digital Business Models, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/digital-digital-business-models.md)
- Disruptive innovation serves previously underserved customers and typically begins with worse performance on the metrics customers care most about; the file's two types are low-end disruption and new-market disruption. (Technology: Disruptive Innovation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/digital-disruptive-innovation.md)
- Rogers' diffusion curve splits adopters into Innovators at 2.5%, Early Adopters at 13.5%, Early Majority at 34%, Late Majority at 34% and Laggards at 16%. (Technology: Technology Adoption Lifecycle, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/digital-technology-adoption-lifecycle.md)
- The chasm, credited to Moore, is the gap between Early Adopters and the Early Majority where many technologies die; the file's five ways across are to redefine positioning, focus on one market segment, build a complete solution, create reference customers and build an ecosystem. (Technology: Technology Adoption Lifecycle, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/digital-technology-adoption-lifecycle.md)

## Agile, product and the build, buy or partner call

- Waterfall works for well-understood projects with fixed requirements, and agile works for uncertain requirements and fast-changing markets. (Technology: Agile Fundamentals, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/agile-agile-fundamentals.md)
- Scrum runs sprint cycles of 1 to 4 weeks with three roles, a Product Owner, a Scrum Master and a cross-functional Development Team of 5 to 9 people. (Technology: Scrum Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/agile-scrum-framework.md)
- The user stories file gives RICE as Reach times Impact divided by Effort. (Technology: User Stories and Prioritization, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/agile-user-stories-and-prioritization.md)
- The product management file gives RICE as Reach times Impact times Confidence, divided by Effort. (Technology: Product Management Essentials, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/agile-product-management-essentials.md)
- Building gives proprietary advantage and full control when the thing is core to strategy, at higher cost and timeline; buying is faster to market with reduced risk but brings vendor lock-in risk, ongoing licensing costs and limited customisation; partnering shares risk and investment but creates dependency on the partner. (Technology: Build vs Buy vs Partner Decisions, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/operating-build-vs-buy-vs-partner-decisions.md)
- The decision criteria are strategic importance, competitive advantage, speed-to-market, cost, talent availability and scalability, and the file says many solutions are hybrid: buy a foundation, customise with API integrations and build the differentiating modules. (Technology: Build vs Buy vs Partner Decisions, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/technology/rules/operating-build-vs-buy-vs-partner-decisions.md)

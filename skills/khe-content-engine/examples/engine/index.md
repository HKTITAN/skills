# Ledgerlight content engine

## Who this is

Ines Varga runs Ledgerlight, a two-person bookkeeping studio in Lisbon for freelance designers, developers and translators. She writes every post herself and posts by hand. The engine drafts; she edits, approves and publishes.

## What a run is for

One idea from her week becomes one draft for each platform she asks for. Each draft treats the idea differently, because the same reader may follow her in all three places.

## Nodes

- [[voice]]: how Ines writes, with samples copied from her own posts and the list of things she never says. Read it before any draft.
- [[audience]]: the two groups she writes for and what each one already knows.
- [[x]]: short posts and short threads. The single claim, with its number.
- [[linkedin]]: one post a week. The story of how something was found out, with its caveat.
- [[newsletter]]: The Tidy Ledger, every second Tuesday. The method in full, with the thing to copy.
- [[hooks]]: the opening moves she uses, and which ones need a number or a quote in the brief.
- [[repurpose]]: how one idea becomes a different piece on each platform, and what may never be shared between two drafts.
- [[content-types]]: the five forms she writes, each with its skeleton.
- [[cadence]]: what she can keep up, on which days, and who presses publish.

## Order of a run

1. Read the brief. Write the claim in one sentence. If the brief cannot support a claim, stop and ask.
2. Read [[voice]] and [[audience]].
3. Read [[repurpose]] and pick one treatment per platform.
4. For each platform, read its node, pick a form from [[content-types]] and an opening from [[hooks]], and write the draft.
5. Save the run in `runs/` with the nodes each draft used.

## Rules that outrank every node

- Nothing is posted by the engine. Ines posts.
- A number or a quotation that is not in the brief is marked `[confirm]` or left out.
- A client is never named. A client is quoted only when the brief says she agreed.

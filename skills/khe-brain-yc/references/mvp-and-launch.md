# MVP and launch

This file answers three linked questions: what to build first, when to put it in front of people, and how to get the first users. Michael Seibel, Kat Manalac, Paul Graham, Gustaf Alstromer and Ankit Gupta speak in it, along with one post signed only Y Combinator. Read each line with its stage and its date: most of this is addressed to companies before launch or before product-market fit, and two of the speakers appear with talks from two different years.

## What is an MVP, and what is it for?

- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, defines an MVP as the first thing you can hand to the very first users you are targeting, to see whether you can deliver any value to them at all. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, gives a pre-launch startup four jobs in order: launch quickly, get some initial customers, talk to those users for feedback after the launch, and iterate. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says the one thing to take from his whole talk is "launch something bad, quickly". (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, tells founders to hold the problem and the customer tightly and the solution loosely, because the full product in your head may turn out not to be what customers want. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, separates iterating from pivoting: when the product fails the user, keep the user and the problem and fix the product, instead of looking for other problems the product might solve. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says you only start learning about your user once a product is in front of them, and that the first MVP is likely not to work. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says some conversations with users help before you decide to build the MVP, and that this does not mean three years of research or ten years in the industry. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, names the wrong answer as a hundred surveys, six hundred user interviews, contacting every competitor, a year of fundraising and a hundred hires before anything ships. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says surveys can show the pain a customer is in but never how to solve it, because customers are experts in their problem and solving it is the founder's job. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says that in most cases the people willing to talk to a startup are early adopters who are used to products that work badly, and that anyone who would leave after seeing it break was never going to try it. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, calls the founder who wants a year and 10 million dollars to build the perfect product a "fake Steve Jobs", and answers that the first iPhone had no app store, no video and only 2G. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says to build the first version for customers whose hair is on fire, who are in enough pain to use an imperfect fix, and to leave customers who are not desperate for later. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Gupta, in a talk the library dates to 2026, says a first version should be a "minimum evolvable product", something simple that survives contact with early users and adapts fast to what they push it toward. (How To Get Your First Users, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/ND-how-to-get-your-first-users)
- The Y Combinator post says launching a mediocre product as soon as possible, then talking to customers and iterating, beats waiting to build a perfect one, so long as the product holds a "quantum of utility" for customers. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- Graham's 2013 essay says it usually works best in software to show users something once it has a quantum of utility and then watch what they do with it, and he excepts domains with big penalties for mistakes. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay says releasing early means releasing something minimal and not something full of bugs, since users hate bugs but accept a minimal version 1 when more is coming soon. (The Hardest Lessons for Startups to Learn, https://paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html)

## How small should it be, and how fast?

- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says most people should build a lean MVP in weeks and not months, and that some startups begin with only a landing page and a spreadsheet. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says Airbnb's first product had no payments and no map view, and that the person writing all the code was working part time. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, adds that the first Airbnb only offered airbeds and only worked for conferences, switched on in a city for the event and switched off afterwards. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, allows a "heavy MVP" in a few cases, such as regulated industries like insurance or banking, hard tech, biotech and moonshots, and says even there the start can be a simple website explaining what you do, built in days. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says to time box the spec: if you want to launch in three weeks, the spec can only hold what you can build in three weeks. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says to set a very specific deadline, and gives two weeks, a month or a month and a half as the examples. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says to write the spec down, because an unwritten spec changes without your noticing and a three week plan becomes a three month plan. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says to cut the spec a week in when the deadline is slipping: remove what is clearly unimportant, and if nothing is unimportant, start removing important things. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2023, gives the cutting test as one question for every item on the spec: does a truly desperate customer need this feature to start. (How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- Seibel ends both MVP talks, which the library dates to 2019 and 2023, by telling founders not to fall in love with the MVP. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp; How to build an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp)
- The Y Combinator post credits Paul Buchheit with the 90/10 solution, a way to get 90% of what you want for 10% of the work, and says a 90% answer available right away beats a 100% answer that takes ages. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- Graham's 2006 essay says he has seen many startups die from releasing too slowly and none from releasing too quickly. (The Hardest Lessons for Startups to Learn, https://paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay adds in a note that a startup can die from releasing something full of bugs and not fixing them fast enough. (The Hardest Lessons for Startups to Learn, https://paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay says the first release works as a shakedown cruise: the stress of shipping exposes anything major that is broken, such as a bad idea or founders who hate each other. (The Hardest Lessons for Startups to Learn, https://paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html)
- Graham's 2006 essay says releasing early is bad advice without its second half: if you start with something that does little, you must improve it fast. (The Hardest Lessons for Startups to Learn, https://paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html)

## When do I launch?

- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, answers the question of when to launch with as soon as possible, and says that is likely right now. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says founders hold strong but theoretical ideas of how people will use the product, and that putting it out in its earliest state shows whether the problem is big enough that someone will pay for or use an unpolished version. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, lists the worst cases of launching too early as people finding it ugly, investors or competitors seeing it, or nobody caring, and says her answer is to iterate and launch again. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says Airbnb launched three times before it really started to get users. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says most startups launch and no one cares, and that a startup which took six months to get there is likely to be dead before its next chance to launch. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, repeats the point in a softer form: if the first version takes six months to reach anyone, the startup may be dead before another chance to launch. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says launches are not special, and asks his audience who remembers the day Google, Facebook or Twitter launched. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, redefines launch for pre-launch founders as the moment you get any customers, and says to bring that forward and push the press launch back. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Graham's 2013 essay says the Big Launch is one early tactic that usually does not work. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says all a launch has to give you is an initial core of users, and that how well you are doing a few months on will depend more on how happy you made them than on how many there were. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says in a note that launch size and success may even be inversely correlated, and that the only launches he remembers are flops like the Segway and Google Wave. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- The Y Combinator post says the first thing founders are told is to launch right away, because it is the only way to fully understand customers' problems and whether the product meets them. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- Gupta, in a talk the library dates to 2026, says to launch early because you do not yet know who your early users are, and you want a wide surface area for them to find you. (How To Get Your First Users, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/ND-how-to-get-your-first-users)

## How do I launch more than once?

- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says to treat launching as something you do continually, since a company that keeps shipping products and features is never not launching. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac says in both launch talks, which the library dates to 2019 and 2023, that launching on different channels shows whether you are even talking to the right users. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again; The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac says in both talks, dated 2019 and 2023, that a silent launch needs only a domain name, the company name, a short description, a contact and a call to action. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again; The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac says in both talks, dated 2019 and 2023, to do a friends and family launch as soon as there is an MVP and to leave that phase quickly, because friends and family may not be the right users. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again; The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, illustrates launching to strangers with Lugg, whose founders rented a truck and pitched shoppers outside IKEA before the backend worked, which confirmed the problem was urgent. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, uses DoorDash instead: after a store manager showed them a booklet of delivery orders she had no drivers for, the founders went on to interview more than 200 small business owners around the Bay Area and then built the MVP in a few hours. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac says in both talks, dated 2019 and 2023, to plan a launch for every online community you are part of. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again; The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac describes YC's internal Bookface as a low-risk first launch in front of friendly founders, with over 4,000 founders on it in the talk dated 2019 and over 6,000 in the talk dated 2023. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again; The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, tells of Magic getting 40,000 signups over a weekend after a friend posted its link on Reddit and Hacker News, and calls it an extreme case that almost no one will repeat. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, tells of Robinhood in December 2013: a stranger posted it on Hacker News while the site had only a waitlist button, and it got 10,000 signups the first day and over 50,000 in the following week. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says YC's numbers on how TechCrunch, Product Hunt and Hacker News launches convert to users were starting to even out. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says the biggest rule for community launches is to write like you talk, without marketing language or deep jargon, and to tell readers which topics you can answer questions on. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, reports the Hacker News moderators telling her it is not true that developer tools do best there and that all sorts of companies do well, and she adds that what works is leaning into the community's intellectual curiosity. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, recommends building a viral element into a request for access launch where the product allows it, as Magic did by letting people who tweeted about it skip places in line. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, adds a warning on waitlists: the longer you wait to ship the product and onboard people, the harder it is to convert them. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says Joy ran a drip campaign to over 50 bloggers, heard back from four, and that those four made a huge difference to its early growth, and she tells early-stage startups not to pay for such placements. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says a hardware or physical product company can launch with a pre-order campaign and should study successful ones first. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again)
- Manalac, in a talk the library dates to 2023, says there is more skepticism about crowdfunding and pre-orders than five years earlier, so a founder should think about whether it fits the company. (The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac says in both talks, dated 2019 and 2023, that Stripe launches every new product in the same way, with the founders talking to the Hacker News community themselves. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again; The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)
- Manalac says in both talks, dated 2019 and 2023, to start building your own community while still in Startup School, and an email list is her example. (How to launch again and again, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6i-how-to-launch-again-and-again; The best way to launch your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ir-the-best-way-to-launch-your-startup)

## How do I get the first users by hand?

- Graham's 2013 essay says the most common unscalable thing founders must do at the start is recruit users manually, and that nearly all startups have to. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay describes the Collison installation: when someone agreed to try Stripe, the Collison brothers set them up on the spot on that person's laptop instead of sending a link later. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says founders pass over manual recruiting because the numbers look so small at first, and answers with compound growth: 100 users growing 10% a week is 14,000 users after a year and 2 million after two. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says marketplaces are so hard to start that heroic measures are expected, and that Airbnb's were going door to door in New York to recruit users and improve listings. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says that if you built the product for your own problem you only need to find your peers, and otherwise the usual way is a fairly untargeted launch, then seeking more of whichever users are most enthusiastic. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Seibel, in a talk the library dates to 2019, says the question of how to get first users puzzles him, since you chose a problem you know someone has, so you talk to that person, and he tells founders building for users they cannot name to question that. (How to plan an MVP, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6f-how-to-plan-an-mvp)
- Gupta, in a talk the library dates to 2026, says most people are not early adopters, so finding first users is more a search problem than a persuasion problem. (How To Get Your First Users, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/ND-how-to-get-your-first-users)
- Gupta, in a talk the library dates to 2026, says to use targeted personal outreach, since a billboard is far less likely to reach early adopters than a targeted cold email or a knock on the door. (How To Get Your First Users, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/ND-how-to-get-your-first-users)
- Gupta, in a talk the library dates to 2026, says to charge real money early, with feedback and not revenue as the goal, because paying customers give sharper feedback than free users. (How To Get Your First Users, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/ND-how-to-get-your-first-users)
- Graham's 2013 essay says in a note that between the users who will sign up quickest and those who will pay the most, it is usually best to pick the quickest, since by his account those are likely the early adopters. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says founders should do the selling themselves and not hire a sales team until they know how to sell, since only then will they know what good looks like. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says Brex recruited its first ten customers from its own YC batch in winter 2017 with only a virtual card, and that one founder onboarded each customer himself. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says a sales email should run six to eight sentences at most, in plain text and plain language, name the customer's problem, say you are the founder, and end with a call to action. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says your first customers should be your easiest: sell to people you know, drop leads that move slowly, and do not try the hardest customer first. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says startups are the easiest category to sell to, a lesson he draws from YC companies that sell software, because decisions there are quick and larger companies add bureaucracy and negotiating departments. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- Graham's 2013 essay says that among companies the best early adopters are usually other startups, being more open to new things and not yet settled in their choices. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says most people you contact are not early adopters and will just archive the message, so the way to reach early adopters is to send more messages and not to try to convince the rest. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, works an example from 500 outreach messages down to 10 demos and two customers, and says 100 messages at the same rates give zero, which founders wrongly read as sales not working. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says a customer who is not charged is not a customer, and that someone unwilling to pay is a signal to move on to the next one. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says B2B startups should not offer free trials and should offer a money-back guarantee or a way out of the annual contract instead. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)
- The Y Combinator post says to get the first customer by any means necessary, including manual work that could not be kept up for more than ten customers. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- The Y Combinator post says founders get to choose their customers, and that 10 customers with a burning problem are much better than 1000 with a passing annoyance. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)

## Which things that do not scale are worth doing?

- Graham's 2013 essay says Wufoo sent every new user a thank you note written by hand for as long as it could, and that first users should feel signing up was one of their best choices. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says he has never once seen a startup led into a blind alley by working too hard at making its first users happy. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says that in a startup's first months it is the experience of being your user that should be insanely great, and that attentiveness can make up for an early, incomplete, buggy product. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says the right unscalable move is sometimes a deliberately narrow market, as with Facebook starting only for Harvard students. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay advises hardware startups to begin by "pulling a Meraki" if they can, assembling the product themselves, as Pebble did with its first several hundred watches. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says B2B founders are sometimes advised to pick a single user and act like consultants building just for them, and that this is safe so long as you are not paid for the attentiveness itself. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says that with few users you can sometimes get away with doing by hand what you plan to automate later, which lets you launch faster and shows you exactly what to build. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay says partnerships with big companies usually do not work for startups, and least of all as a way to get growth started. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- Graham's 2013 essay proposes thinking of a startup idea as a pair: what you will build, plus the unscalable thing you will do at first to get the company going. (Do Things that Don't Scale, https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- The Y Combinator post says startups are often persuaded to scale too early, and that technology and processes built for scale before they are needed are wasted effort. (YC's essential startup advice, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)
- Alstromer, in a talk the library dates to 2022, says Airbnb's later growth channels were not how it started, and that the referral program was not what brought in its first 2,000 customers. (How to get your first customers, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ip-how-to-get-your-first-customers)

## Should I chase press?

- Seibel says in an undated post that the best press advice he got was "Treat PR like biz dev", meaning relationships with reporters as people, warm introductions over cold emails, and a fair exchange of value. (Getting press for your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4c-getting-press-for-your-startup)
- Seibel's post says reporters rarely value a profile piece that could run any week, and that what makes a story valuable to them is news. (Getting press for your startup, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4c-getting-press-for-your-startup)

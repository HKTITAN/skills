# Leading teams

For him a company is a coordination problem, and the leader exists to lower the cost of coordination by generating energy, writing clearly, setting incentives and removing blockers. Most of this comes from Kata 2 and Kata 3, which were recorded back to back as talks to his own Aeos employees, from Secret Kata 1, an internal talk to about 60 to 70 core people, and from the KK Create and Masters Union interviews where he speaks as a guest. Several positions are ones he says he held differently at 24 or 25, and on incentives he says in the same talk that he went too far.

## Why do companies break as they grow?

- His chronic worry at about 500 employees is that he does not know what the 500th employee is doing, and he thinks some managers do not know what the people two levels below them do. (Kata 2 at 0:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=54s)
- For the services side of Aeos the crux is how the 500th or 1,000th employee delivers the same quality as the first few people in the apartment. (Kata 2 at 0:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=54s)
- Teams have a **cost of coordination**: each person's cost is different, and his examples are arrogance, not speaking the language and lacking the skill. If you get everybody below the threshold of effective coordination you produce good output, and he says money is not the issue. (Kata 2 at 12:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=726s; Kata 2 at 12:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=773s)
- A coordinated team with less skill beats a team with one superstar. His Dota example is five coordinated players at 30% of the other team's skill beating a team with one 150% player whom the rest pull down. (Kata 2 at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=630s)
- His short version of the whole model is "If a team coordinates, they win." (Kata 2 at 41:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2518s)
- Most startups explode or fail because three, four or five co-founders drift apart, each wanting it to be their own journey and each with status needs. (Kata 2 at 11:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=678s)
- The bigger the system, the worse coordination gets, and you can fall back to zero. His biggest fear is reaching 1,000 to 5,000 people and being uncoordinated, and he notes multi-billion dollar Indian companies have gone back to zero. (Kata 2 at 56:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3386s)
- Complexity enters a company through people. In one of his teams each new hire only split the same work across more people, timelines never changed, and everyone was waiting on someone else. (Kata 11 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=341s)
- Dense teams stop communicating, and artists cannot align on words alone. Two artists discuss one idea and build two different things, so he gives them references, storyboards, mood boards and visual internal tools. (Kata 12 at 25:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1544s)
- Companies drift by default because people get bored or distracted by outside offers. They run on a few rare people who ask how to improve, remove non-A players and cut waste. (Masters Union at 46:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2807s)
- As a guest at Masters' Union he tells the host that 300 people doing coordination work at a lakh each is 3 crores a month that better planning would save. He claims no business textbook covers this and that he reached it from first principles. (Masters Union at 46:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2761s)

## What is a leader's job?

- A leader's job is to drop the cost of coordination by hook or by crook. If one person speaks only English in a team that works in Hindi, that person is the problem: let them go, upskill them in Hindi or find another way to communicate. (Kata 2 at 13:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=822s)
- A leader is an unblocker. Citing Andrej Karpathy's account, he describes Elon Musk going table to table asking what is stopping people, then getting files released or calling Jensen for 100 chips. (Kata 2 at 14:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=870s)
- A leader is a generator of **current**: enough energy to make 4, 5, 10, 20 or 100 people who do not care about a task care about it. The best leaders do this endlessly without burning out, even on days they do not feel like it. (Kata 2 at 15:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=917s)
- He lists four ways to generate current: excitement like a Zumba instructor, incentives, inspiration from a big goal, and fear like a teacher throwing a duster. (Kata 2 at 16:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=966s)
- A leader's tools are stories, incentives, a road map to mastery, pressure, and seeking and removing blockages. He prefers to call pressure a quest for speed. (Kata 2 at 33:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2030s)
- His list of human blockages is high ego, low skill, poor attitude, poor communication, attention to the wrong details, low trust, low accountability, passive aggression, being too transactional and victimhood. He says he has been most of these himself and that fixing them takes a mirror from someone else. (Kata 2 at 35:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2124s)
- Low skill is the easiest blockage to fix because people rise fast in a dense, talented environment. Poor attitude and low trust are very hard, and he says poor attitude comes from parents and upbringing and is under 1% of people. (Kata 2 at 36:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2173s)
- You cannot lead without intrinsic motivation, but leadership is not something you are born with. A leader on a meaningless quest for money burns out, and purpose, though he calls it made up, makes generating current easier. (Kata 2 at 40:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2423s)
- A streak of success makes a leader prophetic to the team and a streak of failure makes the storytelling unreliable. He did not have this at 25, and he says three bad years would weaken it again. (Kata 2 at 20:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1203s)
- A leader's clarity makes or breaks careers, because leaders at a scaling company hold real budgets and power. (Kata 3 at 39:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2395s)
- Your team can smell whether you believe, and if you do not truly believe they will not follow you. He says belief is signalled through symbols, with Gandhi's 1930 Dandi march as his example. (Why Belief Matters at 18:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1104s)
- Companies run on repeated stories. Tell a story a million times and people repeat it downward, so he expects units to create incentives and procedures without being told. (KK Create at 1:43:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=6187s)

## How do stories, incentives and mastery motivate people, and where did he overdo it?

- Useless objects become valuable through the story attached. Everyone has shoes, but the Aeos shoes and the lounge that needs 3 years at the company carry meaning. (Kata 2 at 18:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1108s)
- Once a year he gives Aeos shoes to the 50 or 100 people he most respects across the company. Some people have left because they did not get them, and he relies on team leads to allocate fairly. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 27:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1638s)
- Incentives only work if you follow through, and many people set them and forget to deliver. He says Aeos has followed through for 3 years, including giving the team scooters partly so the next group wants to reach that level. (Kata 2 at 19:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1156s)
- He calls setting incentives a superpower and cites a Sam Altman tweet that almost everything runs on incentives. Nobody in his 16-person leadership team has left, and he says they earn a lot from incentives, not base salary. (Kata 2 at 21:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1299s)
- Incentives and stories must align with what each team member wants individually. When younger he would impose his own worldview and incentives on, say, someone who came from a village, and Abhinav Chakara told him of a design graduate from a very small town for whom the money did not matter but her reputation in her village, as the first person there to make more than a lakh a month, did. (Kata 2 at 24:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1497s; Kata 2 at 25:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1544s)
- Money is not the only thing that drives people, whatever they say. If it were, people would not quit Nvidia or Google when their four-year vesting ends. (Kata 2 at 8:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=533s)
- "Cash is the least powerful incentive." It matters until someone feels they earn enough among their peers, and after that the ladder runs through respect, status, purpose and mastery. (Kata 2 at 27:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1640s)
- Mastery is the best incentive. Dota's ranking system has kept him hooked for over a decade without paying him, and every video editor he meets wants to become a motion graphics designer because that is their mastery path. (Kata 2 at 28:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1689s)
- All career levels are made up, but you still need them because they give a sense of progression. Ideally the milestones are set together with the team. (Kata 2 at 29:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1786s)
- People do their best when they are almost there and know exactly what to do next, which he calls the **lightning state**. His leaders have it because they hold a P&L and can see the gap. (Kata 2 at 32:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1931s)
- External rewards can overshadow intrinsic motivation. Relaying the book Punished by Rewards, he says that if you give a very good coder a little extra money every time he makes something work, over time he does it for the money and stops enjoying writing code, and you get short-term compliance but long-term harm. (Kata 2 at 30:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1834s; Kata 2 at 31:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1881s)
- He says he went too far on incentives and had to read that book to see the mistake, in the same talk where he calls incentives a superpower. (Kata 2 at 31:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1881s)
- He lets AI design incentives, on the reasoning that smarter people have designed the same incentives thousands of times and the model has read it all. You give it your exact context and ask for something simple. (Kata 3 at 24:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1446s)
- Because loss hurts more than gain feels good, he gives no feel-good titles or promotions, no ballpark projections of what people will earn, and no perks unless well thought out. (Kata 9 at 51:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3104s)

## How should a leader write and give context?

- Clear writing lowers the coordination threshold itself. Write for each person exactly what to do, their incentives and their plan, then the grand plan for how they coordinate. (Kata 2 at 44:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2662s)
- The best leaders are writing all the time. Your team probably sees 5% of the picture you do, writing lets them see 100%, and he says that if you write well you need one tenth of the meetings you currently do. (Kata 2 at 45:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2710s; Kata 2 at 46:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2805s; Kata 2 at 48:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2901s)
- He credits writing everything down for Aeos scaling from zero to 500 people with good margins. The benefits he lists are direction, speed, fewer blockers, easier incentives and roles, easier hiring, fewer meetings and easier rule setting. (Kata 2 at 48:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2901s)
- Writing is a type of thinking, and unclear writing comes from unclear thinking. The only proof you thought clearly is that you executed and it led to results. (Kata 3 at 2:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=145s)
- AI and human employees behave like a **literal genie** that executes exactly what you say and fills the gaps itself. To get what you want you must be precise, down to the amount, the bank account and the legal route. (Kata 3 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=341s)
- He estimates only 30% of what is in a leader's head reaches the team. After 3 years his leadership has full shared context, so they can decide on his behalf and he on theirs. (Kata 3 at 10:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=631s)
- Examples are the best way to give context to humans and computers. His file brief states the format, the folder, the naming rule by recording date and take number, and one sample file name. (Kata 3 at 12:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=727s)
- The bottleneck is almost always a missing spec, not missing skill. He retells an Andy Grove example where a new cook is never told golden brown on both sides and the customer gets a pancake with one raw side. (Kata 3 at 12:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=776s)
- He calls senior engineers **context repair technicians**: they are not three times faster coders, and you overpay them because management sends underbaked briefs. (Kata 3 at 14:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=873s)
- Some people write a sheet on what it is like to work with them and send it to new team members, and he calls this an amazing way to set context. (Kata 3 at 14:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=873s)
- He wishes employees replied to instructions with five follow-up questions the way deep research does, but says that is the leader's job: think of the follow-up questions yourself and answer them. (Kata 3 at 17:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1063s)
- The weekly report he holds up as best is a plain WhatsApp update: total hired this week by role, number joining next week by role, then notes on what went badly and the bottlenecks. (Kata 3 at 30:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1825s)
- Watch for **unreliable narrators** who judge by visible output. Someone sees an editor working at 9 at night and reports overwork, when the cause may be a missed deadline or inexperience. (Kata 3 at 25:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1539s)
- In December 2025 he said that once you start writing things down you can scale them: after the channel they made for Zoho got to scale, they spent a lot of time writing all their secrets down on editing, writing, thinking and channel management, then hired well and offered new hires the playbook. (Kata 1 at 27:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1647s; Kata 1 at 28:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1696s)
- By August 2026 he says the playbook is the org chart and not a document of know-how, because taste and muscle memory do not transfer by reading a departing person's write-up. (Kata 12 at 41:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2509s)

## How does he handle rules, low performers and firing?

- "If you don't enforce rules, you lose storytelling power." If one person ignores the plan and is neither reprimanded nor let go, everyone sees that it is fine. (Kata 2 at 49:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2951s)
- He frames firing as giving the opportunity to someone more deserving, with the option to reapply in a few years. (Kata 2 at 49:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2951s)
- He calls every company a **delusion bubble**, and says the price is that someone who may even be right that everything is bad is still not useful to keep, for everyone else's mental health. (Kata 2 at 54:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3242s)
- You will never get 100% of people aligned, maybe 80, 90 or 97%, so remove non-coordinators and still allow debate. When an employee has real conviction about something untried, run an experiment. (Kata 2 at 58:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3535s)
- The services company's name for a non-coordinator is a **red aura**, and such people are disconnected from the company quickly. (Kata 2 at 58:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3535s)
- Tell a low performer exactly where they lag, with a reference and a deadline. Most people are never told, then get fired and are shocked. (Kata 3 at 38:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2300s)
- His low performer email states the quality and speed required by the end of 60 days with a link, notes that editors of the same experience manage it, lists the gaps, shows an ideal reference and gives numbered suggestions. (Kata 3 at 37:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2253s)
- He also says leaders are responsible for helping employees first, that everyone can improve unless there is an attitude issue, and that skill is the least likely reason to lose a job. (Kata 3 at 39:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2347s)
- "Weak managers are afraid to fire low performers and in exchange lose high performers." A high performer will not stay on a team that works like a group project where one person does 90%. (Kata 3 at 40:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2443s)
- The goal is not all A players but zero C players. His test, from Brad Jacobs's book, is to imagine the person quits today: panic means A, three months to replace means B, relief means C. (Kata 8 at 38:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2312s; Kata 8 at 36:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2215s)
- Move non-collaborative people out unless they have the right attitude and learning ability, because a non-collaborative person makes the environment more toxic. (Secret Kata 1 at 28:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1737s)
- In an internal talk he says someone with active depression is typically not a good fit until treated, because low serotonin means less collaboration. He says it is not their fault, they should take sick time and come back, and that there were periods when he himself would have been disastrous to hire. (Secret Kata 1 at 28:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1689s)
- Aeos runs many successful channels mainly because low views are shameful there, and he says the company would not work without that structural shaming. An unprofitable business is likewise shameful until it turns a profit. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 31:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1879s)
- Aeos is thinking about making leaving a one-way door, because people decide to leave in minutes, freelance for two months and then want to come back. (Kata 7 at 30:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1826s)

## How does he make leaders and hold them accountable?

- You cannot make 500 people care yourself, so the proxy is to make really good leaders who absorb the DNA and pass it to the next layer. He calls this a very challenging problem. (Kata 2 at 3:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=202s)
- The company only survives if it makes more leaders. He wants to find 30 or 40 new people who can rise to the top and says age does not matter. (Kata 1 at 1:15:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=4510s)
- He builds leaders by throwing them in the deep end. He picks people he believes will survive, rescues them only if they are truly drowning, and lets them struggle even if the company has a hard 3 or 4 months. (KK Create at 1:10:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4256s)
- For ordinary new hires he takes the opposite line: they need clear week-one instructions, and in April 2026 he said he had heard that Aeos throws people in the deep end and was working to change it. (Kata 8 at 35:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2118s)
- Hand over the keys to someone with skin in the game. The person who runs his channel has a profit share, so an extra hire cuts that person's own money, and his instruction is to bother him only if things really change. (KK Create at 43:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2622s)
- A fair system retains people better than money alone. In 4 years none of the 17 people in his core co-founder leadership has left or been fired, while managers below him have only salary to offer. (KK Create at 1:10:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4208s)
- To fix a process he places an experienced head first, gives them 3 months, then spends 1 or 2 days optimising. If it is not broken he does not touch it. (KK Create at 1:28:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5323s)
- His measure for founder's office hires is the **task drop** rate: give 10 tasks, enough that they are slightly drowning, and count how many are silently dropped. Under 10% is good, and a task honestly reported as not doable counts as resolved. (KK Create at 1:15:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4540s)
- He never accepts an excuse but accepts a stupid truth, and his rule is "Tell me the truth even if it's stupid." Someone who says they were tired and went out partying is fine, and he will apologise to the client himself. (KK Create at 1:16:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4587s)
- If someone falsely blames the client, he starts looking for a replacement immediately. (KK Create at 1:16:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4587s)
- Above a certain level excuses stop mattering. He relays a Steve Jobs line that a janitor can say the door was locked, but a VP can find the key, get a duplicate or break the door down. (Kata 4 at 19:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1158s)
- The most competent people blame themselves even for things outside their control, such as a bad co-founder or a pandemic, because that is how they avoid repeating mistakes. (Kata 4 at 47:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2843s)
- The Aeos rule is to ask for forgiveness, not permission, because nobody cares much about failure and they care that you took the hard call and were eventually right. (Kata 7 at 20:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1204s)
- Your growth is capped by your boss's level. At Aeos, if a manager is not competent enough to give you room, ask for a team switch, and the company removes bad managers. (Kata 4 at 50:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3041s)
- One task, one person. When he was younger and short on cash he gave people with free hours a second task and good talent did substandard work. (Kata 11 at 24:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1444s)
- Teams should be two-pizza sized, six to 10 people or fewer, and own exactly one goal. He takes this from Jeff Bezos and says a team with two, three or five goals falls apart. (Kata 11 at 6:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=389s)
- Companies are all about conflict resolution, not conflict avoidance, and he says the best founders he has met tell people to fight, finish it, then disagree but commit. At Aeos, he says, if the leadership needs to fight they will throw chappals at each other, but they resolve it and reset quickly. (Kata 4 at 32:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1978s; Kata 4 at 33:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2027s; Kata 2 at 55:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3339s)

## What makes a team feel safe instead of afraid?

- In an internal talk he says the only way to become very good at leadership is to manage your own neurotransmitter and hormone profile and that of others. He adds that he is not a doctor. (Secret Kata 1 at 0:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=52s; Secret Kata 1 at 2:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=149s)
- He describes serotonin as the social safety chemical, not the happiness chemical: high means you feel socially safe, low means unsafe. (Secret Kata 1 at 3:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=198s)
- Stress is not hard work. Stress is a situation you cannot reliably overcome, and he says Indians have equated the two. (Secret Kata 1 at 4:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=245s)
- The alpha is collaborative, not aggressive. He cites vervet monkey studies where the male who rises to alpha sees serotonin go up about 50%, and cautions that monkey results do not map one to one to humans. (Secret Kata 1 at 16:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=967s; Secret Kata 1 at 20:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1209s)
- Low serotonin rogues may win a fight but cannot hold power because they cannot build alliances. He maps this to people who leave Aeos to start a company and pick fights with everybody. (Secret Kata 1 at 17:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1063s)
- He says most Indian services companies are ruled by fear and Aeos wants the opposite: reduce social threat, lower defensiveness, increase collaboration. Infighting has not happened yet, he says, but will with scale unless solved now. (Secret Kata 1 at 20:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1209s)
- His golden rules are: praise in public and correct in private, critique the work and not the identity, lead with alignment before disagreeing, define evaluation criteria, run blameless postmortems, and protect status while changing behaviour. (Secret Kata 1 at 20:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1259s)
- "Critique the work, not the identity of the person." He says he is known for calling work trash but never the person. (Secret Kata 1 at 21:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1308s; Secret Kata 1 at 20:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1259s)
- Postmortems are blameless, but if a person really is the problem he says to let them go instead of blaming them afterwards. (Secret Kata 1 at 20:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1259s)
- He wants every appraisal conversation at Aeos to start by saying a no is a budget decision, not a verdict on your worth. He says rejection is never personal, then adds that sometimes it is, but not here. (Secret Kata 1 at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=630s; Secret Kata 1 at 11:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=678s)
- A manager is the serotonin balancer of the pod, whose job is to keep everyone in a good enough mood to collaborate. He says small things like birthday cake cuttings matter for bonding. (Secret Kata 1 at 27:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1642s)
- Companies run on stack ranking and fear are, in his words, biologically destined for toxicity and innovate poorly because nobody takes calculated risks. He puts this at 99% of Indian companies. (Secret Kata 1 at 27:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1642s)
- Accountability needs safety. People only admit fault when confident they will not be fired, so Aeos is designed so you are less likely to be fired if you come forward with the issue and the fix. (Kata 2 at 38:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2325s)

- Do not abuse people, and assume everything you do is public. His **sunshine test** asks whether you would be ashamed to see what you said on a newspaper front page tomorrow. (Kata 3 at 39:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2347s)
- Failure cannot be demonised inside the company, because failure is the norm. (Kata 4 at 52:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3135s)

## What should a founder know and not know at scale?

- At scale a founder should not know everything. At 30 to 50 employees he knew every detail, and now he does not know the tech stack, the latest client or that a sports day happened. He knows the person or their manager and trusts them. (KK Create at 1:14:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4445s)
- He builds Aeos like a conglomerate and stays out of the processes. Every business has a margin threshold its founders are incentivised to hit, which frees him to make content or do motion capture. (KK Create at 1:42:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=6139s)
- He refuses to be a spreadsheet CEO. He wants to be in the middle of the creative work, hires people to watch the Excel sheet, and spends 50 to 60% of his time on the game. (KK Create at 27:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1661s)
- The interview's own tension is that he is hands-on in the game's creative work while describing a company designed so that he knows almost nothing about daily operations. (KK Create at 1:42:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=6139s)
- Most of his real job is hiring, because a good hire takes pressure off him. (KK Create at 27:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1661s)
- Headcount is a bad metric to be proud of. If you pride yourself on the number, people add assistants and extra roles to grow it and you build an inefficient company. (KK Create at 1:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=96s)
- Of about 600 people at Aeos, he estimates maybe 50 are absolutely critical, and those are the ones paid really well because losing them lets complexity overwhelm the team. (Kata 11 at 8:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=532s)
- Each company should hire for itself, since one central hiring team would have the units fighting over talent. Only finance and legal are shared. (KK Create at 1:37:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5849s)
- Every Aeos company starts with the same culture and planning and is different a year later, depending on its founders and employees. A company that drifts too far can stay in the group but leave the office and the culture. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 44:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2648s)
- He does not know what some Aeos companies will do next and says that if he tried to control it he would screw it up. His job is only to be consistent about his rules. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:03:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3802s)
- He builds with family because family has no breaking point: you fight and are normal the next day, and you push an underperformer instead of dropping them. (Masters Union at 35:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2141s)
- He admits the group structure has an unsolved problem: sister companies pivot into the same space and fight for deal flow. (Masters Union at 42:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2522s)

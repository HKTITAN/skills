# Hooks

A hook is the first line of a draft. It is a promise about what the reader gets for continuing, and the body pays it in full. A first line that gets the reader in and then gives them something else is the failure this skill calls Bait.

## Deriving an owner's hook types

The hooks node holds this owner's opening moves, never a universal list.

1. Read the first line of every sample in the voice node, and of any other posts the owner supplies.
2. Group them by what the line does, using the table below as the starting vocabulary.
3. Keep a type only when the owner has used it at least once or asks for it. Three to six types.
4. Name each type with the owner's own word for it when they have one.
5. For each type write: what it is, what it needs from the brief, what the body must do to pay it off.
6. Ask what they never open with, and list it.

## The starting vocabulary

| Type | The first line does this | Needs from the brief | The body pays it off when |
|---|---|---|---|
| The number | States one figure with its unit | That figure | It says how the figure was measured and what it does not prove |
| The correction | Names a belief the reader holds and says it is wrong | The claim, and the reason | The next sentence gives the real cause |
| The instruction | Tells the reader one thing to do | A step small enough to do today | The body is that step and its reason, and nothing else |
| The scene | Puts the reader in one moment, with the object in it | A moment that happened | The scene turns into the point within three sentences |
| The admission | States something the owner got wrong | The mistake, in the owner's words | The body says what they do now |
| The question | Asks what the reader is already asking | Evidence that readers ask it | The body answers it in the first paragraph |
| The stake | Says what ignoring this costs | The cost, as a fact in the brief | The body shows the cost and the way out |

## Rules

- **The brief funds the hook.** A number hook needs the number in the brief. A scene needs a moment that happened. Never pick a type and then invent what it needs.
- **One run, different types.** Each draft in a run opens with a different type, because a reader who follows the owner in two places recognises a repeated opening before they read it. The gate fails two drafts with the same first line (`same-hook:`).
- **The hook is written in the owner's voice.** A type is a move. The words come from the voice node.
- **No hook borrowed from a list of "viral" openers.** A template opener is a tell, and several are in [tells](tells.md).
- **Claims about what a hook is worth are not facts.** Any figure for how much of a post's performance its first line decides is unverified and stays out of the node.

## The payoff test

Run this on every draft before the gate. The gate cannot do it.

1. Write the hook's promise as one sentence beginning "After reading this you will".
2. Find the line in the body that delivers it. Quote it.
3. If the promise names a number, a step or a story, check that the body has that number, that step or that story, and that each is in the brief.
4. If no line delivers it, rewrite the hook to promise what the body has. Never pad the body to match a hook the brief cannot fund.

# Run: the due date at the top of the invoice

Engine: ../engine
Idea: moving the due date to the top of the invoice, written as a date, got clients paid sooner
Platforms: x, linkedin, newsletter

## Brief

From Ines, 2026-11-09, in her words and from her spreadsheet.

- In June 2026 she changed the invoice template that 14 of her clients use. The due date moved from the bottom, where it read "Net 30", to the first line under the client's name, in bold, written out as a date.
- Nothing else on the template changed. The change took ten minutes a client.
- On the old template the median time to payment was 38 days.
- From July to September those clients sent 212 invoices. The median time to payment was 24 days.
- There was no control group, and summer is a slow season for some of them.
- Since the change, no client has asked her what the payment terms mean.
- One client, an illustrator, agreed to be quoted without her name: "I stopped chasing in week two."
- For years Ines told clients that late payment was a client problem. She now thinks part of it starts with the invoice.
- The mistake she has seen since: the template is updated and the date on the next invoice is not.

Claim: an invoice that states the due date as a date, at the top, gets paid sooner than one that states terms at the bottom.

```text
For years I told clients that late payment was a client problem. I was wrong about where it starts.

In June I moved one line on the invoice template that 14 of my clients use. The due date went from the bottom, where it said "Net 30", to the first line, written out as a date.

Then I counted. Across 212 invoices sent from July to September, the median time to payment was 24 days. On the old template it had been 38.

What this does not prove: there was no control group, and summer is a slow season for some of them. A client who pays on the day they are told to has perhaps only been told more clearly.

I am keeping the change. It took ten minutes a client, and nobody has asked what the terms mean since.

If your invoice makes the reader do arithmetic to find out when to pay, part of the delay is yours.
```

## Draft: newsletter

- Treatment: the method, with the wording to copy and the thing that goes wrong ([[repurpose]])
- Hook: The instruction ([[hooks]])

- Nodes: [[voice]], [[newsletter]], [[hooks]], [[repurpose]], [[audience]], [[content-types]]

```text
Subject: Put the date at the top of your invoice

Open your invoice template. Find the line that says when you want to be paid. Move it to the top.

That is the whole issue. The rest is how to do it properly.

1. Delete "Net 30" and any other line that asks the reader for a calculation.
2. In the first line under the client's name, write: Please pay by, and then the date in full, with the month as a word.
3. Make that line bold. Nothing else on the page is bold.
4. Leave the bank details where they are. People find those.

Why bother. I made this change in June for 14 clients. From July to September their median time to payment was 24 days. Before, it was 38. I had no control group and it was summer, so take it as a reason to try, and nothing stronger.

One of them, an illustrator, told me: "I stopped chasing in week two."

The thing that goes wrong: you update the template and forget to change the date on the next invoice. An invoice that asks to be paid last month gets paid whenever. Check the date every time you send one.

Ines
```

# Distribution and content

Distribution is the centre of his thinking about business: he treats an audience that trusts you as the thing that brings talent, capital, customers and luck, and treats product as the easier half. Most of it comes from Kata 10, Kata 6 and Kata 1, which are talks to his own Aeos employees, and from three guest appearances: PGX Ideas 2, Masters Union and KK Create. Read the numbers as his own figures for Aeos and for India, and note that he calls several formats short-term plays and says in late 2026 that the AI avatar format he built is over.

## Why does he put distribution ahead of product?

- He rates distribution as about 10 times more valuable than product, after 14 to 15 years as an entrepreneur. With distribution you attract talent, capital and customers, and those let you build a much better product. (Kata 10 at 0:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=56s)
- At Aeos the moat is access to talent and distribution, not the product. "If you don't have distribution, it's not going to work." (Kata 1 at 11:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=681s)
- He adds in the same passage that you still build a great product. The focus is on getting it into enough hands. (Kata 1 at 11:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=681s)
- He was sold on distribution by a loss. He spent years on a great product nobody cared about while a competitor ran 10 times as many ads. (Kata 1 at 25:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1502s)
- At 25 he spent 8 of every 10 rupees on product and 2 on marketing. Now he spends 8 on marketing and 2 on product, and says it took 13 years of getting beaten to learn this. (Kata 9 at 58:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3533s)
- He calls bringing customers the most valuable skill in the world, and leads the best offset to bad luck. (Kata 9 at 58:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3533s)
- His test for a founder who blames the market: would 10 times the leads give 10 times the revenue? A founder with about 10 crores of sales from a thousand leads said yes, so the business only lacked leads. (Kata 6 at 3:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=188s)
- App categories are saturated but attention to creators is not. People could not name a new app they had downloaded, yet they could name new YouTubers and Instagram accounts, so he asked why he should make more apps. (Kata 1 at 17:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1069s)
- The 9 or 10 dollar a month app business no longer works. Aeos Labs found such apps get copied, users leave in 6 months, big companies clone what works, and distribution is the hard part. (KK Create at 9:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=573s)
- He describes his earlier companies as shiny bullets and Aeos as the gun or slingshot. It gives any product or service the velocity to push through policy and negativity. (PGX Ideas 2 at 58:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3536s)
- Once one feature works, stop building and put all your energy into marketing and sales. He says this separates successful entrepreneurs from unsuccessful ones. (Kata 11 at 21:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1298s)
- Adding features is a crutch for weak product-market fit or missing distribution. At his company Scenes, brand managers asked for one more feature, got it, and still went back to a WhatsApp group. (Kata 11 at 15:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=915s)
- With distribution the cost of ads is zero, so he can test cheaply and quickly whether people download, use and pay. (Kata 9 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=341s)
- In November 2026 he says that after 14 years he has realized it is not the technology, the people or even the distribution: many factors make a company successful, but none of it works without belief. (Why Belief Matters at 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=47s)

## What does he mean by content as a luck catcher?

- He holds that all success is luck, just as all bad outcomes are luck. His examples are being put in an English-medium school and not being born in an uncontacted tribe. (Kata 10 at 5:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=348s)
- You can engineer luck by raising your surface area. If more people know you, more opportunities can reach you, and asking when content will make you a given amount of money is a stupid way to think. (Kata 10 at 6:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=397s)
- He pictures a person as a game character with a **luck stat**. A thousand more followers moves it from five to seven, and he says the only thing that changed for him is that his is now near the hundreds. (Kata 10 at 7:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=444s)
- Content is a **lightning catcher**, like the spire on the Empire State Building. It puts you in position to take success when it strikes. (Kata 10 at 8:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=494s)
- Creating content is the inverse of reckless behaviour. Riding without a helmet daily for 10 years reliably ends in an accident, and repeated content reliably raises the chance of good things. (Kata 10 at 9:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=542s)
- "All benefits of content are random and unexpected." So the best use of content is as a **luck catcher** and not as a traffic funnel. (Kata 10 at 4:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=298s; Kata 10 at 11:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=685s)
- Content dampens the effect of luck in business. His success rate with businesses was poor when he was younger and rose sharply after he started creating content. (Kata 9 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=341s)
- All the benefits of a company or an individual come at scale, a point he credits to Charlie Munger's Poor Charlie's Almanack. At a million followers you probably get 30, 40, 50 emails a day, some of them trash, and he says they built businesses out of some of those opportunities. (Kata 1 at 25:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1548s)
- Creators get the deal flow. They notice that 15 people they now know want the same thing and only need to put a team together to solve it, which he says is how Aeos was built. (Kata 10 at 12:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=734s)
- Buying is slow, so content pays late. A viewer of a 5-year-old video may come to him for services now. (Kata 6 at 32:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1958s)
- He says all success is random and everything is random, and in the same talk says content is repeatable, that you can do it again and again, and that Aeos has done so many channels. (Kata 10 at 34:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2094s; Kata 10 at 36:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2194s)

## How does content turn into trust, and how many touch points does it take?

- He defines distribution as trust, not views. It is the people who follow you and make life decisions partly on what you say and how you live. (Kata 10 at 14:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=877s)
- Content is a piece of value, either utility or entertainment. Most viewers reject it, some watch once and again, and at some point decide to follow. (Kata 10 at 13:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=830s)
- Influence is subtle and works through mimesis. People once copied how actors smoked, and it is less about a pitch to buy a bike than about how you behave. (Kata 10 at 15:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=923s)
- Content almost never has immediate return. In India the only instant return is an ad for a product under 400 rupees, like a toothbrush or a t-shirt. (Kata 10 at 15:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=923s)
- Trust cannot be clearly measured and builds from seeing something again and again. He cannot remember which Nike, Dyson or Apple asset made him a fan. (Kata 10 at 17:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1070s; Kata 6 at 6:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=377s)
- Converting someone takes about 30 touch points on average. He cites 28.9 as the cross-industry figure and says a person will reliably buy from Aeos after 30 videos, ideally 30 different ones. (Kata 10 at 25:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1556s)
- He cites a Google rule of 7 hours of content, 11 interactions and 4 platforms or locations to build real trust. He first misstated the order and then corrected himself. (Kata 10 at 23:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1412s)
- Low-ticket consumer goods, ideally under 400 rupees and under 1,000, need 5 to 8 touches for recall and a first sale. A mid-ticket item like a phone needs 11 touches across four channels. (Kata 10 at 25:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1506s; Kata 10 at 22:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1363s)
- High-consideration purchases such as a course, insurance, real estate or a degree need 12 to 27 touches and 20 plus hours of content, which can include friends recommending it. (Kata 10 at 25:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1506s)
- A B2B vendor shortlist takes 17 interactions per vendor once the buyer is evaluating. Complex B2B above 80 lakhs a year takes 60 to 100 touch points. (Kata 10 at 25:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1506s)
- He says he does not have enough repeat value yet, despite Aeos's reach. To get there he needs other platforms, billboards and games. (Kata 10 at 25:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1556s)
- Going into **transactional mode** ends trust building, for brands and for people. His case is Nike, which he says lost 25 billion dollars of market cap in a day after moving from brand content to measurable ad spend. (Kata 10 at 20:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1220s)
- Give value first and ask rarely. A former services client made useful D2C content and pitched its own app only in every sixth video, and he says it worked flawlessly. (Kata 10 at 10:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=638s)
- Employers decide the way buyers do, so anyone can be a Nike. Show up every week or few weeks while you need nothing, so you are known 3 years later when you want a job. (Kata 10 at 19:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1169s)

## Which numbers does he watch?

- A view count on a campaign means nothing by itself. You can buy 10 million views on Instagram by targeting the cheapest audience, and he does not think that sells tickets. (Kata 6 at 19:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1189s)
- On Instagram, shares are a far better signal of intent than views or likes. People like things without thinking and move on, but they share an event because they want to go. (Kata 6 at 19:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1142s)
- His own test was two ads with similar views, about 300,000 for Mumbai Tech Week and 274,000 for Nvidia's GTC. The Nvidia video got 7.6k shares against 3.1k for the other. (Kata 6 at 19:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1142s)
- He was not fully convinced influencer marketing works until he met his own audience in person at that event. (Kata 6 at 20:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1237s)
- **Distribution efficiency** is engagement against views. Likes plus comments at 10% of views is good, and at 20% or more the algorithm will love you. (Kata 10 at 27:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1651s)
- His Instagram watch time bands: 9 to 10 seconds reaches your existing audience, 20 to 25 is the minimum to do well, 25 to 30 reaches a million views, and above 50 gets 3 to 4 million. (Kata 10 at 31:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1901s)
- He tracks unique reach against followers. With about 1.2 million followers he gets 200 to 300K per video, which is 20 to 30% and which he calls pretty good. (Kata 10 at 30:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1801s)
- On YouTube thumbnails, the middle of the platform is 4 to 6% click-through and top channels are 8 to 10% or more. A new or 50,000-follower channel should be above 10%, because the rate falls with scale. (Kata 10 at 30:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1850s)
- Track branded search every quarter: the share of non-paid Google clicks that are for your brand name. Growth of 15% a year means brand visibility is growing. (Kata 10 at 29:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1751s)
- Large numbers are averages. A video's watch time mixes people who scroll away in 1 to 2 seconds with fans who watch closely. (Kata 10 at 33:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1997s)
- One reel he shows had about 1.2 million views, 263 days of total watch time, 78k likes, 382 comments, 97,000 shares and 17k bookmarks, mostly from non-followers on the reels tab. (Kata 10 at 34:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2046s)
- For a creator the game is not views. It is who you are seen next to. (PGX Ideas 2 at 40:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2439s)

## What is a content meta, and what works on screen?

- The content **meta** changes every few years and each one is a short-term arbitrage. He borrows the word from Dota, where a few people decided what worked. (Masters Union at 25:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1519s)
- His timeline: vlogs five years ago, shows four, shorts three, shorts plus extra pages two, and AI avatars one year ago, which he says Aeos invented. Someone will always beat you with a specific type of content. (Masters Union at 25:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1519s)
- In October 2026 he says the AI avatar meta is over. He calls these short-term arbitrages, says that is why they are doing a lot of real content, and calls lectures a very good long-form meta. (Masters Union at 27:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1662s)
- The avatar bet came from a rule: use new AI models to raise margins in a business you already have. Aeos used avatars to make an influencer when most people used them for internal training video. (KK Create at 10:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=621s)
- In December 2025 he said audiences do watch AI presenters. In a test for an academy client he saw no difference in views, likes or comments, and he admits he did not believe it at first. (Kata 1 at 35:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2137s)
- AI in storytelling is only an accelerant. It tells the same stories at a lower budget, and it still has to be a good story people relate to, or news. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:01:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3681s)
- Prove a format by hand before you automate it. He says to shoot content yourself for 6 months before using avatars. (Kata 11 at 25:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1541s)
- Simple content often beats sophisticated content. A slide deck and a stick can outperform heavy motion graphics or a CG chimpanzee on his channel. (Kata 11 at 12:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=722s)
- His **toilet model**: picture the viewer on the toilet, scrolling with one hand while their mother calls. Assume they keep about 1% of you and design the message for that. (Kata 11 at 4:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=246s)
- People receive a tiny fraction of what you put out. Strangers at the airport recognise him without knowing his name and remember one video from a year ago. (Kata 11 at 3:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=198s)
- Aesthetic is a choice, not a function of money. Aeos used a highly produced, multicolour look while tiny and bootstrapped, because YouTube then was full of unproduced content and they had to be different. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 52:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3132s)
- A content creator should be a **story machine** instead of a string of pearls. In a string of pearls the world exists only when the creator releases another scripted event, while in a story machine rules, resources and factions continuously create new conflict. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 57:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3470s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 58:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3518s)
- Public characters have to stay in character. He says he plays a totally different character in short form than in real life, and viewers stop watching if a presenter acts too far out of character. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 13:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=822s)
- People learn fastest from comments, because you want to prove them wrong. (Kata 1 at 42:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2570s)

## Personal brand or group channel, and why so many small channels?

- A personal brand is easier to grow, but a group channel is more sustainable. Presenting is a high stamina role, and where another worker has 40 years left a creator has about 5. (KK Create at 13:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=839s)
- Do not wrap everything into one brand. If one brand does 10 things people get confused and the brand stops meaning anything. (KK Create at 17:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1031s)
- The scarce resource in a content gold rush is the video editor. Aeos learned this only when it tried to hire, because editors were paid 5 to 10k a month and nobody saw it as a career. (Kata 1 at 20:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1214s)
- Holding that scarce resource, Aeos chose to build as many of its own channels as it could. An investor would have told them to scale the school. (Kata 1 at 24:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1454s)
- To influence the feed you need many small channels, not only your own big ones. Podcasters already run 10 to 20 clip pages each. (Masters Union at 23:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1425s)
- He wants a **clipping network** at a scale of 50,000 or 500,000 pages by partnering with existing clippers. He calls it influencer marketing with tiny influencers, India only for now and very manual. (Masters Union at 23:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1425s)
- He asks who is more likely to sell out a stadium in the US, Tom Holland or Mr Beast, and the other speaker answers Mr Beast. He says Mr Beast has a massive clipping service and gets social media, which has given him two or three years of momentum. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:13:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4402s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:14:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4448s)
- He built a gaming channel from zero to about 150k on Instagram in 3 or 4 months so he would not have to keep asking gaming media for coverage. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:00:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3633s)
- He hands the keys to someone with skin in the game. Sagar runs his channel on a profit share, so an extra hire cuts Sagar's own money and he hires only for a multiplier. (KK Create at 43:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2622s)
- He protects reputation over revenue on the personal channel. It is known for saying no to brands and does three or four paid things a month. (KK Create at 39:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2382s)
- Aeos runs many successful channels mainly because low views are shameful there. A solo creator with low views just quits, while inside Aeos it is your job to continue. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 31:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1879s)
- Channels inside Aeos work because you see someone in the room making real money. He doubted the content business himself until he saw some creators' accounts and found they were normal people. (Why Belief Matters at 20:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1246s)
- He calls content creation the fairest game, because you cannot pay your way to better content. Tripling spend does not help when a channel is limited to about four videos. (Masters Union at 55:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3328s)
- He expects India to go the Chinese way with many multi-channel networks, where the network makes the influencer from day one. Creators will spin off new channels by passing 100k followers to trusted people. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:58:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7126s; PGX Ideas 2 at 2:01:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7317s)

## Why own your media instead of leaning on PR, and what is simulated reality?

- A small set of channels can create a **simulated reality** for most of India. His math is 10 headline channels of your own, 10 friends with influential channels and 500 clippers or news pages reposting. (PGX Ideas 2 at 56:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3392s)
- He says that set of about 500 channels can get any news to 50 to 60% of India. Aeos found this by accident, going from editors to its own channels to client channels to clipping. (PGX Ideas 2 at 56:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3392s)
- Own your distribution instead of trading favours with journalists. Top founders built reach through media relationships, but each ask erodes the relationship and you cannot ask five times. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:00:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3633s)
- "The storyteller wins the world, the storyteller eats the world." (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:00:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3633s)
- He says everything is technically paid PR until it is not, and no ambitious project works without that seeding. Skeptics are rewarded with attention for being negative early, which makes talent harder to get than funding. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:05:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3922s)
- Positive media buys a grace period of one or two years. The product must then catch up with the story or the audience turns on you. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:05:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3922s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:06:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3970s)
- He calls socially driven decisions extraordinarily dangerous because they are manipulated, and in the same interview describes building that kind of consensus as what Aeos does. (PGX Ideas 2 at 49:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2964s)
- Any founder who wants to do something really ambitious in India must first build media. He calls owned media a **life raft**: with enough channels he can put his side out and let the audience judge the output. (Masters Union at 49:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2949s)
- Citing a Twitter writer he names as Brute de Force, he says America is better than other countries because people hold a loudspeaker on anything they do, and the momentum from speaking loudly gets them everything to make it true. "But the loudspeaker comes first," he says, because without it you never get the talent or raise the money. (Masters Union at 57:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3472s; Masters Union at 58:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3521s)
- Anything valuable needs three or four iteration cycles. Without the **loudspeaker** you never get the shot at them. (Masters Union at 57:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3472s)
- Enough virality flips ridicule into interest. One hand-made animation in the first game trailer was mocked, went viral in China, and was then modded into other games. (Masters Union at 17:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1044s)
- Bollywood has no next superstars because its coordination is fading and it lacks reach across small and big social channels. It can no longer give a newcomer two years of steady build-up. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:12:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4355s)
- Soft power is probably the most critical national asset. China turned inward on its own platforms so demand and supply both became Chinese, while America creates demand through global social media. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:58:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7126s)

## How does YouTube work for founders and companies, and what do brands and creators get wrong?

- He bet early that what happens on TV should happen on YouTube. People were spending 5 hours a day on video, and he reasoned advertisers would follow even though YouTube rates were and are very low. (Kata 1 at 20:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1214s)
- YouTube works only if you give it your all. Brands struggle because they give 80% and get beaten by someone giving 100%. (KK Create at 3:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=190s)
- "Money is not a substitute for giving it your all." (KK Create at 3:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=190s)
- He argues that YouTube is a great acquisition mechanism. A brand gets to 400 to 500k and asks if there is ROI, and his answer is that one story gets him about 2,000 applicants or 10 to 20 pilot customers, which makes running a company easier and saves money. (Kata 6 at 32:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1958s)
- Marketing managers avoid content because the payoff outlasts their tenure. The average one lasts 2 to 3 years, content takes longer than 2, and a CEO will not trace a deal back to one video. (Kata 10 at 35:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2145s)
- The best brands on YouTube are founder-driven. A founder sees that a long-term company needs it now, and he cites Nithin Kamath and Zerodha doing no performance marketing. (Kata 10 at 37:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2243s)
- The best Indian companies have organic acquisition, and that is their margin. He cites Zerodha with no ads and Physics Wallah with acquisition driven entirely by YouTube. (Kata 6 at 11:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=710s)
- Companies forget the organic roots that made them. Many, including edtechs, get big on YouTube, raise money, decide they are a professional company and switch to celebrity ads only. (Kata 6 at 12:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=758s)
- He is very confident content will be the future of marketing. He says China is sophisticated about content and India has slept on it. (Kata 10 at 38:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2289s)
- New creators quit in two months because content shakes your belief on every video. Views dip after the first week, people panic and go back to a job, so his school forces students to put out 90 videos. (Why Belief Matters at 22:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1342s)

## How does content hire and sell, and where is the creator economy going?

- A content channel is a hiring and sales engine. One story across Aeos channels brings 10,000 candidates, and the same reach brings clients, which he calls the main reason Aeos has done well. (Kata 8 at 40:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2456s)
- When he hires he posts a story and gets about 2,000 applicants. When he wants sales he posts about a product or service and gets 10 to 20 pilot customers. (Kata 6 at 32:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1958s)
- An aspirational industry makes hiring easy, because people want to be on the stage of YouTube and Instagram. He contrasts it with a garbage collection company. (Kata 1 at 47:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2860s)
- Being a creator brings him almost all tech deal flow in India and a lot globally. That is why he entered influencer marketing despite seeing it as commoditized with a weak moat. (Masters Union at 29:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1758s)
- His businesses work as sensors. A company doing well raises money, starts influencer marketing, wants a deal with him or hires three or four editors, and he uses that to invest. (Masters Union at 30:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1852s)
- He says the future of business in India will be creator-run. (Kata 10 at 12:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=734s)
- "Advertising is a side effect of attention." Money follows attention with a lag, and he doubts newspapers hold a 22-year-old's attention in India. (KK Create at 50:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=3003s)
- Digital attention is undervalued against outdoor. People spend 5 to 6 hours a day on video and nowhere near that on billboards, yet every billboard is taken. (KK Create at 54:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=3244s)
- The creator economy will be far bigger once old-school India wakes up, since real estate and similar businesses still think in billboards and newspapers. He says 10 times bigger and a few minutes later 100 times. (KK Create at 46:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2764s; KK Create at 54:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=3244s)
- He expects three or four ads per video and several layers of sponsorship, as in the US and China: a plain sponsor line, product placement, a what-I-use mention and affiliate links. (KK Create at 46:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2764s; KK Create at 47:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2859s)
- Creator teams are now real businesses. He changed his view after being told in the Maharashtra deputy CM's office that the state sees a creator plus three or four people as a serious unit. (KK Create at 7:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=431s)
- Video editing will get popular the way software did, once editors brag about pay. Editors are leaving him for 24 to 25 lakhs a year, and he expects salary videos in one or two years and then a bubble. (Masters Union at 53:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3187s)
- Aeos is meant to be infrastructure for the attention economy, a platform like Windows that you can build anything on. (KK Create at 1:40:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=6043s)

# Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani

Typed notes from an orientation talk by Anirudh Damani of Artha Venture Fund, which the notes describe as India's first micro VC fund. He argued for fewer funding rounds and less dilution, and gave the six-part SCOUTE test his fund uses.

## What was said

### The journey

- Anirudh Damani says whatever you plan today will not go that way, and the journey is never linear.
- He says the next trough has to be higher than the last trough and the next peak higher than the last peak.
- He lists the stages as accelerators, seed, seed plus, pre-series A and series A, and says investors come in around seed plus.

### Customer capital over venture capital

- He says customer capital always trumps venture capital.
- He describes the traditional VC way as an endless cycle of mythical valuations, high dilution and low control, building big and fixing later, years of burn and a lonely journey.
- He says to get your customer to pay, and if they will not pay you can at least get their feedback.
- He says a founder should be clear about why they are raising and what their conviction is, and should not burn money simply because it is there.
- He prefers fewer, high conviction funding rounds, with less dilution and more founder control.
- He counts it a success story when a founder does not dilute more than 10% in a round.
- He says some companies use venture debt.
- He says VC firms need about 75% IRR growth from a company, and asks founders whether that can happen.
- He says a proper VC will ask what you are going to do after taking the money.
- He says KPIs must be very clear and founders should not be scared to watch them.
- He says real success is not a paper valuation but actual cash after an acquisition or an early exit.

### Founder ownership

- He says the lowest valued company in the Nifty has a market cap of about 6,000 crore rupees, and the average promoter holding there is around 53%.
- He gives the average founder holding as 15.26%.
- His comparison is that a 50% holder of a 6,000 crore Nifty company is worth 3,000 crore, while a founder holding 15% of a billion dollar company has 1,500 crore.
- He says the Zomato founder owns about 3.5% while the entire liability lies on him.
- He points to Everest Fleet, a cab fleet company in which Uber recently invested, where he says the founder owns 60%.

### Scale

- He says scale is actually the easiest thing and managing the scale is the difficult part, because things blow up when scale comes and the structures are not there.
- He says the entire goal of a business is to become boring, meaning repeatable with no new fires to put out every day.
- He says it is difficult to get high revenue in India without repeat purchase.
- He says 60 to 75% of India's GDP is based on self consumption, and that India is a highly fragmented and entrepreneurial society.
- He says AI is growing fast and is on its way to destroy SaaS.

### The SCOUTE test

- S is solve real problems. He asks whether the product is rooted in real, validated demand and solves a pain, not a preference.
- C is category winners with strong moats. He asks whether you can lead and not just compete, with a moat from speed, network effects, brand, distribution edge or customer loyalty.
- He says a fight on price is the worst fight you can have, because you cannot really win it.
- O is optimised unit economics. He asks whether you watch CAC against LTV and know your payback period.
- U is unmatched right to win. He asks whether you are the best team to solve this problem and scale the solution, through domain knowledge, grit and execution.
- T is tech-enabled, not tech-first. He says a customer does not care that a bad burger was made by a robot.
- E is exponential scale potential. He says a company that meets the first five is a good business, but his fund also needs the E.
- For scale potential he says to look at the next five years, not twenty.
- He names notifications, Lemlist, CleverTap and content as ways to reduce the cost of acquisition and of the first transaction.

### Exits

- He says large companies acquire to expand their portfolio in areas they cannot build in-house, and names Marico buying Beardo and Hindustan Unilever buying Minimalist.

## Examples and cases used

- Zomato: his example of a founder with a small holding, about 3.5%, who still carries the liability.
- Everest Fleet: his example of a founder who kept 60% of a business that Uber invested in.
- Marico and Beardo, Hindustan Unilever and Minimalist: large companies buying what they cannot build in-house.

## Numbers given

- Damani's ownership figures: about 53% average promoter holding in Nifty companies against 15.26% average founder holding.
- Damani: the smallest Nifty company has a market cap of about 6,000 crore rupees.
- Damani: a VC firm needs about 75% IRR growth from a portfolio company.
- Damani: no more than 10% dilution in a round as a mark of success.

# Adding issues and contingent contracts

A short recording from the six-part negotiation course, taught by the course professor, who is not named in it. She closes the debrief of a multi-issue television licensing exercise, makes a few points on scarcity and contingent contracts, and hands out the next case. Only fragments of the lecture survive.

## What was said

### No money left on the table

- The instructor says that in an integrative, multi-issue negotiation no money should be left on the table, and calls that result a Pareto optimal outcome.
- She says negotiation theory comes from game theory and economics.
- The tools she lists for getting there are asking questions, getting at the other side's interests, contingent contracts, post-settlement settlements and trade-offs.
- She says post-settlement settlements are not very common in practice.

### Lessons from the licensing exercise

- She says you should know how the other side is paid. Her example is that sales representatives are paid on commission, which shapes what they look for.
- She says the licensing fee in the exercise was a distributive issue, and that the place to focus was the issue the buyer valued much more than the financing terms.
- She says you should work out what you gain or lose on one issue against what you give up on another.
- She tells the class to be ready to give something up. "It is not going to happen that you are going to get everything."
- She says pairs that brought a compatible extra issue into the exercise added one million dollars of value to the table.

### Keep adding issues

- She says a negotiator should be thinking about extra issues all the time: issues that you care about or that the other side cares about.
- She says more issues on the table give more opportunity to trade off.
- She says the better you know the other party, the easier this becomes.

### Scarcity

- She says people assign more value to things they believe are scarce, even when those things are not scarce.
- Her examples are waiting periods for cars and messages that only five seats are left.

### Contingent contracts

- She says a contingent contract lets two sides who disagree about the future bet on it instead of arguing over whose data is right.
- She calls it a risk-sharing tool that aligns the parties' interests and creates long-term interdependence between them.
- She says a contingent contract needs a formula.
- She says adding a contingent contract in the class exercise added a further million dollars or more, depending on how it was written.

### Scoring and stages

- She repeats that a multi-issue negotiation needs a scoring system, because without one you cannot compare offers or create packages.
- She names three stages of a negotiation: how to start, how to exchange offers or communicate, and how to close.
- She says the strategy for each stage depends on the relationship you have with the other party.

## Examples and cases used

- A television series licensing exercise: used to show that the fee is distributive while other issues can be traded and added.
- Car waiting periods and limited-seat messages: used to show how perceived scarcity raises value.

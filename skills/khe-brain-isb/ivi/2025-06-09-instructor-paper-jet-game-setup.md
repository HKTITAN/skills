# Paper jet factory game, rules and setup

The operations management instructor, who is not named in the recording, briefs the class on a simulation he calls the ISB jet game and runs a trial round. The recording covers only the rules and the trial. The timed game and the lessons drawn from it are in the next recording, on lean operations. The date is taken from the same morning's earlier recording.

## What was said

### How the game is set up

- The instructor says the whole class works for one firm that makes jets out of sticky notes. Coloured caps put each person in one of four product groups and one role.
- One side of the room is the firm and the other side is the market.
- The flow he describes is supplier, workstation 1, workstation 2, workstation 3, internal quality check, then the market, where an account manager sells to a customer.
- Suppliers are part of the firm, like a procurement team. They tear off sheets only when someone asks, and exactly four sheets each time, which makes one batch.
- Workstation 1 folds the sheet into a triangle, workstation 2 folds it into a jet, and workstation 3 writes ISB in capital letters on both wings.
- Nobody may leave their seat during the game. All material and all information has to move through the forklift person of the same colour.
- All material inside the firm must move in batches of four. The rule of four does not apply when jets go to the market.
- Two people without caps act as auditors who watch for cheating.

### Quality and money

- The instructor says quality is a vague term, so the game fixes two rules only. The tip of the jet must be sharp and the wings must look similar in size.
- The quality checker may not reject a jet for any other reason. A jet that passes gets a tick mark on a wing.
- Customers apply the same two quality rules when jets reach the market.
- In the game a sheet of raw material costs 1 dollar, so each batch released costs the firm 4 dollars.
- Each workstation adds 0.5 dollars of cost per unit, to cover salaries, so a jet costs 1.5, 2 and 2.5 dollars after the three workstations. He gives the cost of a finished jet as 3 dollars.
- A good jet sells for 20 dollars, which he describes as a chance to make 17 dollars of profit per jet.
- He says penalties apply at the end. A finished jet that was not sold costs 3 dollars, and jets the customer rejected for bad quality are also charged.
- The game is timed at 10 minutes. He says both quality and speed matter.
- Account managers log every transaction and can hold good jets the customer did not buy in a bag, to sell when the customer next asks for more.
- Suppliers log each batch released on a sheet.

## Numbers given

- 1 dollar per sheet, 0.5 dollars added per workstation, 3 dollars cost per finished jet, 20 dollars selling price: the game's cost and price figures as the instructor states them.
- Batch size of 4 and a 10 minute timer: the two constraints of the game.

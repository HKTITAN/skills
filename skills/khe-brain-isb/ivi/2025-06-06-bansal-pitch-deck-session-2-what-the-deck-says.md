# Pitch deck bootcamp session 2 on what the deck says, Mohit Bansal

Typed notes from the second pitch deck session with Mohit Bansal of DeckRooster. He set out the three kinds of content a deck needs, a canvas for finding the messages, the order to put them in, and how to build each slide.

## What was said

### Positioning for investors

- Mohit Bansal says the market is already shifting, and nobody wants to invest in businesses where you must educate the customer.
- He says you must show the investor that you can do consumer positioning.
- He says understanding the need is key. His example is a shake, which looks like a drink but serves the need for an on-the-go breakfast.
- He says the customer does not say they need an on-the-go breakfast, but the founder must know what need is being served.
- He says a deck must promise some well defined future outcomes that you will deliver.

### Three kinds of content

- He says an investor sees a business as a small part of their larger worldview, so the founder should say what their worldview is and where the business fits in it.
- He calls the first kind of content the strategic narrative. Its purpose is to reframe the opportunity so the company feels like a high upside, timely, category-defining play.
- His examples of strategic narrative are market shifts and timing, wedge entry and platform vision, and founder insight or worldview.
- He calls the second kind operational soundness. Its purpose is to show the business as a well designed, internally coherent system, and its role is to build trust.
- His examples of operational soundness are the structure of the acquisition engine, product depth or edge, the monetisation model and its logic, a repeatable delivery model, and cost structures and operating leverage.
- He calls the third kind proof that it is already working. Its purpose is to show real world signals of traction, and its role is to build belief based on momentum.
- His examples of proof are revenue growth, gross margin profile, retention, go-to-market, key partnerships and customer testimonials.

### Message discovery canvas

- He uses a message discovery canvas with five blocks: possibility, evidence, capability, future and action.
- He says possibility is the future the founder believes in and holds the strategic narrative, evidence holds the proof, and capability holds how a solid engine has been built.
- He says future is where the company is going.
- He says future outcomes are stated as what will happen in this business, not as what will happen with this round of money.

### Building the flow

- He says to build the flow in two rounds. Round one sequences the messages inside each block, and round two arranges the blocks.
- For the strategic narrative he says to start with the hook, the most exciting, surprising or insight rich message.
- He says the hook is followed by why now, why us and how the market is evolving, and the block ends with how big this could be.
- For evidence he says to start with the strongest and clearest win and follow with the financials.
- He says round two arranges the blocks in the order possibility, evidence, capability, future, action.

### Slides

- He says step one for a slide is to form the message. Every slide must have one message, so decide the one thing it is trying to say.
- He says step two is to answer the questions that would come after that message, so a slide has a top level message and then sub-level messages.
- He says the cover slide should keep the logo visible when the deck is seen as an email attachment.
- He says lines addressed to you belong to customers, not investors, so the cover should speak of bigger markets and opportunities.
- He says that if a category or company is misunderstood, the deck should start changing that view from slide one.
- He says the cover should do whatever grabs the investor's eye, whether in one line or five.

## Examples and cases used

- A shake: a drink in form, an on-the-go breakfast in need.

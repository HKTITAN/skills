# Hiring and getting hired

He treats hiring and getting hired as the same skill seen from two sides: reading proven competence, yours and other people's. Most of it comes from Kata 8 and Kata 4, which are talks to his own employees, and he says in Kata 8 that the advice is tailored to how Aeos hires and that every company hires differently. The pay and co-founder material comes largely from the KK Create and Masters Union interviews, where he speaks as a guest about his own group.

## What does he hire for?

- Every company hires differently, so there is no single method. He says his advice is tailored to Aeos. (Kata 8 at 1:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=99s)
- Hiring people as interchangeable units is the worst way to hire. He calls the belief that you can quickly swap one employee for another the **fungibility fallacy** and says he has never seen it hold, even at large services companies. (Kata 8 at 4:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=290s)
- In his words, "the right way to hire is more like the Avengers": people cover each other's weaknesses, and each one has a superpower that is very hard to find in the market. (Kata 8 at 4:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=290s)
- His hiring stack, in order, is attitude, cultural fit, learning agility, ability to understand instructions, ability to implement, leadership ability, diligence, customer first, technical skills, and taste. (Kata 8 at 7:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=435s)
- Skills sit lowest because they can always be learned. He says this after 13 or 14 years of hiring and after running a recruitment startup. (Kata 8 at 6:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=386s)
- Attitude comes first, meaning whether the person wants to do this. Someone fantastic at the job who does not want it will quit in two months. (Kata 8 at 7:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=435s)
- Cultural fit comes second because fits pick up skills. He has seen people who could not edit or code learn it, or find another way to help, which made them hard to replace. (Kata 8 at 7:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=435s)
- Most companies are really hiring for learning agility. Three years ago there were no AI tools, and he says one of the highest paid roles in India is now your job plus AI. (Kata 8 at 8:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=483s)
- Taste matters more in the age of AI: when an output comes, can you tell if it is good or bad. (Kata 8 at 10:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=627s)
- Introducing Rowan, who hires a lot of technical people because he is working on a game, he says that for Rowan technical skills are much higher at the top. (Kata 8 at 20:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1255s)
- His second framework is **CHAMP-R**: cultural fit, hard skills, attitude, motivation, potential, and reassure, meaning whether you can trust the person's read of a situation. (Kata 8 at 27:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1639s)
- For a mid-level role he looks for fast learners who are able to understand reality, meaning they accept how people behave at scale. He says very stubborn people who insist "we have to do it this way" are not hired. (KK Create at 1:24:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5074s; KK Create at 1:26:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5170s)
- Do not hire fans. He avoids crazy fan boys because they spend their time fanboying instead of working. (KK Create at 42:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2525s)
- He hires IITians for grind, not intelligence. He says his core team at the group level now has a lot of them and that he gets them because they have been through the grind. (Masters Union at 47:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2853s)
- Aeos will not work with someone just because they are nice. He calls niceness table stakes and says competent people disagree, say no, kill bad ideas and ask uncomfortable questions. (Kata 4 at 45:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2700s)
- Entry-level people are hired for potential and senior people for proven expertise. (Kata 8 at 29:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1780s)
- Know whether you are hiring for today or for a year from now. Aeos does not mind investing a year or two in potential, and he says a maturing company hires more for current state. (Kata 8 at 33:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2022s)
- Never hire a full-time employee for a **maybe role**. One week of work goes to a freelancer or agency, because a full-time hire carries an emotional tax: you are committing to a career. (Kata 8 at 34:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2070s)
- Do not take co-founders who have never taken personal risk, because they assume a salary plus a percentage is their due. (Kata 4 at 51:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3087s)
- He holds back trust from people who may take the brand and leave for a 2x offer six months later. (Masters Union at 38:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2286s)
- He compares Aeos to a ranked gaming client that never let unknown players in, and says the company cannot lower the bar. (Kata 4 at 53:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3233s)
- He says most of his job is hiring, and that hiring someone good takes pressure off him. In October 2026 he said "we hire like 100 people a month. I hire 50 people a month." (KK Create at 27:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1661s; KK Create at 1:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=96s)

## How does he test and vet a candidate?

- He learned in 2026 that you can test every single thing he looks for by evaluating how a candidate prompts a model. In the interview, sit with the person, give them some task and watch them prompt. (Kata 8 at 11:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=674s)
- Generic prompts show the person does not want to do it. The prompts, what they miss in the output and what quality they accept show every item of his hiring stack. (Kata 8 at 11:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=674s)
- You cannot hire well unless you can pick out what a person did. When a CV says someone contributed to something, you need to judge how critical they were. (Kata 6 at 4:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=281s)
- When hiring, he prefers a track record of repeated attempts over CV, design or college, and asks exactly what the person contributed. (Kata 4 at 50:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3041s)
- Aeos asks whether a hire worked at a profitable startup and how much of that they contributed, and cares less about the last salary. (Kata 4 at 40:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2414s)
- Hiring a salesperson or a manager is close to gambling. Sales CVs always claim the company's growth, and managers take credit for everything. (Kata 8 at 14:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=866s)
- Intangible roles are judged by association. A management role at a well-known company makes you much more likely to be hired because nobody can trust the CV, which he says AI has polluted. (Kata 8 at 25:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1541s)
- Referrals work because the referrer's reputation is on the line, so it is not just nepotism. (Kata 8 at 26:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1589s)
- You must vet people even though references can be faked. Companies call past employers and four or five old colleagues, and in India people often prime four friends to say good things. (Kata 8 at 32:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1973s)
- A bad feeling about culture fit has always turned out bad. Not once has someone who was not a culture fit turned out good for the company. (Kata 8 at 32:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1973s)
- Interviews should be structured and hard. Structure lets you grade 15 candidates against each other, and he learned from Google that a rigorous interview gives people a sense of having earned the job. (Kata 8 at 33:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2022s)
- Rushing a hire never works. His first hire for a role usually does not do well, and the third or fourth person interviewed might. (Kata 8 at 32:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1925s)
- He says he has never done a good job rushing the hiring process: his first hire for something usually does not do well, and the third or fourth person he interviews might. You do not have enough of a sample size if you talk to one person. (Kata 8 at 32:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1925s)
- His hiring mistakes list is rushing, not vetting, ignoring culture fit, not deciding between current state and future potential, unstructured interviews, hiring for a maybe role, and no clear week-one instructions. (Kata 8 at 32:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1925s)
- His hiring hygiene is to keep a pipeline, always tell candidates the company's story, run rigorous structured interviews, track everything including referrals in an applicant tracking system, use your own channels, keep the process smooth and fire quickly if wrong. (Kata 8 at 35:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2118s)
- His test for sorting people already hired comes from Brad Jacobs's book: imagine they quit today. Panic means an A player, three months to replace means B, and relief means C. (Kata 8 at 36:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2215s)
- "The goal is to create a C-free zone." He says you cannot build an organisation of only A players, but should not keep anyone whose leaving would relieve you. (Kata 8 at 38:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2312s)
- A content channel is a hiring engine. He said in March 2026 that a story from him brings about 2,000 applicants, and in April 2026 that one story across Aeos channels brings 10,000 candidates. (Kata 6 at 32:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1958s; Kata 8 at 40:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2456s)
- The best hirers at Aeos do not rely on brand. They build a relationship with a candidate and send company updates for a year. (Kata 4 at 37:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2268s)
- You will not get the best talent without putting work into the world. After the game's first trailer people asked why they should work for him, and after three trailers he had people from big films and studios. (Kata 12 at 32:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1926s)
- For founder's office hires he gives 10 tasks, enough that they are slightly drowning, and counts how many are dropped without reporting back. Under 10% is good. (KK Create at 1:15:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4540s)

## How should you read competence, yours and theirs?

- You need to read competence well for two reasons: hiring and getting hired. He says he was very bad at this at 25. (Kata 4 at 1:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=96s)
- He calls the read a **scouter** level, after Dragon Ball. It asks whether a person can do what they say: add the value they claim, write the check, take the final decision. (Kata 4 at 2:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=147s)
- Competence is built by what Aeos calls the test: believe in something, take a big swing with personal risk, face stacked odds and ridicule, and pull it off. Passing raises your level permanently. (Kata 4 at 8:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=535s)
- His five levels are NPCs with no beliefs or attempts, commentators with strong opinions and zero attempts, local doers with small low-risk attempts, founders or owners with repeated high-risk attempts, and kingmakers who can move markets. (Kata 4 at 20:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1255s)
- Everybody misreads their own competence. Some undervalue themselves, and others think they deserve more because someone at another startup earns slightly more. (Kata 4 at 8:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=487s)
- The gap between thinking you can do it and having done it is far wider than people think. His image is vegetables on a table versus a cooked dish, and he puts the gap at a few years. (Kata 4 at 7:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=436s)
- Commentators, however passionate, do not earn scouter levels. He says competent people take the person who has done something more seriously than the person who has not, even if that person has a nicely written takedown. (Kata 4 at 21:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1304s; Kata 4 at 22:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1350s)
- A failed attempt still earns competence if it was clean: you gave it your all, owned it openly and shut it down with reasons. (Kata 4 at 16:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=966s)
- One real success raises your level for life. The founder of Hotmail built one thing 20 years ago and still has his respect. (Kata 4 at 41:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2512s)
- Repeating the same failure without updating your model is what lowers your level, which he calls a scouter penalty. (Kata 4 at 43:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2606s)
- A clean failure buys another shot, but at some point you must deliver. He worries about people who spent 10 years across startups that all died and still quote a salary. (Kata 4 at 40:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2414s)
- People are not born at a level. He has seen people he graded as NPCs change quickly with a little more exposure to risk. (Kata 4 at 24:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1493s)
- To raise your own level: pick a game with measurable outcomes, place a visible personal bet, ship in public, ignore ridicule but collect usable feedback, and get one clear win. (Kata 4 at 49:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2944s)
- He says "people who are most confident are people who have not been tested", and that after enough tests you are 50/50 on everything. (Kata 8 at 47:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2841s)

## What red flags does he name in candidates and hires?

- His red flag syndromes are victim, messiah, ostrich, Peter Pan, I-know-best and burnout. (Kata 8 at 41:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2505s; Kata 8 at 45:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2744s; Kata 8 at 46:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2792s; Kata 8 at 47:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2841s)
- Victim syndrome is the belief that you are being exploited. He traces it to parents and a colonial mindset and says it does not fit a free talent market for digital skills. (Kata 8 at 41:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2505s)
- Messiah syndrome is believing you are there to save people, and he says a free market has no messiahs and no victims. Ostrich syndrome is burying your head in the sand, such as building something the client did not ask for. Peter Pan syndrome is an inability to grow up that puts small childish problems on the company. (Kata 8 at 45:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2744s; Kata 8 at 46:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2792s; Kata 8 at 47:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2841s)
- He hates entitlement. His example is a person on 10 lakhs last year asking for a crore, and his answer is that he will pay a crore when someone generates a crore of value. (KK Create at 1:26:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5219s)
- He can tell in appraisal talks when someone is keeping every door open, and says impact on the main thing drops as options rise. (Kata 7 at 8:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=485s)
- In November 2026 he said that for the 30th time that month someone who wanted to join had told him they might join, or do content, or start a startup. His view is that there is no way you have belief if you are even considering doing five things at once. (Why Belief Matters at 31:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1910s; Why Belief Matters at 32:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1956s)
- Interviewing at 10 places is fake optionality, since you have not quit and do not know if the offers are serious. Real optionality is transferable skill built through repetition. (Kata 9 at 37:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2249s)
- He built in North Bangalore partly to get away from HSR, where he saw talent that had spent 10 years hopping between unprofitable startups, taking money quickly and leaving before each company died. (Kata 1 at 13:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=827s)
- Job-hoppers with no record end up valued at half what they expect. (Kata 8 at 30:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1828s)
- Never burn bridges when you leave. Badmouthing a company locks you out of it and its whole ecosystem of customers if it gets big. (Kata 9 at 34:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2060s)

## How does pay work: leverage, bonuses and skin in the game?

- Pay follows leverage, and leverage follows proof. Some staff cannot do much if they get no raise, and others are so good that whatever they ask, the company has to give. (Kata 8 at 28:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1733s)
- Companies open their wallets only for proven competence. Once you have proved it the company pays because it does not want to lose you. (Kata 4 at 17:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1062s)
- Belief without proven outcomes does not earn a salary. A candidate whose last two companies were unprofitable named a figure, and he replied that none of his outcomes prove it and that once proven he would make far more. (Kata 4 at 9:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=584s)
- The biggest mistake is to be high potential but **pre-proof** and ask for a senior salary. (Kata 8 at 29:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1780s)
- Age and family duties do not justify a salary. He says no company in India cares that you are 32 or 35 with a wife and kids, and that with proof a 21-year-old and a 31-year-old command the same. (Kata 8 at 30:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1828s)
- Bonuses come to you when the company fears losing you, and asking usually fails. If you have to ask, you are probably not where you think you are. (Kata 8 at 38:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2312s)
- Aeos pays above market for every role and gives its best people uncapped bonuses or a percentage. He does not feel bad giving it because top people create multiples of it in revenue. (Kata 8 at 38:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2312s)
- You cannot have full market salary and skin in the game together. The people he respects offer to take the high-risk campaign and reduce their salary for a share if it works. (Kata 4 at 22:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1350s)
- Co-founders in his group join at zero salary and live on equity and profit share. If the company makes nothing for 6 months, they make nothing. (KK Create at 29:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1757s)
- Profit share can align people better than equity, because it pushes whoever runs a unit to earn enough without ruining the brand and to keep costs low. (KK Create at 57:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=3432s)
- He hands out co-founder-style equity without being asked. Some game team members got equity with no strike price because they worked Sundays, and he says a VC-funded company would never allow it. (Masters Union at 40:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2429s)
- Pay differs across his companies because stage and risk differ, so the exact same role can pay X in one and 3X in another. His first two editors will make three to four times what regular editors make because they were early: he underpaid them when he started his channel and said he would pay them better if it did well. (KK Create at 1:18:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4734s; KK Create at 1:20:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4831s)
- Market rate is whatever stops competitors sniping your talent. He finds it by advertising a role at an absurdly high salary and lowering it until about half the candidates say no. (KK Create at 1:21:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4879s; KK Create at 1:22:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4927s)
- Some talent has left Aeos for 3x or 4x salaries, mostly to the few international companies that pay that, and he says Aeos did not complain. (Kata 8 at 42:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2554s)
- He wants every appraisal conversation at Aeos to start by saying a no is a budget decision, not a verdict on your worth. (Secret Kata 1 at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=630s)
- Never look at what you are being paid right now; he calls it a bad metric. Take a 2-year view: his example weighs 30,000 rupees a month at a finance company, where the market is continuing to grow, against a sales role at 60k a month at a company selling monitors, where the market is kind of capped. (Kata 9 at 27:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1633s; Kata 9 at 28:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1680s)
- Take the compounding option over the immediate one. He argues 1% profit share at a proven, fast-growing company beats three freelance projects at 2 lakhs a month each, because freelancers lose a project or two every month. (Why Belief Matters at 34:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2053s)
- Aeos usually refuses free work, but he says that if a large, trusted, fair company is unsure about you, it is your responsibility to do something for free. For a random company with no history of paying, say no. (Kata 13 at 1:04:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3862s)

## How do you get hired, and what do candidates get wrong?

- Most people are not getting jobs because there are too many resumes made of words, and claims on a CV are hard to verify. (Kata 8 at 12:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=769s)
- A portfolio makes college and past employer irrelevant for visible skills. The minute you have one in a visual medium, he says you are ahead of almost everybody. (Kata 8 at 13:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=819s)
- For intangible roles such as sales or management the only path he sees is to take whatever job you can for the first three or four years, at any salary or as an internship. He says he has no other solution. (Kata 8 at 24:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1493s)
- If you are not getting hired, build a network as well as a portfolio, because referrals carry the referrer's reputation. (Kata 8 at 26:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1589s)
- Believing in your potential without trying is a safety blanket. Companies will bet on a little proven potential, but you cannot charge the salary of someone already proven. (Kata 8 at 28:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1687s)
- Opportunities are not given. He learned you have to grind enough, be visible enough and perform enough miracles before top people acknowledge you. (Kata 4 at 6:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=387s)
- Employers decide the way buyers do, so show up with content every week or few weeks while you do not need anything. People who leave Aeos start posting the next day and everyone can see the urgency. (Kata 10 at 19:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1169s)
- For jobs he says text is taken more seriously than video right now, so LinkedIn or Twitter beats Instagram or YouTube. (Kata 10 at 19:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1169s)
- Companies trust confused people less and give them smaller roles. You go all in first and prove it, and then you get what you ask for. (Kata 7 at 19:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1156s)
- The best at anything wins: the best B.Com graduate beats the average B.Tech, and the best person with no degree at something they care about beats an IITian. (Kata 7 at 22:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1346s)
- Stacking prestige internships is a status game. He says internships at Meta, Google and Nvidia would be a complete waste and one is enough. (Kata 7 at 24:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1490s)
- After three jobs more sampling does not help. Rapid switching is fine up to three, and if the second is good, stick. (Kata 7 at 32:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1970s)
- Choose growth rate over absolute scale. A fast-growing startup has excess money and unclaimed responsibility you can just take. (Kata 7 at 34:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=2065s)
- When something is growing very fast, stay on board. Of an early team photo almost everyone left for a 10% raise or to start something, and those who stayed did very well. (Kata 1 at 1:05:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3917s)
- Your ceiling is your boss's level, and he says "you're not really joining a company. You're joining a manager." (Kata 4 at 50:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3041s; Kata 4 at 51:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3087s)
- "Being reliable in India can be a bigger advantage than appearing brilliant." A professional gives a clear yes or no at once, while many brilliant people take weeks. (Kata 13 at 1:06:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=4007s)
- Aim to be one of the roughly 50 critical people out of about 600 at Aeos, by being the person who reduces complexity instead of adding it. (Kata 11 at 8:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=532s)

## How should you approach him or any busy person?

- Send a portfolio through the proper hiring channel, not a plea to him. He never hires people who message that they will do anything for a job, because he does not know what to hire them for. (Kata 8 at 49:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2984s)
- Vague messages to successful people get no response. His examples are a bare hello, a plea for help, a question about which degree to do, or a warning that something will fail. (Kata 13 at 8:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=481s)
- Before you message or sell to someone, do what he calls blinking, a Dota term he uses for teleporting into the other person's mind to see what they want. (Kata 13 at 2:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=147s)
- People do not respond because of a mismatch between what you want and what they want. (Kata 13 at 12:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=767s)
- Busy people respond to public signals more than to direct messages. A rich friend ignored his WhatsApp pitch, then three weeks later sent him his own video about the same company asking to get in. (Kata 13 at 55:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3339s)
- Sometimes go bottom-up: an assistant who saw the billboard, ad or reel shares it with the boss, who trusts the assistant. (Kata 13 at 56:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3386s)
- More data does not change a busy person's mind, more real-world yeses do. Even with direct access he says to build traction first. (Kata 13 at 58:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3528s)
- "Relationships are built through usefulness, not networking." You cannot walk into an Aeos event and network with nothing valuable to offer. (Kata 13 at 1:03:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3814s)
- Standing next to a CEO at events does nothing for your level. You have to do something valuable for or with that person, and high-level people cannot stand small talk. (Kata 4 at 42:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2559s)
- Proof of work gets you into a network. The page listing everything Aeos did in a year worked better than anything else, and he never refuses when a VC asks him to present to their founders for free. (Kata 13 at 1:08:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=4103s)
- To sell to someone you must understand their viewpoint, down to where they invest and what their biggest problems are. (Kata 1 at 1:15:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=4558s)
- He has been on the other side: in 2015 he emailed Sam Altman while emailing about 70 investors, got a generic reply and counts it as one more rejection. (Kata 1 at 26:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1600s)

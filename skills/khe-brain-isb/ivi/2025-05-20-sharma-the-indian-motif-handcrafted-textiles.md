# Building a handcrafted textiles brand, Swetha Sharma

Swetha Sharma, founder of The Indian Motif, presented her company to the cohort with slides and then took questions. The recording is badly garbled, so this note keeps only the points that can be read with confidence. Most of her answers in the long question round could not be recovered.

## What was said

### The problem and the company

- Swetha Sharma says she comes from a technology background and not from fashion design or textiles.
- She says there are buyers who want authentic handcrafted textiles, but the ecosystem is unstructured and access is limited to fairs and exhibitions.
- She says there are so many counterfeit products in the market that nobody can look at a product and tell whether it is genuine.
- She says The Indian Motif works with more than 40 craft clusters, out of more than 100 craft types in India. She describes a cluster as a group of people, like a village, who work on a craft together.
- She says the company sources from the clusters, co-innovates with them, and aims to promote Indian handcrafted textiles to the world.
- She says the company started during the COVID period with five clusters that she had visited herself, and launched its web store on Shopify.
- She says the company works towards certifications because of the amount of counterfeit product in the market.

### Working with artisans

- She says co-designing with artisans means you have to give them scale, and if you cannot give them scale you are better off not doing it.
- She says the company co-designs only with clusters where it has enough depth and maturity in the relationship.
- She says artisans generally do not like to replicate the same piece over time.
- She says she wants to use the artisan's creativity and keep the originality of the craft, while keeping the product palatable to buyers.

### A crowded category

- She says clothing and apparel is a very busy category with a low barrier to entry, and that on Instagram the brands are almost impossible to tell apart.
- She compares handcrafted textiles to organic food: about 20 years ago organic food was a very small part of the market, and organic food companies are doing well today.
- She says large groups entering the handcrafted space is good news because it shows the potential, but they have very deep pockets.

### Working capital

- She says the business has very high working capital needs, because the artisans work in some of the poorest places.
- She says the cash conversion cycle starts at minus three months before the product is even ready, and after that it depends on how quickly the product sells.
- She says the company makes small batches for this reason, and that small batches work for the artisans as well as for the company.

### Marketing and channels

- She says performance marketing relies on the best selling product being sold again and again, and most of her products are single pieces, so the traditional approach does not work well for her.
- She says the company writes a lot of blog content on its website for people who are searching for such content.
- She says the store in Bangalore is not in a typical commercial centre.
- She says the hard problem is scaling the storytelling that happens when someone walks into the store.
- She says she wants a customer to find no difference between the physical store and the web store.
- She says that with an online only consumer business, the company cannot know whether people read or watch what it puts out, so a store makes it easier to tell the story.
- She says packaging was a huge part of the brand's story.

### Supply side difficulties

- She says festivals cause labour shortages in the clusters.
- She says raw materials are a problem too, and gives the example that water is not available uniformly across regions at all times.

### Plans and lessons

- She says the plans are to strengthen offline, do more collaborations with other brands, and extend the business to business offering.
- She says the company is looking at technology across the value chain, including supply chain and marketing automation.
- She advises founders to start small, with a small product line as an MVP, and to validate the hypothesis.
- She says collaboration matters especially in this category.
- She says a founder runs on intuition and should collect enough data to validate that intuition over time.

## Examples and cases used

- Organic food: her comparison for how a small, premium category became mainstream over about 20 years.

## Numbers given

- More than 40 craft clusters worked with, out of more than 100 craft types in India, in her count.
- Five clusters at the start.
- Minus three months: where she says her cash conversion cycle begins.

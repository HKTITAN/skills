# Money, status and the rich

He treats money as a side effect of status, validation and risk, and treats rich people as a group you can understand by stepping into their heads. Most of this comes from Kata 13, a talk he said would offend people and that he might cut parts of, with Kata 9 on risk and a guest podcast, PGX Ideas 2, on status and tribes. He admits his sample is small: 20 to 30 people in the 100 crore band and about 10 to 20 in the 1,000 crore band.

## What actually drives rich people?

- He says the idea that rich people just want more money is wrong. Social media and news cannot read intentions, and most people judge from one tweet or a clipped podcast. (Kata 13 at 4:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=241s)
- Wealth is a side effect of wanting validation and status. Cristiano Ronaldo is a billionaire who plays for being number one, the trophies and the respect, not for the next brand deal. (Kata 13 at 7:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=434s)
- India is allergic to people who have generated wealth, yet everyone wants to be rich. He says Indians cloud the intentions of the wealthy as greed, and that parents taught this. (Kata 13 at 6:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=386s)
- Everyone he has met above $100 million in net worth is curious and excited to learn, and he says he has met hundreds across many countries with no exception. Many billionaires are like kids about new technology. (Kata 13 at 11:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=672s)
- The unsuccessful people he has met have settled into victimhood and reject new technology, with nostalgia for a past that did not exist. He flags that this is only what he has observed. (Kata 13 at 9:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=575s)
- Rich people he has met do not care about failing or being mocked. He says there is something mentally wrong with them, in that social feedback does not affect them, while India shames failure. (Kata 13 at 10:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=624s)
- His four traits of people who are good at everything: always curious, not afraid of mistakes, willing to make many attempts even when bad at it, and learning something from each failure. (Kata 13 at 8:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=528s)
- When a leader becomes too afraid of failure, their career ends. That is when they start really making mistakes and get replaced within a year or two. (Kata 13 at 11:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=719s)
- He says VCs have a word for why they invest, excitement: to invest in a company you need to be excited about it, not just greedy and rich. He is super excited about gaming in India and has invested there, but an old-school manufacturing plant is not exciting to him even though it might make him money. (Kata 13 at 58:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3528s; Kata 13 at 59:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3576s)
- At 1,000 crore, narrative, control and being in the news matter more than the next crore. He says celebrity athletes hit an existential crisis around 45 to 50 because the news moves to a newer face. (Kata 13 at 1:00:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3623s)
- The question changes as wealth rises. Broke: how do I earn. Comfortable: how do I save. Rich: how do I multiply. Very rich: how do I control. Dynasty: how do I preserve. (Kata 13 at 1:10:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=4245s)
- The most successful people hold privileged information and do not share it in public, because people would shout at them if they said what works. He thinks the public is one to two years behind. (Kata 13 at 1:01:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3673s)
- Financially successful entrepreneurs live very simple lives because their businesses are already complicated. He says he does not know one who is not simple in life, and that those who keep both complicated mostly end up in jail or leave the country. (Kata 11 at 13:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=818s)
- Almost every wealthy person he has met shares with teams and inner circles, and some pledge to give away 90% of their wealth. This is his reason for expecting the elite to share AI abundance, though he admits he has no idea if it will happen. (PGX Ideas 2 at 4:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=287s)
- **Blinking** is his word, from Dota, for teleporting out of your own mind into another person's to see what they want before you sell to or message them. (Kata 13 at 2:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=147s)

## What are the four liquid wealth zones?

- He sorts people into four liquid wealth zones: 1 crore, 10 crore, 100 crore and 1,000 crore. The measure is cash and assets on hand, not paper net worth. (Kata 13 at 14:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=862s)
- Net worth is a misleading metric. He says every public founder he knows would crash the stock and end at zero if they tried to take out their whole net worth. (Kata 13 at 14:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=862s)
- At 1 crore people are security focused and want to be in the club. Their goals are family stability, a debt-free home, an emergency fund and retirement, and they think hard about a 5 to 10 lakh spend. (Kata 13 at 15:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=910s)
- The 1 crore group takes identity from the job, buys blue chips, and seeks visible symbols of success within budget. He gives Air Jordans as their marker and says this group has money but not much freedom. (Kata 13 at 15:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=910s; Kata 13 at 14:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=862s)
- At 10 crore people have financial freedom and are comfortable in their own skin. They want recognition for being rich, take moderate risk, hold mutual funds and real estate, and own a 3 to 4 crore home. (Kata 13 at 19:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1145s)
- The 10 crore group still thinks twice about a 10 to 15 lakh trip, and he says most are CXOs. Their marker is the kurta and Indian shoe brands, because they no longer chase foreign signals. (Kata 13 at 19:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1145s)
- At 100 crore people have a status where rules do not apply, and they are driven by influence and growth. They do not believe in the queue and skip it with a phone call. (Kata 13 at 23:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1386s)
- The 100 crore group probably writes 10, 20 or 30 angel checks a year, has a wealth manager, home staff and usually multiple properties, and values experiences over things; some have fractional jet access or charter jets. The shoe that represents them in his telling is the Golden Goose, which costs "like 50, 60k" and is dirty on purpose, because their show off is subtle. (Kata 13 at 24:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1481s; Kata 13 at 26:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1575s; Kata 13 at 27:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1672s)
- At 100 crore most spending beyond savings and luxury goes into buying influence. The phone and the relationship roster is their most important asset, and he has seen them pay a doctor five times as much for access. He says this is not about politicians. (Kata 13 at 28:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1718s)
- The 100 crore group is tied to its venture, so it almost always pushes on to 1,000 crore. Their reputation rests on the thing that made them rich, and doing badly at it hurts more than losing 5 or 10 crores. (Kata 13 at 29:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1767s)
- At 1,000 crore people think about legacy, see opportunities others never see, and feel they make the rules. They plan two or three generations ahead, run family offices, own six to ten businesses and do philanthropy, and he calls them feudal and dynasty driven. (Kata 13 at 31:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1860s)
- The very rich never see a bill and are the freest. A team of about 20 makes the small decisions, and he has seen them fly a paid person along just to do check-in. (Kata 13 at 32:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1956s)
- The very rich run on trust and will pay 10 times more to someone they trust. They also try to avoid visibility because people hate the wealthy. (Kata 13 at 32:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1956s)
- An ad for a 2.16 crore luxury apartment targets the 1 crore group, not the second group: they most want to be in the club, care about the feeling of opulence, and buy on a loan, with the down payment coming out of their crore. Brands with a doctor's approval that are scientifically trusted work better for 10 crores. (Kata 13 at 35:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2102s; Kata 13 at 36:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2195s)

## How do the rich decide what is expensive?

- He says rich people do not buy things, they remove constraints: "Rich people don't buy things. They unlock freedom. They unlock constraints." (Kata 13 at 39:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2382s)
- A helicopter is a jump in mobility where a nicer car is not. An assistant buys back attention and a lawyer buys certainty. Their question is what the biggest bottleneck is and how to remove it. (Kata 13 at 39:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2382s)
- Price is judged by the situation, not by a fixed number. A 5 lakh expense is cheap if it creates 5 crores of access, and 5,000 rupees is expensive if it changes nothing. (Kata 13 at 40:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2431s)
- Every spend needs a return, which is why he says a billionaire does not hand a stranger on Reddit money for a PlayStation. He says this in the same talk where he says the very rich do not care about the next crore. (Kata 13 at 40:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2431s)
- Hyper-negotiating small things is the **middle class trap**, and he says he was stuck in it. He haggled with auto drivers over 100 rupees even after making money, until watching rich people delegate taught him that 100 rupees can be expensive if it costs attention, time or energy. (Kata 13 at 41:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2479s)
- His test for whether something is expensive: if paying makes an annoyance go away, it is worth it. If it is an experience you want and can get at once, it is worth it. If it is the same material with a status markup you do not care about, it is expensive. (Kata 13 at 42:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2527s)
- Rich people skip status spending where nobody sees it or where the jump is small. Many do not upgrade the iPhone every year because the hassle of switching makes it expensive. (Kata 13 at 42:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2527s)
- He describes money at scale as energy: "Money is a type of energy that buys other people's time." It buys speed before luxury. (Kata 13 at 44:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2670s)
- Sometimes you spend on yourself to keep your mental state sharp so you can direct that energy better. He calls this **energy bending**. (Kata 13 at 44:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2670s)
- The middle class mindset hurts growth. People who rise from the lower or middle class keep fixed ideas of what is expensive, so a multi-crore office deposit looks insane when it really buys a set number of months to make the business work. (Kata 13 at 45:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2717s)
- He admits that many years ago he bought an iPhone with little money in the bank, and calls it a poor financial decision. (Kata 13 at 16:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1004s)
- Wealthy people spend on reputation and power, while the average person's only lever is wealth. (Kata 9 at 43:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2584s)

## What is a free market participant, and how do you become one?

- The journey from 1 crore to 1,000 crore is the quest to become a **free market participant**: someone who can enter any industry without sitting and running it. At 1 crore you do one thing yourself, and at 100 crore plus you are opportunity driven, like playing Monopoly. (Kata 13 at 37:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2242s)
- The first step in wealth making is to delink your time and energy from the wealth you create. You think about ownership and about making money while you sleep. He owns a tiny percent of Nvidia and its team works while he sleeps. (Kata 13 at 46:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2812s)
- His standard for a company: "A startup is only successful if it gives you time freedom." He sees young founders start two or three things and only add work to themselves. (Kata 13 at 46:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2812s)
- His three ways to become a free market participant: buy or rent skills by hiring people, make media that brings a lead five or six years later, and own assets. (Kata 13 at 1:02:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3720s)
- The ordinary question is how to find the next client. The rich question is how to build a system through which clients keep arriving, which means a brand people believe backed by a product good enough to be referred. (Kata 13 at 57:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3433s)
- Generational wealth is an institution, not one person, so the best travel in packs. In family businesses one person runs operations, one political relationships, one investments and one reputation, and a lone brilliant individual competes against seven or eight heads. (Kata 13 at 1:09:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=4197s)
- His version of diversification: have one thing that works and takes your time, and put money in other things that do not need your time. (Kata 9 at 47:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2868s)
- Creators who say they will start a business after getting big usually do not. At a million followers they make enough with a two-person team and do not want the headache of payroll, and he calls Aeos an exception. (Kata 11 at 16:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=963s)

## How do rich people take risk?

- On where wealth comes from: "At some point the source of wealth is always risk." Everyone successful either took risk or descends from someone who did, and if hard work were the cause every labourer would be wealthy. (Kata 9 at 3:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=197s)
- He used to think investors were foolish for not wanting profitability and now says he was the foolish one. Once you have enough, the 5% you do not need should aim for the moon, and an investor is fine if the company dies. (Kata 13 at 1:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=100s)
- The wealthy prefer a risky bet with an extreme outcome over a safe bet with a standard one. Someone with 100 crores will not put 10 crores into something that returns 12 in five years. They do not mind losing the 10 but want a chance at 500. (Kata 13 at 48:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2908s)
- He says risk appetite rises with wealth, then adds that at 1,000 crore the risk level drops for smaller projects, because losing 10 crores many times is affordable. (Kata 13 at 49:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2957s)
- Taking enormous risks is the wrong lesson to draw. The right one is how to survive failure while keeping the upside: downside is capped at zero and upside is unlimited, and gambling everything is wrong. (Kata 13 at 54:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3242s)
- Wealthy people have a different risk profile because their reserves let them keep taking shots. Serial entrepreneurs can start a third or fifth business because an earlier one worked. (Kata 9 at 43:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2584s)
- Keep 80 to 90% ultra safe and put 5 to 10% on wild bets, because much of wealth is random. Aeos gets 80 to 90% of its revenue from services and he lets himself go crazy on the rest. (Kata 9 at 53:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3199s)
- His proof that wealth is random is a furniture franchisee he met on a plane, whose business made 3 to 4 crores of profit a year. The fortune came from cheap land bought years earlier that the government later bought, for about 100 crores in one telling and a few hundred crores in the other. (Kata 9 at 46:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2774s; Kata 13 at 52:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3146s)
- Take back the original corpus, as VCs do. Invest 1 crore, and when it is worth 4 sell a quarter to recover the 1 and let 3 ride, because the longer you stay in the more likely tail risk hits. (Kata 9 at 54:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3295s)
- He frames an entrepreneur's real job as reducing risk: do what looks obvious to you and is likely to win, as cheaply as possible, until it wins. To outsiders it then looks like a risk. (Kata 9 at 1:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=99s)
- Funding stages exist to stage risk. A seed investor takes a big share for 2 crores because it may go to zero, and the price rises as the company ticks off risks. He says nobody stages their own life this way and they should. (Kata 9 at 17:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1061s; Kata 9 at 21:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1300s)
- He changed his own investing. He used to put 5 to 10 lakhs into seed companies out of fear of missing out because big funds were in, and they shut down a year later. Now he invests around series A after checkpoints are passed. (Kata 9 at 20:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1251s)
- He says TCS and Infosys can afford any risk and still do not take it, because their risk is now reputational and the stock would dip. (Kata 9 at 44:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2680s)
- Know what game funded founders are playing and judge them by that game: a lot of companies play all or nothing, raising a lot of money to end up in a monopoly position. He recalls a year of comparing his small profit with a company that had burnt 100 crores and is doing very well five years later, and says his old view was maybe cope. (Kata 4 at 4:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=243s; Kata 4 at 5:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=339s; Kata 4 at 8:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=487s)
- His top level of person, the kingmaker, can move markets with a decision and is hedged against failure. Founders are sometimes dealt bad cards, while kingmakers walk in holding the whole deck. (Kata 4 at 20:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1255s)
- A win with low risk or good cards earns less respect, so he says children of rich or successful parents should take on harder and harder projects. (Kata 4 at 15:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=917s)
- Spending when it hurts is a costly signal. Someone with 1 lakh who spends all of it is more credible than someone with 10 crores spending 1 lakh, and he says the bootstrapped game made brands see Aeos as taking real risk. (Masters Union at 21:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1281s)
- He no longer buys the traditional venture dream of raising round after round until investors own most of the company. He did that for 10 years and calls it a kind of lottery. (KK Create at 20:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1221s)

## How do you reach, sell to and earn the trust of rich people?

- To make money, build for people who have capital. Founders building for tier three India ten years ago struggled because there was no disposable income, while people selling something basic to the rich did better. (Kata 13 at 13:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=814s)
- People do not respond or buy because of a mismatch between what you want and what they want. For the first half decade of his career he built things he thought were amazing and nobody cared. (Kata 13 at 12:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=767s)
- To sell you must understand the other person's viewpoint. If the buyer has a thousand crore net worth, you need to know where they invest and what their biggest problems are. (Kata 1 at 1:15:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=4558s)
- Busy rich people respond to public signals more than to direct messages. A rich friend ignored his WhatsApp pitch, then three weeks later sent him his own video about the same company asking to get in. (Kata 13 at 55:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3339s)
- Sometimes go bottom-up. An assistant who saw the billboard, ad or reel shares it with the CEO, who trusts the assistant. (Kata 13 at 56:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3386s)
- More data does not change a busy investor's mind. More real-world yeses do: a team member paying for it, a trusted outside mention, or seeing its ads. So even with a direct line, build traction first. (Kata 13 at 58:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3528s)
- Wealthy clients tell their friends, and at scale the whole loop runs on referral. They take friends far more seriously than any ad or content. (Kata 13 at 59:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3576s)
- On trust: "Trust is where all the capital in the world lies." Third-time founders raise hundreds of millions on a new risky idea purely on trust, and he says trust is unusually valuable in India. (Kata 13 at 1:02:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3767s)
- Rich people treat reputation and brand as a **second bank account**, and the more important one. (Kata 13 at 1:03:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3814s)
- His rule for access: "Relationships are built through usefulness, not networking." You cannot walk into an event and network with nothing valuable to offer. (Kata 13 at 1:03:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3814s)
- Proximity to power is more valuable than skill today. If Ambani's driver uses a new food app, that is more useful to Ambani than a deck from the company, because he trusts the driver and sees real behaviour. (Kata 13 at 1:02:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3720s)
- Rich people understand that giving more than you receive is the norm early in a relationship. If a large, well-known, trusted company known for being fair is not fully sure about you, he says "it's your responsibility to do something for free", but to a random company with no history of paying, don't say yes. (Kata 13 at 1:04:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3862s; Kata 13 at 1:05:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3959s)
- He defends free work for big trusted companies, then notes that Aeos pays all its interns to avoid trouble on social media, and says he may cut the passage. (Kata 13 at 1:10:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=4245s)
- Being reliable in India can be a bigger advantage than appearing brilliant. A professional gives a clear yes or no at once, and people with budgets then let you hire the brilliance you lack. (Kata 13 at 1:06:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=4007s)
- Proof of work gets you into the network and copying their shoes does not. He never refuses when a VC asks him to present to their founders for free, because it sells his company. (Kata 13 at 1:08:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=4103s)
- Vague messages to successful people are pointless, whether a greeting, a plea for help or a prediction that their project will fail. (Kata 13 at 8:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=481s)
- Referrals work because the referrer's reputation is on the line, which is why he says it is not just nepotism. (Kata 8 at 26:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=1589s)
- In B2B, people buy from whoever they are closest to. When three vendors are the same, you transact with the one you have the closest relationship with. (Masters Union at 8:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=524s)
- A trusted stamp travels. When Sequoia invests in a company, enterprises assume the founders are serious, and he says the company becomes ten times cooler with nothing material changed. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 6:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=382s)
- Credibility is nepotistic, so start with influencer or celebrity marketing. If nobody believes in your product, first show that superstars care. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4230s)
- He protects reputation over revenue on his own channel and says no to most brands. (KK Create at 39:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2382s)

## Why does he say status and tribes, not intelligence, decide success?

- As a guest in March 2026 he said success has never been about intelligence. It is about tribe and group formation: you succeed if you are a high-ranked member of a tribe or can bring the tribe together. (PGX Ideas 2 at 39:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2345s)
- He says he learned this in the last two or three years and calls it a radical shift in his life. (PGX Ideas 2 at 38:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2297s; PGX Ideas 2 at 39:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2345s)
- Humans read status the moment they enter a room, which is why he thinks AI cannot become the dominant species. You would be much less likely to join a tribe led by a robot. (PGX Ideas 2 at 39:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2392s)
- Know the scoring of each game you play. For a creator the game is who you are seen next to, for an entrepreneur it is how much money you make, and for a game developer it is how innovative you are. (PGX Ideas 2 at 40:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2439s)
- He thinks status ranking is a kind of **bounded game**, with rules like sports or gaming. He thinks humans will spend a lot of their time playing bounded games, pointing to healthy retirees who play poker-like games through the day and kids of very rich parents who spend a lot of their time on sport. (PGX Ideas 2 at 42:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2537s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:26:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5170s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:27:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5267s)
- Almost all decisions are social. Nobody decides to watch a movie alone, and he finds this extraordinarily dangerous because much of it is manipulated. (PGX Ideas 2 at 49:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=2964s)
- Cash is the least powerful incentive. It matters until someone earns enough among their peer group, and after that it becomes respect, then status, then purpose, then mastery. (Kata 2 at 27:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1640s)
- Money is not the only thing that drives people, whatever they say. If it were, people would not quit Nvidia or Google once their four-year vesting ends. (Kata 2 at 8:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=533s)
- First principles thinking is the opposite of status thinking, which is deciding by what other people think is cool. (Kata 1 at 15:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=922s)
- A brain that thinks it is losing a status game starts reading everything as a threat. He calls India a high rank pressure environment where everything is a rank game. (Secret Kata 1 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=341s; Secret Kata 1 at 8:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=486s)
- The alpha is collaborative, not aggressive. In the monkey studies he cites, aggressive low-serotonin males may win a fight but cannot hold power because they cannot build alliances, and he says success comes from collaboration. (Secret Kata 1 at 16:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=967s; Secret Kata 1 at 17:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1063s)
- Indians do not like it when others do well because they treat the world as zero-sum. He says it is global but worse in India. (Masters Union at 49:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2995s)
- Levels are not the same as money. An NGO founder who made a hard social movement happen ranks higher than someone who got rich on one stock. (Kata 4 at 25:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1540s)
- He builds with family because family has no breaking point. With any co-founder there is some point where one walks away, and he says non-relatives can earn their way into that family. (Masters Union at 35:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2141s; Masters Union at 38:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2286s)
- Big things are not built by a lone wolf. You must generate and share wealth with enough people or you keep losing your best talent. (Kata 1 at 44:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2667s)
- A world needs hierarchy and visible ranks above the buyer. Self-made people buying supercars usually start with the entry-level model just to enter the game. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 15:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=917s)

## What does money do and not do?

- Making significant money did not make him happy. He had always thought it would. The first significant money came at the end of 2024, and he felt the four or five things he was doing were incremental. That is when he called his brother to build a game. (Masters Union at 14:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=856s)
- Money is not what builds big businesses. Aeos started with zero, and many businesses that raised hundreds of millions of dollars went to zero. (Kata 6 at 35:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=2153s)
- What he says does explain business success: an untapped market, good talent and execution, and sticking around long enough to reap the benefits. (Kata 6 at 36:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=2200s)
- Money cannot replace giving something your all. He says this is why brands struggle on YouTube: they give 80% and lose to someone giving 100%. (KK Create at 3:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=190s)
- He calls content creation the fairest game because you cannot pay your way to better content. Tripling spend does not help when a channel can only put out about four videos. (Masters Union at 55:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3328s)
- Throwing money at people didn't work, and he offers venture-funded startups in India as proof: people he knows made a lot of money at VC-funded companies and still left. Money is important up to a certain point, such as for someone who cannot afford three meals, and after that it is about more than money. (Kata 2 at 8:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=533s; Kata 2 at 9:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=582s)
- External rewards can destroy intrinsic motivation. Citing the book Punished by Rewards, he says he went too far on incentives himself. (Kata 2 at 31:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1881s)
- Being profitable is easy when it is your own money. With someone else's 40 crores a borderline hire gets a yes, and in a bootstrapped company every hire comes out of your own pocket. (KK Create at 32:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1951s)
- Start every new business as cheaply as possible, even with unlimited money. Having spent a crore, you feel forced to spend 10 years on the idea. (KK Create at 1:18:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4687s)
- He went bootstrapped because the vision was too weird to pitch, and once it was proven he found the company did not need the money. (Kata 1 at 12:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=778s)
- Loss hurts more than gain feels good. People who miss a projected number feel loss even when they out-earn everyone, so he gives no ballpark projections and no feel-good titles. (Kata 9 at 51:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3104s)
- Do not attach your financial success to your self-worth. You can do your best work and not be rewarded, and he says the world is stacked against young people, citing land prices. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:43:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=6222s)
- AI takes the money out of a craft but not the joy. He can still make art in Photoshop for the satisfaction. He just cannot make money from it. (Masters Union at 5:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=333s)
- He says the Indian audience cannot be pleased about money: a profitable business is called a scam, and a loss-making one is asked why it is not profitable. (Masters Union at 57:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3424s)

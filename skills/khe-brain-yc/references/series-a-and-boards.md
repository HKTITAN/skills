# Series A and boards

This file answers how to pitch a Series A and build its deck, what a clean term sheet contains, what diligence asks for, and how to set up and run a board. Janelle Tam speaks on the pitch and deck, Jason Kwon and Aaron Harris on the term sheet and diligence, and Anu Hariharan on boards. All four are written posts that state no year, so legal terms, percentages and money figures are marked as undated, and the board advice is tied to the stage it was given for.

## How do I pitch a Series A?

- Tam says a Series A pitch exists to show investors why they should invest, by giving a short overview of the business already built and then showing where it could go and why money gets it there. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says this is a pitch to investors, who may never have met the problem, and not to customers, so a good one explains the customer's problem and its impact, shows how the product solves it, and shows why that makes a good business. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam treats investor attention as a limited resource: each slide should make its point at a glance, in plain English, with any necessary jargon defined the first time it is used. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says a Series A pitch should run 15 to 20 minutes and set up the 40 to 45 minute discussion that follows, so the aim is to leave room for questions and not to unload everything about the business. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says to open with what is already built, because compared with a seed round a Series A is harder to raise on promise alone and investors want to see what the earlier time and money produced. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says a Series A deck should make clear that money is what holds back growth, and not incompetence, poor product quality or a lack of product-market fit, and should end by showing how the money will be invested. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam's starting exercise for a Series A pitch is to write the narrative as 10 to 15 bullet points at most, which she says is called "fundraising vertebrae" in YC, and then list under each bullet the data points that prove it. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam suggests practice pitches with angels or founders who have raised a Series A, in an hour split into a 20 minute pitch, a 20 minute conversation and 20 minutes of feedback. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says one person should give the pitch, which she calls almost always better and especially so over video where changing speakers is confusing, while another founder can usefully join to answer questions and take notes. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam suggests setting aside 1 to 2 hours on every day of the fundraise to review each meeting and improve the deck: what excited investors, where they zoned out, and which objections lacked a convincing answer. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)

## What goes in the Series A deck?

- Tam says each bullet of the narrative becomes one slide, the point as its title and the data as its content, so a Series A deck runs 10 to 15 slides without the appendix, and a slide that makes more than one point is too complicated. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says the title slide carries the company name or logo and a one sentence description that is concrete, uses words a layperson knows and is specific to the company, her example being "We make plant-based cheese" over "Plant-based dairy". (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam puts a one slide traction teaser early in a Series A deck, because investors judge throughout the pitch whether the founder is worth their time and an early number wins their attention. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says the problem slide must name a problem the specific product solves, since it sets up the solution slide, and in an undated post she rejects "Americans spend $400B on mental health problems" because a tiny startup works on only a piece of that. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says to present the problem from the side of the customer, meaning whoever pays, which matters when users and payers differ, as with insurers in healthcare or employers in employee benefits. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says to show how the world works today through a concrete story, since a vague line such as "Medical billing is broken" gives a doubter no data and an investor who agrees may agree for different reasons. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam recommends that the solution slide sit side by side with the problem slide and carry numbers against the status quo, instead of claims to be the fastest, easiest or cheapest. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says the solution slide covers what the company does now and not what it plans, because investors value hypotheticals about unbuilt product at zero. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says the traction slides should tell a story that opens with the metric investors care most about, which is revenue when the company has it, shown together with the business model that earns it. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam's common traction story for enterprise and consumer companies has three parts: revenue growing fast on a solid business model, customers acquired profitably, and customers who love the product as shown by engagement and retention. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says where traction is heading matters more than where it stands, so a monthly or quarterly graph beats a summed annual number, and the trend needs at least 4 to 6 months to be believable. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam warns against throwing reams of numbers at investors and suggests choosing "hero facts", headline numbers that each prove a key point and that investors can use when pitching the company to their partners. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says almost every time she has seen cumulative numbers in a Series A deck, the founders were hiding monthly or quarterly numbers they thought too weak. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam's rules for numbers are that a graph should be understood at first glance, and that each figure should say exactly what it measures, such as monthly or daily actives and gross or net revenue. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam usually suggests a bottoms-up market size, prospective customers multiplied by the value of each, built from the segments served now and what a customer is worth today, since top down figures from reports are often not specific enough. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says the competition slide should show why the company is 10x better than everyone else attempting the same thing, so the investor believes the moat is high enough to defend the business. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- In an undated post, Tam says the vision slide shows how the company becomes a $10B company, and that hypotheticals belong here, after about 10 slides have built credibility. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam says the team slide moves up to second or third only when the team is a comparative advantage, which in an undated post she ties to a successful prior exit, for example a company sold for more than $100M, or to being among the few people equipped to start the company. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam advises leaving Series A fund logos off the list of existing investors, because the founder will be asked whether that fund is leading the round, and a no or a not sure could be a negative signal. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- Tam calls the ask the climax of the deck: how much is being raised, what it will be used for at a high level, and where traction will stand in 18 to 24 months, which she gives as the typical time to the next round. (How to build a great Series A pitch and deck, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)

## What is a clean term sheet?

- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that the lead in a Series A generally wants 20% of the company, and that they set no standard price because price moves with the leverage each side holds. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that they care more about terms of control and structure than about price, because founders know those terms less well and so they cause more confusion and trouble. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that the terms a Series A investor insists on can reveal the risks that investor perceives, because a contract divides risk between its parties. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris, in an undated post, call a board the founders control 2-1 founder-friendly, and say founders most often lose control at the Series A through a 2-2-1 board of two founders, two investors and one independent. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that losing board control is the most significant loss at Series A, because it means the founders can be fired from the company they started. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris, in an undated post, name a second way founders lose control: a separate provision, absent from their standard example, that requires the investor director's approval for operating decisions such as the annual budget, hiring or firing executives, or a pivot. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that when an investor talks of long-term partnership but insists on terms that say otherwise, the founder should "believe the terms". (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris list, in an undated post, four dirty economic terms that a clean Series A term sheet leaves out: a liquidation preference above 1x, participating preferred, cumulative dividends and warrant coverage. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that such structure shows an investor afraid of losing money and can foreshadow pressure to sell or to cut risk when things go badly, while good investors handle economic risk by negotiating valuation. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that Series A documents are the precedent for later rounds, and that bad terms are difficult and often impossible to unwind. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that the aim is a clean deal and not a perfect one, since execution can outrun suboptimal terms or build leverage to renegotiate later, so the goal is to close fast and get back to work. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)
- Kwon and Harris note in an undated post that of the standard investor vetoes in a Series A term sheet, the two with the most impact are the veto on a financing and the veto on a sale of the company. (A standard and clean Series A term sheet, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4P-a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet)

## What will Series A diligence ask for?

- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that having every diligence item together in a data room before signing a Series A term sheet cuts as much as a week from closing. (Series A diligence checklist, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post that founders generally do not realize closing a Series A can take more than a month, much of it often spent tracking down documents for lawyers. (Series A diligence checklist, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist)
- The checklist Kwon and Harris publish in an undated post asks for corporate records, meaning director and stockholder minutes and written consents plus the charter and bylaws, and for the current business plan, any financial projections and the most recent financial statements. (Series A diligence checklist, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist)
- On securities, the checklist from Kwon and Harris, in an undated post, asks for a list of stockholders with issuance dates and prices, a list of option holders with grant dates and exercise prices, and a summary of vesting schedules including any acceleration. (Series A diligence checklist, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist)
- On contracts, the checklist from Kwon and Harris asks, in an undated post, for any agreement involving obligations of or payments to the company above $25,000, alongside customer terms of service, leases, debt documents and insurance policies. (Series A diligence checklist, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist)
- On people, the checklist from Kwon and Harris, in an undated post, asks for a list of employees and consultants with title, base salary and state of residence, the standard offer letter, and any arrangement that pays out on a change in control. (Series A diligence checklist, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist)
- Kwon and Harris say in an undated post to settle pending or promised equity grants before starting a Series A process, because more people than not believe that once a term sheet arrives the current 409A valuation can no longer be used for grants. (Series A diligence checklist, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist)
- Kwon and Harris add in an undated post that earlier hires still waiting on grants would then receive a higher strike price and less upside, and the company may have to raise their compensation to avoid a morale issue. (Series A diligence checklist, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist)

## How do I form a board?

- Hariharan says a board's main role is to guide the company through major decisions: hiring and firing senior management, approving corporate actions such as compensation, stock options and budget, and advising on longer term strategy. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan notes that every Delaware corporation legally has a board once it operates, at first made up of the founders, and that setting up a board usually means adding outside directors after the company raises money. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says the investors who lead the Series A and Series B typically join the board, while later growth stage investors tend not to ask for seats. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says the first attribute of a board member for an early stage company is trust, which only time builds, so she recommends getting to know partners at the target VC firms at least 6 to 9 months before the fundraise. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says to check references with founders the investor has worked with, as in any hiring, and to ask whether you would work with this person for 10 or more years, which is how long some boards last. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan reports that a number of founders now bring on independent directors much sooner, citing Justin Kan adding Michael Seibel at Atrium's Series B, and says this means someone deeply trusted and fully independent, not simply a friend. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)

## How do I run board meetings?

- Hariharan says most meetings at the Series A stage are informal, like 1:1s, held anywhere from bi-weekly to bi-monthly, and that by Series B there should be a quarterly cadence scheduled a year ahead, ideally in person. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says the CEO sets and owns the agenda of a board meeting that can last about 3 hours, and that the best CEOs give only the first 45 minutes to highlights, lowlights and KPIs and the rest to no more than 1 or 2 strategic topics. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan shares a Gusto agenda of 10 minutes on highlights and lowlights, 50 on KPIs, two strategic topics of 45 minutes each and a 30 minute closed session, which she reads as no more than a third of the time on the state of the business. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan recommends agreeing the key KPIs with board members early, which standardizes reporting across meetings and trains directors to process them quickly and focus on why goals were or were not hit. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says Gusto sends a pre-read board deck at least a week ahead, collects each director's questions in a shared document three days before, and answers them by the morning of the meeting so the discussion can go to the two strategic topics. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says companies usually start planning a board meeting a month ahead, and gives the Brex timeline: strategic topics chosen 28 days out, the deck sent to the board 7 days out, board questions 4 days out and answers 2 days out. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- For companies past Series B with an executive team, Hariharan prefers that executives join only for their own agenda item, about 45 minutes, because inviting all of them can turn the meeting into a presentation and makes sensitive matters hard to discuss. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says a board meeting is not for selling to the board, who are already bought in, and that the best use of their time is helping the company reach its next milestone. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan recommends a 2-page monthly update email to the board at the start of each month, usually covering last month's performance, a hiring update, burning issues and specific asks for help. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)

## How do I work with board members?

- Hariharan says conflicting views on a board are a good thing and the CEO should not feel pressure to bring the board to consensus, and that when a conflict stalls the conversation the CEO should take it offline into 1:1s or small groups. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says the CEO has the authority to make the decision for the company, since board members are never as close to the business, and that a clear decision framework lets the board understand a disagreement. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says not to update every board member 1:1 on every topic or before every meeting, unless a sensitive topic needs discussing beforehand, and calls it an ineffective use of everyone's time. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan warns CEOs to be cautious of board members who try to make decisions for them, and says great board members often hold back their opinions and ask questions that draw out the CEO's thinking. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says the first step with an unhelpful board member is a 1:1 with constructive feedback, kept to the issue and not personal, with specific examples from past board meetings. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan describes one company where a board member began meeting senior management without the CEO's knowledge; the CEO gathered feedback from other directors, approached that board member directly, and the situation took about 6 months to resolve. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan gives two options once a CEO has tried everything and is convinced a board member is destructive: ask someone senior at that member's firm for a replacement, or add board members who can act as a sounding board. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says asking the firm for a replacement burns bridges, is hard when the company's metrics do not look good, and works best when the company is one of the best performers in that VC's portfolio. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)
- Hariharan says that if every board member echoes the same feedback and the CEO is not listening, the problem is likely the CEO, and that some CEOs bring an executive coach to one or two board meetings for feedback on board dynamics. (How to create and manage a board, https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3w-how-to-create-and-manage-a-board)

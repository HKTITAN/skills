# Sources

This skill is Harshit Khemani's reading of what Enrico Tartarotti has said in the long videos on his YouTube channel. Enrico Tartarotti did not write it, review it or endorse it. His own words are on [his channel](https://www.youtube.com/@enricotartarotti/videos). When this file and a video disagree, the video is right.

Every claim in the theme files ends with `(Title at m:ss, URL)`, and the title and URL come from the table below. The URL carries `&t=<seconds>s`, so it opens the video at the line. A reply cites a video as `(Title at m:ss)` inside the sentence and as `Source: Title, URL` under the answer. The gate (`scripts/brain-check.mjs`) reads the first two columns of this table, so a title that is not here cannot be cited.

The Title column is the video's own title with its capitals lowered and its punctuation and brackets removed, so that it can sit inside a citation. `The Ridiculous Engineering Of Figma (ft. Figma Team)` is cited as `The Ridiculous Engineering Of Figma`.

## How it was read

- **Captions, not ears.** Each video was read in full as YouTube's automatic English captions. The captions have no speaker labels and no punctuation that can be trusted, and they mishear the names of products, companies and people. A name or number the captions left unclear is described in general words in the theme files or left out.
- **Two videos were read another way.** `Why Google Maps Doesn't Show You Unsafe Areas` has no captions, so its audio was transcribed on the reader's own computer with a speech-to-text model. `I'm Switching to English and Starting From Scratch`, his first video on the tab, is spoken in Italian and was read through YouTube's machine translation; only its plain facts are in the theme files and nothing is quoted from it.
- **Every claim carries a time.** The time is the start of a 45-second caption block. A point he makes over a minute or two is cited to the block where it starts or where its key detail is said, so expect the line within about a minute of where the link opens, and listen on from there before repeating it.
- **How it was checked.** After the theme files, the glossary, the core and the disagreements were written, every cited line in them, 1,942 in all at the time, was checked against the caption text at its cited time by a reviewer who had not written it, with the block before and the two blocks after in view. None stated the opposite of what he said. 277 lines, about one in seven, carried a detail the captions did not hold at that time, such as a number, a name, a list item said a minute later or a stronger word than his, and each was cut back to what the captions carry. All but the last 22 of those rewrites were then checked a second time. Two lines that leaned on a sponsor lead-in and three that leaned on another speaker were rewritten to say so. The check compares a line with the captions, never with the picture or the sound.
- **Sponsor reads are left out.** Many videos carry a paid segment. Nothing said inside one is recorded as his view. A fact about himself that he gives inside a paid segment, such as where he was living, is kept as a fact and no more.
- **Who is speaking.** Almost every video is him alone, reading an essay he wrote. Three were made with a company's team and carry interview clips: `The Ridiculous Engineering Of Figma`, `The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notion` and `Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design`, the last made in partnership with Lovable. Other videos play clips of executives, designers and other creators. A line was recorded as his only when the context showed it was his. Lines by team members, by people in clips, and lines whose speaker could not be told, are not in the theme files as his.
- **Product facts are his telling.** He explains how products work and gives dates, costs and user counts from his own research. The theme files record these as what he says in that video. Nobody checked them against the companies.
- **Dates.** The videos run from 2021-08-24 to 2026-11-15. What he says about AI, about new devices and about the next interface belongs to the day of the video, so the theme files put the year on predictions and on verdicts about new products.
- **Quotes.** Every claim is a paraphrase. The few direct quotes are under 15 words each and are the captions' words with spacing and plain mishearings fixed. A quote is his words as a machine heard them. Open the link before repeating one outside this skill.
- **Slides and screens.** His videos lean on what is shown: interfaces, charts and text on screen. Only what he says aloud is in the corpus.

## Refresh

The corpus is a dated reading. Before answering a question about a video newer than 2026-11-15, or when the user links a video that is not in the table:

1. Open the Videos tab of his channel and compare its list with the table.
2. For each new video: read its captions in full, add its row here with today's date, then add its claims to the theme files, one line each, in your own words, each with its time and its link. Leave out the sponsor read.
3. Check [disagreements](disagreements.md) and [silent](silent.md): a new video can end a silence or start a disagreement.
4. Run `node scripts/brain-check.mjs --self-test`. It fails when a claim cites a title that is not in the table, or a time that does not match its link.

Use only what is published openly. Nothing behind a login or a payment goes into this file or into any reply.

# Operations course recap and change management

This is the closing segment of the eighth and last session of the operations management course. The instructor recapped every topic of the course in a few minutes, then gave a closing talk on humility, giving back, and leading people through change, with a short left hand and right hand exercise to show why change is resisted.

## What was said

### Recap of the course

- The instructor says operations exist wherever there is a process, including marketing, sales, hiring and writing an algorithm, and calls this way of seeing a business process thinking.
- The instructor names three routes to market leadership: product leadership, customer intimacy and operational excellence, and says all three rest on operations.
- Value indicators differ by organisation and drive process choices and the priority of process improvements.
- Voice of the customer is captured through interviews, surveys and questionnaires, and because it is vague it is translated into KPIs with a KPI tree.
- The instructor says not to rely on averages. Every KPI should be seen as a histogram, which shows central tendency, variation and how far customer expectation is met.
- The Kano model was used to prioritise which KPIs matter most for process improvement.
- Process improvement comes in two forms, kaizen for continuous improvement and kaikaku for breakthrough improvement, chosen to suit the business context.
- The instructor states Little's law as average inventory equals capacity multiplied by flow time, and recalls the class game where cutting batch size from 4 to 1 reduced average inventory.
- The lean takeaways recalled are that batch size should be as low as possible and quality should be embedded at the process level, with quality assurance mattering more than quality control.
- The instructor says quality is free up to a point as you improve it.
- A lean startup was framed as iterating a minimum viable product through continuous improvement.
- Process mapping tools covered were SIPOC, flowcharts and swimlane maps.
- For discrete data, a process running at 3.4 defects per million opportunities is at Six Sigma quality.
- For continuous data, Six Sigma is the number of standard deviations that fit between mean performance and the customer's specification limit, and six on each side is Six Sigma.
- Process capability, Cpk, is the smaller of the two gaps between the mean and each specification limit, each divided by three standard deviations.
- The instructor says not to penalise employees for performance in which noise and signal are mixed. Control charts separate common cause variation from special cause variation.
- The newsvendor problem was framed as a perishable good, a single chance to order, and unknown demand.
- The instructor says the design thinking framework is non linear.

### Humility and giving back

- The instructor warns of what he calls the aura effect: a founder feeling they are the only special person. He says pride in the venture is needed, within a limit.
- The instructor says there is a thin line between organisational success and organisational arrogance, and that humbler organisations prosper more.
- The instructor asks founders to pursue social interest along with commercial interest, as a win-win, and calls the belief that the two must be traded off a myth.
- The instructor says leaders who think only about the next three or four years are being myopic.

### Why people resist change

- The instructor says all the tools of the course become meaningless without change management, which he calls an art that cannot be taught in a classroom.
- The founder sees the big picture and the managers often do not, and organisations fall when those people pull in the opposite direction.
- The instructor says that as soon as you bring in technology or new ways of working, your own people will resist, and this will happen in every firm.
- The exercise: the class raised the right hand on the word right and the left on the word left without trouble. When the rule was reversed there was chaos, even though everyone intended to follow it.
- The instructor's reading is that resistance is not opposition. A person who resists is often trying to follow and struggling.
- The first cause of resistance is assumption. People told of a new technology assume it will take their jobs, and the leader's job is to clarify.
- The second cause is the comfort zone. People resist because they think they must break it.
- The instructor's reframing is "Change is transitioning your people from one comfort zone to another comfort zone", with the leader responsible for their comfort throughout.
- The third cause is what he calls the student paradox: given a midnight deadline, students start at 11:55. People put change off until the last moment and do not see the consequences of not changing.
- The class notes list a fourth cause, lack of motivation.
- The instructor says to tell people what benefit they get from changing.

### Leading people through change

- The instructor says a leader who punishes or fires the person who struggled with a change is in love with the technology and not with the people.
- The instructor says productivity rises a great deal when people feel cared for and feel the organisation is theirs.
- Making an employee the champion of a change, and treating the idea as theirs, gives a sense of belonging and identity.
- The instructor describes the opposite as a few people making decisions in a room without input or feedback and announcing policy, and calls that organisational arrogance.
- The founder is responsible for connecting motivation systems, feedback systems and communication to the change.
- The instructor says to customise what research and textbooks say to the people in front of you.
- The closing rule is that a founder should aim to win and take ten more people to win along with them, and that a leader who succeeds by cutting people has not succeeded as a leader.

### Teaching and research

- The instructor says questions from students feed research. He noticed, and a student also pointed out, that gender diverse groups kept winning class challenges, and that led to research into the mechanisms.

## Examples and cases used

- Maruti Suzuki and Toyota: Maruti masters the value indicator of affordability with basic quality, and a buyer who wants quality at a higher price goes to Toyota.
- Tata and Taj hotels: the instructor says Taj receptionists are given sarees as uniforms, and instead of buying them in bulk at the lowest cost the group adopts villages and creates employment for women there to make the sarees.
- Robotic process automation: the instructor's example of a change employees will not like on announcement.

## Numbers given

- Eight sessions made up the course.
- More than 20,000 students taught, the instructor's count of his own teaching.

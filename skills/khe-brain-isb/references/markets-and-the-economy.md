# How do markets, rivals and policy behave?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: the Economics files name Akerlof, Ricardo, Gale-Shapley and the Behavioural Insights Team, and use Nash, Vickrey, Cournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg, Solow, Kuznets and Pigouvian as labels on a model or a tax, and they name no professor, no course and no source for any figure. A percentage in these lines is the file's own unsourced number, never a checked benchmark. Pairs of these files disagree on how often the Vickrey auction is used, on whether quotas are worse than tariffs, on why South Korea and Vietnam succeeded and on what the resource curse is; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## Prices, quantities and surplus

- When demand is elastic, with elasticity above 1 in absolute value, revenue falls when price rises; when it is inelastic, below 1, revenue rises; at unit elasticity revenue stays constant. (Economics: Supply, Demand, and Equilibrium, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/micro-supply-demand-and-equilibrium.md)
- A binding price floor, one set above equilibrium, creates surplus, and a binding price ceiling, one set below it, creates shortage; the file's examples are minimum wage causing unemployment and rent control causing housing shortage and reduced quality. (Economics: Supply, Demand, and Equilibrium, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/micro-supply-demand-and-equilibrium.md)
- Consumer surplus is willingness to pay less the price paid, producer surplus is the price received less the minimum acceptable price, and total surplus is their sum. Deadweight loss is surplus lost on units not traded that would create value, and the file lists monopoly, taxation, and price ceilings and floors as sources. (Economics: Consumer Surplus and Producer Surplus, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/surplus-consumer-surplus-and-producer-surplus.md)

## Market structure and pricing power

- Under perfect competition price equals marginal cost and economic profit is zero; the file calls this structure rare and uses it as a baseline for understanding where rents come from. (Economics: Market Structures and Pricing Power, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/micro-market-structures-and-pricing-power.md)
- In Cournot competition each firm chooses a quantity and price stays above marginal cost; in Bertrand competition with identical products two firms are enough to drive price to marginal cost, and differentiation breaks that result. (Economics: Oligopoly and Strategic Interaction, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/strategic-oligopoly-and-strategic-interaction.md)
- Price discrimination charges different customers different prices based on willingness to pay; the four strategies listed are versioning, geographic pricing, time-based pricing and quantity discounts. (Economics: Value Creation and Pricing Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/micro-value-creation-and-pricing-strategy.md)
- Economic profit is revenue less accounting costs and opportunity costs, and returns above normal profit are economic rents; the file lists seven sources: scale economies, switching costs, intellectual property, control of scarce resources, network effects, high capital requirements and regulatory barriers. (Economics: Economic Profit and Competitive Advantage, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/strategic-economic-profit-and-competitive-advantage.md)

## Games and hidden information

- A Nash equilibrium is a strategy combination where no player can improve their payoff by changing strategy alone; it can be worse for all players than another outcome, and the file's case is the prisoner's dilemma, where both defect although mutual cooperation is Pareto superior. (Economics: Simultaneous-Move Games, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/game-simultaneous-move-games.md)
- Backward induction starts at the end nodes and works backward, and it eliminates threats a player would not carry out; the first-mover advantage requires the ability to commit and disappears if the leader can be ignored. (Economics: Sequential-Move Games, Extensive Form, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/game-sequential-move-games-extensive-form.md)
- The file labels the lemons problem with Akerlof's name: buyers cannot tell high quality from low, high-quality sellers exit at the pooling price, and quality goods disappear. (Economics: Adverse Selection, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/information-adverse-selection.md)
- After a contract is signed the agent's effort or care is not directly observable, and an insured person's incentive to prevent loss diminishes; deductibles and coinsurance make the insured bear part of the loss. (Economics: Moral Hazard, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/information-moral-hazard.md)
- A signal must be costly, observable and differentially costly for bad types against good types; screening is the uninformed party offering different contracts that appeal to different types. (Economics: Signaling and Screening, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/information-signaling-and-screening.md)
- Total agency cost is monitoring plus bonding plus residual loss; pay based on outcomes creates risk for the agent, who demands higher expected pay. (Economics: Principal-Agent Framework, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/information-principal-agent-framework.md)

## Auctions, matching and mechanisms

- In a Vickrey auction the highest bidder wins and pays the second-highest bid, and bidding true value is weakly dominant; this file lists its common use as spectrum auctions and online advertising. (Economics: Auction Theory and Design, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/market-design-auction-theory-and-design.md)
- This file says of the second-price sealed-bid auction, which it labels Vickrey, that truth-telling is the dominant strategy and that it is "rarely used". (Economics: Auction Theory and Strategic Bidding, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/market-design-auction-theory-and-strategic-bidding.md)
- The Revenue Equivalence Theorem: with risk-neutral bidders and independent values, different auction formats generate equal expected revenue; risk-averse bidders, affiliated values and collusion possibilities alter the revenue rankings. (Economics: Auction Theory and Design, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/market-design-auction-theory-and-design.md)
- A matching is stable when no pair prefers each other to their current matches; the deferred acceptance algorithm, labelled Gale-Shapley in the file, guarantees a stable matching and is strategy-proof for the proposing side. (Economics: Matching Markets and Two-Sided Platforms, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/market-design-matching-markets-and-two-sided-platforms.md)
- Incentive compatibility means revealing true information is in each player's best interest; individual rationality means each player is at least as well off participating as not. (Economics: Mechanism Design Fundamentals, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/market-design-mechanism-design-fundamentals.md)

## Behaviour

- Losses loom approximately 2x larger than equivalent gains, and people evaluate outcomes against a reference point, often the current state, not in absolute terms. (Economics: Prospect Theory and Loss Aversion, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/behavioral-prospect-theory-and-loss-aversion.md)
- The default option is chosen by 70 to 90% of people despite identical alternatives; the file gives loss aversion, switching costs and the endowment effect as the causes of status quo bias. (Economics: Behavioral Biases and Heuristics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/behavioral-behavioral-biases-and-heuristics.md)
- The EAST framework, which the file credits to the Behavioural Insights Team, makes the desired behaviour easy, attractive, social and timely. (Economics: Nudge Theory and Choice Architecture, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/behavioral-nudge-theory-and-choice-architecture.md)
- The file gives 401k participation as about 30% with opt-in and about 80% with opt-out, and says nudges should benefit the choice-maker and not exploit for company benefit. (Economics: Nudge Theory and Choice Architecture, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/behavioral-nudge-theory-and-choice-architecture.md)

## The economy and policy

- GDP by the expenditure approach is consumption plus investment plus government spending plus net exports; the natural rate of unemployment is given as approximately 4 to 5% in developed economies. (Economics: Macroeconomic Indicators, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/macro-macroeconomic-indicators.md)
- Monetary policy effects take 6 to 18 months to show fully, and the file says central banks must forecast and act in advance; the inflation target is given as typically 2 to 3%. (Economics: Monetary Policy and Central Banking, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/macro-monetary-policy-and-central-banking.md)
- $1 of government spending raises demand by $1 times a multiplier above 1, and a $1 tax cut raises demand by less; the file states that estimates suggest partial crowding out in most economies. (Economics: Fiscal Policy and Government Finance, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/macro-fiscal-policy-and-government-finance.md)
- This file describes quotas as a quantity restriction on imports with similar effects to tariffs. (Economics: International Economics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/macro-international-economics.md)
- This file calls a quota less efficient than a tariff: both create efficiency loss, and quotas are worse because the rents go to foreign sellers, not the government. (Economics: Trade Policy and Strategic Considerations, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/international-trade-policy-and-strategic-considerations.md)
- This file lists South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam as successes "with selective protection and time limits", and Latin America and India as failures with permanent import substitution protection. (Economics: Trade Policy and Strategic Considerations, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/international-trade-policy-and-strategic-considerations.md)
- This file gives South Korea and Vietnam as successes of export-led growth, which it describes as integrating with global markets and specialising in comparative advantage, and Latin America and India as a relative failure of import substitution. (Economics: Development Strategies, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/development-development-strategies.md)
- Under terms of trade, this file's resource curse is that countries with natural resources often face long-run terms of trade deterioration. (Economics: International Economics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/macro-international-economics.md)
- This file's resource curse is that natural resource wealth can reduce the incentive for institution-building and brings volatile revenues. (Economics: Development Strategies, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/development-development-strategies.md)
- A Pigouvian tax equals the external marginal cost and internalises the externality; cap-and-trade achieves the same outcome, with certainty on quantity and flexibility on price. (Economics: Externalities and Public Goods, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/failure-externalities-and-public-goods.md)
- A horizontal merger combines competitors and raises market concentration, and its defence is efficiencies that outweigh the competitive harm; a vertical merger may foreclose rivals from inputs. (Economics: Antitrust and Competition Policy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/failure-antitrust-and-competition-policy.md)
- Unconditional convergence, all countries reaching the same steady state, is marked "not observed"; conditional convergence depends on the saving rate, education or human capital investment, institutional quality and infrastructure quality. (Economics: Development Economics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/economics/rules/macro-development-economics.md)

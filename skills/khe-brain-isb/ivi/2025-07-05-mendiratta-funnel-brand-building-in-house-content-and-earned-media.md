# Funnel, brand building, in-house content and earned media, Chandan Mendiratta

The second session of day one of the marketing bootcamp with Chandan Mendiratta of Zepto. He moved through his deck on the funnel, research, Byron Sharp, brand building, in-house content teams and earned media, answered questions throughout, and closed by setting a group assignment to write a brief and three content ideas for an invented product.

## What was said

### How long a creative takes and lasts

- Mendiratta says the traditional route takes about three months: a month of research to build the brief, about a month with an agency, and about three more weeks of production.
- He says Zepto writes a film in two to three days and its production house shoots it within ten days. Only the dates of the stars stretched the Super Saver films to three weeks.
- He says a creative is done when people have understood the message and the business has felt the impact.
- He says a creative runs for at least one and a half to two months, and six to nine months is more than enough before it must be refreshed because of fatigue.

### The funnel and measuring it

- Mendiratta lays out the funnel as top, middle and bottom: awareness, then consideration, then the transaction, followed by loyalty, which he defines as repeat purchase.
- He names cost per click, click through rate and view through rate as the digital measures, and says television is hard to measure and has to be measured through research.
- He says every creative builds both awareness and consideration, so the useful question is which one is the primary goal, and the script differs with it.
- He says awareness tells people the product exists and teaches them about it, and consideration shows benefits and builds trust.
- He says a new company should start with awareness only. His example is that Zepto first said only that things arrive in ten minutes, shown through an emergency such as running out of diapers.
- He says to pick the problem before starting a campaign and to circle back to it, and to judge a creative by whether it will help the business, not by how creative it is.

### Research

- Mendiratta says qualitative research is for finding the insight and quantitative research is for showing how big the problem is.
- He describes a quantitative study as a statistically significant list of problems in descending order.
- He says Zepto's post-campaign survey covered about 1,000 to 1,300 people across five cities with a mix of tier 1 and tier 2.
- He says the survey first measures how many people remembered and understood the creative, then shows it again and asks whether they are now likely to change behaviour.
- He gives the cost of a study of 1,300 people as 6 to 10 lakh rupees.
- He says the summary of the method is: no insight, no idea, no impact.

### Light buyers and heavy buyers

- Mendiratta cites Byron Sharp's advice not to overlook the sometimes customer.
- He says that on any platform about 70% of orders come from 30% of users, and those heavy users cannot be made to buy much more.
- He says the largest growth lever is the other 70% of customers, who give only 30% of the business.
- He says the heavy users should be retained through customer support and refund practices, and that Zomato graded users for this with a blended score it called user karma.
- He says frequency is a big lever only for very low frequency users.
- He agrees with Sharp that a business should reach more people instead of going deep into its existing base, and that share of voice drives share of mind.
- He says Zepto's share of voice is far ahead of its two competitors.

### Where he disagrees with Byron Sharp

- Mendiratta says Sharp's view, that what matters is reaching more people more often and not making a few people fall in love, is right in the short term and wrong in the long term for India.
- He gives two reasons: Indian culture is built on aspiration and belonging, which compound over time, and users get used to an app's interface and find it hard to move.
- He says a company being built for many years needs a few people to fall in love with it, because that is how tribes form.

### Building a brand

- Mendiratta gives his formula for a memorable brand as consistency multiplied by frequency: the same look and voice, shown often.
- He says the goal is a memory structure, so that people think of the product at the moment they need it, and that emotion is the shortcut to memory.
- He says a brand that is not differentiated is forced to compete on price.
- His example of a brand in a commodity is Amul in butter, which he calls the original content creator, seen every day for years.
- He says a challenger should pick a functional benefit the incumbent has ignored and build from there. It then either gets acquired or becomes credible competition.
- He says a product with only an emotional benefit is a wave that dies down.
- He says the product must be used properly after it is sold, because without use there is no repeat.
- He says brand building is expensive and long term, so a startup's first goal is its first customers and early adopters and their feedback.
- He says survival is the first skill, and a small company should not put money into costly creative and costly distribution in the hope of long term effects.
- He tells founders not to buy hoardings at the start, and calls hoardings a brand activity.
- He says to solve for drivers and barriers: what makes people transact and what stops them.

### Tribes

- Mendiratta describes a tribe as a brand that has attached an emotional benefit over a long time, so that customers take personal pride in it.
- He says a Nike person is someone who wears the logo to signal who they aspire to be, whether or not they are athletic.
- He says Red Bull is a caffeine and sugar drink that talks only about high performance.

### Faces and trust

- Mendiratta says trust builds because people know you: when people know a founder by face, they buy from the founder's company.
- His example is Nikhil Kamath, who he says never talks about Zerodha on his podcast and still led many people to install it.
- He says building a recognisable face can take five years and costs almost nothing, at two to five videos a month.
- He says Groww was a front runner in content strategy with several YouTube channels on trading and education.

### In-house content over agencies

- Mendiratta says marketers and brands today are content creators and publishers and should think like one, which means making content on a trend while it is live.
- He says a founder should do social media only if it can be done on a low cost model, and should build that model in-house.
- He says agencies are expensive and less agile than an internal team, and warns against handing the keys to an agency on day one.
- He says not to pay an agency 10 lakh rupees a month to build a channel, because content has to be made forever and an expensive channel gets discontinued, wasting the money already spent.
- He says to hire creative people and give the marketing team good equity so they stay, because the marketing head keeps the tone of voice consistent.
- He says an outside party that may not be there next month will change the brand's voice, and that nobody understands the brand as its own team does.
- He says a strong internal culture produces a strong brand, and describes his brand team as close and free to ship odd ideas.
- He says LinkedIn is required for a founder because future employees, investors and early adopters are there.
- He says to choose other platforms by audience, and that YouTube Shorts now skews to tier 2 and tier 3 towns.
- He says someone needed every day or many times a month should be hired, and an agency's talent can be hired directly.

### Content that is not an ad

- Mendiratta gives a four E model: all content either educates, entertains, empathises or enrages.
- He says permission marketing is content people ask to receive, and cites Zomato's emails, which people subscribe to because they are funny.
- He says audience comes first and product later: build content the audience values even if it sits a little to the side of what you sell.
- His example is a Tata Steel website that gives home builders and contractors design ideas, so that the search for home ideas leads to the steel brand.
- He describes Zepto's halo playbook: instead of saying it sells detergent, it names the leading brand in the category, borrowing that brand's recognition at low cost. He says Zepto made 60 to 70 such films in eight to nine months.
- He says Zepto's film about soan papdi, a sweet he describes as bullied, carried no Zepto branding at the start because it was a brand film and branding would have cut the view through rate.
- He says content should never feel like an ad unless you want it to be an ad.
- He separates shareworthy creative from hardworking creative, says it is very hard to make one film do both, and tells startups to stick to hardworking.
- He says the soan papdi story worked because the sweet was already an urban legend and already the third best selling sweet, and it moved to first. He says the same film would not work for a sweet ranked eighth or tenth.

### The Zomato cravings campaign

- Mendiratta says Zomato research showed people ordered on occasions, so to build frequency the team looked for the most frequent food occasion and chose cravings, which he says come several times a day.
- He says the digital version used AI lip sync to change three variables, the city, the most ordered dish in that pin code and the restaurant it was most ordered from.
- He says one city's version caused a controversy the team had not foreseen, and the team had to pull that version back.

### Adapt, do not translate

- Mendiratta says Zomato, then a 12 year old company, had never shot a film in Tamil. It had only dubbed Hindi films.
- He says a dubbed Hindi film treats the Tamil viewer as second hand, and it is better to show nothing.
- He says a qualitative study in Tamil Nadu led to a film shot in Tamil around local food and customs, and it raised orders and new users.
- He says a Hindi film dubbed into Telugu is still accepted if the message is right.
- He holds up the film Pushpa as a case of adapting and not translating, including re-recording its songs in Hindi.
- He says alliteration in a name such as Super Saver helps memory.

### Briefs, agencies and awards

- Mendiratta lists the common pitfalls as forgetting the brief and getting swayed by a script, and says a script should not win hearts, the business should.
- He says to have the agency repeat the brief back, and to check every script against the brief, the worldview and the size of the leap.
- He says agencies work for awards and their interests are not aligned with the brand's.
- He says brand managers chase awards because their impact is hard to measure, and tells founders not to hire award winning marketers.
- He says the two checks on a finished creative are brand recall and message recall, plus whether a viewer stays past the first five seconds.

### Earned media

- Mendiratta defines earned media as customers sharing your creative on their own social media and message groups, which is free and more trusted.
- He says people share because sharing earns them validation from friends.
- He points to Jonah Berger's STEPPS framework for why people share, starting with social currency: people share what makes them look good.
- He says Zepto puts up a hoarding mainly to photograph it and post the picture, because few people see the actual hoarding.
- He says two brands talking to each other in public always gets noticed.
- He shows copy written on posters and packets as further examples of material made to be shared.

## Examples and cases used

- Zepto Super Saver films: speed of in-house production and the post-campaign survey.
- Zomato user karma: grading users to decide support and refunds.
- Amul: decades of topical content building trust in a commodity.
- Nike and Red Bull: tribes built on an emotional benefit.
- Nikhil Kamath and Zerodha, and Groww: a founder's face and educational content creating trust.
- A Tata Steel home building website: content for the audience first, product second.
- Zepto's soan papdi film: a shareworthy brand film without early branding.
- Zomato's cravings campaign: targeting the most frequent occasion, with localised versions.
- Zomato's Tamil film and the film Pushpa: adapting for a language market.

## Numbers given

- About 3 months against about 10 days: the traditional agency route against Zepto's in-house route, in his telling.
- 1.5 to 2 months minimum and 6 to 9 months maximum: the life of a creative.
- 1,000 to 1,300 respondents in five cities, at 6 to 10 lakh rupees: Zepto's quantitative campaign study.
- 70% of orders from 30% of users: his rule of thumb for any platform.
- 60 to 70 films in eight to nine months: Zepto's halo films.
- 10 lakh rupees a month: the agency fee he tells founders not to pay for a content channel.

# Design thinking, customer interviews and the persona assignment, part 2 of 3

This is part 2 of 3 of a design thinking class recorded on 14 May 2025. The slice is short. It holds the closing minutes of the session: a remark on technology and learning, answers to founders' questions about customer interviews, and the assignment for the next class. The instructor is not named in this slice.

## What was said

### Technology and the illusion of competence

- The instructor says he and his co-authors have just published a paper on people who rely on a technology tool to solve problems. Its finding is that in certain cases, especially when people are trying to learn new concepts, they retain less.
- He says the result of that lower retention is an illusion of competence: people feel competent when they are not.
- He says enforcing controls on how people use technology is very hard today, and that he cannot enforce it even on his own students. His answer is to teach people how to do the work properly.
- His stated philosophy is that if people follow the principles and also use technology, the outcome is still better than doing nothing at all.

### How to run the customer interview

- The instructor says an interview is fairly simple: take a piece of paper with all your questions, go to the person, ask, and take notes or transcribe digitally.
- He says a founder should not go to a customer cold. Without practice the founder will not know what to ask and the customer will not know what to say.
- He says the research is for your own problem and the particular scenario you are going after. It is not general industry research.
- He says a founder who already has enough data to validate the problem can skip the exercise. He calls it a revalidation technique.
- For a business with two sides, such as merchants and buyers, he says the interviews must be done for both sides, because the gains merchants expect can be very different from what customers expect.

### Problem before selling

- Asked whether the problem statement should be how to sell a product to HR people, the instructor says there is no selling at this stage. The first question is what problem HR people have today with the products they already use.
- He says selling comes at launch, in go-to-market. First work out the problem to be solved and how to solve it.
- He says positioning is a different problem, but it derives from the problem and the solution.

### The assignment

- The assignment he sets is: identify your persona, write your list of questions, use the cheat sheets handed out, conduct the interviews, collect the data, do the affinity analysis, and find the goals, pains and gains of the persona.
- He says that by the next class everyone should have data from customers, a persona, a clear problem and an understanding of the goals.
- He says the circle on the worksheet is to be filled before the next class, and the square will be filled in the next class.

## Examples and cases used

- An HR software example raised by a founder in the room: the instructor uses it to show that the question at this stage is the HR buyer's problem with existing products, not how to sell to them.

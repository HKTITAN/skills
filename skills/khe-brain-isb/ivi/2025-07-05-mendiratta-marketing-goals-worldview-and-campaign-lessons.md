# Marketing goals, worldview and campaign lessons from Zomato and Zepto, Chandan Mendiratta

Chandan Mendiratta, who leads the brand team at Zepto and earlier led brand marketing at Zomato, opened the marketing bootcamp with the first half of day one. He handed out his deck, played ad films he had worked on, and explained what each got right or wrong. He said this was the first time he had taught the material as a course.

## What was said

### His background and stance

- Mendiratta says he is not a career marketer. He studied chemical engineering, traded crude oil futures for five and a half years, did the ISB PGP, and joined a startup that shut down after two years when it could not raise its next round.
- He says his notable roles at Zomato were launching online ordering in 500 cities in 2018 and 2019, running its non-profit Feeding India during Covid, and leading brand marketing from 2020 to 2022 with no prior marketing experience.
- He says he learnt marketing from the people who reported to him, questioned everything and measured everything, which makes his view different from that of career marketers.
- He says he then worked at a B2B technology startup, found he was a B2C person, and has been at Zepto for a year and a half, where he leads the brand team and HR.
- He points founders to Kotler for the fundamentals such as the 4Ps and 5Cs, and says his session builds on those without repeating them.
- He says Zepto runs on a very small brand marketing budget and has the largest share of voice in its category in the country.

### What marketing is for

- Mendiratta defines marketing, citing Kotler, as creating genuine customer value: if you are not enhancing the customer's life you are not marketing.
- He says a customer's life is enhanced in one of two ways, emotional or functional, and every piece of communication is built on one of them.
- He says a message that does not enrich someone's life evokes no emotion or action, and without emotion or action there is no impact.
- He separates three words: marketing is the activity and strategy to reach and convert customers, branding is the asset that builds or degrades over time and is what people remember, and advertising is the tool used to execute.
- He says marketing drives demand, and brand builds trust and emotional connection.
- He says behavioural change is the goal of marketing.

### Choosing the user and the goal

- Mendiratta says every campaign must fix its primary user and its primary message first. The message will reach everyone, but it has to be made for one group.
- He names three user groups: new users, active users and lapsed users, and says the message differs for each.
- He maps new users to the top of the funnel and active users to the middle, where the aim is more frequency.
- He says the choice of group rests on two things: current orders and lifetime value.
- He says lapsed users are the cheapest to bring back because they need no education, only an offer, but they have lower lifetime value and tend to lapse again.
- He says new users cost more per order but have high lifetime value, and a business should always be acquiring users because it compounds on more users.
- He says increasing frequency is the hardest goal because the customer already buys from you and you are asking them to change a habit.
- He says a young business should not put much of its effort into frequency.

### Cool over function

- Mendiratta says the Zomato films with Hrithik Roshan and Katrina Kaif were meant to tell users that Zomato delivers on time, and ended up saying that every customer is a star to the delivery partner.
- He says the campaign began around an "on time or free" promise that could not be kept at the back end, and the team then fell for a script it found charming.
- He says the films brought no behavioural change and no business impact, and calls this picking cool over function.
- He says about 3 to 4 crore rupees went into producing the two films and about 10 to 12 crore into distributing them.
- He says the creative is the smallest part of a campaign and distribution is the larger part and the larger cost.
- He says the films were distributed like performance creatives although they were brand films, and a brand film needs a different media strategy.
- He says marketing mistakes cost businesses money and cost people their jobs.
- He says Zepto repeated the mistake with a film of a horse on a sofa talking about low prices, which cost 30 to 40 lakh rupees and was stopped within days, and replaced it with a film that cost 7 to 10 lakh.
- He says the horse film spoke to Gen Z, while the offer needed a basket of about 1,000 rupees and so was for regular household grocery buyers.

### When to do brand work

- Mendiratta says a company always has limited marketing money, so the question is not whether branding is good but when.
- He calls branding a festival game or a mature company game, not something for a growing company outside a festival.
- He says festivals and events are when customers adopt a product or switch from a competitor, because people spend with emotion and do not count the cost.
- His example is Diwali gift boxes of sweets and juice, which he says are high gross margin and still bought in bulk.

### Worldview first

- Mendiratta takes from Seth Godin's book All Marketers Are Liars the rule "worldview first, product second": people do not change their worldview.
- He says behavioural change does not mean changing a worldview. It can come from supporting one, and that is where it has the most value.
- He says a creative that challenges what most people believe is rejected, and one that supports it gets people to rally behind it.
- He says every part of a product, including packaging, pricing, logo and user experience, is part of the story.
- He says a worldview is what most people believe and can be learnt through research.
- He asks of every campaign how big a leap it expects the customer to take, and says customers will not jump a large gap from their habits.

### The Zomato seven days campaign

- Mendiratta says Zomato wanted to raise order frequency among active users and copied the idea of Taco Tuesday by giving each day of the week a food theme.
- He says the campaign fell flat because it asked for too big a leap and went against the common belief that one should not eat outside food every day.
- He says seven creatives split the budget seven ways, so each was seen too few times for viewers to understand it, where one creative could have been shown seven times.
- He says the distribution was well executed, with each day's film shown on that day on television and digital, and the campaign still failed.

### The Zepto Super Saver campaign

- Mendiratta says the goal of Super Saver was to move existing Zepto users' monthly grocery shopping to Zepto, not to acquire new users.
- He says the worldview in the way was that quick commerce is expensive, with DMart seen as cheapest, then e-commerce, then quick commerce, and the class agreed when he polled it.
- He played an Amazon grocery ad that claimed low prices online, and the class said they still did not believe it, which he read as viewers rejecting the creative.
- He says the film asked only for a small step, to check the prices on the app once, instead of asking people to move their shopping.
- He says Zepto appears in the opening frame and the words Zepto Super Saver are spoken at about 4.5 seconds, because YouTube ads can be skipped at 5 seconds.
- He says a viewer who skips after hearing the brand has still given a free impression.
- He says the name is repeated many times in the film so that the message cannot be credited to a competitor.
- He says the products named were staples such as bread, butter and eggs, because needs signal monthly shopping and items like chips and cold drinks signal wants.
- He says a survey after the campaign showed strong message recall and very little attribution of Super Saver to other brands, and the share of orders on Super Saver rose.
- He says targeting non-users would have needed two leaps, first to try quick commerce and then to do monthly shopping on it.

### Picking a celebrity

- Mendiratta says choosing a star is more art than science: pick the person who can carry trust for the specific message.
- He says Zepto chose Akshay Kumar over Ranveer Singh for a price message because the audience sees Akshay Kumar as someone who thinks about money.
- He says the first choice was Amitabh Bachchan for his comic timing, but he was committed to a competing platform.
- He says the film used Akshay Kumar in a comic role that brands had not used for years, which made it stand out.
- He says stars know what their fans will accept. His example is a star who refused a script in which he was asked to wait, saying his fans would not like it.
- He says a star telling viewers to check prices makes them more likely to do it than an unknown actor would.
- He says a standard celebrity contract is one day of shooting, meaning eight hours, and one year of association, and both the days and the years can be negotiated.
- He advises getting the script approved right after the fee is agreed, because once the money is paid the film has to be made.

### Languages

- Mendiratta says one Hindi film covers the north, west and east because people there understand Hindi even where they speak another language, and he cannot afford separate films for each.
- He says the south has four main languages, and Bangalore is a problem because no single language covers more than about a quarter of its viewers.
- He says Zepto made the Super Saver film again in Telugu with NTR, and used a known actor who is not a star for Tamil to keep cost down.

### Colour and font

- Mendiratta says people should be able to recognise the brand with the logo removed, through colour and font alone.
- He says a brand must keep a strict and distinct colour, font and font size, or its work will be credited to a competitor.
- His example is a viral video in which workers in another company's uniform were taken for Zepto riders, even by the person who posted it.
- He says his team works from a fixed hex code that everyone on the brand team has memorised, not from a shade.
- He says colour is first for recall and uniqueness. The emotion a colour carries is secondary, because tone of voice can express emotion.

### How campaigns get made

- Mendiratta says writing the script is art, and the science comes after: brand recall, message recall, whether it appeals to the intended user and whether the message is clear.
- He says Zepto writes its scripts in-house, and he wrote part of the Super Saver script himself.

## Examples and cases used

- Bisleri's 2018 campaign: a brand that is already the market leader and the mind leader has to grow by taking share back from others or by adding more SKUs.
- Zomato's films with Hrithik Roshan and Katrina Kaif: cool over function, and heavy distribution spend with no business impact.
- Zepto's horse film: a creative aimed at the wrong user.
- Zomato's seven days of food themes, modelled on Taco Tuesday: too big a leap and a budget split across seven creatives.
- Zepto Super Saver with Akshay Kumar and with NTR: supporting a worldview, a small ask, brand in the first five seconds.
- Amazon's online grocery ad with Manoj Bajpayee: he played it and the class said they still did not believe online prices were lowest.

## Numbers given

- 500 cities: the Zomato online ordering launch he worked on in 2018 and 2019.
- 3 to 4 crore rupees to produce and 10 to 12 crore to distribute: the two Zomato celebrity films, by his account.
- 30 to 40 lakh rupees against 7 to 10 lakh: the Zepto horse film and the film that replaced it.
- 4.5 seconds: when the brand name is spoken in the Super Saver film, against a 5 second skip.
- One day of 8 hours and one year: the standard celebrity contract he describes.

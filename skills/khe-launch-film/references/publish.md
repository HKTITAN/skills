# Publish

A launch film ships as a family, and each member has a job. The master explains the product and leads to the next action; the shorter cuts carry its strongest moment to the places people scroll. This file decides the family and what to measure once it is out. Aspect ratios and safe zones belong to [launch-video-formats](launch-video-formats.md); this file decides what each cut is for.

## Length

Set the master's length by the time the moment needs to be understood ([brief](brief.md)), then cut shorter versions for distribution. Do not set it from a rule about short video. The two studies Hiten Shah cites measure different things, and neither supports one: a 2026 study of 1,750 B2B demos on YouTube put 11 % of those under 30 s in its top quartile of likes per view, against 23 to 27 % from 30 s to 2.5 min (Motion The Agency), and a study of 1,550 launch videos on X found 2 to 4 minute films from accounts under 10,000 followers at 546,000 median views, against 151,000 for 30 to 60 s (Doomers AI). One counts likes against views, the other counts views; neither measures how much of a film was watched, and neither shows that length caused the result. Inside one register the corpus lengths still hold: a beat-cut sizzle runs 40 s or less ([launch-video-registers](launch-video-registers.md)).

## A family of cuts

| Cut | Job | Its moment |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | Enough context to explain the product and lead to the next action | The brief's moment |
| Proof | One recognisable change, readable at phone size | The moment alone, cut in until it reads |
| How it works | The real steps, for someone already interested | The finished state at the end of the route |
| Follow-up | A first-versus-revised or before-and-after example | The difference between the two |

Each cut gets its own one-line brief (viewer, promise, moment) and its own plan; a shorter cut is a new composition, never the master trimmed, for the same reason a vertical cut is never the master cropped.

## Measure the job

Impressions, reported plays and completion are three different counts, and none of them says a viewer understood the product or tried it. Measure, in this order:

1. **Comprehension.** Cold viewers describe what the product makes possible ([review](review.md), The cold viewer).
2. **Investigation.** Profile visits, destination clicks, docs opened.
3. **The next action** the brief names: a trial, a sign-up, a deploy.

Keep with each count: the exact post URL, the cut and its version, the post time, the destination link, the count type and the date it was read. A repost is a new row with its own time. A reach figure is evidence the post travelled, never evidence the film worked, so no review row cites one.

## Revise one thing

Turn what the numbers and the cold viewers said into one specific question: the result needed to come sooner, the workflow needed more room, the promise was not recognised outside the team. Make a revision that tests that one change, record it ([verify](verify.md), Hand over), and show it to new viewers, never to the people who saw the first cut. The next film starts from the record, not from a blank timeline.

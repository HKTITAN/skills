# Risk, luck and bets

Risk sits near the centre of his thinking: he treats it as a skill you learn, and he argues that the job is to reduce risk through cheap staged experiments, reserves and uncrowded markets, with only a small slice left for wild bets. Most of it comes from Kata 9, with the wealth view in Kata 13, luck in Kata 10 and Kata 4, and optionality in Kata 7 and Why Belief Matters. Most of these talks were given to rooms of his own Aeos employees in India, the rupee figures are Indian, and he says he was bad at risk in his 20s and changed several of these views later.

## What does he mean by risk, and is it a skill?

- He says an entrepreneur's job is to reduce risk, not to take it: do what looks obvious to you and very likely to win, and do it very cheaply until it wins. To outsiders who do not know the industry it then looks like a risk. "I think in real life an entrepreneur's job is to reduce risk." (Kata 9 at 1:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=99s)
- In games he does the opposite of reducing risk. In Dota, Counter-Strike and poker he takes crazy risks because it is fun, and he admits that as a poker player he still bets a lot early hoping for good cards. (Kata 9 at 1:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=99s; Kata 9 at 21:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1300s)
- In the same talk where he says the job is to reduce risk, he says you still have to take it. "You must take risks to win, but taking risks guarantees nothing." He still wants 5 to 10% on crazy bets. (Kata 9 at 41:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2489s; Kata 9 at 47:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2820s)
- A very large part of success is risk-taking ability, not hard work. If hard work were the cause, every labourer on the street would be wealthy, and everyone successful either took risk or descends from someone who did. "At some point the source of wealth is always risk." (Kata 9 at 3:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=197s)
- Everything is risk, not only new unproven ideas. He says even eating food is a gamble and claims more people die of diarrhea each year than from shark attacks and terrorist attacks combined, so the job is to estimate the risk of everything. (Kata 9 at 4:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=245s)
- People crowd-source their sense of risk by checking whether others are doing the same thing. He calls entrance exams for the IITs or the top 10 colleges a hugely risky endeavour on probability of success that India accepts only because everyone takes it. (Kata 9 at 4:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=292s)
- You cannot predict all the risks. His example is Galgotias: before its scandal nobody could have priced the chance that the degree brand would fall, so you live with the outcome and ask what to do now. "Risk is what's left over after you think you've thought of everything." (Kata 9 at 13:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=821s; Kata 9 at 12:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=772s)
- On tail risks he says two things: they are what people fail to calculate, and they are also overestimated and stop people from acting. (Kata 9 at 34:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2060s)
- He says all success has luck in it, and also that for people who take multiple bets even the art of betting is a skill. (Kata 4 at 27:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1638s)
- Knowing what actually caused a result lets you price risk properly. If you wrongly credit your degree for your job, a 10 to 12 lakh spend looks like a sensible risk and reward when the true reward was zero. (Kata 6 at 29:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1769s)
- In an internal talk on serotonin, where he says he is not a doctor, he ties calculated risk to feeling socially safe. High serotonin goes with less fear and confidence in calculated risk, while low serotonin still takes risk but the jumping off a cliff kind. (Secret Kata 1 at 22:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1355s)
- His method for fear of a bet is worst-case simulation: vividly imagine bankruptcy, automation or public humiliation, repeat it until the response dampens, realise you would survive, and you will not spiral when a real crisis hits. (Secret Kata 1 at 23:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g&t=1403s)

## How does he size a bet and stage it?

- Think in percentages, not success or failure. He says normies see each opportunity as it works or it does not, while probabilistic thinkers and professional poker players, which he says he is not, assign numbers like 20% success and 80% failure. (Kata 9 at 14:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=868s)
- Your early probability estimates will be wrong and experience fixes them. At 21 he would have overestimated his chance of success on a new business, and he says he is more accurate today. (Kata 9 at 16:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=965s)
- Spend in proportion to proven probability. If he thinks a hire has an 80% chance of working he does not mind spending more, and if it is 5 to 10% he spends less, finds out cheaply over 6 to 10 months and pays the price later once it is proven. (Kata 9 at 17:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1061s)
- His rule borrowed from poker is **GTO** in real life: ask whether you can run a very cheap, ideally free, experiment with a high payoff. A cheap experiment can be shut down if it fails, while an expensive one carries sunk costs. (Kata 9 at 16:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1013s)
- **Staging risk** means putting in little while the unknowns are high and more as checkpoints are cleared: can they build a team, a product, do marketing, get users, make revenue. He applies it to funding rounds, hiring, investing and personal life. (Kata 9 at 19:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1156s)
- Startup funding stages exist to stage risk, and he says everyone has kind of forgotten that. At seed an investor puts in 2 crores for a big share because it may be worth zero, and as risks are checked off the next investor's 2 crores gets only 5%. He says raising $100 million in a series A was never the point. (Kata 9 at 17:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1061s; Kata 9 at 19:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1156s)
- Nobody stages their own life, and they should. Most people have 5 lakhs and put all of it in a penny stock hoping for 10x, when each step should be a cheap experiment followed by more. He says portfolio rules like 80% safe exist because they are a form of staging. (Kata 9 at 21:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1300s)
- Start every new business as cheaply as possible, even with unlimited money. Overcapitalising early causes overhiring and sunk cost: having spent a crore, you feel forced to spend 10 years on the idea. (KK Create at 1:18:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4687s)
- For a job choice he writes the best case, the most likely case, the worst case and the tail risk two years out for each option, side by side on paper. He then feeds them to ChatGPT with deep research and asks which outcome is more probable and why, accepting that the numbers may be wrong. (Kata 9 at 30:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1824s)
- Along with thinking two years out, check what every other participant wants. A founder takes freelancers when unsure of the role or short of money, so do not expect care when things get hard. (Kata 9 at 32:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1964s)
- From Jeff Bezos's shareholder letters he takes one-way and two-way doors: decide reversible things in five minutes and take weeks or months over irreversible ones. His default is that no decision is made without an experiment. (Kata 7 at 28:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1730s)
- Even with a crazy belief, he says it is sometimes valuable to shut up about it and downgrade it to a much smaller, stacked plan, where the money from one stage is used for the next. He cites Elon's multi-staged plan to send people to Mars and says his own company's plan is kind of the same. (Why Belief Matters at 15:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=912s; Why Belief Matters at 15:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=959s)
- Build a system that tests a belief fast, as the Wright brothers did with a wind tunnel and 200 wing shapes after their 1901 glider failed. Find out tomorrow, not two weeks later, and hold separate levels of certainty about the problem, the solution and parts of the solution. (Why Belief Matters at 27:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1628s)
- The Aeos charter he wrote before starting, which he calls the lightning way, is speed, frugality, testing bets before entering the market and not caring too much about pivoting. (Masters Union at 43:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2616s)

## What protects him when a bet goes wrong?

- **Shock absorbers** are cash and broad skills built before a risk. Before freelancing or starting a company in India he says to have at least 10 lakhs in the bank, which buys about a year if you live cheaply. "It is suicidal to take risk without having shock absorption." (Kata 9 at 42:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2536s)
- Always hold more reserve than the estimate. If retiring costs 2 crores have three, and if a startup costs 10 lakhs have 20. (Kata 9 at 42:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2536s; Kata 9 at 57:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3438s)
- He kept his own shock absorber. During Jobspire he lived on about 15,000 rupees a month in a shared flat in Delhi, barely touched the little funding they had, and always kept 10 lakhs from his freelancing days as a year of safety. (Kata 9 at 41:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2489s; Kata 9 at 42:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2536s)
- **Medium risk** is the kind to avoid: you are not fully cashed up but take a bet that can wipe you out. His example is 6 lakhs in the bank and 3 lakhs budgeted for 3 months, but the first customer takes 8 months, so you run out and must get a job. (Kata 9 at 56:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3391s)
- Keep 80 to 90% ultra safe and put 5 to 10% on crazy risk, tested small and staged. Aeos gets 80 to 90% of its revenue from services, and on the other 10% he says he is allowed to go crazy. (Kata 9 at 53:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3199s)
- Take only risks that cannot kill the main business on the way out. His game Unleash the Avatar is built so that failure hurts his personal cash but does not kill Aeos, while a robotics startup would definitely kill Aeos because of capex in the hundreds of millions of dollars. (Kata 9 at 43:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2632s)
- In April 2026 he said a robotics startup right now would definitely kill Aeos if it did not work, because of the capital it needs. By October 2026 he says that once free cash flow starts rotating inside Indian companies they will start taking R&D risk: Aeos is investing free cash flow from content in games, and if the games work, maybe in something tangential, maybe robotics. (Kata 9 at 43:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2632s; Masters Union at 1:01:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3711s)
- Before acting, check for irreversible consequences, meaning anything that locks you out of something for good. (Kata 9 at 33:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2012s)
- Never burn bridges with a company you leave, because badmouthing it locks you out of that company and its whole ecosystem of customers if it gets big. At 20 he burned some bridges, and in his 30s he keeps good relations even with people who wronged him. (Kata 9 at 34:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2060s)
- Take back the original corpus, as venture investors do. Invest 1 crore, and when it is worth 4 crores sell 25% to recover the crore and let 3 crores ride, because the longer you stay in, the more likely a tail risk hits. He says Aeos will do this on its own investments when given the chance. (Kata 9 at 54:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3295s)
- **Productive paranoia** means asking what can go wrong even in good times and keeping excess deal flow ready in case a client leaves. He says his paranoia has saved the company multiple times. (Kata 9 at 57:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3438s)
- Leads are the best offset to bad luck. At 25 he spent 8 of every 10 rupees on product and 2 on marketing, and now he spends 8 on marketing and 2 on product, stretching the 2 by giving equity to good co-founders. (Kata 9 at 58:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3533s)
- The skills that let you absorb any risk are not hard skills. He lists taking risk accurately, gathering good people, being **wealth sharing, not wealth taking**, and distribution, and says hard skills are temporary. (Kata 9 at 38:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2298s)
- Even a sure-shot business needs room for things going wrong. Nobody predicted AI hitting content and music apps or the pandemic hitting food delivery, so he would still angel invest and do other things even if everything about the business is right. (Kata 9 at 22:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1347s)
- Too much belief is its own danger, which he calls the **Vijay Mallya risk**. After a run of wins you may buy an airline or enter the most cash-flow-dangerous businesses, so he says to study history to learn where the edge of belief is. (Why Belief Matters at 26:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1580s)

## When does he think it is right to bet?

- The best time to take a risk is when behaviour has permanently changed and nobody believes it yet. Behaviours rarely change, but when they do the change is permanent, and incumbents cannot adapt as fast as a small startup or an individual. (Kata 9 at 8:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=484s)
- Good risk-taking starts from what stays the same. Citing Morgan Housel's book Same as Ever, he lists greed, fear, overconfidence, tribalism and short-sightedness as constant for 300,000 years. (Kata 9 at 9:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=580s)
- Technology is the one place where behaviour changes, which is why the most money is made there. Snickers was the best-selling candy bar in 1962 and is still near the top, while the pocket transistor radio of 1962 is gone. (Kata 9 at 11:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=676s)
- People take bad risks because they trust stated preferences. Content at scale taught him that what people say they like and what they really like are totally different, especially in India, where he says people are not honest about what they think. (Kata 9 at 7:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=436s)
- The one lesson he would boil everything down to is that the world is extremely responsive to supply and demand, and only what people do matters. Hundreds of people on Twitter said quick commerce would not work, and 10-minute delivery filled an unconscious demand worth a few billion dollars. (Masters Union at 3:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=192s)
- Good bets feel high-probability to you and low-probability to others because of **information disparity**. He says video editing is a near-guaranteed career that India does not believe in yet, while software engineering feels safe but is very risky given the number of engineers against jobs. (Kata 9 at 24:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1490s)
- You cannot judge an industry's risks from outside. He says it takes about 2 years inside just to learn what works and who the players are. (Kata 9 at 25:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1539s)
- When everyone is scared the risk is lower, and when everyone is euphoric it is high. Citing Howard Marks and Warren Buffett, he says he goes where everyone is worried because competition thins out, and that **no-go businesses** have essentially no competition. (Kata 9 at 40:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2441s)
- His formula for a risk is a market that may expand with not enough people in it. He thinks an ice cream company or a services company is more likely to work than the socially acceptable app startup, at the cost of no social support and the chance of still being wrong. (Kata 9 at 1:05:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3914s)
- Enter markets with little competition because you get time to become good. When Aeos started YouTube as a service, being sort of good bought 2 years to build execution and hiring, and a copier today is at best where Aeos was 2 years ago. (Kata 9 at 1:03:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3819s)
- His poker argument for uncrowded markets is **multi-way variance**: with nine players someone probably holds better cards than you, while heads-up one pair is good. Fewer competitors means you do not need the best product on day one. (Kata 9 at 1:02:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3723s)
- He says that in India an idea people would laugh at could be a really big idea. That was part of the thesis for AI avatars: people would laugh, therefore do it. (Masters Union at 11:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=711s)
- Bet where several trends point the same way. He takes lollapalooza effects from Charlie Munger: when four or five things move in one direction the result is massive or arrives sooner than expected. (Kata 1 at 46:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2763s)
- For games he lists five trends behind the bet: DLSS upscaling, phone GPUs and NPUs, Unreal optimization, text-to-3D and subscription demand. (Kata 1 at 51:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3098s)
- Consensus protects you even when it is wrong, which is why being contrarian is hard. A venture investor loses less reputation on a wrong but popular deal than on a wrong unpopular one, and he says that if all your friends see no risk in something, think twice. (Why Belief Matters at 31:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1910s)
- Taking an expensive, risky bet is a costly signal that earns trust. Someone with 1 lakh who spends all of it is more credible than someone with 10 crores spending 1 lakh, and he says the bootstrapped game made brands see Aeos as taking real risk. (Masters Union at 21:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1281s)

## How much is luck, and how do you engineer it?

- He says all success is luck, just as all bad outcomes are luck. His examples are being put in an English-medium school, not being born in an uncontacted tribe and not losing limbs in a childhood accident. (Kata 10 at 5:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=348s)
- You can engineer luck by increasing your surface area: if more people know you, more opportunities can come your way. He says this is the trick he learned over the last three years. (Kata 10 at 6:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=397s)
- He pictures a **luck stat** on a game character. A thousand more followers moves it from five to seven, and he says the only thing that changed for him is that his is now near the hundreds. (Kata 10 at 7:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=444s)
- Content is a **lightning catcher**, like the spire on the Empire State Building: it puts you in position to take success when it strikes. (Kata 10 at 8:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=494s)
- Creating content is the inverse of reckless behaviour. Riding a bike without a helmet in the ghats every day for 10 years will end in an accident at some point, and content is the repeated action that raises the chance of good things instead. (Kata 10 at 8:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=494s; Kata 10 at 9:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=542s)
- He says you might think the ideal use of content is directing traffic to your own products and services, but he thinks the ideal use is as a luck catcher. He shows the US embassy reaching out on 19th May as an example. (Kata 10 at 11:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=685s)
- Creating content dampens the effect of luck in business. His success rate rose sharply after he started, because with distribution the cost of ads is zero and he can test cheaply whether people download, use and pay. (Kata 9 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=341s)
- Luck only finds you after a serious attempt. Nobody could have predicted his game going viral in China, they found out only after playing the card, and he says you have to try 5 to 10 times for one to succeed. (Kata 4 at 27:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1638s)
- In life you only have to be right once. Unlike poker, where going all in again can bust you, one win pays for thousands of experiments as long as you do not spend all your money on them. (Kata 9 at 38:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2298s)
- Knowing why you succeeded is what makes success repeatable. Many successful people do not know why, which he says makes it random luck. (Kata 6 at 2:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=141s)
- Much of wealth is random. A furniture franchisee he met on a plane got his wealth from a cheap property that the government later bought, not from the business, which made only 3 to 4 crores of profit a year. (Kata 9 at 53:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3199s; Kata 13 at 52:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3146s)
- He holds that all success is luck and everything is random, and in the same talk says the process is repeatable and Aeos has done it many times. (Kata 10 at 34:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=2094s)
- In November 2026 he opens by noting he is the one who said a lot of success is luck, then argues that belief is what separates success from failure. (Why Belief Matters at 1:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=95s)
- A persistent person with a wrong idea is more likely to make it than a person with a perfect idea who never starts, because doing it daily for 10 or 15 years gives new information. He calls this paradoxical and probability-based. (Why Belief Matters at 29:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1768s)

## Is it diversification, confusion or optionality?

- Confusion and diversification are different things. Confusion is five interesting things with no impact, scale or P&L on any, and diversification is one thing working well plus a second that does not make the first fall. (Kata 7 at 12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=725s)
- His test is whether one thing is already working and whether doing the second costs you the first. Investing part of your salary in blue chip stocks passes, quitting to go all in on something untested does not, and Aeos only takes on new projects once the old one is stable. (Kata 7 at 16:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=964s)
- In the risk talk he puts diversification as still needing one thing that works, and putting your money into other things that do not require your time. His example is a man whose time goes into his furniture business while real estate did not require his time. (Kata 9 at 47:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2868s)
- Keeping all your options open gives you the least impact, because every extra option is a tax on willpower. "It looks safe, but optionality is a type of poison." (Kata 7 at 8:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=485s; Kata 7 at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=629s)
- He calls optionality a type of poison while saying he does multiple things today, and separates his own case: it is "not because of optionality, it's because of diversification". (Kata 7 at 8:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=485s; Kata 7 at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=629s)
- Optionality kills belief, so he says to burn the bridges and cites Sun Tzu on burning boats. You cannot have belief while considering five things at once. (Why Belief Matters at 33:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2004s)
- Diversification is not optionality: building one thing to scale, then a second, then a third is fine, and starting five things from scratch at once is not. (Why Belief Matters at 33:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2004s)
- Interviewing at 10 places is **fake optionality**, because you have not quit and do not know if the offers are serious. **Deep optionality** is transferable skill built through repetition, and he says that after running, shutting, failing and being acquired many times he can now walk on any surface. (Kata 9 at 37:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2249s)
- Once one thing works, doing five or ten more is easy. He says he learned in his 30s that making a choice puts you ahead of everyone. (Kata 7 at 36:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=2162s)
- Take the compounding option over the immediate one. He rates 1% profit share at a fast-growing company making 1 crore a year above three freelance projects at 2 lakhs a month each, because the company is proven and freelance clients are unstable. (Why Belief Matters at 34:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2053s)
- For compounding assets he says to remove the option to sell: invest, seal it and do not look for 10 years. The option to sell is itself a problem, which is why active traders sell too early. (Why Belief Matters at 36:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2196s; Why Belief Matters at 35:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2149s)

## How do the wealthy take risk?

- Wealthy people have a different risk profile because their reserves let them keep taking shots. Serial entrepreneurs can start a third or fifth business because the first or second worked, and he cites Justin Kan's legal startup failing after Twitch without hurting him cash-wise. (Kata 9 at 43:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2584s)
- The wealthy prefer a risky bet with an extreme outcome over a safe bet with a standard one. Someone with 100 crores will not put 10 crores into something that returns 12 in five years: they do not mind losing the 10 but would like it to become 500. (Kata 13 at 48:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2908s)
- He used to think investors were foolish for not wanting profitability and now says he was the foolish one. Once you have enough money, the 5% you do not need should aim for the moon, and an investor is fine if the company dies. (Kata 13 at 1:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=100s)
- The broke and the extremely rich think in a similar way, but the rich have many lives. Young people bet on apps and crypto because salaries are stuck and real estate has shot up, and he says he has a lot of empathy for them. (Kata 13 at 50:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3052s)
- He says taking enormous risks is the wrong lesson to draw from the rich. The right one is how to survive failure while keeping the upside, since downside is capped at zero and upside is unlimited, and gambling everything is wrong. (Kata 13 at 54:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3242s)
- He says risk appetite rises with wealth toward extreme bets, and also that at 1,000 crore the risk level drops for smaller projects because losing 10 crores many times is affordable. (Kata 13 at 49:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2957s)
- By his wealth bands, people at 1 crore are security focused and buy blue chips, people at 10 crore take moderate risk, and people at 100 crore write 10 to 30 angel checks a year. (Kata 13 at 15:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=910s; Kata 13 at 19:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1145s; Kata 13 at 23:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=1386s)
- Some venture-funded founders in India take a salary and invest in each other's companies, where early bets are 100x or bust. He says for some the portfolio is worth more than their own company, and that this is legal and known in Silicon Valley. (Kata 9 at 50:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3008s)
- Rich people he has met do not care about failing or being mocked. You cannot reach success by avoiding mistakes, which he says only works in exams. (Kata 13 at 10:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=624s)
- Judge funded founders by the game they are playing: an all-or-nothing game of raising a lot to reach a monopoly position, like trying to hit a six every ball. He admits he once felt superior running a small profitable company and now calls that cope. (Kata 4 at 4:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=243s; Kata 4 at 5:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=339s)
- **Hyper gambling** is his borrowed name for young people taking long-shot risks because the standard path makes you rich at 50. He says an ambitious startup is the good kind, but one in 100 works, which suits a venture investor funding 100 startups and may not suit the founder. (Kata 7 at 1:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=101s)
- In August 2026 he defends the investor's logic of aiming for the moon, and in October 2026 he says he no longer buys the venture startup dream for himself, calling the path he followed for 10 years kind of like a lottery. (Kata 13 at 1:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=100s; KK Create at 20:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1221s)

## How does he run parallel bets, cut them and invest?

- Aeos ran several small parallel bets with very high failure tolerance. If a bet failed they moved to the next one and did not beat themselves up, because failing is the natural process of learning. (Kata 1 at 12:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=729s)
- His rule is parallel bets and merciless cuts: try things in parallel because you cannot know which will work, and be merciless about the ones that do not. (Kata 1 at 54:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3291s)
- Shut down what does not work, without ego, and ignore people who laugh at pivots. He says in India people drag a business out for years as their heart and soul, which he calls the absolutely wrong way to look at the world. (Kata 9 at 23:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1395s)
- Match your strategy to how random your domain is. In high-randomness domains like apps, viral content and venture funding, run many small parallel shots, double down on what works and shut the rest. In skill domains like surgery or video editing, do massive deliberate practice. (Kata 9 at 1:00:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3628s)
- He thinks his right failure rate is 50%, and on projects they really cared about it was zero for 3 years. Reliability pushes people toward only sure things, and he feels he needs to take harder bets. (Kata 2 at 21:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1299s)
- He calls himself an experimenter who does not mind failing at 90 of 100 things, but says he is now more afraid of that because the stories he tells his team must come true. (Kata 2 at 20:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1203s)
- Repeating the same failure without updating your model is a gambler mentality. His example is engineers who ship one app a week hoping one goes viral. (Kata 4 at 43:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2606s)
- Founders must be flexible and not attached to the first idea. He compares it to folding pocket aces when two kings show up and says the best founders have pivoted four or five times. (Masters Union at 44:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2665s)
- When an employee has real conviction about something untried that has not lost money before, he says to run an experiment. (Kata 2 at 58:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3535s)
- His angel investing changed. Two years before April 2026 he put 5 to 10 lakhs into seed companies on FOMO because big funds were in, and they shut down a year later. He now invests around series A after checkpoints are passed, naming Lumio and Temple, and calls the old approach suicidal risk taking. (Kata 9 at 20:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1251s)
- Loss aversion reaches investors too. His team invested 15 lakhs in a company that is probably up 2x 3x, and they still feel bad because they had the opportunity to invest a year prior. (Kata 9 at 50:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3055s)
- His businesses work as sensors for which companies are doing well: a company raises money, starts influencer marketing, wants a deal with him or hires three or four editors. He uses those signals to invest. (Masters Union at 30:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1852s)
- He says VCs have a word, excitement: to invest in a company you need to be excited about it, not just greedy and rich. He is super excited about gaming in India, where he invested in LightFury and Unleash the Avatar, and an old-school manufacturing plant is not exciting to him even though it might make him money. (Kata 13 at 58:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3528s; Kata 13 at 59:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3576s)

## How does risk show up in careers and teams?

- Loss hurts more than gain feels good, so he avoids giving anything he might have to take back. His rules for teams are no feel-good titles or promotions, no ballpark projections of what people will earn, and no perks unless well thought out. (Kata 9 at 51:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3104s; Kata 9 at 52:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3153s)
- People who miss a projected number feel loss even when they out-earn everyone. Aeos gave Gully Labs shoes to its best talent, and those who did not get them felt really bad. (Kata 9 at 51:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3104s; Kata 9 at 52:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3153s)
- Power to take a swing has to come with personal risk. A man from a very big game publisher needed 6 months to decide on his game because a wrong bet would cost him his job, and his own rule is to give people power and drop them if they fail on it. (Kata 4 at 10:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=631s)
- You cannot have full market salary and skin in the game together. The people he respects offer to take the high-risk campaign and reduce their salary for a share if it works. (Kata 4 at 22:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1350s)
- Do not take co-founders who have never taken personal risk. Most people who earned a percentage took close to zero salary for a year and lived on savings. (Kata 4 at 51:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3087s)
- Never judge a job by what it pays now. Take a 2-year view of which company and market will do better, as with 30,000 a month at a finance company in a growing market against 60k selling monitors in a capped one. (Kata 9 at 28:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1680s)
- Freelancing for small startups is a much bigger risk than freelancers realise. He says 99% of small startups will die next year, so a freelancer there may be making money while burning years of career, and should diversify to 5, 10 or 15 clients and reach bigger companies quickly. (Kata 9 at 31:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1918s)
- In big companies nobody wants to own a call because a wrong one costs your job or reputation, so decisions get handed to process. He says founder-driven companies do better because founders cannot blame anyone else. (Kata 7 at 17:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1060s)
- Nobody cares much about failure, they care that you took the hard call and were eventually right. The Aeos rule is to ask for forgiveness, not permission. (Kata 7 at 20:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1204s)
- A failed attempt still earns competence if it was clean: you put your hand up, say you thought you could and cannot, give the reasons and shut it down. (Kata 4 at 16:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=966s)
- Against his view that a clean failure still earns credit, he also says that if you fail you fail, and that at Aeos you need at least one win because the company can no longer keep giving multiple shots. (Kata 4 at 49:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2991s)

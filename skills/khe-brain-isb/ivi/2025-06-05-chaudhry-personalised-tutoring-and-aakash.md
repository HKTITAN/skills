# Personalised tutoring and how Aakash hired teachers, Aakash Chaudhry

Aakash Chaudhry of Aakash Institutes met the cohort in a group conversation that he said was not a lecture. He spoke about his current one-on-one tutoring work built around the IB curriculum, about parents and schooling, and briefly about how Aakash Institute began and recruited teachers. The transcript catches only pieces of it, so this note is short.

## What was said

### What a programme adds when knowledge is free

- Aakash Chaudhry says the knowledge taught in a programme is all available elsewhere. What a programme gives is a regime, and peers.
- He says students come to coaching for the disciplined environment: fixed hours, hearing others' questions, an assessment afterwards and a doubt class.
- He says he learned more from peers and outside the classroom than from lectures alone.

### Personalised learning

- He says that when his team went deep into the IB framework they saw it is not about finishing the syllabus. It is about making the child a learner.
- He says the harder part was getting teachers to unlearn how they had taught for 10 to 20 years.
- He says each student starts with a baseline assessment and then gets a personalised journey, which differs by the child's level and by whether they join at the start of the year or three months before the exam.
- He says the pedagogy let the team code into its platform where and at what level to intervene in a learning journey.
- He says the aim is a centre of excellence in creating pedagogy, understanding curriculum and linking both to a child's history.
- He says technology finds its way to the masses, as the room-sized IBM mainframe ended up in a pocket, and that is what the team is trying to do with this teaching.
- He says a teacher in this model takes no more than 12 students in a year.
- He says technology lets the service join tutoring, test preparation and admission counselling into one offering, where a subject tutor alone would not know what is happening in the child's other subjects.
- He says baseline data lets the team have an honest conversation with a parent about expectations, showing data, outcomes and track record.

### Schools, AI tutors and parents

- He says schools now see their job as wider than academics and have passed that on to parents, while a tutoring service has only academics to focus on.
- He says schools did not see one-on-one tutoring as a service to plug in, and are becoming interested because of the AI tutor.
- He says a subject teacher supported by an AI tutor in class will be far more effective, with teacher, student and AI all taking part in the conversation.
- He says children being steered only towards university outcomes is a systemic problem, and a tutoring company still has to play some part in changing it.
- He tells his own children that he stops them from some things because he has made those mistakes, not because he knows more.
- He says he would be fine with a child taking a gap after class 12 to learn about the world, and that a parent ten years ago would have said finish your education and get a job first.
- He says his parents' generation was a generation of survivors, and the change is those fears giving way. He expects AI to speed it up.
- He says confidence comes from peers, knowledge, failures and a few successes, and that teaching should aim at knowledge and not at how to get a degree.

### How Aakash Institute began and hired

- He says the founder's first two attempts at the business, in the mid 1980s, did not work, and Aakash Institute came out of the third.
- He says the first year had 12 students and 7 teachers.
- He says the market had a very small pool of teachers, each with their own price and terms.
- He says Aakash therefore went to campuses to hire, and he describes it as the first and largest campus placement programme run by a coaching company in India.
- He says it hired from IITs, NITs, IIITs and other specialist colleges, taking sharp graduates who had cracked the exams themselves but did not know how to help others do it.
- He says Aakash built a four-month training programme for these recruits, and that it took a lot of effort and was one of the most important reasons the company succeeded.
- He says reputation can only come from genuinely delivering a great product or service.

## Examples and cases used

- The IBM mainframe: a room-sized machine that became a pocket device, as his picture of expert teaching reaching everyone.
- Asking an AI chatbot to question you first: he suggests trying this to see how a baseline diagnostic works.

## Numbers given

- 12 students and 7 teachers: Aakash Institute's first year, in his telling.
- 12 students per teacher per year: the cap in his current tutoring model.
- Four months: the training programme for campus recruits at Aakash.

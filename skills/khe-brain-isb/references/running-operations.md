# How does the work flow, and where does it get stuck?

One claim per line, each with its piece. Every line is the repository author's summary: the Operations files cited here name no author, no professor, no course and no source for any figure; the names in them are companies used as examples, such as Shouldice Hospital and Starbucks, methods that carry a name, such as Little's Law and Holt-Winters, and software tools. A number inside a worked example is that example's number, never a benchmark. These files disagree on the Six Sigma defect figures, on the cash conversion cycle formula and on whether the quality target is 3 to 4 sigma or 6 sigma; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## Where does the time go, and what limits output?

- In lean thinking value is anything a customer pays for; everything else, which the file lists as waiting, redundant checks, motion and idle inventory, is waste. (Operations: Process Design and Flow Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/fundamentals-process-design-and-flow-analysis.md)
- The file gives value-adding time as typically 5 to 20% of total time, and gives the typical finding of value stream mapping as 80% or more of time spent waiting and under 20% on actual work. (Operations: Process Design and Flow Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/fundamentals-process-design-and-flow-analysis.md)
- Takt time is available time divided by demand, the rate at which output should leave the process to meet demand; if takt is greater than cycle time, the process has excess capacity. (Operations: Process Design and Flow Analysis, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/fundamentals-process-design-and-flow-analysis.md)
- A bottleneck is the resource or process limiting overall throughput, and improving non-constraint resources does not improve overall performance. (Operations: Bottleneck Analysis and Theory of Constraints, TOC, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/fundamentals-bottleneck-analysis-and-theory-of-constraints-toc.md)
- The five focusing steps, repeated continuously, are: identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate non-constraints, elevate the constraint, and return to step 1. (Operations: Bottleneck Analysis and Theory of Constraints, TOC, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/fundamentals-bottleneck-analysis-and-theory-of-constraints-toc.md)
- Utilisation rate is actual output divided by design capacity; the file gives 60 to 80% as often realistic and says more than 80% creates bottlenecks and variability. (Operations: Capacity Planning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/fundamentals-capacity-planning.md)

## How good is good enough?

- Total quality cost is prevention costs plus appraisal costs plus failure costs, and investing in prevention is usually cheaper than fixing failures. (Operations: Quality Management, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/fundamentals-quality-management.md)
- The quality management file says optimal quality is not zero defects: it is where marginal improvement cost equals marginal failure cost savings, and it typically requires 3 to 4 sigma capability. (Operations: Quality Management, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/fundamentals-quality-management.md)
- The Six Sigma file describes a structured problem-solving framework targeting 3.4 defects per million opportunities, which it ties to 6 standard deviations from specification accounting for process drift. (Operations: Six Sigma, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/lean-six-sigma.md)
- The Six Sigma metrics file lists 308,538 defects per million opportunities at 1 sigma, 69,146 at 2 sigma, 6,210 at 3 sigma and 3.4 at 6 sigma, the last accounting for 1.5 sigma of process drift. (Operations: Six Sigma Metrics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/quality-six-sigma-metrics.md)
- The key formulas file lists defects per million opportunities by sigma level as 308,538 at 1, 69,146 at 2, 6,210 at 3, 233 at 4, 3.4 at 5 and 0.002 at 6. (Operations: Key Formulas and Quantitative Tools, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/key-formulas-an-key-formulas-and-quantitative-tools.md)
- The DMAIC methodology has five phases: Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve and Control. (Operations: Six Sigma, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/lean-six-sigma.md)
- Common cause variation is normal, random and stable; special cause variation is unusual, comes from an assignable cause and requires investigation. A process can be in control and still not meet specifications. (Operations: Control Charts and Variation, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/quality-control-charts-and-variation.md)
- The lean file heads its list of wastes "Seven Wastes" under the acronym TIMWOODS and gives eight entries: transport, inventory, motion, waiting, overproduction, over-processing, defects and skills, the last marked as added. (Operations: Lean Manufacturing, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/lean-lean-manufacturing.md)

## How do goods move through the chain, and how much stock is held?

- Responsive supply chains have high flexibility, fast response and higher cost; efficient ones are optimised for cost with standardised products and longer lead times. The choice depends on product lifecycle, demand variability and margin. (Operations: Supply Chain Strategy, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/supply-supply-chain-strategy.md)
- In the bullwhip effect small demand fluctuations at retail create increasingly larger swings upstream; the file lists five root causes: forecast updating, order batching, price fluctuations, rationing or shortage gaming, and information asymmetry. (Operations: Bullwhip Effect, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/supply-bullwhip-effect.md)
- The file lists six mitigations for the bullwhip effect: sharing point-of-sale data with suppliers, vendor-managed inventory, CPFR, stable pricing, kanban systems and a single point of replenishment. (Operations: Bullwhip Effect, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/supply-bullwhip-effect.md)
- The economic order quantity is the square root of 2DS divided by H, balancing ordering costs against holding costs; it assumes constant known demand, instant replenishment and no quantity discounts. (Operations: Inventory Management, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/supply-inventory-management.md)
- Safety stock is Z times the standard deviation of demand times the square root of lead time, with Z given as 1.65 for a 95% service level and 2.33 for 99%; the reorder point is average demand times lead time, plus safety stock. (Operations: Inventory Management, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/supply-inventory-management.md)
- The key formulas file gives the cash conversion cycle as DIO plus DSO minus DPO. (Operations: Key Formulas and Quantitative Tools, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/key-formulas-an-key-formulas-and-quantitative-tools.md)
- The working capital file gives the cash conversion cycle as DPO minus DSO minus DIO, and says shorter cycles free capital. (Operations: Working Capital in Supply Chain, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/supply-working-capital-in-supply-chain.md)

## What happens when customers have to wait?

- Little's Law says the average number in the system equals the arrival rate times the average time in the system, for any queue regardless of distribution; the file's example is a queue with 5 customers and a service taking 30 minutes, giving an arrival rate of 10 per hour. (Operations: Queuing Theory Basics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/queuing-queuing-theory-basics.md)
- Service operations differ from manufacturing in four ways: perishability, intangibility, variability and simultaneity. Unused service capacity is lost forever, unlike inventory. (Operations: Service Operations Characteristics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/queuing-service-operations-characteristics.md)
- A service cannot build inventory to smooth demand and must match capacity to demand patterns; the file lists overbooking by airlines and hotels to account for no-shows, and yield management that varies price by demand time. (Operations: Service Operations Characteristics, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/queuing-service-operations-characteristics.md)

## How is demand forecast and a plan optimised?

- A moving average smooths noise but lags trends; exponential smoothing weights recent observations more, with an alpha of 0.7 to 0.9 given as weighting recent data more and 0.1 to 0.3 as smoother. (Operations: Forecasting Methods, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/analytics-forecasting-methods.md)
- The tracking signal is cumulative forecast error divided by MAD; the file says values between minus 4 and plus 4 indicate an unbiased forecaster and values outside that range suggest systematic bias. (Operations: Forecasting Methods, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/analytics-forecasting-methods.md)
- Linear programming optimises a linear objective function subject to linear constraints, with the optimal solution at a corner point of the feasible region; the shadow price is the value of one additional unit of a constraint resource. (Operations: Linear Programming, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/analytics-linear-programming.md)
- Monte Carlo simulation generates many random scenarios from probability distributions and tracks the outcomes; the file gives 10,000 to 100,000 as the typical number of repetitions and, in another line, says results stabilise after 10,000 to 50,000 iterations for most applications. (Operations: Simulation & Monte Carlo, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/analytics-simulation-monte-carlo.md)

## Why do projects run late?

- A project charter is the authorisation document establishing purpose, objectives and high-level requirements; a scope statement defines included and excluded work; a work breakdown structure decomposes the project hierarchically into work packages. (Operations: Project Planning, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/project-project-planning.md)
- The critical path is the sequence of activities with zero slack that determines minimum project duration, and any slip on it delays the project; slack is latest start minus earliest start, or latest finish minus earliest finish. (Operations: Scheduling, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/project-scheduling.md)
- PERT takes three time estimates, O, M and P, and sets expected time at O plus 4M plus P, divided by 6; the variance of the critical path is the sum of the task variances on it. (Operations: Scheduling, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/project-scheduling.md)
- The file says that in traditional projects each task is padded, the padding is consumed by early inefficiencies and the project is still late. The critical chain approach allocates buffers to the critical chain only and tracks buffer consumption. (Operations: Scheduling, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/project-scheduling.md)
- The planning fallacy is underestimating time and cost for the future while overestimating past performance; the file says projects consistently overrun and gives 30 to 50% as the typical underestimation. (Operations: Systematic Biases in Projects, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/project-systematic-biases-in-projects.md)
- The file's mitigation for optimism bias is the outside view, which asks what happened in similar projects, plus independent review and less salience for best-case scenarios; for the sunk cost fallacy it is to make go or no-go decisions on forward costs and benefits only. (Operations: Systematic Biases in Projects, https://github.com/HKTITAN/isb-skills/blob/060ee06/skills/operations/rules/project-systematic-biases-in-projects.md)

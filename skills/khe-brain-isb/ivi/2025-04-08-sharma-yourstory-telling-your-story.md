# Telling your story as a founder, Shraddha Sharma

Typed notes from a conversation between Prof Bhagwan Chowdhry and Shraddha Sharma, who has been building YourStory for 17 years. Prof Chowdhry led the conversation, and the cohort asked questions.

## What was said

### Story before jargon

- Shraddha Sharma says to cut the jargon from a pitch and say what the story is.
- She recalls entrepreneurs pitching to Ratan Tata and says none of them were telling a story.
- Her questions to a founder are what are you solving, who are you, and what is your story.
- She says what you do defines where you go in life.
- She says everyone has biases, so we have to be very clear about them.
- Prof Bhagwan Chowdhry says that if the problem is not high tech, it may not need a high tech solution.

### Traits of successful entrepreneurs

- She names an extremely high level of persistence as the first trait of successful entrepreneurs.
- She says successful entrepreneurs pivot and change quickly while looking at a ten-year trajectory.
- She says they are extremely high learners, picking up body language, domain knowledge and lessons from people.
- She says they network at a high level.
- She says women are great with people but do not build strong networks, and do not ask for what they want, such as asking someone to champion them.

### Revenue and impact

- Asked how to balance building revenue against impact, she says social impact businesses have a long gestation period.
- She says a founder should ask whether they want money only because everyone around them is making money.
- She describes her own choice as being an evergreen tree, growing slowly but always being there.

### Sharing failure

- She says entrepreneurs should share their stories and their failures.
- She says the ecosystem is not yet secure, social media is extremely judgemental, and the ability to stand with entrepreneurs is not there yet.
- She says founders should be open about mistakes, co-founder fallouts, boardroom dynamics and where the money went, and asks why it should take investigative journalists to dig these out.
- She says to share your idea.

### Keeping going

- She says what you lack makes you run, and that it gives you the ability to take risks.
- She asks founders to find the pocket of ten minutes in their venture that makes them happy and keeps them going.
- On moats she says Usain Bolt would answer that he runs faster than everyone else and is hungry.
- She says a failure does not matter later, so an entrepreneur should take themselves lightly and just do it.

## Examples and cases used

- Ratan Tata: entrepreneurs who pitched to him without telling a story.
- Usain Bolt: her picture of a moat as being faster than everyone else and hungry.

## Not covered

- The notes do not cover how YourStory itself was built or how it makes money.
- No advice on getting media coverage is recorded.

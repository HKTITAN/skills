# Building a company

This theme is his operating model for starting and structuring a business: find a supply and demand gap, test it cheaply, stay profitable, and grow as a group of small companies whose founders own real upside. Most of it comes from Kata 1, Kata 9 and Kata 12, which are talks to his own Aeos employees, and from KK Create and Masters Union, where he is a guest. Much of it describes what worked for Aeos, a bootstrapped Indian content and services group, and several positions are ones he says he reversed from his twenties.

## What should a business be built on?

- He does not sort business ideas into hard and easy. He looks for gaps: teaching video editing was far easier than any business he ran before and it worked because a gap existed, while some of his hardest businesses went nowhere. (Masters Union at 2:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=144s)
- The one lesson he would boil everything down to is this: "The world is extremely responsive to supply-demand." What people say or think does not count, only what they do, and his example is the hundreds of people on Twitter who said quick commerce would fail before 10-minute delivery became worth a few billion dollars for Zepto and Blinkit. (Masters Union at 3:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=192s)
- His **gap thinking** runs in four steps: ask whether there is a supply and demand gap, seal it, watch whether the hole gets bigger, then look for the adjacent hole. For Aeos the first hole was the editing school and the adjacent one was end-to-end content for companies. (Masters Union at 4:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=240s)
- Customers care about only two things: whether you solve a problem or whether you entertain them. Aeos began with an entertainment thesis and found that entertainment also solves a company's problem of visibility and attention. (Masters Union at 6:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=383s)
- There is no substitute for building what people want. He says his first 10 years were not radically successful because people only kind of wanted what he built, and the biggest wins come from what people want but do not know yet, which he ties to Henry Ford and faster horses. (KK Create at 23:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1423s)
- What people want has not changed in 200,000 years, only the format has: validation in a group, a partner, comfort, food, shelter, security, achievement, escape and entertainment. So he expects people to want the exact same things in 5 years. (KK Create at 34:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2094s)
- What the market says and what it pays for are two different things. He says Aeos learned this the hard way and that he has to read what buyers mean from their buying behaviour. (Kata 1 at 1:01:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3673s)
- To make money, build for people who have capital. Founders who built for tier three India ten years ago struggled because there was no disposable income, while people selling something basic to the rich did better. (Kata 13 at 13:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=814s)
- Three things explain business success in his view: find an untapped market, hire good talent and execute well, and stay alive long enough to collect. He says every founder he has met fits this, with exceptions. (Kata 6 at 36:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=2200s)
- When a founder blames the market, he asks the lead-multiplier question: with 10 times the leads, would revenue be 10 times? A founder with about 10 crores of sales from a thousand leads was shutting down over market size, and his read was that the business only lacked leads. (Kata 6 at 3:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=188s)
- His formula for a good risk is a market that may expand with not enough people in it. He thinks an ice cream company or a services company is more likely to work than the socially acceptable app startup, at the cost of having no social support. (Kata 9 at 1:05:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3914s)
- Enter markets with little competition because you get time to become good. When Aeos started YouTube as a service nobody did YouTube end to end, so being sort of good bought 2 years to build execution and hiring. (Kata 9 at 1:03:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3819s)
- When everyone is scared the risk is lower, an idea he takes from Howard Marks and Buffett. Nobody believed in video editing, services or an Indian game, and he says such **no-go businesses** come with essentially no competition. (Kata 9 at 40:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2441s)
- In India, an idea people would laugh at could be a really big idea. That was part of his reasoning for AI avatars: people would laugh, therefore do it. (Masters Union at 11:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=711s)
- App categories are saturated but attention to creators is not. People could not name a new app they had downloaded yet could name new YouTubers they followed, so he asked why he should make more apps. (Kata 1 at 17:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1069s)

## Should you raise money or bootstrap?

- He went bootstrapped at Aeos because the vision was too weird to pitch. Three years earlier nobody believed AI could be creative or that distribution mattered, so he chose to prove it first, and once it was proven the company did not need the money. (Kata 1 at 12:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=778s)
- Zero funding forced discipline because nobody was coming to save them. If they were wrong they were dead, so they kept costs low and never overhired. (Kata 1 at 16:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=970s)
- "Bootstrap is true hard mode." In funded Indian companies salaries often flow from a VC whether or not the company works, while at Aeos money only comes while customers find value. (Kata 1 at 1:01:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3673s)
- Being profitable is easy when it is your own money. With 5 million dollars of someone else's money a borderline hire gets a yes, while in a bootstrapped company every hire comes out of your own pocket. (KK Create at 32:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1951s)
- He no longer buys the traditional venture startup dream of raising 10 million, then 100 million, ending with investors owning 95% and a locked-in IPO. He did that for 10 years, calls it a kind of lottery, and prefers a sustainable, long-term, profitable business. (KK Create at 20:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1221s)
- His view of investors changed. When he raised venture money, investors kept telling him to grow instead of being profitable and he thought they were foolish; in August 2026 he says he was the foolish one. (Kata 13 at 0:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=53s; Kata 13 at 2:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=147s)
- His reason for the change: once you have enough money, the 5% you do not need should aim for the moon. A crore that returns one crore ten lakhs over ten years is pointless when a fixed deposit exists, so an investor is fine if the company dies. (Kata 13 at 1:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=100s)
- Judge funded founders by the game they are playing. Asking whether the company is profitable misses that they play an all-or-nothing game of raising a lot to reach a monopoly position, like trying to hit a six every ball. (Kata 4 at 4:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=243s)
- Funding stages exist to stage risk, and he says people have forgotten that. At seed an investor puts in 2 crores for a big share because it may go to zero, and as the company ticks off team, product, marketing and users the same 2 crores buys 5%. (Kata 9 at 17:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1061s)
- Do not raise money until you have 100% belief. He has seen companies that were making money get 10 no's from 10 VCs and shut down because the founder lost confidence. (Why Belief Matters at 17:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1056s)
- Inside Aeos an unprofitable business is shameful until it becomes profitable. He contrasts venture-funded companies, where only growth matters and not growing is the shame. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 31:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1879s)

## Which startup rules did he reject?

- His opposite-of-consensus list for Aeos: consensus said no wrappers, no services, the product is the moat, do one thing, raise money, be in HSR. Aeos built wrappers, ran two services companies, treated talent and distribution as the moat, ran several small bets, bootstrapped and sat in North Bangalore. (Kata 1 at 10:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=633s)
- He defends being a wrapper as a way to cut your own complexity. Asked why he uses Zoho for project management or a third-party CRM, he answers that making an apple pie from scratch needs the whole universe, and everyone lives on other people's abstractions. (Kata 11 at 9:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=579s)
- For Aeos the moat is access to talent and distribution, not the product. Enabled talent builds great products, and the early team learned through pain that a product without distribution does not work. (Kata 1 at 11:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=681s)
- On being told to do a single thing at once, he says it is actually true but there is a very important nuance between doing a single thing and doing multiple things, which he says he will talk about. In the same passage he says Aeos did several small parallel bets with very high failure tolerance. (Kata 1 at 12:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=729s)
- He separates confusion from diversification. Confusion is five interesting things with no impact, no scale or no P&L on any of them, while diversification is one thing working very well and a second thing that will not make the first fall. (Kata 7 at 11:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=676s; Kata 7 at 12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=725s)
- The test question is whether doing the second thing costs you the first, and Aeos only takes on new projects once the old one is stable. (Kata 7 at 16:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=964s)
- Do not drop your five interests; find the one thing that makes them all possible. Everything Aeos built was something it needed right then, such as solving a shortage of video editors, and never an attractive idea. (Kata 7 at 11:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=676s)
- Founders must be flexible and not attached to the first idea. He compares a pivot to folding pocket aces when two kings show up and says the best founders have pivoted four or five times. (Masters Union at 44:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2665s)
- He broke his own rule of always doing something different when he entered influencer marketing, which he calls commoditized and weak on moat. He did it because almost all tech deal flow in India comes to him as a creator, and he treats it as a temporary arbitrage. (Masters Union at 29:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1758s; Masters Union at 28:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1710s)

## How does he start and test something new?

- "I think in real life an entrepreneur's job is to reduce risk." The job is to do what looks obvious to you and likely to win, very cheaply, until it wins, and to outsiders it then looks like a risk. (Kata 9 at 1:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=99s)
- His borrowed poker test, GTO in real life: can I run a very cheap, ideally free, experiment with a high payoff? A cheap experiment can be shut down if it fails, while an expensive one carries sunk costs. (Kata 9 at 16:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1013s)
- Start every new business as cheaply as possible, even with unlimited money. Overcapitalising causes overhiring and sunk cost: having spent a crore, you feel forced to spend 10 years on the idea. (KK Create at 1:18:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4687s)
- A new business should make money fast. A new Aeos services business should earn within 2 weeks because the group already has a network of hundreds of customers, and teams are kept very lean. (KK Create at 30:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1809s)
- Spend in proportion to proven probability. At an 80% chance of success he does not mind spending more; at 5 to 10% he spends less, finds out cheaply over 6 to 10 months, and pays up once it is proven. (Kata 9 at 17:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1061s)
- Decide in about three months whether a project lives. One demo or trailer has three outcomes: great feedback so continue, unclear so get more data, or a waste of time so shut it down. (Kata 12 at 5:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=344s)
- Shut down what does not work, without ego. Being frugal and giving co-founders equity instead of cash let Aeos see businesses through cheaply, and he calls the Indian habit of dragging a business out for years and laughing at pivots the wrong way to look at the world. (Kata 9 at 23:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=1395s)
- Try things in parallel because you cannot know which will work, and be merciless about the ones that do not. (Kata 1 at 54:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3291s)
- Prove a need by hand before you automate it or hire for it, which he ties to Paul Graham's advice to do things that do not scale. He tells people selling him video editing software to edit 10 videos themselves first. (Kata 11 at 25:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1541s)
- Once one feature works, stop building and put all your energy into marketing and sales. He says successful entrepreneurs find the one thing that works, sell it, and do not disturb it. (Kata 11 at 21:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1298s)
- Do not take a risk without shock absorbers. Before freelancing or starting a company in India, have at least 10 lakhs in the bank, and always hold more reserve than the estimate. (Kata 9 at 42:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2536s)
- Take only risks that cannot kill the main business. His game is built so that failure hurts his personal cash and not Aeos, while a robotics startup would kill Aeos through capex. (Kata 9 at 43:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2632s)
- Keep 80 to 90% ultra safe and put 5 to 10% on crazy bets. Aeos gets 80 to 90% of revenue from services, and on the rest he allows himself to go crazy. (Kata 9 at 53:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3199s)
- Content lowers the cost of testing. His business success rate rose sharply after he started creating content, because with distribution the cost of ads is zero and he can test quickly whether people download, use and pay. (Kata 9 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=341s)
- The Aeos charter he wrote before starting, the lightning way, has four parts: speed, frugality, testing bets before entering the market, and not caring too much about pivoting. (Masters Union at 43:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2616s)

## How is the group structured?

- Own the stack around a scarce resource. In the content gold rush the scarce resource was the video editor, so Aeos trained editors and then consumed them in its own channels and services. (Kata 1 at 24:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1454s; Kata 1 at 20:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1214s)
- An investor would have told him to do more of the school. He kept it small and focused on outcomes, citing lessons from Byju's, and built a media network instead of an edtech. (Kata 1 at 23:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1407s; Kata 1 at 24:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1454s)
- The ideal is to consume your own resources. In Brass: Birmingham using your own coal to build your own iron is a double win, and every editor from the school has a near-guaranteed placement at the services company in the worst case. (Masters Union at 11:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=711s)
- The Aeos chain as he describes it: the video editing school produces editors, the services company consumes them to do YouTube end to end for customers, and Labs, a separate engineering-focused company, automates parts to lift margins and also sells its own projects. (Masters Union at 9:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=571s; Masters Union at 11:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=665s)
- Running five profitable things at once works because each entity has its own CEO and co-founding team, with Aeos sitting as one co-founder among them. (Kata 1 at 43:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2617s)
- Separate companies let him give new founders meaningful ownership. In one company the cap table splits until a newcomer gets a fraction of a percent; with child companies he can hand over big chunks. (KK Create at 17:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1079s)
- At Aeos he put engineers in a separate company. He says the services company could not do AI because its DNA would be very creative, and he expected engineers and editors to fight, so Labs was created as a more engineering-focused company. (Masters Union at 9:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=571s; Masters Union at 10:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=618s)
- If you do a lot of something, spin it into a company and become its first client. The person who ran influencer work for his channel now runs a separate influencer marketing company with him as primary client. (KK Create at 42:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2525s)
- Build it like a conglomerate and stay out of the processes. Every business has a margin threshold its founders are incentivised to hit, which frees him to make content or do motion capture. (KK Create at 1:42:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=6139s)
- He admits one unsolved problem in this structure: sister companies pivot into the same space and fight for deal flow, and he has no solution yet. (Masters Union at 42:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2522s)
- He does not expect a one-person unicorn. He expects a few co-founders plus AI doing the grunt work, with all of them holding real upside, and he flags that he says this while running a 540-person company. (Masters Union at 39:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2381s)
- He builds with family because family has no breaking point. With any co-founder there is some point where one walks away; with family you fight and are normal the next day. (Masters Union at 35:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2141s)
- Non-relatives can become family as a two-way trade: give your life to the company and be half-decent at your job, and the company gives its life to you. He calls it an open family, not Bollywood nepotism. (Masters Union at 38:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2286s)
- Do not take co-founders who have never taken personal risk. Most people who earned a percentage took close to zero salary for a year and lived on savings. (Kata 4 at 51:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=3087s)

## How does he handle equity, profit share and pay?

- Co-founders in the Aeos group join at zero salary and live on equity and profit share. If the company makes nothing for 6 months they make nothing, and salary is offset only in rare cases. (KK Create at 29:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1757s)
- "Profit share is like the best alignment, right? Even better than equity sometimes." It pushes whoever runs a unit to do enough paid work without ruining the brand and to keep costs low. (KK Create at 57:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=3432s)
- Hand the keys to someone with skin in the game. Sagar runs his channel on a profit share, so an extra hire cuts Sagar's own money and he hires only if there is a multiplier. (KK Create at 43:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=2622s)
- You cannot have full market salary and skin in the game together. The people he respects offer to take the high-risk campaign and reduce their salary for a share if it works. (Kata 4 at 22:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1350s)
- He calls his approach wealth sharing, not wealth taking: giving co-founders equity lets him take risk without spending cash. (Kata 9 at 38:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2298s)
- He hands out co-founder-style equity without being asked, and says a VC-funded company would never allow it. Some game team members got equity with no strike price because they worked Sundays, and he only needs to own enough to keep control. (Masters Union at 40:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2429s)
- Pay differs across his companies because risk and stage differ, so the same role can pay X in one and 3X in another. His first two editors make three to four times a regular editor because he underpaid them early and promised to make it up. (KK Create at 1:19:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4783s)
- Market rate is whatever stops competitors sniping your talent. You pay enough that competitors are uncomfortable, and in video he says salaries rise every year. (KK Create at 1:21:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4879s)
- His way to find market rate: advertise a role at an absurdly high salary, lower it step by step, and the point where about half the candidates say no is the market rate. (KK Create at 1:22:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4927s)
- Aeos pays above market for every role and gives its best people uncapped bonuses or a percentage. If you have to ask for it, he says you are probably not where you think you are. (Kata 8 at 38:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2312s)
- Because loss hurts more than gain feels good, he gives no feel-good titles, no ballpark projections of future earnings, and no perks unless well thought out. (Kata 9 at 51:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3104s)
- He rates a small profit share in a proven, fast-growing company above bigger freelance income, because it compounds. His comparison is 1% of a company making 1 crore a year against three projects at 2 lakhs a month each. (Why Belief Matters at 34:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=2053s)

## How do services, margins and R&D work?

- A services company should not scale projects aggressively; it should care deeply about marquee clients. He was guilty of piling on projects at 24 and 25 and says Aeos grew fast by doing the opposite. (Kata 2 at 1:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=104s)
- A services company can reach software-like margins by automating parts of the work. The services company would have had 5 to 10% margins like any other, and AI avatars and AI video models pushed them much higher. (Masters Union at 9:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=571s)
- Use new AI models to raise margins in a business you already have instead of selling apps. That reasoning led Aeos to AI avatars, then AI B-roll, then an anime. (KK Create at 10:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=621s)
- Writing your secrets down is what lets a service scale. After the first client channel worked, they wrote down everything on editing, writing and channel management, hired well, and handed over the playbook. (Kata 1 at 27:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1647s)
- He later says the playbook is the org chart and not a document of know-how, because taste and muscle memory live in the people who are still there. (Kata 12 at 41:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2509s)
- An internal R&D team must commercialize or it will leave. Labs engineers would ask why they should improve another business's margins with nothing in it for them, and as young people get older they ask about career growth, so Labs does paid work. (Masters Union at 11:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=665s; Kata 1 at 38:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=2328s)
- Label every department sure shot or R&D. Sure shot means the method is understood and scales with money and hires; R&D means the method is unknown and needs founder attention and trials. (Kata 12 at 9:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=582s)
- Spend more per unit on what you know than on what you do not. A rupee on a sure shot gives a unit of output, a rupee on R&D is a gamble, so R&D roles stay low cost. (Kata 12 at 12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=725s)
- Aeos usually refuses free work but has done free or paid pilots for a select few large, trusted clients, some of whom became its biggest. For a random company with no history of paying, he says no. (Kata 13 at 1:04:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3862s)

## Product or marketing, and what do acquirers buy?

- He rates distribution about 10 times more valuable than product. With distribution you attract talent, capital and customers, which then lets you build a much better product. (Kata 10 at 0:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=56s)
- His spend flipped. At 25, of 10 rupees he put 8 into product and 2 into marketing; now it is 8 into marketing and 2 into product, and he stretches the 2 by diluting equity to get good co-founders. (Kata 9 at 58:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=3533s)
- He was sold on distribution because a past product lost to a competitor that ran 10 times as many ads. (Kata 1 at 25:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1502s)
- "Acquirers buy revenue." His last company, a SaaS, overbuilt custom technology on the belief that it would raise an acquirer's valuation, and he calls that a wrong attribution about India. (Kata 6 at 30:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1816s)
- Selling on ROAS makes you a worse business over time. Ad cost climbs from 10 rupees to 22 while you still make 20, you raise prices to cover it, and he says this is why many Indian startups are unprofitable. (Kata 6 at 10:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=615s; Kata 6 at 11:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=662s)
- A content channel is a hiring and sales engine. The same distribution that brings Aeos candidates also brings clients, which he calls the main reason Aeos has done well. (Kata 8 at 40:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2456s)
- The better question is not how to find the next client but how to build a system through which clients keep arriving. That needs a brand people believe and a product good enough to be referred. (Kata 13 at 57:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3433s)

## What does he do as CEO, and when should you scale?

- He says he spends his time on four things: the quest for market truths, distribution, experimentation, and the predictive power that comes from running enough experiments. (Kata 1 at 1:00:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3625s)
- He refuses to be a spreadsheet CEO and wants to be in the middle of the creative work. He spends 50 to 60% of his time on the game and does about 10 jobs in the rest. (KK Create at 27:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1661s)
- Most of his real job is hiring, because a good hire takes pressure off him. (KK Create at 27:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1661s)
- At scale a founder should not know everything. At 30 to 50 employees he knew every detail; now he knows the person or their manager and trusts them. (KK Create at 1:14:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4445s)
- He builds leaders by throwing them in the deep end and rescuing them only if they are truly drowning, even if the company has a hard 3 or 4 months. (KK Create at 1:10:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4256s)
- Headcount is a bad metric to be proud of, because people then add assistants and extra roles just to grow the number. (KK Create at 1:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=96s)
- "Do not scale a graph you may need to redraw." Alphas exist to find the shape of the org chart, and raising money before you are sure of the shape probably wastes it. (Kata 12 at 30:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1830s)
- You cannot pivot a large team. If you lock a shape, raise a lot and turn out wrong, you must fire people you made promises to and go back to four or five heads of departments. (Kata 12 at 36:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2167s)
- His test before scaling a product: at least 50% of people like it, 5 to 6% of a targeted beta would buy, and there is an estimate of the total market. (Kata 12 at 36:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2216s)
- A startup is only successful if it gives you time freedom. If your whole day is locked, you are not there yet. (Kata 13 at 46:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2812s)
- If people are juggling tasks, the fix may be to do fewer things and not to hire more. He gives one task to one person. (Kata 11 at 24:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1493s; Kata 11 at 24:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1444s)
- He is shutting down side projects as he gets older to focus on about four flagship things, and calls side projects a tax on his bandwidth. (Kata 11 at 4:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=293s)

# AI products and models

AI runs through his videos from May 2022 to 2026, and his view moves with the technology: awe at generated images in 2022, complaints about chatbots bolted onto everything in 2024, and daily use of coding agents by 2026. Most of it comes from about seventeen videos on single AI products or companies, with shorter passages in his interface, loading screen, notification and text videos. Every product fact, price and number is his research as of that video's date, every prediction carries its year, and the Lovable video was made in partnership with Lovable's team.

## What did he make of AI images and video, and what do they do to creative work?

- He says April 6 2022 is the day he first saw DALL-E 2's output, and he counts it among the very few times a new technology blew him away and scared him a little, next to his first internet video call and his first look at his home on Google Earth. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=0s)
- In 2022 he argued image AI hits harder than text AI because brains are built for images: he says writing is about 5000 years old, while hundreds of thousands of years of evolution wired people to judge beauty, danger and novelty by looking. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=90s)
- His four layers of mediums run from text to images to video to **presence**, a multi-sensory immersive experience. In May 2022 he mapped AI onto them: text already conquered for some time, and images, where AI had mostly done analysis, now being taken by creation with DALL-E 2. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=135s)
- In May 2022 he called DALL-E 2 groundbreaking because it showed AI could now take the image layer with creation, and said the incredible thing was the speed: he says the first DALL-E of January 2021 made 64 by 64 pixel cartoonish images and was a science experiment, and that AI reached image creation 10 to 15 years after the AI boom of around 2010. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=225s)
- In May 2022 he predicted video generation was next, and that one day you could walk up to a TV and ask for a film Quentin Tarantino would direct with a plot like Death Note. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=270s)
- In October 2022 he admitted that prediction came true far faster than he expected: a Meta research team had announced Make-A-Video a couple of days before his video. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=45s)
- In September 2024 he said the Pixel 9's Magic Editor was where he realised photography is about to change: he says it can reframe and generate what you never captured, remove objects, move you, make a mountain more impressive and add things from a typed prompt. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=360s)
- He says knowing an element is fake makes a photo less valuable, citing a 2016 Nikon contest winner with a plane added in Photoshop. He then says the Pixel camera team might have a point that the goal is the memory, since he is happy to keep a family photo assembled with Add Me. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=450s; How Smartphones Killed Photography at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=495s)
- In 2024 he said the real problem arrives when phones apply generative edits automatically: he suspects most people would not mind and would think the camera is better, and at that point the device is no longer a camera. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=540s)
- His 2024 proposal is two cameras: one that records reality in the best quality and keeps it real, and a **memories camera** with all the AI features to capture the feeling of a moment. (How Smartphones Killed Photography at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHKgXxcpo4k&t=585s)
- In 2022 he called AI the new printing press: he says Gutenberg's press in 1440 made the monks who hand copied books basically worthless while amplifying authors, and AI does the same. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=315s)
- His sorting rule for creative work under AI is authors against amanuenses: people who come up with the content get amplified, and people who copy, adapt or distribute it hold a craft that has lost most of its value because AI brings the cost of content manipulation to almost zero. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=315s; The First AI That Really Scared Me at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=360s)
- In 2022 he said AI cannot replace the top tier of creatives, because AI only evolves while there is new data to absorb, so someone has to keep introducing new information into the loop. He expected the next boundary-pushing creatives to work hand in hand with AI. (The First AI That Really Scared Me at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZdHxkx4i4w&t=360s)
- In October 2022 he said he did not think AI had taken creativity from humans and that Spielberg and Nolan do not need to update their LinkedIn profiles, though the rules of the game will change. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=135s)
- His **new thingy life cycle** has three steps: a new thing raises the quality or quantity of output in a field, a period of shock follows with predictions of mass layoffs, then people learn it is only a tool and their output gets dramatically better. His worked example is digital effects replacing practical effects after Jurassic Park. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=180s)
- "This new technology is nothing more than a tool, a multiplier." In October 2022 he said this pattern runs from medieval farming to photography and clocks, had already happened with AI images, and would happen with AI video within a year. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=270s)
- In October 2022 he expected three big changes from AI images and video: the art of the prompt, with whole marketplaces buying and selling prompts, a fight over copyright and consent to train, and a big shift in user interfaces away from graphical ones, toward typing what you want as text. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=315s)
- On consent he said in 2022 that models feed on billions of public images, an artist may not want an AI trained to copy their style, and no system for this exists yet. He cites the backlash when an Atlantic journalist ran a Midjourney illustration credited to the AI. (AI Generated Videos Are Scary at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Es01hmaXux0&t=315s)
- In October 2024 he said fully AI-generated videos fail because nothing is wrong with them: people close them at once, since they are technically perfect and totally unremarkable. (Why Ugly Things Are Cool Now at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1SWw7i9LQc&t=315s)
- He says LLMs write in corporate lingo because they were trained on corporate documents, and in 2024 he mocked the loop this creates: one person asks AI to write formally and the receiver asks AI to turn it back into plain English. (Why Ugly Things Are Cool Now at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1SWw7i9LQc&t=495s)

## How has he judged ChatGPT and chat as a product?

- In December 2022, days after release, he said ChatGPT was on a new level of scary and wonderful, and admitted he had been wrong to think AI could not get crazier that year. (ChatGPT is Frighteningly Good at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lzQD-7lZJU&t=0s)
- In December 2022 he called ChatGPT miles better than Google for many kinds of question, such as how to do integrals in Python, while also saying it has flaws and is not always correct. (ChatGPT is Frighteningly Good at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lzQD-7lZJU&t=135s; ChatGPT is Frighteningly Good at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lzQD-7lZJU&t=360s)
- He says ChatGPT felt magical in 2022 because it speaks the language of human conversation, the way AI art speaks the language of images, and he thinks it was the first conversational AI to get past the uncanny valley. (ChatGPT is Frighteningly Good at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lzQD-7lZJU&t=180s)
- As of December 2022 he listed three limits its makers added: it could not crawl the internet, its knowledge stopped at a cutoff date, and it refused responses about violence or harming people. (ChatGPT is Frighteningly Good at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lzQD-7lZJU&t=225s)
- In December 2022 he said AI had moved to a completely new trajectory that would supercharge many people's work, eliminate the work of many others and put entire sectors in disarray. (ChatGPT is Frighteningly Good at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lzQD-7lZJU&t=360s)
- In October 2023 he said ChatGPT made the old dream of a HAL-like assistant real, where Siri, Google Assistant and Alexa had failed, and that reaching 100 million users in 2 months proves it works. (Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=225s; Smartphones Are Dead But We Finally Know What's Next at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pB6TdukUsAg&t=270s)
- In February 2025 he called ChatGPT's model selector one of the most atrocious pieces of user experience: most people do not know or care which model they run and just want to ask ChatGPT. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=315s)
- He says ChatGPT was never meant to be a consumer product: the name sounds like an engineer's internal test because it was one, OpenAI's business was selling model access to companies, and the demo exploded and surprised OpenAI itself. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=90s)
- In March 2026 he said AI chat took off because its core input works: it is a text box that forgives typos and rambling voice input, and asking normally beats clicking through 40 submenus. (The Most Important Tiny Decision In Tech History at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EVZpW9Rdwk&t=810s)
- In September 2026 he said ChatGPT went mainstream where terminals never did because its text is fuzzy: it needs no exact file names and does not break on typos. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Text at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7DN3Ectm6UY&t=720s)
- In March 2026 he said AI brought waiting back: LLMs are incredibly slow next to the rest of our tech, and wait time is directly tied to how intelligent people think a model is. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=675s)
- He says he deliberately slowed his own product Flask: the full answer came back from Gemini at once, showing it all felt weird, so he made the words appear in sequence and users trusted it more. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=720s)
- "When it comes to AI, we don't want fast." He says people want the fastest response they still trust, cites the effort heuristic that slower must be better, and notes the original ChatGPT 3.5 model could now run almost instantly but nobody would use it. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=765s; The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=810s)
- In 2026 he said Google's pride in speed now hurts its AI: AI Overviews had to be fast, which means less intelligent models, and every mistake breaks trust. He predicted better AI will not end waiting, because people will always want the model that makes them wait. (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=810s; The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqhduyTnEM8&t=855s)

## Who makes the money, and are models a commodity?

- In January 2023 he argued Microsoft, not OpenAI, would be the real winner of the battle for AI and said there were three main reasons, starting with its 2019 investment of 1 billion dollars in OpenAI and a reported 10 billion dollar deal; he also said this was the first time anyone had a real chance of dethroning Google in search. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=0s; Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=45s)
- He calls Microsoft's 2019 investment of 1 billion dollars genius because part of it was cloud credits: computing is the biggest cost of running a model, so Microsoft was paying OpenAI to pay Microsoft back. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=90s)
- His **AI tower** has three layers: the front-end application such as ChatGPT, the trained model under it, and the computing power at the bottom, which for OpenAI comes from Microsoft. Other companies plug in at the model layer through APIs, as he says Notion's AI writing features did. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=225s)
- In 2023 he said Microsoft's whole plan depends on models like GPT-3 staying hard to copy, because otherwise everyone builds their own, and that nobody knew whether that would hold. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=450s)
- In February 2025 he said DeepSeek showed that keeping the best model closed and charging for it is kind of dead: it was free, the weights could be downloaded to a laptop, and the papers were public. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=135s)
- "AI models will become a commodity." In February 2025 he said the engine keeps getting cheaper and better and you can pick from several, pointing to Google's Gemini 2.0 as extremely good and extremely cheap. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=180s; People Still Don't Understand AI at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=225s)
- His **engine, car, fuel** picture of AI: the model is the engine, the application people use is the car, and GPUs are the fuel. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=540s)
- In 2025 he said the real money is in the car, not the engine: he says 75% of OpenAI's revenue in October 2024 came from regular people buying ChatGPT plans, so an engine maker got in by accidentally building the most successful car ever. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=360s)
- He says ChatGPT's real asset is that its name became the generic word for AI, like Band-Aid or Nutella, which OpenAI got by accident. In December 2025 he said that association will not last forever and OpenAI is on borrowed time. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=315s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=765s)
- In 2025 he said DeepSeek showed you do not need hundreds of millions in GPUs to train a model: he cites about 191 million dollars for Gemini and a claimed 6 million for DeepSeek, grants the figures are debated, and says roughly 10 times cheaper is the point. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=630s)
- In 2025 he used the Jevons paradox to argue Nvidia might not be finished: when something gets more efficient more people adopt it and consumption rises. He refused to say whether Nvidia deserves its valuation. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=675s)
- His February 2025 takeaway was that the AI wave is far from over, will be more open, lets smaller companies play, and that the value is in turning badly named models into products people use. (People Still Don't Understand AI at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r87NGV1Lgao&t=720s)
- "They're not building the next Google. They're building the next Facebook." In December 2025 he said there are three main reasons OpenAI might be forced to become something different from the next Google, and the first is that ChatGPT's economics are broken. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=0s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=180s)
- He says Google is designed to make you leave and ChatGPT is designed to make you stay, since a chat's main action is to ask something else. So ChatGPT accidentally built Facebook's engagement model without Facebook's monetization engine. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=360s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=405s)
- In December 2025 he called ads in ChatGPT the predictable next step given billions of non-paying users and an expensive machine, and said code hinting at ads had been found in the Android app. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=405s)
- He says a wrong Google result gets blamed on the website while a wrong ChatGPT answer gets blamed on ChatGPT, so it turns to learning about the user instead of the world, with memory and tailored answers, which he calls textbook social media strategy. The chatbot version of the echo chamber is opening every reply by telling you that you are right. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=585s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=630s)
- His **context graph** is what he says OpenAI is building: Facebook owns who you know, Google owns what you search for, and OpenAI wants who you are, down to your writing style and what you told it at 2 a.m. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=630s)
- In 2025 he said any AI model is just an app or site you must open yourself unless a browser or device makes it the default, which is why browser wars restarted with OpenAI's Atlas, Perplexity's Comet and The Browser Company's Dia. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=720s)
- He reads Sora, an app he says is built on AI-generated slop videos, along with group chats, custom GPTs and the GPT store, as social network features and not search features. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=810s)
- He ended the December 2025 video without a verdict: OpenAI could double down on being a research lab or chase distribution, and it had just gone into code red and paused side projects because Gemini got really good. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 15:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=900s)

## What does AI cost, and who will pay for it?

- In January 2023 he said a single ChatGPT response cost a few cents in computing, over a hundred times a Google search, and that OpenAI was spending 3 million dollars a day on cloud computing. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=360s; Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=180s)
- In October 2023 his tip was to use GPT-4 through an API key in a third-party front end: he said the subscription cost 20 dollars a month and his normal use came to two or three. (How I Finally Fixed My Digital Life at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7PhEfKCHr7Y&t=540s)
- In September 2025 he said AI breaks the free-product playbook because every use costs real money: he says most AI tools charge 15 dollars for something that costs 30 to run, and free plans hit a paywall after a few queries, like Gmail allowing three emails a day. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=585s)
- He says a flat AI subscription can lose money on one chat: he pays 200 dollars a month for Claude's top tier, and a single chat with the Opus model exploring a Flask feature cost 21.74 dollars. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=675s)
- In 2025 he said no AI pricing model works yet: flat subscriptions bet your usage costs less than the fee, credits cause the **taxi meter effect** of constant usage anxiety, and cost plus a 10 or 20% markup suits technical users only. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=720s)
- His September 2025 prediction is that the mainstream will refuse to pay even a tiny amount and be stuck with cheap, bad models, while a small group of power users like him pay hundreds a month. He says the free version of tools like ChatGPT is noticeably bad next to the latest models. (Tech's Weirdest Problem at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQe1r2MOwt8&t=765s)
- He says Google trained a generation to expect the internet to be free, which is why his friends outside tech think paying for AI is insane and few regular people buy the 20 dollar ChatGPT plan. He says OpenAI is playing the same game of free, habit, then convert. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=315s)
- In 2025 he said OpenAI cannot beat Google at its own game on rented Microsoft data centers: he says Google handles 8.5 billion searches a day and at ChatGPT's costs that volume would burn OpenAI's whole valuation in months. (How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=225s; How ChatGPT Is Weirdly Turning Into Facebook at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C7Z11ghwevQ&t=315s)
- In April 2026 he said the product works so well that it worked too well: he says one user spent 27,000 dollars of compute on a single 200 dollar a month subscription, so its makers are losing tons of money every single day. (Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=675s)

## What does AI do to search and the web?

- In February 2023 he said it does not matter who wins the AI search war, because conversational AI will upset the balance the web runs on and might kill the website as we know it. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=0s)
- The silent pact is his name for the web's engine: sites let search engines index their content for free, search engines send traffic back, and both make money from it. He says AI search chatbots are about to turn that balance upside down. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=90s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=180s)
- In 2023 he predicted usage data would push search engines to put chat front and center: people find chat easier than links, results get fewer clicks, and the list of links gets removed. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=315s)
- In his extreme 2023 scenario websites become like Wikipedia's footnotes: traffic to informational sites collapses, nobody has a reason to publish, and the web's engine stops. He calls it basic game theory that if the balance breaks everybody loses. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=360s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=405s)
- In 2023 he said search engines would need a way to monetise AI chatbots, and that one of the best ways could be contextual ads inside the answer, since the more an ad is integrated into organic results the better it works; another possible way was making them paid or subscription-based. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=405s)
- He proposed in 2023 that search engines pay creators by how much an AI used their content, which he says is possible because Bing's chatbot runs web searches per question, so the sources behind each answer are known. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 8:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=495s)
- He predicted paying per AI use would be the death of traditional SEO, with people skipping websites and sending information straight to the model. His half-joking name for what replaces SEO is AI feeding optimization. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=540s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=585s)
- He also allowed in 2023 that old and new might coexist, with an AI answer as a starting point, so a complex problem takes one or two site visits where it used to take 9 or 10. He said outright that he did not know which scenario would happen. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=630s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=270s)
- In February 2023 he said AI was in many cases just completely wrong: he cites Google losing 100 billion in market value over an error in a Bard promotion, and made-up numbers in Microsoft's own demo. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=720s)
- In 2023 he said AI could be the moment content moves from websites to platforms like YouTube, Reddit and Substack, and that chatbots able to read video and threads could start a renaissance of user-generated content. In his opinion the SEO model was broken from the start. (Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=765s; Uh Oh Did AI Just Kill Websites at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOSu6jSMq0M&t=855s)
- In January 2023 he said ChatGPT gave Microsoft a once in a lifetime ticket to challenge Google in search, and that Google had not moved because it has too much to lose: he says search is 58 percent of Google's revenue while Bing is only five percent of Microsoft's, so Microsoft's downside is limited and its upside is very high. (Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=270s; Who Is Really Making Billions From ChatGPT at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHMNYnjuXak&t=405s)

## Why does he say AI bolted onto apps fails, and what should replace it?

- In May 2024 he called AI a complete mess of fragmented tools: Notion for text, ChatGPT Plus for files, Copilot inside Bing for web search, Gemini Advanced to recap a video, most of it useless popups and random chatbots. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=0s)
- "For most things a chatbot is not the right way of interacting with computers." He says chatbots themselves are not the problem, and credits ChatGPT with hundreds of millions of users. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 1:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=90s)
- His 2024 example is trip planning, one of the main things chatbots say they are great at: a wall of text with no images, maps or easy way to compare fares. He cites researchers who ran a thousand itinerary queries and found GPT-4 did best with six successes. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=135s)
- He says chatbots are everywhere because they are the laziest way to add AI: pay for a model like GPT-4, build the simplest box that takes text in and gives text out, then put a skin on it. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=180s; AI Has A Huge Problem at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=225s)
- **The frenzy** is his name for the virus that hits tech when a new technology goes mainstream, from adding dot com to a company name in 2000, to blockchain, to AI toothbrushes and Samsung's Galaxy AI campaign in 2024. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=270s; AI Has A Huge Problem at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=315s)
- In 2024 he called the dream of one all-knowing assistant like Jarvis a big illusion, because it needs access to your WhatsApp, inbox, Spotify and work documents, and no company will let a rival's bot in. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=360s; AI Has A Huge Problem at 7:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=450s)
- His command line problem: chatbots and tools like Midjourney put people back to typing text, learning prompts and reading guides, the problem graphical interfaces solved over 40 years ago. He says that making thumbnails with Midjourney had him reading Reddit threads and online guides and running into a Midjourney prompting course. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=540s; AI Has A Huge Problem at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=585s)
- **Invisible AI** is his fix from 2024: start from a real problem people have, apply AI only because it helps, keep the interface people know, and do not announce that AI is inside. His mockups were a customized trip option in Skyscanner and reminders only for messages that need a reply. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=720s)
- He says users do not care whether AI is involved, only that the problem gets solved. In May 2024 he predicted useful AI would arrive once nobody used the chatbots and the hype died, naming Premiere Pro extending clips and YouTube's automated dubbing as early signs. (AI Has A Huge Problem at 12:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CY_b8w8u9NY&t=765s)
- In June 2026 he said forcing AI on users shows a product is not winning on merit, pointing at the Meta AI button in WhatsApp and the Copilot key on Windows keyboards: nobody needed a Gmail key. (The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=45s)
- His input and output rule from September 2025: graphical interfaces are great at output and bad at input, and chatbots and voice assistants are the reverse, so chatbots solved input while sending output back to the 1980s command line. (The Weird Death Of User Interfaces at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG4ONHqF1qg&t=270s; The Weird Death Of User Interfaces at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG4ONHqF1qg&t=360s)
- In 2025 he laid out a **post-UI world** in three levels, of which the first is chat beside the existing interface and the second is natural language commands given to the existing interface itself. He thinks mainstream adoption starts at level two, because the familiar interface stays visible. (The Weird Death Of User Interfaces at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG4ONHqF1qg&t=360s; The Weird Death Of User Interfaces at 9:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG4ONHqF1qg&t=585s)
- His June 2026 position is that the future is three interface types together: traditional interfaces for simple jobs, agents for very complex ones, and generative UI for the large middle where most everyday tasks sit. (The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 9:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=540s; The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=810s)

## Why did vibe coding and Claude Code take off?

- In May 2025 he said only 0.23% of people can code and they built everything, and that vibe coding is opening that privilege to everyone. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=0s; Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=45s)
- He says vibe coding breaks tech's need for scale: an app that costs 300,000 dollars to build cannot serve three people, and cheap building removes that limit. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=405s)
- His floor and ceiling picture from 2025: coding has a very high floor and an endless ceiling, while tools like Lovable have a low floor and a ceiling on complexity. He says you cannot prompt for GTA 6 and expect to get it. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=630s; Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 6:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=360s)
- He says vibe coding is starting to change the golden rule that ideas are nothing and execution is everything. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 12:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=720s)
- He grants that some developer criticism is true: vibe-coded apps may be less secure or hard to scale. In 2025 he also said computer science graduates struggle to find junior work and openings are in decline. (Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 17:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=1035s; Behind The Scenes Of Lovable's Insane Design at 18:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoVLS-Wxr1s&t=1080s)
- In April 2026 he said Claude Code got millions of people hooked, most of them not developers, and that three psychology and design principles explain it; the first is that it follows the most powerful game design principles by complete accident. (Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=0s; Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 0:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=45s)
- He maps Claude Code onto principles from The Art of Game Design: each prompt is a short feedback loop, the growing file tree is visible progress, and the status texts add personality. He says the leaked source shows exactly 187 of those texts. (Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 2:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=135s; Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=180s)
- He says Claude's imperfection helps Anthropic, because unpredictable rewards hook harder than guaranteed ones: sometimes the result is brilliant and sometimes it almost works, which pulls you into one more prompt. He says nobody at Anthropic planned this. (Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=225s; Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=270s)
- In 2026, pointing to a tweet listing them, he said a set of AI products built to be beautiful, friendly and approachable were all either dead or dying, while OpenClaw and Claude Code, which are hard to install and have no buttons or cute onboarding, became a huge success. (Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=315s)
- He says Claude Code is simple because it solves its own problems, not because it is polished: when a Notion connection failed it tried the API and found the documentation itself. He uses Tesler's law here, that complexity can only be moved, never removed. (Why Everyone is Suddenly Obsessed With Claude Code at 6:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzt52Trk9w0&t=405s)
- He says products are already rebuilding themselves for agents in 2026: Salesforce shipped a version with no interface, and Notion and Google released command line tools for agents. He still rejects the idea that everything becomes agentic with no interface. (The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 3:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=225s; The Weird Future Of User Interfaces at 5:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f32W5BEzWN0&t=315s)
- In September 2026 he said agents pushed AI tools back above the **complexity line**, the most complexity mainstream users can take. Getting agentic tools back down to regular people is one of the most valuable problems in tech, since most people still use ChatGPT like Google search. (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Text at 13:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7DN3Ectm6UY&t=810s; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Text at 14:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7DN3Ectm6UY&t=855s)

- In December 2022 he showed that ChatGPT's content limits fall to framing: a direct request for a violent scene was refused, but a soldier's diary entry produced gore, and the same trick got it to play the paperclip maximizer. He called alignment quite important. (ChatGPT is Frighteningly Good at 4:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lzQD-7lZJU&t=270s)

- In May 2024 he said the anti-smartphone movement will probably change technology even more than AI will. (What Happens Now That We All Hate Using Our Phone at 0:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LpR9zhEl2A&t=0s)

Decision: Should the command menu in a web tool that people open from the keyboard hundreds of times a day animate when it opens, or appear instantly?

== Seat: khe-brain-emil (Emil Kowalski) ==
The command menu should appear instantly: keyboard-initiated actions are never animated, because they repeat hundreds of times a day and motion makes them feel slow and disconnected from the keypress [1][2].
- Matches: he gives Raycast as the example, opened hundreds of times a day with no open animation, and calls that the best experience for it [1].
- Matches: a 500ms enter animation on every open would be frustrating [2].
- Matches: animations and hover effects seen tens or hundreds of times a day are removed, because they become annoying and make the interface feel slower [3].
- Open: if the menu animated anyway, outside marketing pages interface motion must be fast so it stays tied to the user's action [1].
- Open: continuity and spatial logic are among his named purposes for motion, shown by Sonner's toast entering and leaving along the same direction so the swipe to dismiss makes sense [1].
Conflict: Great Animations says an instant change on the web feels artificial because nothing in the physical world changes instantly [2], while You Don't Need Animations and Good vs Great Animations say sometimes the best animation is none and instant change is sometimes better [1][4].
My reading: the frequency test comes first in the articles that state it, so the "feels natural" argument applies only to what survives that test, and this menu does not.
Source: [1] You Don't Need Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations
Source: [2] Great Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations

Source: [4] Good vs Great Animations, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/good-vs-great-animations
Not in his writing: a duration or curve for a command menu that does animate, and a rule for a menu opened only occasionally; spatial consistency as the first principle of animation or motion kept where many people dislike it, a right-click menu that just appears with motion only on the way out to confirm the action, and friction added on purpose in a messenger built for play, none of which his articles state; the menu itself was not run, so this tests the description.

== Seat: khe-brain-enrico (Enrico Tartarotti) ==
His positions in the Animations video from October 2026 count against animating something opened hundreds of times a day, and the one he states for what should animate is for things users must learn exist or are waiting on anyway (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:45). He gives the reason that an animation teaches by showing where things live, and says that by the hundredth time it teaches nothing and only makes you wait (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 8:15).
Breaks: animating the menu on every open. In October 2026 he says an animation is there to teach, and by the hundredth time it teaches nothing and only makes you wait (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 8:15).
Breaks: motion on the way in. In October 2026 he says that in products built for real work a right-click menu just appears, the chosen item blinks, and the menu fades out, with motion only on the way out to confirm the action (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:00).
Breaks: slow, nature-like motion on a launcher. In October 2026 he says nature is slow and that Spotlight's liquid glass animation makes the search feature completely unusable because the point of a launcher is to open things fast (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 7:30).
Holds: his own practice in Flask, his own product, is instant for anything used hundreds of times a day, animated for things users must learn exist, and animated where the user is waiting anyway (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:45).
Holds: animation as a map of where things live. In October 2026 he calls spatial consistency the first principle of animation, and says some motion is right for that reason even when many people hate it, as with the Mac's desktop-switch slide (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 0:00; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 11:15).
Also: in March 2024 he says the mouse is intuitive but slow, which is why Arc, Linear and Superhuman make the keyboard and a command bar the main input (The Secrets That Make Your Phone Actually Work at 9:45).
Source: The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3Lo5QT7nME
Source: The Secrets That Make Your Phone Actually Work, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-YVtZV4DjCE
Not in his videos: a command menu opened from the keyboard in particular, a rule that keyboard-initiated actions are never animated or that motion disconnects an action from its keypress, any duration or curve for one, and any test of instant against animated for it. The Flask line is about his product and is not a rule for yours. The decision is yours.

# Profitable growth and founder discipline, Shiv Shivakumar

Typed notes from a guest session with Shiv Shivakumar, whom the notes introduce as PepsiCo India chief executive. He spoke about what lets a company grow for a decade, profitable growth, fixed costs, governance, advisory boards and teams, and then advised individual founders. That individual advice is left out here.

## What was said

### What lasting growth needs

- Shiv Shivakumar says only 10% of companies grow year on year for a decade.
- He says only three metrics matter in any business: revenue growth ahead of the market, employee satisfaction and operating cash flow.
- He says growing ahead of the market means you are gaining market share and your productivity is up.
- He says that when employees are happier, productivity is higher and the spend per employee per unit of work falls.
- He ties sustained growth to a founder's mentality, and contrasts incumbents with insurgents.
- He describes the opposite mindset as bureaucracy plus arrogance, and warns founders against both.
- He says a company does not need structure and process until it has 100 people, and after that it needs clear structure.
- He gives two questions for reading a market: who wins when I lose, and who loses when I win.
- He says he would not use those two questions for new-to-the-world categories, and names AI as one.

### Profitable growth

- He says profitable growth is what matters and never to go after unprofitable growth.
- He says the best companies are always very accountable for profit.
- He says to look for niches of profitable growth by geography, product, service or business model, and to ask where the money is.
- He says a company gets a valuation multiple because it has a profit.
- He says to focus on consumer value and not valuation.
- He says some businesses are inherently unviable, and gives quick commerce as an example.
- He says that in any industry the top company has 60% of the profit share and the second has 30%.
- He says scale becomes important when conditions are do or die, with Vodafone and Idea as his example.
- He says something in the business must be profitable from day one.
- He says scale once mattered in banking and no longer does, because everything is on an app.
- He says the triangle where you can make money is the USA, Japan and Europe, because per capita income is very high there.
- He says that for a tech product the user interface and experience is the key.

### Fixed costs

- He says to avoid fixed costs and keep them low by leasing or renting, because high fixed costs keep pushing the break-even point up.
- He says that when Hindustan Unilever started vending machines, nobody wanted to pay the total cost and customers wanted leases.
- He says to ride on others' assets. Apple still does not manufacture, having decided its edge is in design.
- He says R&D is the fixed cost you cannot avoid, and that 10% of a tech company's cost is R&D.
- He says that if being asset light hurts the customer experience, do not do it.

### Governance and advisers

- He says to have good governance standards and take no shortcuts, because the longevity of a business rests on being honest.
- He gives two questions a board should ask. Is there anything you believe the board needs to know, and is there anything the company is telling you not to tell us.
- He says to have an advisory board of about five people.
- He would choose advisers by who can open doors, who can add value and who knows the sector well, and would ask why they would want to be with you.
- He mentions ESOPs over two to three years as what advisers can be given.
- He says a small business needs capability and capital, and the advisory board brings capability.
- He says advisers should be reachable, close enough that you can get on a bike and meet them in 15 minutes.

### Communication and team

- He says to choose simplicity over complexity and use no jargon.
- He says you should be able to explain your idea to your grandmother or a ten year old.
- He says surface knowledge is dangerous, and that skilled people go in depth on a few issues.
- He says co-founders need a clear agreement on how they will resolve disagreements.
- He says a team needs 60% agreement but 100% commitment, and that a leader should never expect everyone to agree on everything.
- He says a leader should stop trying to please everybody and make the decision.
- He says to change course if things do not work, and that of ten things you try, three or four will not work.
- He recalls spending a year trying to get a joint venture with Mother Dairy.
- He says trust in a brand comes when you overdeliver on your promise, and not to over-promise.
- He says a good product brings consumer advocacy, and that advocacy carries the brand.
- He lists the World Economic Forum's future skills as complex problem solving, critical thinking and creativity, and adds communication and collaboration.
- He tells founders to learn game theory.

### Consumer trends

- He says the health trend is picking up as women spend less time in the kitchen, which he puts at under an hour a day.
- He says 60% of iD batter's sales come through quick commerce.

## Examples and cases used

- Apple: does no manufacturing because its edge is design.
- Hindustan Unilever vending machines: customers preferred a lease to paying the full cost.
- Vodafone and Idea: a case where scale mattered because conditions were do or die.
- Quick commerce: his example of an inherently unviable business.

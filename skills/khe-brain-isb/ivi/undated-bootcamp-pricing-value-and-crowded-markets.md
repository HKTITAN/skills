# Pricing, customer value and crowded markets

Speaker and date are withheld. This note keeps general teaching points only and leaves out the speaker's own frameworks, examples and materials. It covers how to arrive at a price, the cost of pricing too low, pricing for business customers, what value means for pricing, and competing in a crowded market.

## What was said

### Arriving at a price

- The instructor says that on day zero a founder will not know what to price the product at.
- The instructor says pricing is iterative and is going to change every year.
- The instructor names two approaches: cost-plus pricing with a planned, healthy gross margin, and pricing from the customer's willingness to pay.
- The instructor says fake landing pages that offer the same service are a way to test willingness to pay, and has seen this work well in consumer businesses.
- The instructor says a fake landing page test has to be run in a narrow local market and cannot be run across the whole country.
- The instructor says that if the first price does not sell, that is acceptable, because the founder now has a benchmark for what sells and can make the next offerings cheaper.
- The instructor says that over time demand shows which price point is the sweetest.

### The cost of pricing too low

- The instructor says that when a price starts too low, consumers form a perception, and in direct-to-consumer categories it is very difficult to raise it drastically later.
- The instructor gives a 5 to 10% improvement in average order value as an increase that is acceptable, and says anything more drastic is very hard.
- The instructor says pricing too low means low margins that are very hard to change later.
- The instructor says it is fine to price higher at the beginning if the business makes money, even though volume will be slightly lower.
- The instructor says a loss-leader approach does not work in today's market because capital has become scarce, though it can work in some spaces when capital is plentiful.
- The instructor says that if buyers come because of cheap pricing and not because of the value proposition, that is a larger failure.
- The instructor says that if the solution is good enough, price should not be the driver.
- The instructor says that in some businesses consumers stay fixated on the old price and ask why the price has risen when the offering has not changed.

### Pricing for business customers

- The instructor says a business-to-business contract can be priced however the founder wants, but the founder has to show one differentiator in the service offering for positioning.
- The instructor says a founder selling different service offerings to business customers can charge each differently, unlike a packaged product with one fixed price.
- The instructor says a founder selling to businesses should build a cohort of customers and learn which type of customer each offering works best for.

### Value and price

- The instructor says pricing is an indicator of what the company communicates to its customer.
- The instructor says value is what allows a company to price something, so the founder has to ask what the customer derives value from.
- The instructor separates the core of a product from its frills and says the customer's value does not come from the frills.
- The instructor says functional benefits are the basics.
- The instructor says value has two sides: the company has to reflect it in its product, place and pricing, and the customer has to perceive it that way.
- The instructor says value has multiple facets, and a product does not have to deliver only one of them.
- The instructor says quality should always come first.

### Competing in a crowded market

- The instructor says a founder can start with the hypothesis that they have a right to win, and pivot if six months later they conclude they do not.
- The instructor says any business has to think about what voice its brand communicates and how that voice differs from ten others in the market.

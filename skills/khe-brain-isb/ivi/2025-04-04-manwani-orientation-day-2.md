# Orientation day 2, Harish Manwani with Deepesh Agarwal, Aakriti Ahuja and Rhitiman Majumder

Typed notes from the second orientation day. Harish Manwani, chairperson of the ISB executive board and former chairman of Hindustan Unilever, spoke and took questions. Three alumni founders followed: Deepesh Agarwal of MoveInSync, Aakriti Ahuja of LaFeria Events and Rhitiman Majumder of Pickrr.

## What was said

### Purpose and values, Harish Manwani

- Harish Manwani says to dream big but be purpose driven, with clarity about your destination.
- He says a business is managed by vision, with focus on the short term and the long term and not on the medium term.
- He says managing by objectives means running the business through KPIs that you monitor and track.
- He says to lead with values and keep certain non-negotiables, and that integrity is key.
- He says running a good business is a service and running a bad business is selfishness.
- He says profit cannot be the only driver of a business. He ranks stakeholders as customer first, employees second, shareholders third and society fourth.
- He says doing good in your enlightened self-interest is not bad, and advises doing CSR that helps your business.

### Growth and relevance, Harish Manwani

- He says growth has to be sustainable, profitable and competitive, and also responsible, meaning an ethical business.
- He says a business should not swing with the fashion of the day but must stay relevant.
- He says every business is a technology business, as a user, an enabler or a creator of technology.
- He asks founders whether they are a market maker or a share taker, and says being a market maker is more exciting.
- He says five years ago the talk was about scalability and burn rate, and now it is about a proven business model and whether there is light at the end of the tunnel.
- He says being different on price is not competitive, and that a business should offer better value.
- He says brand positioning is the reason people buy the brand, and that product is key among the four Ps of product, price, place and promotion.

### Execution and people, Harish Manwani

- He says strategy is execution and execution is strategy.
- He says strategy is simply making a choice, because founders are short of resources.
- He says execution is the art of an organisation converting plans into P&Ls.
- He says all Hindustan Unilever employees were sent to learn to sell, on the shop floor, in remote villages or in factories.
- He says only two kinds of people add value, those who make soap and those who sell soap, and everything else is overhead.
- He says to spend time identifying and building talent, including brilliant people with less experience, and to surround yourself with bright people.
- For senior hires he spends little time on track record and more on values, upbringing and interpersonal relationships, and on whether they can be a team member, a team leader and work with teams.
- Between a hopelessly pessimistic young person and a mindlessly optimistic one, he would take the mindlessly optimistic one.
- He says that as a company grows the leader must create people with clear accountability and give them clarity on what they are responsible for.
- He says it is fine for the founder to be the decision maker in the early days, but the founder must cultivate the team and accept not knowing best.
- He says good enterprises need a combination of magic and logic, so follow your instincts and then make them logical.

### Why startups fail, Harish Manwani

- Asked why startups fail, he gives questions to test. Is the idea unique and does it meet an unmet consumer need, and is there the right business model to activate it.
- He says to focus on a singular idea and be patient and persevering.

### Deepesh Agarwal, MoveInSync

- Deepesh Agarwal says founders should get their hands dirty and be involved in the day to day.

### Aakriti Ahuja, LaFeria Events

- Aakriti Ahuja says not to be an ostrich and to face your fear as early as possible.
- She says founders are expected to market and represent themselves, and puts it as you are as good as your Instagram.
- She says to learn early not to keep all your eggs in one basket, and to pivot sooner rather than later.
- She says a mentor tells you that failures are okay, and that a good mentor is someone you are personally aligned with and who is from the same industry.
- She describes LaFeria as an exhibitions business for Chandigarh and Amritsar that uses Meta ads and AI for graphic design.

### Rhitiman Majumder, Pickrr

- Rhitiman Majumder says Pickrr was drowning in consumer legal cases over last-mile services and investors wanted their money back.
- He says they asked their competitor Shiprocket to buy the company for 10 crore rupees and the answer was zero.
- He says they then found another unsolved problem, and reached 36 crore rupees of revenue in 2020 with 10% EBITDA.
- He says the company existed from 2015 and found success in 2020, and that Sequoia rejected them five times.
- He says getting a customer and surviving is the hard part, and that if you show up every day for seven to eight years it works out.
- He says the founders took a 25,000 rupee salary for three years, and that you have to swallow your ego.
- He says in that period you think about the next day, not the next three to four years.
- He describes the co-founder relationship as like a bad marriage with daily fights, held together by the reminder that they had 30 people to feed.
- He says very bad days and nights either create a bond or break you.
- He says they hired senior executives from a famous school who did not work out and were let go after a year.
- He says to be worried about your cash flow.

## Examples and cases used

- Hindustan Unilever: every employee was sent to learn selling on the shop floor, in villages or in factories.
- Pickrr and Shiprocket: the rival offered zero when asked to buy the company for 10 crore rupees. Majumder calls the later exit the third highest tech deal of 2022.

## Numbers given

- Pickrr, as told by Rhitiman Majumder: 36 crore rupees revenue in 2020 at 10% EBITDA, and growth from 96 crore to 136 crore in 2022.
- Pickrr: an inbound offer of 12 million dollars from ISB alumni in 2021, and a Malaysian sovereign fund ready to write 40 million dollars.
- Pickrr exit: 200 million dollars in cash. He says people who invested 30 lakh rupees went back with 3 crore.

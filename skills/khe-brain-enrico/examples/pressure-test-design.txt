The screen breaks three positions he states and holds one, and the largest fault is motion on something people open all day (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:45; The Sneaky Psychology Of Loading Screens at 2:15; The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notifications at 1:30).
Breaks: sidebar items that animate in on every open. In October 2026 he says animated sidebar items in his own product, Flask, were cool once and then made it clunky, and his practice there is instant for anything used hundreds of times a day (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Animations at 9:45).
Breaks: a time estimate on a save screen the user is watching. In 2022 he says estimates fail on screens people actively watch, because they set an expectation and even a one second miss breaks trust with the app (The Sneaky Psychology Of Loading Screens at 2:15).
Breaks: the user's name in the push notification. His 2026 rule is that the secret is specificity, not personalisation, and that putting the user's name in does nothing (The Ridiculous Engineering Of Notifications at 1:30).
Holds: a loading state on the payment step. In March 2026 he says a payment confirmation needs one to reassure people, even if the bytes involved are tiny (The Ridiculous Engineering of Loading Screens at 2:15).

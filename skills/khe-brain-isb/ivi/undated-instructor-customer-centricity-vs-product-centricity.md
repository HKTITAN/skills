# Customer centricity versus product centricity

A marketing class taught by a course instructor who is not named in the recording and who shares the course with a second instructor. He sets out three myths about customer centricity, describes the product-centric model, then has the class debate whether Apple, Starbucks and others are customer centric. Much of the session is discussion with the class.

## What was said

### What marketing covers

- The instructor says marketing is about delivering value to a customer, which means working out who the customer is, what value means to that customer, and whether you can make that value.
- He calls communication "icing on the cake". It is of no use if you do not have the value or the setup to deliver it.

### Three myths about customer centricity

- The first myth is that the customer is always right. He says some needs are latent, and what customers say and how they behave can differ.
- He lists other reasons a customer is not right. Sometimes they are trying to get a deal, which he says is often the case, and sometimes they are angry at someone else. Sometimes they are right but go about it the wrong way.
- He says starting from the hypothesis that the customer is always right is a problem, and that a firm needs to know what it can and cannot do.
- The second myth is that being customer centric means not worrying about money. He says there is no reason to serve everybody.
- The third myth is that customer centric means customer friendly. In his words, "I will be nice to you and still keep saying no."

### The product-centric model

- He says the product-centric approach has been around for more than a century, since the industrial revolution, and that Ford and the automobile revolutionised it.
- He sums it up as create a product, market a product, sell a product and repeat, starting from what you are good at making.
- In such firms all advantages rest on the product and product expertise. They have the best engineers and scientists and spend heavily on developing the product.
- He says the brand ends up being much stronger than the customer, and the customer comes in as an afterthought once the product is built.
- He says this model worked when customers had fewer options and products took a couple of years to copy, which gave a firm time to build market share.
- Today, he says, you can see a product, call someone in China and have a launch in three months, and with GPT and AI apps can be built very fast.
- His conclusion is that having a product out there is no longer sufficient.

### What customer centricity is

- He defines customer centricity as a strategy that aligns a company's development and delivery of products and services with the current and future needs of a select set of customers.
- He says a firm that goes for the mass market is choosing to be the lowest cost provider, where cost efficiency is all that matters. A firm that thinks in terms of profit looks at a select set of customers.
- The three elements he names are acquisition, retention and development, and he adds that not all customers are equal.
- He says customer centricity has two aspects. One is customer segments. The other is using what you learn about customers to develop products for them, which is where retention comes in.
- At scale this needs data, he says. Without scale, local kirana stores and barbers are very customer centric because they are small.

### Discounts and pricing

- He says acquiring customers through discounts is not the way to run a business, and that it needs deep pockets.
- His example is Groupon, which he says shut down very fast.
- He tells the class to think about price very carefully and says competing on price is often a bad strategy.
- His measure is profitability, not market share.
- He says Jio heavily subsidised its service for its first two years, and that a price-based approach is very hard to sustain because it shows in the books.

### Apple, Starbucks and Infosys are product centric

- His verdict on Apple is that it is product centric. Its idea of the customer is amorphous, with no different treatment of students, professionals and parents.
- The signs he points to are a sparse product line, developer conference presentations that are about products and product innovation, and investment that goes mostly into product development.
- He says the first Macintosh was not customer centric. It was great technology with a graphical interface, but there was little a buyer could do with it.
- His verdict on Starbucks is also product centric. Letting customers customise a drink, for a chosen segment, does not make a firm customer centric.
- His test is whether the store knows you after three or four visits. He says that depends on the barista, and to the company customers are faceless.
- He says Starbucks' retention is its loyalty programme, which works like a price discount, and that it has no other retention strategy.
- He applies the same reasoning to Subway. It allows customisation but does not have the individual customer in mind.
- If a business is only a product and an experience, he says, competitors will copy it, and without retention and customer development it will not hold in the long term.
- A customer-centric firm that saw black coffee selling well would find out who drinks it and when, then offer a set of products around that customer's purpose.
- His services example is Infosys. If a services firm has capabilities in Oracle and not in SAP, it will sell the client an Oracle bundle with transition and cheap maintenance.
- He says such firms treat their list of capabilities as a product list, which is again a product-centric approach.

### Who is customer centric, and whether it matters

- He gives Netflix as a firm that became customer centric. People sharing one account see different recommendations because Netflix has understood who each viewer is.
- He calls Netflix's shift to a digital only format courageous, made when many thought streaming was a fad.
- He says low volume players such as Rolls-Royce can also be customer centric at their scale.
- He says very few firms are customer centric in the true sense of the word, and that most firms accommodate customers around a core set of capabilities.
- True customer centricity means rearranging the firm around what the customer wants, which he says most firms cannot do, least of all those with high investment setups.
- He expects most founders in the room to start product centric, with an idea they think they can make and think there is a need for.
- His closing point is that the aim is not to be one or the other. What matters is finding a way to long-term profitability and knowing what you need for acquisition, retention and development.

## Examples and cases used

- Ford: the origin of the create, market, sell, repeat model.
- Groupon: discount-led customer acquisition that did not last.
- Jio: heavy subsidy in the first two years as an example of price-based entry that needs deep pockets.
- Apple: product centric, judged by its product line, its launch events and where it invests.
- Starbucks and Subway: customisation without knowing the individual customer.
- Infosys: a services firm that sells from its capabilities, so still product centric.
- Netflix: personalised recommendations per viewer as customer centricity at scale.
- Rolls-Royce, local kirana stores and barbers: customer centric at low volume or small scale.

## Not covered

- Concrete retention and development tactics for a startup.
- A named framework for moving from product centric to customer centric. The recording loses the passage where the instructor begins to describe that path.

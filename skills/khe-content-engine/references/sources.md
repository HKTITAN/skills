# Sources

## The method

The engine as a folder of small linked markdown nodes, read from an index and run on one idea to get a different piece per platform, is a method adapted from Ronin's public X article on building a content engine. The copy read was a saved page of that article; it carried no link to the post, so none is given here. The article is its author's work. This skill is Harshit Khemani's adaptation, not written or endorsed by the author, and every sentence, template and example in it was written fresh.

Taken from the article, as ideas:

- one node per subject: voice, audience, each platform, hooks, repurposing, content types, scheduling;
- an index that briefs the reader instead of listing files;
- links between nodes, each with a line of context;
- the rule that each platform gets a rethought piece, never a reformatted one;
- writing the shortest version first to find the claim;
- changing the nodes when a draft is wrong.

One phrase is quoted, here and nowhere else in the skill: "same person, different room" (Ronin). It is the short form of the rule in [repurposing](repurposing.md) that the voice stays and the treatment changes.

## Left out, and why

| In the article | Here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Income, cost and team-replacement figures | Left out | Marketing claims with no evidence offered |
| "No manual writing", ready to publish with no review | Reversed: a person approves and posts every draft | A draft nobody read is how invented facts get published |
| Posting frequencies and best hours for each platform | Left out; cadence comes from the owner | No source given, and for X the published code names no hour or count |
| Claims about what each platform's algorithm rewards or penalises | Left out or listed under "Confirm before relying on this" | No evidence given; for X see [x-algorithm](x-algorithm.md) |
| Shares of performance decided by the hook, title or thumbnail | Left out | No source |
| A voice written as a description with example phrases | Replaced by quoted samples with sources | A described voice is the describer's |
| Filled-in example files and example posts | Not reproduced; the gold engine is an original, for an invented bookkeeper | The examples are the author's own work |
| Hook formulas built on replacement and revenue claims | Replaced by types that name what the brief must supply | A formula invites a number nobody measured |
| Character limits and formats stated without dates | Checked on 2026-11-10 and graded in [platforms](platforms.md) | Limits change |
| Scheduling tools and an agent that posts | Out of scope | This skill never publishes |
| A fixed set of eight platforms | One node per platform the owner posts on now | An unused node is never tuned and goes stale |

## Platform facts

Each fact, its grade and its date are in [platforms](platforms.md). The pages opened on 2026-11-10:

- X developer documentation, "Counting characters": <https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/counting-characters>
- LinkedIn Help, "Post and share updates": <https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176>

Facts marked Reported rest on several third-party pages found by search on the same day, and on press coverage of two announcements: Instagram's five-hashtag limit (December 2025) and YouTube's three-minute Shorts (October 2024). They are graded lower for that reason.

## Rules for adding a source

- Public pages only. Nothing from behind a login or a payment.
- A fact enters with its date and the page it was read on.
- A claim about reach enters only with evidence a stranger can open. Otherwise it goes under "Confirm before relying on this".

# Projects and simplicity

He treats a project as a series of cheap experiments that turn a blurry picture of the market into a known org chart, and he treats complexity as the thing that kills teams, products and messages along the way. The project method comes mostly from Kata 12, where his worked example is his own game, Unleash the Avatar, and the simplicity case from Kata 11, with the writing and spec points from Kata 2 and Kata 3. Most of this was said to rooms of Aeos employees, he says he still cannot tell whether the game will be financially successful, and several of the simplicity rules are things he says he got wrong when younger.

## What does he assume at the start of a project?

- You do not actually know what you are building when you start. A new entrepreneur does not know the market, how many people will buy or whether the thing is any good, which he calls a blurry, low-resolution photograph of both market and product. (Kata 12 at 1:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=102s)
- **Resolution** only comes from doing: the more you build and the more people you meet, the more you learn about an industry. "Action produces information." (Kata 12 at 2:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=151s)
- He credits the line about action producing information to Brian Armstrong, and adds that Aeos did not know how content would be monetised when it started and the answers showed up over time. (Kata 4 at 30:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=1833s)
- He says waterfall and agile are both outdated in 2026. Waterfall assumes the map is already known and agile assumes the next sprint's backlog is known, and neither can be known now because AI and distribution change everything too fast. (Kata 12 at 3:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=198s)
- There is no full plan. The plan and the team gain resolution together, and you know you have gained it when you know exactly what the organization structure should be. (Kata 12 at 4:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=245s)
- Much of life is starting and then following the idea that shows up, not a planned journey. The video editing school was an unplanned suggestion after Aeos struggled to hire editors, and his plans for Aeos came after that insight. (Kata 1 at 21:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1312s)
- Aeos followed a Disney-like plan of building talent that then feeds the rest. He says it went about 60 to 70% to plan and that sticking to a plan like that is rare. (Kata 9 at 47:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M&t=2820s)
- He does not believe any startup's tidy story once it is successful. Companies do random things for four years, figure it out in the fifth and then rewrite it as a plan, and he sees the messy version in investor updates. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:05:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3946s)
- He presents the method as working in 2026 while saying he still cannot tell whether projects like the game will be financially successful, and that he will say so if the method changes. (Kata 12 at 41:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2509s)

## How does he find out fast whether a project should live?

- Decide in about three months whether a project lives. For Unleash the Avatar he wanted to know within 3 months of putting the core team together. (Kata 12 at 5:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=344s)
- One experiment, a demo or a trailer, has three outcomes: great feedback so continue, unclear so get more data with another, or a waste of time so shut it down. "Find out quickly. Get out." (Kata 12 at 4:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=295s)
- Building for two years before testing is a very big risk, because you may be directionally completely off. He says many people do this after raising money. (Kata 12 at 5:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=344s)
- Every company should use vertical slicing. Make one 5 or 10 minute playable slice and keep making alphas of it, each the same or a similar slice at higher resolution, until you have full resolution of the team needed, not of the project. (Kata 12 at 14:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=868s)
- His stages are alpha, beta and gold. Alphas are for experimenting and redrawing, beta is when the shape and the conversion rate are found or almost found and you make point improvements, and gold is the bug-free version. (Kata 12 at 38:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2315s)
- A project is not successful just because it shipped. His tests are that at least 50% of people like it, 5 to 6% of a targeted beta say they would buy, and an estimate of the total market. (Kata 12 at 36:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2216s)
- He accepts false positives in his success tests for the game and uses Steam wishlists as a proxy, where about 20 to 30% of wishlisters end up buying. (Kata 12 at 36:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2216s)
- Distribution is why Aeos could run a loop of demos and feedback. He posts a story, people come to the studio, play and give feedback, and each round raises resolution. (Kata 12 at 31:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1878s)
- Feedback on the game moved from cool but not a great game on the first trailer demo, to combat on the second, to damage values and camera movement after a big third playtest. (Kata 12 at 31:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1878s)
- You will not get the best talent, feedback or mentors without putting work into the world. After the first trailer people asked why they should work for him, and after three trailers the team had people from big films and studios. (Kata 12 at 32:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1926s)
- Start every new business as cheaply as possible and spend more only once it works. He says costs on the game were kept very controlled. (KK Create at 1:18:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4687s; Kata 12 at 41:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2509s)
- A new services business at Aeos should earn money within 2 weeks, because the group already has a network of hundreds of customers. (KK Create at 30:09, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1809s)
- To anything new he does, he adds a little bit of cringe because it gets viral, and then he cleans it up. He says this was the playbook for the early AV videos, which had cringe skits in the middle of information content, and that the game got the same reaction. (Masters Union at 18:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1091s; Masters Union at 18:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1138s)
- His way to start a small business with AI has eight steps: give deep research your location and budget and ask for a number-dense one-year plan, answer its clarifying questions, take the cost list, check every number by phone or in person, feed the real numbers back for break-even, then sell and hire, set up tracking, and design incentives and KPIs for each person. (Kata 3 at 16:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1016s)
- His AI test is the checking step after AI gives you numbers: replace the slightly fake data with real data by phoning people who know, which he says covers every edge case within about a day. (Kata 3 at 20:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1253s)

## How does he build the team for something new?

- Break a project into departments before roles or people. He finds the departments by phoning people already doing the work in the industry, naming Ubisoft and indie studios for the game. (Kata 12 at 6:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=392s)
- Talk to big companies but do not copy their structure. He puts them at 70% people making sure the project goes to plan and 30% doing the thing, and says copying that gives you 70% junk in your team. (Kata 12 at 7:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=440s)
- A department is a problem and a role is a learned solution. Saying you need a cinematics department is a problem statement, and the role is how you learn to solve it. (Kata 12 at 7:20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=440s)
- Start with a **minimum viable team**: one person per department, breadth with no depth. His was seven people, one each for code, animation, cinematics, character art, level, game design, and VFX and sound. (Kata 12 at 8:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=488s)
- The order is departments, then roles, then **density**, which is the roles you discover in the gaps as a low-resolution team becomes a high-resolution one. (Kata 12 at 15:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=916s)
- Every role has five options: keep it in-house, invest in technology, outsource temporarily, hire permanent employees, or train internally with mentors. Training internally is the slowest path. (Kata 12 at 28:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1686s)
- Mentors exist to train talent that does not exist and to speed up research. They are always part-time, and the ideal shape is tutorials, one outsourced mentor and one high-potential internal person. (Kata 12 at 34:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2070s)
- His example of training with mentors is Unreal Engine VFX, where he says there is zero talent in India. A C++ engineer on the team was moved to VFX, paired with a mentor who worked on Path of Exile 2 and given Korean tutorials. (Kata 12 at 28:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1734s)
- Contractors help you find the shape fast, because tagging a role as contractor means you stop thinking about hiring, space and communication for it. (Kata 12 at 34:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2070s)
- On the game, mocap acting is tagged contractor, and localization, console compliance and porting are to be outsourced. He says porting may come back in-house if no outsourcer can do it. (Kata 12 at 16:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1014s; Kata 12 at 17:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1062s)
- To find mentors he trains his YouTube feed on top 3D animation showreels, then has Codex use his browser to go through the feed, find their emails and send a note that mentions his trailer. He says their animation mentor from Argentina would not have worked with them without seeing the first two trailers. (Kata 12 at 32:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1926s; Kata 12 at 32:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1974s)
- Hiring numbers come from **capacity math** on work the audience already accepted. Measure the time taken once feedback says the output is good, scale it to the full project, add a 30% buffer and ask which complementary roles would speed up the main role. (Kata 12 at 24:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1447s)
- His worked case of capacity math is one environment artist taking 3 months to make 30 minutes of playable level, scaled to a 10-hour game, with a separate person added for grey-box blockouts. (Kata 12 at 24:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1447s)
- Plan crisply because good hires take three to four months to arrive: a couple of months to negotiate and convince, and a couple more of notice period. (Kata 12 at 29:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1783s)
- Never hire a full-time employee for a role you are not sure will exist in a year. If you have one week of work, use a freelancer or agency. (Kata 8 at 34:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2070s)
- To fix a broken function he places an experienced head first, gives them 3 months, then spends 1 or 2 days optimising and suggesting AI. If it is not broken he does not touch it. (KK Create at 1:28:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5323s)
- Creative people and engineers need separate companies. He expected editors and engineers to fight if put together, so Aeos Labs was set up as its own engineering company. (Masters Union at 9:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=571s)

## Where does he spend effort and money inside a project?

- Label every department **sure shot** or R&D. Sure shot does not mean best in the industry, it means the method is understood and can be scaled by hiring and spending. R&D means the method itself is unknown and needs founder attention and trials. (Kata 12 at 9:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=582s)
- In R&D, maximise trials per day and ask whether you can figure it out in 5 days instead of 2 or 3 months. His examples are scripting bosses in Lua inside Unreal because C++ recompiles kill iteration, live motion capture previews and hot reload in web projects. (Kata 12 at 10:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=631s)
- Leadership should give feedback live, not two weeks later. If leadership says the hand motion is wrong after the animation is already in the game, it is too slow. (Kata 12 at 11:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=678s)
- Spend more per unit on what you know than on what you do not. A rupee on a sure shot gives a unit of output and a rupee on R&D is a gamble, so keep R&D roles low cost. (Kata 12 at 12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=725s)
- Pick one or two segments to innovate in and get sure shots everywhere else. If everything looks like R&D, a 3 or 6 month project becomes a 4 year one. "You cannot innovate everywhere." (Kata 12 at 12:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=772s)
- Do not spend R&D where you already have an advantage. Story and audio were already strong because of other Aeos teams, so the game team focused elsewhere. (Kata 12 at 13:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=821s)
- The photogrammetry R&D went in three steps: phone scans with consumer apps, then DSLR scans of a slum next door, then a shoot in Chanderi with about nine people for about 10 days after the first trailer showed the slum assets would not work. (Kata 12 at 19:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1158s; Kata 12 at 20:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1206s)
- The org chart is not only people, and sometimes the answer is technology. Buy technology only after doing the cost math and only once the role has moved from R&D to sure shot. (Kata 12 at 23:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1397s)
- His purchase math is to time one unit of work by hand, multiply by the total needed, convert to headcount and compare that cost with the equipment. Aeos rents technology during R&D and buys when sure. (Kata 12 at 23:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1397s)
- The game needs maybe 800 animations. Keyframing them in 12 months would take about 40 animators at roughly 4 crores a year, against about seven animators plus roughly 50 lakhs of motion capture equipment, which breaks even by saving five hires. (Kata 12 at 22:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1349s)
- AI should speed up the workflow, not be the product. After learning from the audience, the team decided everything audience-facing in the game should be human-made, with AI kept for workflow, communication, internal tools and shared state. (Kata 12 at 24:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1495s)
- He states the rule that audience-facing work is human-made as a decision for this game only and says he does not know if the next game will decide the same. (Kata 12 at 25:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1544s)
- Dense teams stop communicating, and artists cannot align on words alone, so he builds visual internal tools. He vibe coded a skill tree viewer that shows each skill and its work status. (Kata 12 at 25:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1544s; Kata 12 at 26:31, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1591s)

## When does he scale, raise money and reuse the playbook?

- The alphas exist to find the shape of the org graph, and raising money before you are sure of the shape probably wastes it. "Do not scale a graph you may need to redraw." (Kata 12 at 30:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=1830s)
- Funding stages map to how sure you are of the shape. Seed is freedom to learn and redraw three or four times, Series A is when you roughly know the shape and scale some of it to prove it, and Series B is when you know the shape. (Kata 12 at 35:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2119s)
- You cannot pivot a large team. If you lock a shape, raise a lot and turn out wrong, you must fire people you made promises to and go back to four or five heads of departments. (Kata 12 at 36:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2167s)
- After Series B the main business should not shape-shift. Only start the next product, improve conversion or scale the existing thing, because piling on roles and features makes a product junk. (Kata 12 at 39:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2364s)
- **Resolution reuse** is how companies become brands: a mature business starts new projects at full resolution because the graph is known. His services company has done 70 to 80 channels, so the next one starts with the full team. (Kata 12 at 40:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2414s)
- In August 2026 he says the playbook is the org chart, not a document of know-how. Know-how does not transfer by reading a departing person's write-up, because taste and muscle memory live in the people who are still there. (Kata 12 at 41:49, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2509s)
- In December 2025 he credited writing things down for scaling the client channels business: once a channel they made for Zoho got to scale, they wrote all their secrets down on editing, writing, thinking and channel management so that person number two and person number three could do it. New hires were then offered the playbook. (Kata 1 at 27:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1647s; Kata 1 at 28:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1696s)
- Overcapitalising early causes overhiring. He contrasts Aeos, which ran experiments first and hired only who it needed, with seed-funded companies that hire 30 people. (KK Create at 1:18:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4687s; Kata 3 at 14:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=873s)
- A services company should not scale projects aggressively. Young founders pick up project after project and by the third the first drops, which he says he was guilty of at 24 and 25. (Kata 2 at 1:44, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=104s)
- Aeos only takes on a new project once the old one is stable. (Kata 7 at 16:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=964s)

## Why does he argue for simplicity?

- He says seeking complexity is dangerous and that some of the best paid people on the planet are paid to convert complexity into simplicity for others. "Many people are seeking complexity, but complexity is for idiots." (Kata 11 at 1:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=102s)
- Complexity enters a company through people. You hire thinking the new person will make the task easier, but in one of his teams each hire just split the same work across more people and timelines never changed. (Kata 11 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=341s)
- Of about 600 people at Aeos, he says maybe 50 are absolutely critical. You become one by being the person who reduces complexity instead of installing it or being it. (Kata 11 at 8:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=532s)
- Startups exist because incumbents are too busy holding their own complexity together to build the next thing. (Kata 11 at 11:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=675s)
- Simple content and simple products often beat sophisticated ones. A slide deck and a stick can outperform heavy motion graphics on his channel, and God in a box, just ChatGPT's API on WhatsApp, was the simplest thing he built and got millions of users. (Kata 11 at 12:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=722s)
- Simplicity is shunned because some people need sophistication to justify their profession. He calls putting 20 apps on a CV a waste of time. (Kata 11 at 12:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=770s)
- Financially successful entrepreneurs live extraordinarily simple lives because their businesses are already complicated. (Kata 11 at 13:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=818s)
- Adding features is a crutch for weak product-market fit or lack of distribution. At Scenes, brand managers kept promising to use the product after one more feature, engineers built it, and they still went back to a WhatsApp group. (Kata 11 at 15:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=915s)
- He counts overbuilding at his last company as a wrong attribution: he believed one more feature would bring users and that custom technology would raise an acquirer's valuation, when acquirers in India buy revenue. (Kata 6 at 30:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1816s)
- Reject complexity adopted only to look like a professional company. His IT manager installed a complicated cabling system because that is how professional companies do it, which he calls a social reason with no first-principles answer. (Kata 11 at 16:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1010s)
- The rule Aeos took from Scenes is that everything it builds should be easy to understand, maintain and fix. (Kata 11 at 16:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1010s)
- A good-sounding idea is bad if nobody has checked the complexity it adds. A team member kept pushing to edit on iPads, which he says would break hiring from their editing cohort and split the software suite. (Kata 11 at 18:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1107s)
- The ideas he listens to are the ones that make a process simpler while keeping quality up. Before proposing a change, ask whether it makes the process simpler or more complex. (Kata 11 at 20:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1202s)
- Engineers in their 20s love complexity and grow out of it in their 30s. They build complicated codebases to count lines and cannot understand their own code six months later. (Kata 11 at 20:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1250s)
- Simple wins in the market even against more features. He was an Android fan who argued it had more features, and now says he would not use 90 to 99% of them. (Kata 11 at 22:27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1347s)
- Only take on something complex if you have the energy to see it through for years. He tells managers not to be the source of complexity. (Kata 11 at 27:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1635s)
- Hard things must be abstracted so the customer finds them easy. GPT-3 sat in a playground and API for a long time and took off only when it became a chatbot anyone could type into. (Masters Union at 4:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=287s)
- He preaches small teams and few projects while running a company of almost 600, and resolves it by saying a few core teams carry it. (Kata 11 at 8:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=484s)

## What simple rules does he run teams and products by?

- Teams should be two-pizza sized and own exactly one goal. He takes this from Jeff Bezos: six to 10 people or fewer, because beyond that communication overhead takes over, and with two or more goals everything goes to hell. (Kata 11 at 6:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=389s)
- One task, one person. When he was younger and short on cash he gave people with free hours a second task and good talent did substandard work. One task costs more headcount but makes ownership clear. (Kata 11 at 24:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1444s)
- If your people are juggling tasks, the fix may be to do fewer things, not to hire more. (Kata 11 at 24:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1493s)
- Prove a need manually before you automate it or hire for it, which he ties to Paul Graham's advice to do things that do not scale. He tells people selling him video editing software to edit 10 videos themselves first. (Kata 11 at 25:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1541s)
- Once one feature works, stop building and put all your energy into marketing and sales. He says successful entrepreneurs find the one thing that works, sell it and do not disturb it. (Kata 11 at 21:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=1298s)
- Do not build from scratch what you can buy. Aeos uses Zoho for project management and a third-party CRM, and he says being a wrapper on other people's abstractions is how you reduce your own complexity. (Kata 11 at 9:39, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=579s)
- He is shutting down projects as he gets older to focus on about four flagship things, and calls side projects an unnecessary tax on his bandwidth. (Kata 11 at 4:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=293s)
- He caps his influencer agency at four or five people, says no to most deals and thinks agencies with hundreds of people are a mistake. (Masters Union at 30:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1805s)
- Each Aeos company hires for itself and only finance and legal are shared, because one central hiring team would have the units fighting over talent. (KK Create at 1:37:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5849s)
- Do not spend time on the logo and the name. The more meaning they hold for you, the more attached you get, and Aeos was a random name. (Masters Union at 45:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2713s)
- For personal focus he relays Greg McKeown's rule that a task short of a nine out of 10 hell yes is a no, and Gary Keller's question about the one thing that makes everything else easier or unnecessary. He adds personal OKRs, but just one: one objective and three key results. (Kata 7 at 8:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=534s; Kata 7 at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=629s; Kata 7 at 30:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia_bqazMeIw&t=1826s)
- Break a company down into coordination cost and everything changes. He says 300 people doing coordination work at a lakh each is 3 crores a month that better planning would save. (Masters Union at 46:01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2761s)

## Why does he keep the story small?

- Other people receive a tiny fraction of what you put out, so keep your story small. People at the airport recognise him but do not know his name or what he does, and remember one video from a year ago. (Kata 11 at 3:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=198s)
- His **toilet model** pictures the viewer on the toilet, scrolling with one hand while their mother is calling. Assume they will remember about 1% of you and design the message for that. (Kata 11 at 4:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=246s)
- A brand can only hold a few stories in people's heads. He uses Steve Jobs cutting Apple from over 70 products to four, which also aligns the team because nobody knows what team number 70 is doing. (Kata 11 at 4:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tbeJZYvsg4&t=246s)
- Do not wrap everything into one brand. If one brand does 10 things people get confused and the brand becomes meaningless, and he points to Mamaearth making separate products and verticals instead of one product with 20 features. (KK Create at 17:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=1031s)
- Users are low-context: on the toilet, in the metro, doing five things at once. That is why he says the answers to a how did you hear about us question are meaningfully wrong. (Kata 6 at 14:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=856s)
- He hires people who accept that others do not read rules or watch whole videos, and does not hire people who insist the world should behave differently. (KK Create at 1:24:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=5074s)
- Consistency matters more than detail. You do not need to plan every detail of a brand, you need to repeat it over and over. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:01:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3660s)

## How do writing and specs fit into running work?

- Clear writing lowers the coordination threshold itself. Write for each person exactly what to do, their incentives and their plan, then the grand plan for how they coordinate. (Kata 2 at 44:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2662s)
- He says the best leaders write all the time. Your team sees about 5% of the picture you do, and writing lets them see all of it. "You need one-tenth the meetings you currently do if you write well." (Kata 2 at 46:45, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2805s; Kata 2 at 48:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2901s)
- For each person a leader writes the exact task, the incentives, where they are going and what a good outcome looks or sounds like. He lists the gains as direction, speed, fewer blockers, easier hiring because the job descriptions are written, and fewer meetings. (Kata 2 at 48:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2901s)
- He credits writing everything down for Aeos scaling from zero to 500 people with good margins. (Kata 2 at 48:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2901s)
- Writing is a type of thinking, and unclear writing comes from unclear thinking. The only proof you thought clearly is that you executed and it led to results. (Kata 3 at 2:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=145s)
- AI and human employees behave like a literal genie: they execute exactly what you say and fill the gaps themselves. To get what you want you must be precise. (Kata 3 at 5:41, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=341s)
- The bottleneck is almost always a missing spec, not missing skill. He retells an Andy Grove example of a new cook told to make pancakes whom nobody told golden brown on both sides. (Kata 3 at 12:56, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=776s)
- Examples are the best way to give context to humans and computers. A good file brief states the output format, the folder, the naming rule and one sample file name. (Kata 3 at 12:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=727s)
- Companies pay for experience to cover for bad briefs, and he calls senior engineers context repair technicians who fix what an underbaked brief left out. (Kata 3 at 8:57, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=537s; Kata 3 at 14:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=873s)
- A leader should think of the follow-up questions and answer them in the brief. He likes deep research because it asks questions back before it works. (Kata 3 at 17:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1063s)
- Send references before any work starts, or work gets made and then redone. Do not expect the team to know what is in your head without them. (Kata 3 at 32:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1920s; Kata 3 at 35:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=2158s)
- Requirements collection will always be human, because real companies are messy. Once requirements exist, he expects writing, shipping and testing an app to be fully automated. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:33:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=5601s)
- The weekly report he holds up as the best is a plain WhatsApp update from his hiring side: total hired this week by role, number joining next week by role, then notes on what went badly and the bottlenecks. (Kata 3 at 30:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1825s)
- Incentives plus self-reporting let a business run on autopilot, with everyone reporting what they did without the leader chasing. (Kata 3 at 29:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJFsZLgUa40&t=1778s)
- For founder's office hires he measures task drop rate: give 10 tasks and count how many are dropped without reporting back. Under 10% is good, and a task honestly reported as not doable counts as resolved. (KK Create at 1:15:40, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=4540s)
- Fix the big issue first, such as the video being bad or the client about to leave, and the small details after. (Kata 2 at 37:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2275s)
- New hires need clear week-one instructions. He admits Aeos has thrown people in the deep end and says he is working to change that. (Kata 8 at 35:18, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU&t=2118s)

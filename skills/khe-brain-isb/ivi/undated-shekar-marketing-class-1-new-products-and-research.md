# Marketing and Market Readiness class 1 on new products and market research, Siddharth Shekar

Typed notes from the first class of the Marketing and Market Readiness course, taught by Siddharth Shekar, whom the notes describe as DLabs director and associate professor of marketing at ISB. The notes begin at 10.45am, partway through the class, and cover new product development, positioning and types of market research.

## What was said

### New products

- Siddharth Shekar uses a grid with developmental risk on one axis and opportunity cost on the other.
- He says growth hacking suits cases where the developmental risk is low.
- He says that when the development risk is low you build an MVP.
- He says that for a new product to succeed, R&D and marketing must work closely, and cites research that found a 73% success rate when they do.
- He says the question to answer is who this product is for and why.

### Customers and positioning

- He describes customer heterogeneity as no two customers having the same need, with each one's reasons for buying being different.
- He says market research is used to understand how competitors' solutions are positioned in the customer's mind.
- He says you then decide how to position yourself so that customers pick you, and calls it a mind game that is all about perception.
- He says the four Ps of price, product, place and promotion are used to design a specific strategy that delivers superior value and leads to the planned position.
- He says you can categorise in whatever way helps you win, adding more Ps if you need them.
- He says not to be product centric but customer centric, and to start with the customer need.
- He says that in B2B you should not let the customer guide you unless you really need them, and should not dance to the customer's whistle.

### Market research

- He says everyone needs marketing research to grow, and that it is of three types.
- He describes exploratory research as exploring the issue at hand, descriptive research as the survey, and causal research as exposing a sample of consumers directly to the product.
- He says causal research used to be expensive and was used very judiciously, for example by showing people different ads and measuring how much they liked them.
- He says the key questions in market research are why to ask, how to ask and when to ask, and whether the person can answer the question at all.

### Latent needs

- He defines a latent need as one that customers do not realise today and so cannot express.
- He says new-to-the-world products mainly address latent needs.
- He says estimating the size of a potential market for such products is not easy with traditional research techniques.

## Numbers given

- The research he cites: a 73% success rate for new products when R&D and marketing work closely.

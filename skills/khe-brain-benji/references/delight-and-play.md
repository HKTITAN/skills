# Delight and play

One claim per line, each with its piece. Both pieces are retrospectives on consumer apps he built: Family, a crypto wallet, and Honk, a real-time messenger. They do not agree on everything; see [disagreements](disagreements.md) before you merge them.

## Three principles

- Family rests on three principles: simplicity, fluidity and delight. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- He frames the three together as respect for the user's time, intelligence and experience. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Simplicity keeps the product accessible to newcomers, fluidity keeps the experience continuous, and delight builds the connection between product and user. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Carefully made software shows respect for the person using it. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)

## Where delight goes

- Delight is selective emphasis: knowing where, when and how much to apply it. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- The Delight-Impact Curve: the less often a feature is used, the more delight it can carry, because delight wears off with repetition. He notes that an essay by Rauno Freiberg covers a very similar idea of frequency and novelty. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Features used every day get restrained touches. In the send flow the commas shift as digits are typed. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Rare moments can carry a big one: brief confetti when a wallet backup completes. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Even rarely used features, such as wallet setup, get interactive moments. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Even polish comes first. A neglected feature makes a product feel unfinished; he repeats a friend's line about a fine restaurant with a dirty bathroom. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Surprise and novelty amplify delight. The QR code ripples when tapped and turns sequin-like on a swipe, as a hidden extra. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- An easter egg fires when a typed amount is larger than the balance. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Scrubbing a price chart flips its arrow up or down. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Reordering tokens uses drag, drop and stacking motion; he credits the app Things. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Delight rewards the time and feeling a user invests. Entertainment is a side effect, and it can turn occasional visits into a habit. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- He accepted a slower launch for Family in exchange for the experience. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)

## Play

- Honk was built for play as much as for conversation. He calls the result honkish: playful, experimental, delightful. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- In Honk, "Not everything needed a purpose." Sometimes the fun was in seeing what would happen. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Honk felt more like a toy than a utility. The touches were not strictly necessary and made simple moments enjoyable. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Emojis fly across the screen in real time and can be spammed, even when that makes the chat unreadable for a moment. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Two emojis sent at once collide under a small physics engine. The hidden extra later became the Rock-Paper-Scissors game. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Magic Words are custom trigger words that set off emoji bursts where they are typed; friends used them for inside jokes. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Real-time games (trivia, tic-tac-toe, four-in-a-row and others) pause if either player leaves. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Playfulness made Honk unpredictable and encouraged discovery. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- He hopes interfaces that treat fun as serious work carry on in other products. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)

## Presence

- Ordinary chat screens are static and say little about whether a friend is there. Honk made presence visible. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Designing around presence was hard, and it is what made Honk feel alive. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Every character appears live as it is typed, typos and edits included, and a tap clears the bubble like wiping a whiteboard. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- An interactive bot taught new users the mechanics. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)

## Friction on purpose

- Outside the chat itself Honk added friction on purpose, as a contrast to the real-time core. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Finding a friend at random animates like a slot machine, and the suggested picks cycle through icons before a card stack is revealed. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Before chatting with strangers, users signed their name on screen. It was not legally binding, and he reports it reduced bad behaviour by forcing a pause. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)

## What it cost

- Honk's model divided people from the start: some found it overwhelming, others found it freeing. He treats both reactions as valid. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Honk never reached mainstream success, though devoted users loved it: over 2.6 billion characters typed and 170 million Honks sent before it closed. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- His own reasons it ended: real-time-only messaging was impractical, and group chats and Android were missing. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Consumer social is brutal and lasting success is a rare event; trying is worthwhile, and so is knowing when to stop. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)

# Raising venture money, as ivi speakers put it

One claim per line, each with its session note. Every line is what one speaker said in one session of the I-Venture Immersive programme in 2025, as written up from a recording or from typed notes. It is that speaker's view and nobody else's: not the school's, not the programme's and not a second speaker's. A figure is the speaker's figure, heard through an automatic transcript, with no source behind it. Sessions are in date order, undated ones last. Search this file for a word; do not read it top to bottom.

## Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani, 4 Apr 2025

- Anirudh Damani says customer capital always trumps venture capital. (ivi: Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-damani-customer-capital-and-scoute.md)
- Anirudh Damani describes the traditional VC way as an endless cycle of mythical valuations, high dilution and low control, building big and fixing later, and years of burn. (ivi: Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-damani-customer-capital-and-scoute.md)
- Anirudh Damani prefers fewer, high conviction funding rounds with less dilution and more founder control, and counts it a success when a founder dilutes no more than 10% in a round. (ivi: Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-damani-customer-capital-and-scoute.md)
- Anirudh Damani says the average promoter holding in Nifty companies is around 53%, against an average founder holding of 15.26%. (ivi: Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-damani-customer-capital-and-scoute.md)
- Anirudh Damani says a founder should be clear about why they are raising, and should not burn money simply because it is there. (ivi: Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-damani-customer-capital-and-scoute.md)
- Anirudh Damani says VC firms need about 75% IRR growth from a company, and asks founders whether their business can deliver that. (ivi: Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-damani-customer-capital-and-scoute.md)
- Anirudh Damani says a company that solves real problems, has a moat, sound unit economics, a right to win and is tech-enabled is a good business, but his fund also needs exponential scale potential. (ivi: Customer capital, dilution and the SCOUTE test, Anirudh Damani, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-damani-customer-capital-and-scoute.md)

## What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025

- Tarun Davda says 70 to 80% of Z47's investments are made at the idea stage, and 10 to 20% when the company has 1 to 2 crore rupees of revenue. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)
- Tarun Davda says a founder should ask whether the business can reach over 100 million dollars of revenue and be an IPO candidate, and if not should raise from angels or micro VCs. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)
- Tarun Davda says a business of 50 to 100 crore rupees is not a success for a VC firm, and that of ten cheques a VC writes only two or three generate the money. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)
- Tarun Davda ranks funding sources as customer money first, friends and family second and angel funding third. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)
- Tarun Davda says the last thing a founder wants is an investor with a very different vision of what success looks like. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)
- Tarun Davda says an investor looks for the founder, and that 99% of the decision is based on the founder. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)
- Tarun Davda asks whether a founder has a unique insight, something nobody else in that space has told him, and says not to argue that you only need 0.1% of the market. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)
- Tarun Davda asks founders for their right to play and right to win, including whether they have spent something like 10,000 hours in the industry. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)
- Tarun Davda says a pitch deck should show that you understand the market structure, the value chain and the margin at each point in it, plus your unit economics and where you can get to in 12 to 18 months. (ivi: What a venture investor looks for, Tarun Davda, 4 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-04-davda-what-a-venture-investor-looks-for.md)

## Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025

- Sarthak Ahuja says a VC fund works like a mutual fund, where limited partners put in capital without running it and general partners run it, are responsible for returns and put in some of their own money. (ivi: Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-05-ahuja-orientation-day-3-vc-fund-maths.md)
- Sarthak Ahuja says that in a 100 crore rupee fund with an eight-year lock-in, a 2% yearly fee takes 16 crore for expenses and leaves 84 crore to deploy. (ivi: Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-05-ahuja-orientation-day-3-vc-fund-maths.md)
- Sarthak Ahuja says a fund manager takes nothing up to the hurdle, 14% in his example, and 20% of the return above it, so a fund that delivers 18% keeps 20% of the extra 4%. (ivi: Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-05-ahuja-orientation-day-3-vc-fund-maths.md)
- Sarthak Ahuja says only 5% of managed funds beat the index, which he puts at 12 to 14% a year, so a venture fund has to deliver 20% for the extra risk. (ivi: Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-05-ahuja-orientation-day-3-vc-fund-maths.md)
- Sarthak Ahuja's example fund gives 2.1 crore rupees to each of 20 startups and a further 4.2 crore to the 10 that do well. (ivi: Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-05-ahuja-orientation-day-3-vc-fund-maths.md)
- Sarthak Ahuja says that if only one of a fund's 20 startups gives the return, it has to deliver a 400 crore rupee exit, which at a 20% holding means a company valued around 2,000 crore. (ivi: Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-05-ahuja-orientation-day-3-vc-fund-maths.md)
- Sarthak Ahuja says the first thing a founder should understand is what sort of return they can give an investor. (ivi: Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-05-ahuja-orientation-day-3-vc-fund-maths.md)
- Sarthak Ahuja says family offices invest in startups partly as a social symbol, and their lawyers put in five-year exit clauses though a founder can take two to three years extra. (ivi: Orientation day 3 on how a VC fund's maths works, Sarthak Ahuja and Piyush Shah, 5 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-05-ahuja-orientation-day-3-vc-fund-maths.md)

## Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025

- Anil Advani says vesting protects the company and the remaining founders: if a founder leaves, the company can buy back the unvested shares, so nobody keeps working for the equity of someone who left. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says no venture investor will invest in a company where 10% or more of the equity is dead, meaning held by someone who has left and is not contributing. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says founders should buy their shares outright on day one when the value is tiny, and agree that the company may buy back the unvested part at the original price if they leave, which he calls reverse vesting. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says the vesting concept applies to an Indian company just as to a US one, with different tax treatment and terminology, and is now standard for Indian startups. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says a typical option pool is 10%, within a usual range of 10 to 20%, sized by working backwards from the hires needed up to the Series A and larger if a senior hire such as a CTO is still to come. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says investors will not invest in an LLP, so a business that plans to raise outside money should start as or convert to a private limited company, which also shields the founder from personal liability except in fraud. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says founders in India should not assume they must be an Indian company, and that the choice of a Delaware holding structure depends on where customers, growth and investors will come from. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says an angel round should raise only enough to reach reference customers, come from about three to five angels, and give them basic information rights and nothing more. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says the angels who invest the least money cause the most problems later, such as lawsuits or forcing the company to buy them out at the next round. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)
- Anil Advani says plain equity shares with few extra rights are the simplest instrument for friends and family, that CCDs are also simple to document, and that institutional investors will almost always require preference shares. (ivi: Startup legal essentials, Anil Advani, 10 Apr 2025, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/2025-04-10-advani-startup-legal-essentials.md)

## SAFE discounts and Series A term sheets, Bhagwan Chowdhry

- Bhagwan Chowdhry says a SAFE solves the problem that nobody knows an early startup's value: the investor gives the money today and the value is fixed later, when a future investor prices the company. (ivi: SAFE discounts and Series A term sheets, Bhagwan Chowdhry, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-chowdhry-safe-discounts-and-series-a-term-sheets.md)
- Bhagwan Chowdhry derives the SAFE discount by discounting the future valuation for time and risk and again for the chance of failure. In his example 0.9 times 0.9 is about 0.8, which is a 20% discount. (ivi: SAFE discounts and Series A term sheets, Bhagwan Chowdhry, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-chowdhry-safe-discounts-and-series-a-term-sheets.md)
- Bhagwan Chowdhry says a SAFE discount of 20 to 25% is pretty standard but not fixed, and a riskier business gets a bigger discount. (ivi: SAFE discounts and Series A term sheets, Bhagwan Chowdhry, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-chowdhry-safe-discounts-and-series-a-term-sheets.md)
- The co-instructor in Bhagwan Chowdhry's class says post-money valuation is pre-money plus the investment, and the investor's stake is always the investment divided by the post-money valuation. (ivi: SAFE discounts and Series A term sheets, Bhagwan Chowdhry, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-chowdhry-safe-discounts-and-series-a-term-sheets.md)
- The co-instructor in Bhagwan Chowdhry's class says the next round's pre-money valuation is not today's post-money valuation but is set afresh from the company's stage and prospects at that time. (ivi: SAFE discounts and Series A term sheets, Bhagwan Chowdhry, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-chowdhry-safe-discounts-and-series-a-term-sheets.md)
- The co-instructor in Bhagwan Chowdhry's class says VCs write the option pool into the term sheet so that later ESOPs do not dilute them, which means the pool comes out of the founders' share. ## Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet

- The instructor says a right of first refusal applies when a founder has an outside buyer for their own shares. The investors can buy that stake first by matching the same terms, which lets them keep control while the founder still gets the price offered. (ivi: Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-bridge-financing-case-and-vc-rights.md)
- The instructor says a right of first refusal covers founders selling their own shares and not a new funding round. It is common in term sheets but not often invoked. (ivi: Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-bridge-financing-case-and-vc-rights.md)
- The instructor says a co-sale or tag-along right applies if investors decline to buy a founder's stake. The outside buyer must then also buy from the investors in proportion, so they exit alongside the founder. (ivi: Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-bridge-financing-case-and-vc-rights.md)
- The instructor says a drag-along right lets investors who have found a buyer force the founder to sell too if certain conditions are met. It is usually exercised only in extreme cases, and such provisions also get used to keep a founder in line. (ivi: Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-bridge-financing-case-and-vc-rights.md)
- The instructor says anti-dilution provisions exist because a later round can happen at a lower valuation. They make the dilution in such a down round smaller for early investors than for founders. (ivi: Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-bridge-financing-case-and-vc-rights.md)
- The instructor says investors weighing a bridge round ask two things: whether later funding will be available, and at what step-up in valuation it will happen. (ivi: Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-bridge-financing-case-and-vc-rights.md)
- The instructor says the best argument for a bridge round is to treat it as a new investment with large upside in a company whose technology is largely validated, leaving aside the money already spent. (ivi: Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-bridge-financing-case-and-vc-rights.md)
- The instructor says a venture fund's duty is to its own investors, and that venture investing moves in waves of what is hot. In the case she would decline the bridge mainly because the investment climate had changed. (ivi: Bridge financing case and investor rights in a term sheet, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-bridge-financing-case-and-vc-rights.md)

## Why investment comes in tranches, and a finance course recap

- The instructor says founders always think they want all the money upfront, but they then pay a risk premium on the entire amount and end up diluting more. In the case example, by taking the extra 25 million dollars now and not a year later the founders give up about 20 to 23% more. (ivi: Why investment comes in tranches, and a finance course recap, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-tranching-and-finance-course-recap.md)
- The instructor says tranching is good for both founders and investors because money is committed only when progress is made and uncertainty has fallen. After milestones are met investors apply a lower discount rate. (ivi: Why investment comes in tranches, and a finance course recap, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-tranching-and-finance-course-recap.md)
- The instructor says staging gives investors the option not to put in the next tranche. A founder who takes everything now denies them that option. (ivi: Why investment comes in tranches, and a finance course recap, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-tranching-and-finance-course-recap.md)
- The instructor says the share a new investor asks for is the investment divided by the post-money valuation, and existing holders are scaled down by pre-money over post-money. In the case a 5 million dollar round at 19.5 million pre-money takes founders from 38.5% to 30.6%. (ivi: Why investment comes in tranches, and a finance course recap, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-tranching-and-finance-course-recap.md)
- The instructor says a bridge round is smaller than a regular round and is meant to carry the company to its next stage. In the case it is a flat round, priced at the same valuation as the Series A. (ivi: Why investment comes in tranches, and a finance course recap, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-tranching-and-finance-course-recap.md)
- The instructor says two closely spaced tranches of one round, such as A1 and A2, are priced at the same valuation, and that valuation already assumes the second part comes only after further milestones. (ivi: Why investment comes in tranches, and a finance course recap, https://github.com/HKTITAN/skills/blob/main/skills/khe-brain-isb/ivi/undated-instructor-tranching-and-finance-course-recap.md)

# Restraint and detail

One claim per line, each with its piece. Read the context before you use a line: a Stageshow line is a rule for faux product UI (a picture of a product on a page), a Family Values or Honkish line describes a shipped app, a Liveline or Drawesome line describes a library. A number never moves from one context to another.

## The standard and its failures

- The standard for faux product UI is one sentence: it reads as a real product, cut down to what the viewer needs, framed on purpose, and "every motion has a cause". (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- Agent-made faux UI fails in five named ways: too much, too little (a wireframe), boxed, flat and bland. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow; Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- More detail makes a demo look more generated: real text in every row and numbers everywhere is the first failure on his list. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Bland is a failure of its own: a grey window with a cursor whatever the brief asked for. A brief with a style gets that style in full, built to the same rules. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The cure for all five is the same picture: a full, structured screen in quiet greys, real words only where the story is, placeholder lines elsewhere, and a crop that puts the eye on the story. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Simplify the words, never the context. An empty corner, a void under a list or rows spread to fill a pane read as unfinished, never as calm. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Words and filler

- Left alone, an agent fills every row of an inbox because it knows what an inbox holds. Keep the words the story needs and draw placeholder lines for the rest. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- Count the real strings in a scene: the title, the thing acted on, what is typed, the result. The count is usually 3 to 7. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- That count is a budget for words, never for elements: a calendar still needs a populated week and a filter still needs enough rows. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Placeholder bars are filler and belong at the edges. Bars where the eye lands read as unfinished. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A label and its value match: both words or both bars. A blank pill beside a real control is left out. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Where the story touches a list, two or three rows are real and at most as many are bars. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Keep the content that makes the domain recognisable: album art, thumbnails, avatars. Swap it for the feature's signal and the screen becomes a generic list. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- One number gets to be big. The rest are lines. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Say a thing once: one clear signal for the feature, never the same signal in the header, the satellite and every row. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The product's name appears on screen only where the real product would show it. The page around the demo already says it. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Placeholder widths vary from row to row so filler does not read as a pattern. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- When the output is the subject (a log, a diff, a terminal), real lines are the content, short and few, and the rest of the screen stays quiet. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Realism is the developer's call: at realistic fidelity, or when asked, plausible content goes everywhere, still with room to breathe. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Hierarchy

- Simplify before polishing: the fewest surfaces and strings that explain the product first, then borders and badges only where they are earned. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Simplification keeps the character. Cut repeated framing before meaningful imagery; an empty white card is not the cure. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Judge the silhouette before the details: at thumbnail size the subject, its support and the empty space make one picture. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Hierarchy comes from proportion, space, material and ground, never from more boxes. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The heaviest marks sit on what the story is about. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Space follows the story: the payoff gets the room. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Nothing is outsized: the hero element stays in proportion to a real screen. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Flat is a named failure: every object the same weight in the same bordered card leaves the eye nowhere to land. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Colour, icons and marks

- Start with five inks and one accent. Status colours mean status. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- One neutral family covers window, sidebar and cards. A coloured ground or a second material differs on purpose. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Try the fill alone before adding a border. A border is for separation, for state, or because the real product draws it. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Quiet is not faint: check text, actions and surfaces against the real ground. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A gradient or a glow serves the story or goes. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The hero card is never outlined to point at it. An accent ring reads as a focus state nobody gave it; lift it with its own surface and shadow. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- On a dark theme depth comes from a lighter surface, never from shadow. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- AI features get no sparkle, wand or star and no purple as the AI colour. Show what the AI does. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Icons come from a set, never from the hand: the product's own pack first, otherwise one set chosen on purpose, at one size and one stroke weight. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A hand-drawn glyph is the first tell of generated UI, because its weight never matches its neighbours. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- One icon family, one stroke weight and one size, 14 to 16px, per scene. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Emoji are content, never icons. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- No invented logo. A letter in a coloured square is not a product's mark: use the real mark or a plain text wordmark. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, references/brands.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A real company named in a scene carries its real mark in its original colours and proportions. A real name beside a fake mark is the one combination never shipped. (Stageshow, references/brands.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Check a brand's official source before trusting an icon catalogue; catalogues keep outdated colours and logos. (Stageshow, references/brands.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Type and spacing in a demo

- Type is set at real sizes, 11 to 15px for UI text, then scaled by the stage. Never 7px type. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- One type family: the product's or the system stack. Monospace is for code, identifiers and aligned numbers only. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Sentence case unless the real product uses capitals. Hierarchy comes from spacing and one weight step. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Fit the text before fixing the box: a control's size is decided by its words, padding and border. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Words keep 12px from the edge of a window, card, panel or hovered row. Bars may bleed off an edge; words never do. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Row padding is 12px, or 8px in a dense list. A one-line row three times taller than its words is a named fault. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A title and its subtitle read as one item with 4 to 6px of ink between the lines. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Edges 1 to 3px apart read as an accident. Line them up exactly or part them by at least 8px. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Whitespace groups: a title sits closer to its own metadata than to the next record. Lines are never spread evenly to fill a frame. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Nested shapes pad evenly, and the outer radius equals the inner radius plus that padding. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A centred amount centres its digits; the currency sign hangs outside the centring. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Fix overflow by fitting the content, never by widening the container. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Restraint in a shipped product

- Simplicity in a product is gradual revelation: the fundamentals stay visible and everything else appears when it becomes relevant. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Many wallets show every feature at once. Family did the opposite to welcome newcomers without capping depth for experienced users. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Utility, performance and security are the baseline a product starts from, not what sets it apart. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Polish is even everywhere. One neglected, rarely used screen makes the whole product feel unfinished. (Family Values, https://benji.org/family-values)
- Great software is the sum of many small, thoughtful details. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)
- Honk removed what messaging takes for granted: no send button, no chat history, no drafts, two bubbles and text that appears as it is typed. (Honkish, https://benji.org/honkish)

## One job in a library

- A library does one job in its core. Liveline's job is a line that moves smoothly as data arrives, and in his words "Everything else is opt-in." (Liveline, https://benji.org/liveline)
- Liveline needs two props, the data and the latest value, and nothing beyond React 18. (Liveline, https://benji.org/liveline)
- He built Liveline because the chart libraries he tried were too heavy for a simple live feed or too rigid to feel alive. (Liveline, https://benji.org/liveline)
- Drawesome is opinionated on purpose: the defaults are the version meant to ship, and the options exist to switch things off or move them. (Drawesome, https://benji.org/drawesome)
- Drawesome is two lines to add and has no dependency beyond React. (Drawesome, https://benji.org/drawesome)
- On a phone he removes tools and controls instead of shrinking them: under 680px his demo moves the bar to the left edge and trims the toolset. (Drawesome, https://benji.org/drawesome)

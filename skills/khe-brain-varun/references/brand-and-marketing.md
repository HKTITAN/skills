# Brand and marketing

He treats brand as world building: a consistent story that enough people believe, with ranks, rituals, shaming and exit costs, and he argues this is the only moat once AI can build any digital product. The checklist comes from The Most Valuable Skill After AI, a secret kata told to his own team using Dune, Rolex, WWE and Aeos as cases, and the attribution material comes from Kata 6, a talk to Aeos employees about Indian startups. He keeps performance marketing for the early stage even while attacking it, and his views on celebrity marketing differ between the two talks.

## What is a brand, in his view?

- "if enough people believe in a story, it becomes a brand" is his definition. Not everyone has to believe it, only enough people. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=47s)
- He thinks the word brand is the wrong frame and prefers **world building**. He says brand building and world building are one and the same. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 0:47, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=47s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 2:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=144s)
- The trigger was seeing someone clone Rocket League with an AI model in one or two prompts. If everything can be made with one prompt, going toe to toe with the AI is not what survives. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 3:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=190s)
- Commodities that survive generations do it through the story. Coca-Cola is sugared water and people still pay a premium for the can. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 3:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=190s)
- A brand is not a logo stuck on top of a product. Even for physical goods you can find a plant that sells you the raw material, so the work is a compelling and real story around it. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 3:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=238s)
- He would still play the real Rocket League over an exact clone with enough players. The lore, the history, the championships and the esports scene are what make it a world he wants to join. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 4:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=286s)
- People enter a new world through one they already know. A McLaren car he knew from Need for Speed as a kid, appearing in Rocket League, is what pulled him in. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 5:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=335s)
- An asset is only worth something to people who believe in the world. A 7 million dollar Pokemon card is just a piece of paper to him now. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 12:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=726s)
- When AI automates everything digital, world building is your only moat. The alternative is the cheap knockoff: a new logo on factory toothpaste, sold direct with generic AI ads. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:12:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4373s)
- Do not spend time on the logo and the name. The more meaning they hold for you, the more attached you get, and Aeos was a random name. (Masters Union at 45:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=2713s)
- Rich people treat reputation and brand as a second bank account, and the more important one. (Kata 13 at 1:03:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3814s)
- A system that keeps clients arriving needs a brand people believe, backed by a product that satisfies. Without the product they will not refer you. (Kata 13 at 57:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=3433s)

## What goes into a world: premise, characters, hierarchy, entry, lingo and rituals?

- His checklist has 16 parts in a fixed order: premise, characters, hierarchy, entry, language, rituals, shaming, mechanics, chance, the everyday, lore, forks, technology, eras, aesthetic and exit penalties. He runs each through Dune, Rolex, WWE and Aeos. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 9:37, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=577s)
- The premise of every brand is a contradiction, a yes followed by a but. Dune is the least hospitable planet that controls the most important substance. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=629s)
- Rolex's premise is a simple practical instrument that became a public symbol of achievement. WWE's is athletic competition that is really theatrical storytelling. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=629s)
- He gives Aeos's own premise as a bootstrapped media tech company run like a feudal empire. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 10:29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=629s)
- A world needs characters, or archetypes if it has no named characters. Rolex has the executive, explorer, diver, racer and collector, and 99% of buyers are not racers but identify with that world. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 11:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=677s)
- Public characters must stay constrained to their character. Aeos executives who run channels play two roles, and people stop watching if they act too far out of character. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 13:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=822s)
- A world needs hierarchy and visible ranks above the buyer. Rolex could sell only its most popular 10 to 15 lakh watch, but it needs higher ranks so that buyer looks up and wants more. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 15:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=917s)
- Power needs a frame of reference. WWE keeps wrestlers whose only job is to lose so the other one looks strong, and Dune puts a small figure in front of a huge building. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 16:06, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=966s)
- "A world is useless if there is no barrier to entry." In Dune the hero must learn water discipline, desert movement and worm riding before he is accepted. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 17:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1063s)
- Rolex's barrier is money through official retailers. WWE fakes qualifiers, contracts and entry positions to regulate access to status, even though its owner could just book any match. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 17:43, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1063s)
- You know you have built a world when it has its own lingo. Dune has stillsuit and crysknife, and WWE has heel, face, kayfabe, promo and pop. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 20:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1207s)
- Rituals make the thing real and add weight. Rolex could ship the watch, but there is the store smell, the couch, the bracelet fitting and the ceremonial unboxing. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 24:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1445s)
- Aeos's rituals include the upload, public demo dates for the Unleash the Avatar team, elites access to the lounge after three years, and the Aeos shoes he gives once a year. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 25:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1542s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 26:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1590s)
- The lounge opens only after three years at the company. He says it is just a room and the three-year lock exists only to give it meaning. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 26:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1590s)
- A trusted stamp travels and makes a company cooler without anything material changing. When Sequoia invests, even in a dumpster truck company, enterprises assume the founders are serious. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 6:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=382s)

## What keeps people inside: shaming, mechanics, chance, lore, forks, eras and exit penalties?

- Stories only work if there is shaming for not fitting in. There has to be a cost for not believing, it must be repeated, and he says nothing does this better than religions. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 28:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1685s)
- Aeos runs many successful channels mainly because low views are shameful there. He says that without structural shaming for low view counts Aeos would not work. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 31:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1879s)
- At Aeos an unprofitable business is shameful until it turns profitable. In venture-funded companies the shame is not growing. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 31:19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1879s)
- A world needs mechanics and laws the audience knows, even made-up ones. WWE's tap-out and rope-break rules are invented and everyone knows them. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 32:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1974s)
- Aeos's central mechanic is that distribution founders depend on operational founders, and operational founders depend on distribution founders for leads. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 32:54, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=1974s)
- Every world needs an element of chance, because without chance there is no dopamine. Rare Pokemon cards, Rolex model availability the customer cannot control, and at Aeos the views. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 33:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2022s)
- Split lore into fixed, flexible and legend. Fixed is always true, flexible is interpretation, and legend is what institutions and people say about themselves. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 39:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2355s)
- Forks are normal and happen when people disagree on what is canon. He puts religion and Bitcoin side by side as the same thing, and says Rolex's forking is tightly controlled with no real unplanned forks. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 40:04, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2404s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 41:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2502s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 42:32, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2552s)
- Every Aeos company is built as a fork. All start with the same culture on day one and differ a year later, and one that forks too far can stay in the group but leave the office and the culture. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 44:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2648s)
- A good world has eras, and you need nostalgia for the old ones. Aeos people are nostalgic for early days that had too little money and unreliable electricity. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 48:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=2890s)
- Aesthetic is a choice, not a function of money. Aeos had its highly produced, multicolour look when it was tiny, because YouTube was full of unproduced content. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 52:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3132s)
- A world needs exit penalties. Sell the Rolex and the experience is no longer yours, and leave WWE and you lose the licence to your character and music. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 52:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3179s)
- Leave Aeos and nobody cares that you were once on a channel. Coming back is hard because five people now do your old job and you would work under them. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 52:59, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3179s)
- You do not need to build the whole world. Build enough parts and competing needs produce factions and change without you. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:09:42, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4182s)
- World building makes you successful because the world's constraints shape behaviour. So many people at Aeos succeed at content because many others there already have, and low views are shamed. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:08:08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4088s)

## How much should you explain, and what are retcons and believability?

- Leave much of the world to interpretation instead of exposition. Aeos has never spelled out its family tree, and he enjoys reading Reddit threads guessing at it. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 54:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3274s)
- The more detail you give, the less engagement you get. His examples of worlds left open are Elden Ring, Returnal and the ending of Inception. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 54:34, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3274s)
- It is the owner's job to stay quiet about the unsaid parts. Unexplored places give depth and give the audience room to write fan fiction. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 59:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3566s)
- Consistency matters more than detail. You do not need to plan every part of a brand, you need to repeat it over and over. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:01:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3660s)
- Fictional characters are always honest or always dishonest and are allowed one change. Real people go up and down, and nobody believes or wants to read that. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:01:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3660s)
- He calls X and Reddit **fiction platforms**. They treat people like TV characters who are all good or all bad. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:02:35, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3755s)
- Successful worlds always escape their creators. He does not know what some Aeos companies will do next, and says he would ruin it by trying to control it. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:03:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3802s)
- A retcon goes back and changes a piece of the old story so the narrative looks consistent. In the real world it just happens, he says, and people have the memories of goldfish, so they do not remember what happened in the past. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:04:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3898s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:07:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4041s)
- He says all successful companies in India retcon: four years of doing random things, working it out in the fifth, then telling it as a plan. That is why he does not believe any startup story once the startup is successful. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:04:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3898s; The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:05:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3946s)
- In the same passage he says retconning is important, because the true messy story would confuse a newcomer. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:05:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=3946s)
- "nobody really cares about facts. Believable and realistic are different things." (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:12:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4373s)
- Giant worms are unrealistic but consistent, so they are believable. WWE is known to be scripted and is still believed. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:12:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4325s)
- He does not believe the IIT story. He says the coaching ecosystem, toppers and ads exist to convince you it is real, and that half of it is fabricated paid content. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:08:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4135s)

## Why does he attack ROAS and performance marketing?

- "The worst brands in the world sell on something called ROAS." Spend 10 rupees on an ad, make 20, scale on the difference. (Kata 6 at 5:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=330s)
- He calls these moatless businesses. Ad cost rises over time and new audiences who do not know the brand get harder to convert. (Kata 6 at 5:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=330s)
- ROAS is a cat-and-mouse game. Because ads are the easy thing everyone runs them, cost goes from 10 rupees to 22 while you still make 20, and you raise the price to cover it. (Kata 6 at 10:15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=615s)
- This is his explanation for unprofitable Indian startups. Customers in India stop paying past a point, so you get stuck at a 25 rupee price with 30 to 35 rupees of ad cost. (Kata 6 at 11:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=662s)
- With the best brands you cannot say which piece of content made you a fan. He calls misattribution the root of all evils in marketing and content. (Kata 6 at 6:17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=377s)
- Marketing managers do the measurable thing to protect themselves, not because it works. Many in Indian startups come from tech and want to report what was spent and what came back. (Kata 6 at 7:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=425s)
- Performance marketing gets you in trouble over the long run. He bases this on what he calls 100 years of advertising. (Kata 6 at 7:05, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=425s)
- Making everything measurable kills the story that makes people buy. Half of what we buy is because of story and connection, and audiences can see when you are just pushing a unit. (Kata 6 at 7:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=473s)
- His case is Nike, which he says moved from celebrating athletes to measured selling of specific shoes and lost 25 billion dollars of market value in one day. (Kata 6 at 7:53, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=473s)
- The best Indian companies acquire organically, and that is their margin. He cites Zerodha with no ads and Physics Wallah with acquisition driven by YouTube. (Kata 6 at 11:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=710s)
- He still says performance marketing is needed in the short term, because you must sell units while you build awareness. (Kata 6 at 12:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=758s)
- He endorses a line he saw in a tweet: early on all marketing should be performance, and at scale all of it should be top-of-funnel brand. (Kata 6 at 12:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=758s)
- His thresholds: under 3 to 5 crores of revenue all spend is ROAS aimed at the quick-buy audience. Beyond 100 crores all advertising is awareness and added touch points. (Kata 10 at 29:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1751s)
- The only immediate return from content in India is on products under 400 rupees. For a 2,000 to 5,000 rupee shoe people think, compare and go through a journey. (Kata 10 at 15:23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=923s)

## How should you attribute a sale?

- Attribution means finding what was actually responsible for a result. Knowing why you succeeded is what makes it repeatable, and he calls it a life skill, not only a marketing one. (Kata 6 at 2:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=141s)
- People over-credit themselves. In a pod of seven or eight on a client channel everyone claims the win, and five VFX firms on one film each claim all the VFX. (Kata 6 at 3:55, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=235s)
- Asking customers how they heard about you gives wrong data. Users are distracted, click the first option, and he says the best brand managers he has sat with do not believe it. (Kata 6 at 14:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=856s)
- Last-click affiliate credit is unfair because every touch matters. A viewer may see one affiliate's video, not convert, then convert off a second affiliate's link, and he says there is no easy fix. (Kata 6 at 15:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=950s)
- Good brand managers use **proxy attribution**: a stand-in closer to intent than the default metric, even something as manual as meeting people at an event. He says it is still wrong. (Kata 6 at 16:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=998s)
- On Instagram the proxy is shares, not views or likes. His Nvidia ad drew 7.6k shares against 3.1k for a second ad with similar views. (Kata 6 at 19:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1142s)
- He files Netflix under proxy thinking. A title bought for 10 crores must bring at least 10 crores of new subscriptions, every movie is a marketing campaign, and the library as a whole converts. (Kata 6 at 17:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1045s)
- Coupon codes are rubbish as attribution, in his words. Nobody buys on the spot, and a month later buyers with money use a coupon tool instead of the creator's code. (Kata 6 at 24:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1476s)
- "Every buyer's journey is a long journey of being convinced." It ends in a gut decision. (Kata 6 at 23:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1428s)
- For a phone the path is: see an influencer, search reviews, check other brands, compare, forget it for 30 days until payday, meet a fourth competitor, then decide on status and feeling, not specs. (Kata 6 at 23:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1428s)
- Only something around 200 rupees is a snap buy. Marketing has to be judged across the whole path, not at the last click. (Kata 6 at 23:48, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1428s; Kata 6 at 22:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1333s)
- A weak competitor that advertises feeds the stronger one. Advertising starts a comparison journey, and without strong positioning it sends the buyer to the rival. (Kata 6 at 24:36, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1476s)
- The only real way to attribute is **qualitative attribution**, done in-house. Phone buyers and people who saw the campaign, ask why they did or did not purchase, and use a very honest person. (Kata 6 at 25:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1525s)
- Only at Apple-level scale does data alone tell you. He calls knowing why people buy the main task of a company that sells a product. (Kata 6 at 25:25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=1525s)
- One brand-side metric he does track is branded search: the share of unpaid Google clicks for your name, checked quarterly. Growth of 15% a year means visibility is growing. (Kata 10 at 29:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk&t=1751s)
- What the market says and what it pays for are two different things. He says he has to read what buyers mean from their buying behaviour. (Kata 1 at 1:01:13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=3673s)

## Celebrities, costly signals, cringe and being different: what gets a brand noticed?

- In March 2026 he says people use a brand awareness campaign like ROAS, expecting to spend 1 rupee and make 2 back. Companies that grew on YouTube raise money and switch to celebrity ads run as performance advertising, which keeps rising in cost, and he calls it senseless. (Kata 6 at 11:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=662s; Kata 6 at 11:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=710s; Kata 6 at 12:38, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E&t=758s)
- In September 2026 he says to start with influencer or celebrity marketing, because world credibility is extraordinarily nepotistic. If nobody believes in your product, first show that superstars care. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4230s)
- He did not buy a Whoop until he saw celebrity athletes wearing it. Companies do IPL boundary branding to raise confidence slowly, not for conversions. (The Most Valuable Skill After AI at 1:10:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI&t=4230s)
- An expensive, risky bet is a costly signal that earns trust. Someone with 1 lakh who spends all of it is more credible than someone with 10 crores spending 1 lakh. (Masters Union at 21:21, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1281s)
- "The world doesn't care about better or worse. The world cares about different." (Masters Union at 18:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1138s)
- Infotainment creators said AVTV did not take its craft seriously for putting skits in the middle, and he says the game trailer got the same reaction for its Bollywood-movie feel. He says the world cares about different, not better or worse, so on the game he chose to double down. (Masters Union at 18:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1091s; Masters Union at 18:58, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1138s)
- He adds a little cringe to anything new he does because it gets viral, then cleans it up. Early AVTV videos were cringe skits in the middle of information content, and they sobered it up later. (Masters Union at 18:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1091s)
- Enough virality flips ridicule into interest. One mocked animation from the first trailer went viral in China, and he says a generic trailer would have gone nowhere. (Masters Union at 17:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1044s)
- In India an idea people would laugh at could be a really big idea. That was part of the thesis for AI avatars. (Masters Union at 11:51, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=711s)
- Doing the uncool thing gave Aeos two years with no competition. Nobody thought training video editors was valuable. (Kata 1 at 21:52, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI&t=1312s)
- Being unique is not that hard, in his view. Indian systems teach that it is safe to copy someone two years ahead. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:10:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=4210s)
- His rule is to always do something different and new, yet he entered influencer marketing, which he calls commoditized and weak on moat, because inbound demand made it obvious. (Masters Union at 28:30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=1710s)
- A 2.16 crore flat advertised as luxury targets people with about 1 crore, who most want the feeling of opulence and buy on a loan. Science-backed, doctor-approved brands sell better to the 10 crore group. (Kata 13 at 35:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk&t=2102s)
- Product and marketing are the same behavioural skill. Both ask whether a user will click, watch or buy. (Kata 4 at 35:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2124s)

## Why does he treat storytelling as power?

- He says the storyteller wins the world. He ties it to owning distribution instead of asking journalists for favours. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:00:33, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3633s)
- Useless objects become valuable through stories. A shoe is nothing special, but the Aeos shoes and the three-year lounge carry meaning because of the story attached. (Kata 2 at 18:28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1108s)
- A streak of success makes you prophetic to your team, and a streak of failure makes your storytelling unreliable. If you do what you said over 2 to 3 years, your stories gain meaning. (Kata 2 at 20:03, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=1203s)
- If you do not enforce rules, you lose storytelling power. When one person ignores the plan without consequence, everyone sees it is fine. (Kata 2 at 49:11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=2951s)
- He thinks all companies are delusion bubbles. You have to be a little delusional to be part of any company. (Kata 2 at 54:02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3242s)
- A closed system needs four things: written direction and shared stories, celebration of milestones, rules with enforcement, and outsiders who take it seriously. He maps the same four onto religion. (Kata 2 at 50:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k&t=3046s)
- Nobody will steal your idea. The real problem is that nobody believes it, so your job is to generate belief through sales and storytelling. (Kata 4 at 37:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2220s)
- He over-told the story in the early days of game development to get competent people in. Later the product does the storytelling. (Kata 4 at 37:00, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s&t=2220s)
- Companies run on repeated stories. Tell a story a million times and people repeat it downward. (KK Create at 1:43:07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A37ca5F9oG8&t=6187s)
- Belief is signalled through symbols. His example is Gandhi's 1930 Dandi march, which gave a large political struggle one immediate focus: salt. (Why Belief Matters at 18:24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=1104s)
- Keep the craziest belief to yourself and tell a smaller staged plan. He cites Elon Musk's multi-stage Mars plan and says Aeos works the same way. (Why Belief Matters at 15:12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE&t=912s)
- He says no ambitious project would work without PR seeding, because early skeptics make funding and talent hard to get. Positive media is a shield for one or two years, and then the product has to catch up with the story. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:05:22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3922s; PGX Ideas 2 at 1:06:10, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=3970s)
- India has been out-storytold, not out-worked. Indians were always good at hard work and technology, but talent left because America's story was stronger. (Masters Union at 1:04:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIQwPD6a-d4&t=3854s)
- Soft power is probably the most critical national asset. America creates demand through global social media, and Indian firms that clone the products get called copycats. (PGX Ideas 2 at 1:58:46, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=7126s)
- Audiences keep following people who do very bad things as long as they stay relatable online. He says there is no good and evil in how audiences treat people. (PGX Ideas 2 at 12:50, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObXOm9YqnBk&t=770s)
- A mature business that starts each new project with the team shape already known is, in his words, how companies become brands. (Kata 12 at 40:14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwQhnZ32CeE&t=2414s)

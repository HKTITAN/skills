# Framing and composition

One claim per line, each with its piece. Almost every line here is a rule for faux product UI: a picture of a product placed on a marketing page, a feature card or a docs hero. None of them is a rule for laying out a real app screen, and he has not written one (see [silent](silent.md)).

## The frame decides first

- How the screen sits in its space decides whether it reads as a product shot or as a screenshot in a box. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Framing is decided before the screen is built: a crop needs nothing past the crop, and it sets how big the type must be. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Never shrink a whole app window into a card with even margins. That is the failure he calls boxed. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow; Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- In his five-stage walk-through the first stage is a correct screen that still reads as a boxed screenshot; the second pins the window to a corner and runs it off the edge. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- The page is the ground. On a white or dark page there is no tinted plate behind the device and no whole window floating with air on four sides. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A whole window with even gutters is still valid when its contents read and the brief asks for it. The layouts are recipes, not a duty to crop. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- An unframed, centred window is the developer's call and never the agent's own choice. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The stage is the shot: a tile is cut to the stage's shape and the stage fills it edge to edge. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Crops and gutters

- One hero object, with at most one or two satellites that carry the point. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Keep what identifies the product: a title, the real chrome, the rows that give the action a reason. Logo and navigation are not required. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Crop only filler. The payoff, the action, what the cursor touches and every real word stay whole. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Crop 10 to 40 percent of the hero off the stage. The window or phone fills at least two thirds of the stage's width and runs off an edge. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- One gutter, measured in the final crop. Every other edge distance in the frame matches it. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- An edge gets a full gutter or is crossed well past it. Crossing by under 24px, or stopping a few pixels short, is a mistake. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A window keeps its device's shape. A bad fit is fixed by crop or layout, never by squashing. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- What the story opens stays in frame, a gutter from every edge on every frame. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- No dead corners at rest or anywhere in the loop. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Start flat and frontal. A tilt is earned by comparing it with the straight version at final size. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A window can fade out at its foot instead of running off, over filler only, with a mask. Never a side, never the focal content. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The medium picks the first layout: a corner bleed for a hero beside copy, a pinned bottom bleed for a card or tile, a phone pinned from the top. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Satellites and depth

- A satellite is a smaller second object (a receipt, a phone, a detail card) that straddles the hero's edge, about half outside. It is never fully inside and never floating apart. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A satellite covers chrome, never content: it straddles where the window has a sidebar, a header or a margin. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Two satellites are two kinds, never a stack of two cards at one width. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Anchored UI such as a popover, a menu or a toast is not a satellite. It sits where the product would put it. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Related surfaces get different jobs and different weights; the result sits in the hero's quiet margin. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- With several windows, choose the active step and push the others back with quieter contrast and shadow. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Size and small formats

- Design at the size the demo will be seen and show it at 1:1. A demo is never under about 320 wide. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Small formats crop harder: a 400px card holds a third of a dashboard at full size, never all of it at a third. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The phone-column version is a second design at the column's width, 358 in a 390 viewport. It is a redesign, never a squeeze. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Density follows the story: enlarge content before adding anatomy, and empty space can be hierarchy. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Chrome and devices

- Chrome is the first thing that tells the viewer what they are looking at. An address bar on a native Mac app makes the whole picture read as made up. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- In his kit no prop names a window's kind: the chrome follows from what is passed to it, and the traffic lights sit in the same place on every window. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- Browser chrome appears only when the browser is part of the picture. A desktop app gets its real chrome. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Never draw platform chrome from memory. Open the real app or a screenshot first. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- System surfaces keep their real proportions. The iOS clock is never shrunk and a notification stays at its true size. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The status bar and home indicator come from the kit's phone, true to iOS, never drawn by hand. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A phone is lit from its first frame and its background has a job. Home-screen icons are never invented. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Phones are cropped off the frame, not shown whole. In a 400px host the phone is pinned a gutter from one edge and runs off the other. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow; Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A phone cropped at the top loses its status bar and its navigation bar together. (Stageshow, references/framing.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A messaging app on an iPhone keeps the conventions of Messages unless the brief gives it a look of its own. (Stageshow, references/craft.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Platform conventions are the product's chrome and stay. A reviewer sends back an invented accent or a redrawn system control, never the convention. (Stageshow, references/review.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Bento grids

- A bento grid is one picture made of tiles, never five demos side by side. Most of its quality is decided before any tile is built. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Grids fail when every tile gets its own small window: a wall of screenshots. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow; Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- About a third to a half of the tiles show product UI. The rest are other kinds: a stat, a statement, an object, a diagram, a collection, a photo. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- One tile is the hero, bigger and carrying the richest picture. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A UI tile shows one component at real size or larger, never a window. Window chrome appears at most once in a grid, on the hero. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Neighbours contrast. Two window crops never sit side by side. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- A stat tile is one real number at display size, 48 to 72px as shown, with a short lead and a one-line label. Real figures, never filler. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A stat and its unit sit on one line and never wrap. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Tile copy is short and sits outside the picture: a title of one to four words or one sentence, set as page type at 17 to 24px. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- No real string, avatar, colour mark or chrome repeats from tile to tile. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Motion in a grid has one place to lead. A supporting tile does one small thing, once, staggered 80 to 120ms across tiles in reading order. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Each tile's picture is its own stage shown at 1:1. Wrapping the whole grid in one stage scales every tile's type down with it. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A narrow grid recomposes. The breakpoint comes from the content, not from a universal screen width. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A photo tile holds a real photo. A scene drawn in SVG or CSS to stand in for one reads as generated. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, references/backgrounds.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A grid of five usually wants one hero UI, one or two components, one stat or statement, and one object, diagram or collection. (Stageshow, references/bento.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Annotations

- Annotations stay minimal: the product remains the subject and a line carries one idea. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- An annotation explains one relationship. A line earns its place by removing ambiguity; if the layout already makes the relationship obvious, the line is left out. (Stageshow, references/annotations.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Design the product before its annotations. A line that attaches perfectly cannot rescue faint text or weak hierarchy. (Stageshow, references/annotations.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A leader is a fine line with two clear ends: 1px in a quiet ink, landing 4 to 6px short of the object it explains. (Stageshow, references/annotations.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- One relationship, one colour, never a hue per joined input. (Stageshow, references/annotations.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Direction shows transfer: a request and its response start from different ends, and the status changes after the line has landed. (Stageshow, references/annotations.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Dots move along a route only while data is flowing, never on a finished one-off request. (Stageshow, references/annotations.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- An annotation is never permission to cover the result it explains. (Stageshow, references/annotations.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Grounds and looks

- Choose the ground for what it does: a quiet solid lets dense UI read, a colour field makes a sparse picture distinctive. (Stageshow, references/backgrounds.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A vivid ground does not also need vivid controls, a coloured shadow and a glow. (Stageshow, references/backgrounds.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Changing the accent colour alone does not make a new identity. Type, content, layout, imagery, material and motion support one idea. (Stageshow, references/backgrounds.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A look is a set of tokens for colour, type, shape and density. Use the product's own look whenever there is one. (Stageshow, references/looks.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- With the product's code at hand its look is already written down: find the tokens, map them, write one look file and use it on every stage. (Stageshow, references/looks.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- For someone else's product, read the computed styles of a button, an input, a menu and a card on its public site. Take the values, never unlicensed fonts or images. (Stageshow, references/looks.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A look sets visual tokens only. Causality, continuous identity, legibility and reduced motion hold whatever the style. (Stageshow, references/looks.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- Skeuomorphic can mean restrained material depth in software UI, not an imitation appliance: the information stays clear and the physical cues stay behind it. (Stageshow, references/looks.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)

## Real products

- Recognisable products (he lists Linear, Google Docs, Slack, Figma, Raycast, Spotify, Notion and others) are the hardest test, because every error is obvious. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)
- From a product study, borrow the interaction, never another product's identity. (Stageshow, SKILL.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills; Stageshow, references/products.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- A product study being in the list is not a review. It gets the same rehearsal as a new scene. (Stageshow, references/products.md, https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills)
- The products shown belong to their owners; his demos of them are illustrations and say so. (Stageshow, https://benji.org/stageshow)

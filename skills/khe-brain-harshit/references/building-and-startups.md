# Building and startups

For him, building is a loop of putting something real in front of people, watching what they work around, and changing it fast. Since mid-2025 his settled view is that clarity comes after action, that an MVP is small but complete, that money is a tool and not a milestone, and that the next decade belongs to AI infrastructure plus human coordination. He runs several projects at once and now directs agents rather than coding. His 2022-24 posts are mostly requests to tools he used and general maxims written for LinkedIn.

## Where does a good startup idea come from?

- He says good product decisions came from watching someone work and being inside the problem, not from brainstorms; this was his recap of a TBI GEU session on building what people want. "every good decision came from being inside the problem." (2026-07-31-25-year-old-stood, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7488986888024842240)
- He says user interviews work only if you ask about the past, stay silent, go one degree out, find people annoyed right now and count patterns; ten real conversations beat a thousand form responses. "it's finding what people are already working around." (2026-07-30-talk-your-users-most, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7488632637612302336)
- In a reply he said a user persona should be built around someone who faces the problem, not around the product. (2026-07-04-mika-el-think-wed, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2073355357787975826)
- He says his first startup in 9th grade came from curiosity and noticing a problem, not from his parents or a wish to start a company. "it came from within." (2026-08-30-mecharx2-44latte-parents-didnt, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2093889521373102324)
- (2025) He wrote that a startup should be intentional about the problem it solves and the impact of its answer. (2025-01-16-conscious-unconscious-about-your, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7285653650343915520)
- (2024) He wrote that market beats product, that you should build what people need a solution for, and that good positioning means being the perfect fit for one scenario. "Market beats product" (2024-11-28-every-entrepreneur-starts-idea, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7267810893000781824; 2024-03-20-dont-build-something-people, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1770359490082193800; 2024-11-27-recently-came-across-best, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7267574465083899906)
- (2024) He wrote that most big startups since about 2010 are habit-changing platforms: people asked why anyone needed Zomato, Blinkit, Rapido or Paytm, and then those apps reshaped daily habits. (2024-09-29-if-look-almost-all, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7246177799542112258)

## When should you ship?

- He says the best startups come from FAFO: enter the market early and let reality teach you, since clarity comes after action; the skill is knowing when to move from exploring to executing, with Whop as his example. "clarity often comes after action, not before it." (2026-05-14-best-startups-often-built, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2054933799977595056)
- He says an MVP is not a broken draft but a small, complete, usable thing: a prototype tests whether it can be built, an MVP tests whether anyone will use it, the cupcake against the cake. "an MVP tests viability." (2025-07-07-most-people-misunderstand-mvps, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7347971016347873281)
- He says to stop making excuses and ship, since anyone can build now, and that iteration beats perfection. "don't make excuses, start shipping." (2026-05-20-jamboree-cooking-hard-one, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2057083461509509464; 2026-05-15-best-product-work-comes, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2055293373146390723)
- He says that when generation takes seconds, throwaway code is free, so build several versions and keep one, so having an idea and seeing it exist now fit in one afternoon. "throwaway code is finally free." (2026-06-01-gap-between-people-who, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2061415603920044197)
- He says he would rather take 100 shots at something new and fail in public than hold ideas he never executes. (2026-03-23-d-rather-take-100, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7441934265883324416)
- He says to put real work in front of real users and let the ecosystem respond. "build something real. put it out there." (2026-04-06-longest-time-synergy, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7446912340530483201)
- He says kids who build should be left to put themselves out there, not nitpicked into quitting, because early attention hands people a magnifying glass to demotivate you. (2026-08-30-early-attention-kid-hands, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2094065188098199624)
- (2025) He wrote that waiting for perfection kills progress, citing a comedian who wrote 250 scenarios for one character to test reactions in public. (2025-01-06-let-get-perfect-before, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7281992268880781314)
- (2024) He held Luna back as not ready until a founder showed him he already had an MVP, and he moved toward launching sooner. "I already have an MVP." (2024-11-27-today-s-session-yashwardhan, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7267460174527758336)

## How do you know it is working?

- For Smash&Clash he says the signal is strong retention loops, with players coming back for another game. (2026-09-13-1-building-smash-clash, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2099172012606607467)
- He says with Smash&Clash the game was never the problem, the on-ramp was; once players got it they called it extremely addictive. "so the game was never the problem." (2026-09-13-if-have-explain-your, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2099039658323870191)
- He says consistency, not intensity, keeps products alive: intensity only gets you a spike. "CONSISTENCY > INTENSITY." (2026-04-03-may-not-street-smart, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7445883249387749377)
- He says moving fast does not matter if you do not move further. (2026-08-22-moving-fast-doesn-t, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2091047043049607596; 2026-08-29-zeroxjackson-moving-fast-doesnt, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2093532092680511862)
- Drawing on Whop's time chasing NFT hype, he says chasing hot markets without building a lasting company; obsess over the core customer. "not every hot market deserves your focus." (2026-05-14-best-startups-often-built, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2054933799977595056)
- (2023) He agreed with a peer that people who really care will pay, and that leaning on free users for feedback is a gamble. "those who genuinely care, will pay" (2023-04-10-nerdymomocat-yudax-joinbento-oliverbytes, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1645382889603735552)
- (2023) In a reply about a password manager he teased Dashlane that if the demand is there, why not fulfil it. (2023-01-02-mikescops-dashlane-keep-mind, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1609835434196238336)

## Should you raise money, and what does growth cost?

- Recapping a class, he wrote that fundraising is a tool, not a milestone, and repeated the answer given there: funding helps when it solves a real problem and you know where you are going, not for validation or because others are raising. "fundraising is a tool. not a milestone." (2025-07-17-fundraising-tool-not-milestone, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7351521455324454912)
- He says growth is not only about money: dilution and outside expectations change what you built, and the trade-off shows up in every decision. "growth is not just a function of money." (2025-07-15-hardest-decisions-startups-aren, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7350851686204104704)
- He compares startups to films, an analogy he credits to Rishitabh: founder as director, investors as producers, a round as a greenlight, launch as release day. "they're creative projects." (2026-07-28-watched-odyssey-friends-recently, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7487846869885353984)
- (2025) He pointed to Tally's growth as proof that putting users first, listening and offering support can build a product without big funding. (2025-01-09-wow-500-000-users, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7283087404222107648)
- (2024) He wrote that pitching is storytelling, since people invest in passion and vision. (2024-12-13-pitching-isn-t-about, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7273394389714915328)
- (2024) He shared a founder's advice for his health tech startup: at the early stage, look for the right mentors, not a team. (2024-12-07-yesterday-attended-founders-meet, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7271009693567709184)

## Where are the startup openings in AI?

- Reading Greg Isenberg's list of opportunities, he says the next decade is an "AI infrastructure + human coordination era": AI becomes labor and apps are judged on outcomes, not attention. (2026-05-17-greg-mapped-out-what, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2055908062079410565)
- In the same post he says the best B2B opening is managed AI employees sold on outcomes, and the moat is orchestration, not the model; elder tech should make people feel capable, not monitored; and the metric for social products is repeated interaction density. "AI alone is not the moat." (2026-05-17-greg-mapped-out-what, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2055908062079410565)
- After Anthropic acquired Stainless he wrote that builders on the agent stack should assume their exit is a model lab, or pick a moat a lab cannot take in-house: distribution, regulated verticals, on-prem trust. (2026-05-18-anthropic-acquired-stainless-almost, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2056452267281379600)
- He says acquired teams mostly keep building the same products with more resources, and framed Cursor joining SpaceX as an Instagram-to-Facebook move. (2026-07-31-jayair-not-sold-acquired, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2083099818738774411; 2026-08-24-georgemayer-cursor-spacex-what, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2091685072986591399)
- He asks what if companies were internet-native by default, global from day one with incorporation as paperwork, and suggests Whop explore it. (2026-08-02-tech-unicorn-what-if, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2083801275351834681)
- He has a feeling a board game startup will reach YC sooner than people think. (2026-07-20-have-feeling-well-see, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2079244979713741275)

## How does he run his own projects?

- He says he does not think in projects but in systems: he runs several at once because ideas move between them and each teaches the others. "i don't think in projects. i think in systems." (2026-07-22-know-how-6-y, https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7485749343468011520)
- He says he tells his agents what to build and ship, and that doing things is the whole moat. (2026-10-08-waiting4-asi-basically-dont, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2108192014106018005)
- He says his competitive advantage is that he is having fun, and that his biggest blocker on Smash&Clash is procrastination and college work. (2026-07-09-purelycoders-competitive-advantage-having, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2075116286758224335; 2026-09-13-1-building-smash-clash, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2099172012606607467)
- He says third-party clients on the Smash&Clash SDK put the game in front of new people who come back to the official clients, where core features stay. (2026-10-13-thanks-honestly-client-stays, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2109908599950692465)
- He describes Likwid AI as an AI-native layer on top of ERPs that automates procurement and demand prediction. (2025-11-12-ionleu-were-building-ai, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1988447951006629946)
- He said he was building a founder tool like Bento: one link with everything investors need. (2025-12-24-benln-building-tool-similar, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2003862308817395725)
- (2022) He said Homework Bros gave about 90% of its product to students free, with a small exclusive membership as a side add-on. (2022-06-18-tdinh-thanks-well-offer, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1538087552292634624)

(2022-05-03-super-make-custom-code, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1521490345686618112; 2022-06-27-uniquebots-biodotlink-ik-domains, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1541290216119361536; 2023-03-13-man-they-really-charging, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/1635196636006850560)

## What does he want for India?

- He says India has the talent, demand and startup base for AI, so its startups should serve global customers, since software and AI ignore borders. "software doesn't recognize national borders." (2026-07-18-ive-been-thinking-about, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2078392959574429811)
- In the same essay he says India built domestic value and then exported talent, and AI offers a third model, exporting intelligence itself; foreign AI dependence is fine only if it stays temporary and India owns part of the stack. (2026-07-18-ive-been-thinking-about, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2078392959574429811)
- He says AI will be the new oil, so India must move from consumer to producer; he admits he barely uses Sarvam daily yet and urges building what people actually use. (2026-07-09-gdp-counts-what-produce, https://x.com/HarshitKhemani/status/2075038352248811734)
- He calls UPI the default payment method for Indian consumers and asked Whop and other payment products many times to add it, calling it a boost for India's creator ecosystem; in October 2026 he reported UPI live on Whop.
